## What was built

**`infrx/media/retention.py` — `RetentionCollector`** drives F2C's `ContentLifecycle` port (D10.b on PostgreSQL) and the M `ObjectStore`. It keeps no state between passes. One pass:

1. `candidates(after, limit=page_size)` in bounded pages from persisted state, following the cursor. The bucket is never listed and no process map is consulted.
2. For each candidate, bounded by `concurrency`: `claim` (a lease with the next fence on one generation), then `tombstone`. The store rechecks references and grace inside that transaction. Any refusal (`reference_live`, `not_eligible`, `claim_held`, `claim_lost`, `not_found`) keeps the object and is counted. A collector whose claim expired gets `claim_lost` and never deletes.
3. The external delete, only after the tombstone commits: outside every lock, idempotent, and addressed by generation. `generation_key(key, 1) = key` (every existing object); `generation_key(key, n) = key.g<n>`. A delayed delete of generation 1 cannot remove generation 2 written later under the same logical key. Rows naming keys outside `media/` `uploads/` `payloads/`, or with a `..` segment, are never deleted (`foreign_key`).
4. `acknowledge_delete`. For `location = database` there is no external step: the acknowledgement is the scrub, per F2C.b ("claimed, tombstoned and scrubbed; the acknowledgement records the scrub").
5. Retain and report. `DependencyUnavailable` from the database (candidates, claim, tombstone, ack) or from the object store ends the pass. Anything not yet deleted is kept. The `Report` carries pages, max batch, max in flight, deleted, retained by reason, delete failures, lost acks, the oldest finished pending delete, and the abort reason.
6. `run(interval_s)` is the schedule hook. It logs each pass, logs a failed pass, and continues.

**Test support: `tests/m/test_lifecycle_standin.py`.** Three worlds implement the same port.

- `f2c`: F2C-L's committed `FakeLifecycle`, reached through the real `admit_ready` / cancel flows.
- `fake`: `DraftLifecycle`, in memory. It implements the coordinator's D2/D3 plus the database bodies, row-level holds and the scrub-on-ack that F2C.a/b's fake does not model.
- `pg`: `PgLifecycleStandIn`. The same contract in real SQL on the task-local PostgreSQL, in a throwaway `m6` schema: row locks, READ COMMITTED, the database clock. `locking=False` is the deliberately broken variant with no reference lock.

The `pg` world is **test support, not a migration**: D10's `0020` replaces it. `fake` and `pg` are deleted once D10.b's adapter commits, and a `d10` world joins `WORLDS`.

## Measured (local PostgreSQL 16, controlled store clock; not a product claim)

`test_pg_load_and_cleanup_together_never_delete_live_content`, three runs:

- **Setup:** 60 eligible old objects, 40 admissions (new source + attach to an old object the collectors are deleting), two collectors (page 8, concurrency 4), every 10th acknowledgement lost.

| Metric | Runs |
|---|---|
| Old attaches won / refused `content_retiring` | 9/31, 10/30, 10/30 |
| Invariant: every attach that succeeded readable | held in all runs |
| max batch | 8 (= page size) |
| max in flight | 4 (= concurrency) |
| max pending-delete age | 60-90 s store time (claim TTL 60 s, a pass every 30 s of store time) |
| passes / drain passes after recovery | 17-19 / 5-7 |
| physical deletes | 110-114 for 100 objects (lost-ack repeats are idempotent) |
| load phase wall time | 3.2-5.4 s |

**Definitions for I8:**

- **Batch** = `page_size`, the most candidates one read returns.
- **Concurrency** = `concurrency`, the most candidates in flight.
- **Pending-delete age** = `claimed_at(re-claim) - tombstoned_at` of a delete finished late.
- **Bound**, while the stores answer: pending-delete age <= claim TTL + one pass interval. Eligible content goes within one interval of `eligible_at`/`retain_until`, plus one extra interval per aborted pass.

The claim TTL must exceed the worst-case object-store call. `s3.py` puts that at about 70 s (2 attempts x (5 s connect + 30 s read) + backoff), so the claim TTL should be at least 300 s. F2C's 30 s window is a test fixture only.

## Point 3 — phase-2 plan (design only; the files are M5's until it merges)

**Process maps.** State after M5, and the change for each:

| Map | Today | Phase 2 |
|---|---|---|
| `MediaUploads.uploads` | M5 replaces it with `UploadRepository` | verify nothing process-local remains |
| `MediaStaging.refs` `(org, handle) -> MediaRef` | the authority for `med_` handles | the authority moves to the content row + 0003 `staged_media`; the map becomes a bounded LRU read-through (OrderedDict, `MAX_PROCESS_ENTRIES`) |
| `by_job` | the local copy of the attach | read from `ExecutionReadiness` (the manifest); delete the map, or cap it LRU |
| `prepared_by_job` | popped by the worker only (`933dda3`) | delete: its only other reader is `gc.py`, which loses object deletion |
| `payloads` | until process exit | pop once admission has read it (the durable `jobs.payload_ref` is the record) |
| `idle_since` | written for `gc.py` only | delete with `gc.py`'s object deletion |
| `ProcessingCache.entries` | TTL sweep (unscheduled) + byte cap | bounded by the keeper below |

The worker's `CountMemo` is already bounded (`MEMO_ENTRIES` + TTL; W5's).

**Writers register before they write** (store/uploads/prepare):

- `row = await lifecycle.register(ContentIdentity(..., origin=written))`, then `_write_once(generation_key(key, row.generation), ...)`.
- `content_retiring` becomes a retryable 503. It is already typed `DependencyUnavailable` with `retry_after_s`.
- Readers build the physical key from the manifest generation: `prepare()` rebuilds `_key(...)`, so it becomes `generation_key(_key(...), source.generation)`, and `local_uri`/cache paths carry the generation too. **Until this lands, the delayed-delete protection is not in effect** for objects the runtime re-creates (they are written at the bare key).

**Local cache (`ProcessingCache` + a `LocalCacheKeeper` replacing `gc.py`'s cache half):**

1. **Index recovery.** A sidecar `source.<ext>.json` is written atomically beside each file: full digest, mime, bytes, profile, preparer version, stored_at, probed facts. On start and lazily, the index is rebuilt from the sidecars. A sidecar/file mismatch (size, then a lazy hash on first use) is a miss and is removed. `.part` files and files without a sidecar are removed after the grace. A restart resets only the stray clock, which errs towards keeping.
2. **Key validation.** The key is (org, digest, profile_version, preparer_version). A sidecar from another preparer/profile version is a miss and is evicted. Model/processor versions do not change profile v1's bytes (the source is the artifact). They key the token-count memo instead (W5, already R105). Cold cache is not cold GPU (no claim).
3. **High-water eviction.** `statvfs` on the cache root. Above the high water, evict least-recently-used unpinned entries until below the low water; the configured byte cap works the same way.
4. **Admission under pressure.** A `put` that would cross the hard floor refuses to cache. `prepare` raises retryable `DependencyUnavailable`, the lease lapses and the job re-prepares later, bounded by the preparation deadline (R93). No partial file.
5. **Never evict an opened input.** The worker holds `flock(LOCK_SH)` on the cache file from engine submit to terminal. The keeper takes `LOCK_EX|LOCK_NB` and skips on failure. This works across processes, and the kernel releases the lock on a crash.
6. **Retention.** Startup refuses `processing_cache_ttl_s` longer than the durable serving retention (P-25), so a local copy never outlives its content.

**Phase-2 tests:**

- (a) 10k finalized uploads/stages/attaches leave every map at or under its cap (M5's "memory bounded under many finalized tickets" too).
- (b) A new `ProcessingCache` over the same root recovers entries and probed facts. A tampered file or sidecar is a miss and removed; `.part` is removed after the grace.
- (c) An entry from another preparer version is a miss and re-prepared, and the old one evicted.
- (d) Filling past the high water evicts LRU down to the low water. An entry held with `LOCK_SH` (a second open file description) survives even over the cap.
- (e) A put past the hard floor makes `prepare` a retryable refusal, with no file; the job is re-prepared once space frees.
- (f) Cache disabled or full: the durable artifact is still written and correctness holds.
- (g) Cross-process: the gateway writes, the worker pins, the keeper evicts around the pin.
- (h) A writer uses `generation_key` (delayed-delete case against the real `MediaUploads`).
- (i) `gc.py` reduced to the keeper. Its object-deletion cases and their ~20 mutants in `tests/m/mutants.py` retire, and the local-only oracle moves into `test_retention.py` as a 10-line class. **The RV-03 probe (`reproduce.py`) flips then**: `MediaCollector` no longer deletes objects.

## Proposed ruling text (coordinator assigns the number)

> Content deletion is authorized only by a committed tombstone of a fenced, unexpired claim on one `(content_id, generation)`.
>
> - The external delete runs after that commit, outside every lock, and is idempotent. It is addressed by `generation_key(key, generation)`: generation 1 is the bare key, generation n > 1 is `key.g<n>`.
> - Database content has no external step: the acknowledgement is its scrub.
> - A pass stops on `DependencyUnavailable` from either store (retain and report).
> - Only keys under `media/`, `uploads/` and `payloads/` without a `..` segment are ever deleted.
> - The claim TTL exceeds the worst-case object-store call.
