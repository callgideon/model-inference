## 1. W5-F5 R2: the post-marker attach cannot refuse a ready job

### Trace: what `media.attach` still does after `admit_ready`

1. **`admit_ready` binds the manifest.** It runs in one transaction, `0019_upload_readiness.sql:834-904`. It calls `bind_source` for every ref (`:890`, body at `:733-788`), which writes `infrx.staged_media` and `infrx.job_media`, the latter with `content_id`/`generation` (`:780`). It then writes the marker `job_readiness` (`:893`). These `job_media` rows are the same 0003 rows that the durable attach reads (`media/attachments.py:40` `_BOUND`).

2. **The worker reads that committed manifest, not the gateway's attach.**
   - `PreparationRunner._manifest` (`worker/preparation.py:348`) reads `readiness.readiness(job_id).sources`.
   - `MediaPreparation.prepare` (`media/prepare.py:531`) reads `attached()`. In the worker process that falls through to `PgAttachments.get` over those same rows (`worker/__main__.py:148`).

3. **What the gateway's attach does on this door** (`relay.py:303` → `MediaUploads.attach`, `media/uploads.py:396` → `MediaStaging.attach`, `media/store.py:359-386`):
   - It re-resolves each ref against this process's records (`store.py:372`).
   - `PgAttachments.put` (`attachments.py:64`) finds the rows already bound and compares them. It is a no-op when they are equal, and `conflict` otherwise.
   - It sets the in-process `by_job` copy.
   - So on the readiness door the attach is **redundant for durability**. What it adds is the process-local copy that `_resume` reads, which falls back to the durable record.

4. **Refusals it can still raise for refs this process staged.** Each one arrives after the marker, so after the job may have run:

   | Refusal | Cause |
   |---|---|
   | `not_found` | `_staged_ref` misses. `self.refs` is a `Recent` map (`store.py:205`, the M6 R5 ponytail): more than 4096 writes between stage and attach evict the hand-off. |
   | `not_found` / `upload_expired` | An upload record no longer resolves field for field. M5 item 3 turns `upload_expired` into a no-op only when `attached()` equals the refs. |
   | `conflict` | `by_job` or the durable binding differs (`store.py:383`, `attachments.py:64`). |
   | `dependency_unavailable` | The attachments table or the ticket repository is unreachable, or the call passes `DEPENDENCY_BOUND_S`. |

5. **Before this change**, each of these reached `_admitted`'s handler:
   - A non-dependency refusal led to `cancel`. That answers the committed outcome of a job that already finished, and then the refusal was re-raised (409 for a `succeeded`, settled job; the fails-before is below).
   - A `dependency_unavailable` was a 503 for a job that runs and is billed. An unkeyed client that follows the 503's retry advice admits a second job.

### Decision

**Chosen:** past the marker, the attach is non-refusing. Moving the attach before the marker is impossible: `attach` needs the job row (`job_org`, and `PgAttachments.put` locks `infrx.jobs`), so it cannot precede the admission. Skipping it on this door is also wrong, because the fake and dev compositions (`MediaUploads` with `attachments=None`) prepare from the in-process `by_job` the attach writes.

**The change** is an inner `except errors.DomainError` around the attach only (`relay.py:303-316`):
- On the pre-D10 door (`self.readiness is None`) it re-raises into the existing handler, so cancel-and-answer and the money-B2 503 are unchanged.
- On the readiness door it logs one warning, `intake.record(self.registry, "inc", POST_MARKER_REFUSED, code=refused.code)`, and returns. The answer is then the committed outcome, or for SSE the handle and stream, exactly as on the F-5 path.
- Since W5-F5 the attach is the only step left in the `try` on this door, and nothing after `_admitted` in `admit` can refuse.

**What still cancels:** a `CancelledError` (the handler cancelled, or the process stopping) still reaches the outer handler and cancels. That is the existing "no identity reached the caller" rule (`test_api_modes__a_task_cancelled_while_attaching_cancels_the_job`), and it answers no refusal to anyone.

**`dependency_unavailable` does not keep the 503 on this door.** Why:
- The retry could not cancel a job that ran, because `_resume` → `_admitted` on this door neither rechecks (W5-F5) nor cancels (this change).
- But the 503 itself is an error answered for a job that may run and be billed, and an unkeyed retry is a second job.
- In the pilot the outage cost nothing: the manifest was already durable.
- On the pre-D10 door the 503 stays, because there nothing is claimable until the refs are bound.

**The metric** follows the WR-I8-3 precedent: `intake.record` writes only once `observe.metrics.FAMILIES` declares the family. An undeclared family never raises on the request path. It is declared by WR-W5F5B-1 below.

## Proposed ruling (coordinator numbers it; extends the W5-F5 ruling)

> A job the admission transaction made execution-ready is answered only its committed outcome (or its handle). Every refusal of the pinned card or capability happens inside that transaction; no later step of the acceptance may answer a refusal for it. In particular the gateway's attach of the staged refs after `admit_ready` is a copy of the manifest that transaction committed: a refusal or an outage from it is logged and counted (`infrx_post_marker_refusals_total{code}`), never answered and never a reason to cancel. The job's own worker reads the committed manifest, and a job the manifest cannot prepare ends by its preparation or deadline rules and is answered as such. Only the pre-D10 door (`jobs.admit`/`admit_credit`, no marker, nothing claimable until the refs are bound) keeps the post-admission recheck, cancel-on-refusal and the retryable 503. A handler cancelled before any identity reached the caller still cancels the job on either door. The contract fake refuses at admission exactly what 0019 `check_pinned_capability` refuses (every part's modality against the pinned revision, both regimes; a missing pinned revision is `not_found`).
