## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, well past "in principle": vLLM `main` nightly (≥ 2026-09-10, e.g.
   `vllm/vllm-openai:nightly-cd10ed6f9f6b37a8ace9cf380007e66fe12ec0c3`) is `"b200": "verified"`
   in DeepSeek's own vLLM recipe, and two independent public B200 datasets exist — an
   InferenceX/SemiAnalysis agentic sweep on 4× B200 and a vLLM performance PR on 8× B200
   [src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash), [src](https://github.com/SemiAnalysisAI/InferenceX/pull/2960), [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/56686). SGLang (`lmsysorg/sglang:dev-dsv41`) also runs but is 8.4× slower at concurrency 64 (§3.4).
2. **Min 4 GPUs, recommended 8.** TP4 fits the 510.29 GB checkpoint with +28.1 GB/GPU of KV
   headroom; TP8 (or TP8 + `--enable-expert-parallel` = EP8) leaves +91.9 GB/GPU. TP2 is
   arithmetically impossible even with the Engram tables offloaded (−0.1 GB at 0.90
   utilisation). The **KV cache is replicated on every TP rank** (MLA latent, `num_key_value_heads: 1`) — measured, §1.2.
3. **Weight format actually executed: native MXFP4 experts as W4A8** (MXFP4 weights × MXFP8
   activations), non-expert tensors FP8 E4M3 on 32×32 UE8M0 tiles, embeddings BF16. W4A8 runs
   on Blackwell's **FP8** line (4,500 TFLOPS dense), **not** the 9,000 TFLOPS FP4 line — only
   NVIDIA's separate NVFP4 W4A4 build can reach that, and no speedup for it has been published ⚠️ (§2, §6.4).
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $0.39–$1.08 per 1M output tokens** at 3,604 output
   tok/s/GPU (measurement-anchored, batch 640, TPOT 22.2 ms), across $5.10 reserved → $14.00
   hyperscaler. **Max-throughput (S4): $0.30–$0.83 per 1M output tokens** at 4,696 tok/s/GPU
   `est.` The TPOT ≤ 50 ms SLO never binds on B200 for this model — the KV pool does.
5. **Confidence: estimate**, with two directly-on-point B200 measurements as anchors. The
   single most important B200-specific fact is in §3.4: at concurrency ≤ 32 B200 matches B300
   and GB300 to within 2.5 %, and at concurrency 128 on 100K-token agentic traffic it
   **collapses** (TTFT 48 s) while B300/GB300 do not. On this pair B200 is capacity-limited, not compute-limited.

## 1. Fit

### 1.1 Inputs, and what can and cannot be sharded

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---:|---|

| Everything else | 307.53 GB | derived |
| KV per token | **890 B** FP4 (720 main + 170 indexer) / 1,650 B FP8 / 3,200 B BF16 | [architecture.md §5.1–5.2](architecture.md) |

| Activation workspace | 6 GB/GPU at n ≥ 4, 8 GB at n < 4 `est.` | [b200.md §2](../../gpus/b200.md) band 2–6 GB, raised for this model's 4× mHC residual stream ([architecture.md §2.7](architecture.md)) and 16,384-token chunked prefill |

**Tensors that cannot be sharded (replicated on every rank), ≈ 0.36 GB/GPU:**

| Tensor group | Bytes | Why it replicates |
|---|---:|---|
| `layers.N.ffn.gate.weight` + `gate.bias` + `bias_vl` (routers) | 0.157 GB | every rank must score all 384 experts to route |
| `layers.N.hc_*_fn / _base / _scale` (mHC coefficients, F32) | 0.157 GB | Sinkhorn mixing runs over the whole 4× residual stream |
| `layers.N.attn.compressor.wkv / wgate / norm` (4 layers, BF16) | 0.037 GB | **one KV head** — nothing to split along |
| `layers.N.attn.indexer.wk`, `weights_proj`, `k_norm` | 0.003 GB | same |
| RMSNorm weights, `attn_sink` (F32) | 0.0006 GB | per-layer scalars |

Everything else shards: routed experts (TP or EP), the low-rank Q/O attention projections
(by head group), shared experts, embedding and `lm_head` (vocab-parallel), and — importantly
— **the Engram tables shard by row**: the DGX Spark deployment reports "48 GB of rows per
worker" across 4 workers ≈ the full 192 GB table
[src](https://github.com/tonyd2wild/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-vLLM-DGX-Spark), and vLLM exposes
`--engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}'` to move them to host DRAM entirely
[src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash).

**The KV cache is the tensor that matters and it does not shard under plain TP.** With
`num_key_value_heads: 1` the MLA latent has no head dimension to split, so each rank holds
the whole compressed cache. Measured confirmation in §1.2. Only **DP-attention** or **DCP
(decode context parallelism)** splits it; neither is in the vLLM B200 recipe, and SGLang's
B200 cell runs TP4/EP4 with `dcp_size: 1` and `pcp_size: 1`
[src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/api/v1/benchmarks?model=DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash).

Two things fall out:

- **TP2 misses by 0.1 GB even with the Engram tables on the host.** At the 0.92 utilisation
  the H100 profile uses it opens to +3.5 GB/GPU — enough for 29 concurrent sequences at 128K
  and nothing else. It is not a serving configuration. **B300 is the part that runs TP2**
  (268 GB/GPU, METHODOLOGY §8), and InferenceX measures it doing so at 1,128 output
  tok/s/GPU (§3.4).
- **Going 4 → 8 GPUs multiplies max concurrency by 3.3×, not 2×**, because the weight share
  falls faster than the budget grows. That is the argument for TP8/EP8 on this part.

### 1.3 Reality check — the KV pool the engine actually allocates

METHODOLOGY's 890 B/token is the architectural figure. The pools vLLM actually reports are
smaller than that figure implies. Two independent same-image pairs from the InferenceX
dataset (`kv_cache_pool_tokens`, `vllm/vllm-openai:nightly-cd10ed6f9f…`):

| Pair | Δ capacity per GPU | Δ pool | implied bytes/token |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| H100 (80 GB, util 0.92) → H200 (141 GB, util 0.90), both TP8 | 53.3 GB | 23,048,847 tok | **2,312 B/token** |
| B200 (180 GB) → GB200 (186 GB), both TP4 | 5.4 GB | 2,302,029 tok | **2,346 B/token** |

Both land on ≈ **2.3 KB/token — 2.6× the architectural 890 B**. A third, independent check:
vLLM PR #56686 reports "421,885 KV blocks per rank" on 8× B200 at
`gpu_memory_utilization 0.92` [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/56686); at
vLLM's DSV4.1 block size of 128 that is 54.0M tokens per rank against ~113 GB of post-weight
budget, i.e. ≈ 2.1 KB/token — the same ballpark, and it again shows the pool sized
**per rank**, confirming replication.

**⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED — the decomposition.** Candidates: (a) vLLM defaults the main compressed
cache to **FP8** rather than the FP4 `nvfp4_ds_mla` layout, which alone costs 1,650 B/token
([architecture.md §5.2](architecture.md)); (b) the 128-token SWA ring is allocated inside the
paged pool rather than as fixed per-sequence state; (c) block-granularity padding at
`block_size 128`. No engine document states which. **Planning consequence: halve to a third
of the FP4-KV concurrencies in §1.2 unless you have verified `--kv-cache-dtype`
/ `use_fp4_indexer_cache` on your build.** At 2,340 B/token:

| GPUs | Engram | 8K | 32K | 128K | 1M |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | HBM | 1,271 | 352 | 90 | 11 |
| 4 | host | 3,567 | 989 | 254 | 32 |
| **8** | **HBM** | **4,160** | **1,154** | **296** | **37** |
| 8 | host | 5,309 | 1,472 | 378 | 47 |
| 16 | HBM | 5,605 | 1,554 | 399 | 50 |

### 1.4 Capacity disagreement, stated once

METHODOLOGY §8 and [b200.md §2](../../gpus/b200.md) pin **180 GB as deployed** and note the
GB-vs-GiB question as ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED "against `nvidia-smi` on a real node". This
document's primary measurement supplies that reading: vLLM PR #56686 reports
"**8 x NVIDIA B200 (sm_100, 183 GB each)**" and "`nvidia-smi` peaked at **170,828 MiB**" per
rank at `gpu_memory_utilization 0.92`
[src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/56686). 170,828 MiB ÷ 0.92 ≈ 185,683 MiB of
addressable device memory, i.e. the node exposes ≈ **183 GiB ≈ 192 GB**, the *physical* stack
capacity, not 180 GB.

**Every number in this document nevertheless plans with 180 GB**, per METHODOLOGY §8 and
because being 6–7 % pessimistic is the right direction for capacity planning. If your
`nvidia-smi` also shows ~183 GiB, every KV budget in §1.2 gains ≈ 11 GB/GPU and every
max-concurrency roughly 12 %. The disagreement is between the METHODOLOGY §8 / Lenovo SKU
sheet figure and this one observed node; both are cited, neither is silently preferred.

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.1 MBU / MFU assumptions — calibrated, not assumed

METHODOLOGY §4 sets MBU 0.5–0.7 for first-generation Blackwell software and says published
measurements take precedence. **Three B200/Blackwell measurements of *this model* put the
realised MBU far below that band**, so this document uses the measured values:

| Anchor | Config | Step time | Implied MBU (vs 7.7 TB/s) |
|---|---|---:|---:|

> DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash reads **123,750 prefill tok/s/GPU** at MFU 0.22. The measurement is
> **13.4× lower**. The GEMM-only MFU implied by 9,267 tok/s/GPU is 1.63 % of the FP4 peak /
> 3.25 % of the FP8 peak. The gap is not a bad datasheet: b200.md §9.3's formula counts only
> `2 × active_params × T` and explicitly excludes attention, and this model's prefill is
> dominated by everything *except* that term — the O(N²) indexer scan at the encoder Full
> layers, the top-k selection, the 24-way random Engram gathers per token, the Sinkhorn mHC
> mixing, and TP8 collectives on a model with only 7.89 B active parameters, where the
> per-GPU GEMM tiles are tiny. **Use 9,267 tok/s/GPU, not 123,750.** b200.md §9.3 should
> carry this correction.

**Read the shape, not just the cells.** Because there are 384 experts at top-6, the decode
step is essentially flat from batch 256 upward — the expert read saturates at 288.8 GB and
extra sequences cost only their KV. The weight-read term is essentially flat past batch 256,

while throughput rises 3.15×, and it is why **TPOT ≤ 50 ms never
binds on this part** ([architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)).

### 4.2 Effect of speculative decoding and prefix caching

**DSpark.** Because the step stays bandwidth-bound out to very large verify widths on this
model (384 experts × top-6 means the expert read saturates and extra verify positions are
almost free — [architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)), the modelled gain does *not* collapse
at high batch the way it does on denser models:

| concurrency | base TPOT (`est.`) | with DSpark γ=5 at acceptance 3.51 | speedup | out tok/s/GPU |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 4.1 ms | 2.2 ms | 1.8× | 31 → 56 |
| 64 | 19.8 ms | 8.7 ms | 2.3× | 405 → 926 |
| 256 | 21.5 ms | 6.6 ms | 3.3× | 1,492 → 4,874 |

At $6.00/GPU-hr the S1 output cost falls from $0.462 to roughly **$0.136/1M** if acceptance
3.51 holds. **Treat that as an upper bound**: 3.51 is a *synthetic benchmark constant* the
harness is told to assume (`"rejection_sample_method":"synthetic"`), not an observed
acceptance, and it is the constant behind every B200 row in §3.4(a)
[src](https://github.com/SemiAnalysisAI/InferenceX/blob/main/benchmarks/single_node/agentic/dsv41flash_fp4_vllm_mtp.sh).
The only *measurement* of DSpark acceptance on this checkpoint anywhere is the DGX Spark
community mean of **3.57 tok/step (range 1.88–5.92; prose ≈ 2)**
[architecture.md §7.3](architecture.md); SGLang's GB300 873.63 tok/s figure likewise uses a
*simulated* accept length of 5.5. At a prose-like acceptance of 2.6 the modelled speedup at
concurrency 256 drops to 2.4× and the cost to ≈ $0.19/1M.

**Prefix caching.** A cache hit costs ~10 % of uncached prefill for our own serving
(METHODOLOGY §6), so at $6.00/GPU-hr cached input runs at **$0.018/1M** against $0.180/1M
uncached. On this model a hit really is nearly pure I/O — 890 B/token loaded plus a 128-token
SWA replay ([architecture.md §11.1](architecture.md)) — which is why the measured agentic
traces sustain 92,073 total tok/s/GPU at 95 % hit rate. At the 90 % hit rate the blended
figure would fall from $0.190 to **$0.147/1M** at $6.00/GPU-hr.

### 4.3 Versus the vendor API, and break-even utilisation

DeepSeek first-party `deepseek-flash`, per 1M tokens
([architecture.md §11](architecture.md), [src](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)):
**output $0.60 off-peak / $1.20 peak; input cache-miss $0.15 / $0.30; input cache-hit
$0.003 / $0.006.** Their cached-input ratio is **2 %**, not the generic 10 % — use 2 % for
API-side comparisons (METHODOLOGY §6). DeepSeek's blended price at the same 75/25 mix with
half the input cached is therefore **$0.2074/1M**.

| | Self-hosted B200, $6.00/GPU-hr | Self-hosted B200, $5.10 res. | Self-hosted B200, $14.00 OCI | DeepSeek API off-peak |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $/1M output | $0.462 (S1) / $0.355 (S4) | $0.393 / $0.302 | $1.079 / $0.828 | **$0.60** |
| $/1M input (uncached) | $0.180 | $0.153 | $0.420 | **$0.15** |
| $/1M input (cached) | $0.018 | $0.015 | $0.042 | **$0.003** |
| **Blended (75/25, 50 % cached)** | **$0.190** | **$0.161** | **$0.443** | **$0.207** |

**Break-even utilisation** — the fraction of wall-clock the node must be producing at the
stated rate for self-hosting to match $0.60/1M output:

| Throughput basis | $5.10/GPU-hr | $6.00/GPU-hr | $14.00/GPU-hr |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| S4 `est.`, 37,568 agg tok/s | **50 %** | **59 %** | 138 % — **impossible** |
| S1 meas.-anchored, 28,832 agg tok/s | 66 % | 77 % | 180 % — impossible |
| PR #56686 end-to-end, 9,524 agg tok/s | 198 % — impossible | 233 % — impossible | 544 % — impossible |

**The verdict is price-tier-dependent and it is the most actionable number in this document.**
On a *hyperscaler* B200 you cannot match DeepSeek's list output price at any measured
throughput. On the cheapest reputable neocloud you match it at roughly **half** utilisation
against the roofline and **three-quarters** against the measured steady-state decode rate —
i.e. self-hosting wins only with a well-batched, continuously-loaded fleet. At DeepSeek's
**peak** rates ($1.20/M output) every break-even above halves and the case is comfortable.
The counter-argument, and it is strong: on *input-heavy agentic* traffic, where the measured
per-total-token cost is $0.018–$0.042/1M, self-hosting beats the API by an order of magnitude
regardless of tier — which is exactly the workload this architecture was built for.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

**Stay inside one HGX B200 node.** The NVLink domain is 8 GPUs at 1.8 TB/s per GPU; TP or EP
beyond 8 crosses a 0.8 TB/s node fabric (400 Gb/s per GPU), a 36× bandwidth cliff
[b200.md §4](../../gpus/b200.md). §1.2 shows TP16 buys only 35 % more KV budget per GPU than
TP8 — nowhere near enough to justify the fabric. **Wide-EP (DeepEP/EPLB across 32–72 GPUs) is
a GB200/GB300 NVL72 architecture and does not belong on HGX B200.**

The right scale-out unit on this part is **replicas**: two TP4 servers per node, or one TP8
server per node with data-parallel routing in front. vLLM ships both shapes for B200
(`single_node_tep` = TP8 + `--enable-expert-parallel`, `single_node_dep` = DP8 + EP)
[src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash/hw/b200/strategies/single_node_dep.json).

**When PD disaggregation pays for this pair.** The general rule from
[serving-optimizations.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md) —
*"disaggregation is a thousand-GPU problem"*, with a **20–30 % performance drop** on small or
untuned deployments — argues against it on a single 8-GPU node. Two things argue for it here:

1. This model's prefill:decode ratio is inverted (7.89 B active prefill vs 16.13 B decode),
   so prefill and decode want genuinely different shapes.
2. §3.4(b) shows B200's failure mode is *KV capacity under long prefills*. A PD split lets
   the decode pool keep its whole KV budget while prefill workers churn 100K-token prompts.

But vLLM's verified 1P1D layout for this model is a **GB200 NVL4** shape (one tray per role,
TP4 each, NIXL); the *"On 8-GPU nodes the same layout becomes TP8 per role"* variant is
documented, not verified, and the verified runs were text-only
[inference-engines.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md). SGLang additionally
**cannot combine PD with speculative decoding**
[inference-engines.md §6.1](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md). **Recommendation: on a
single B200 node, don't. On ≥ 4 nodes of agentic 100K-token traffic, it is the first thing to
try — and it is ⚠️ unverified on B200.**

**vLLM, B200, the vendor's own recipe (TP4)** — verbatim from

**vLLM, B200, TP8 + expert parallel** — verbatim from

**The command the measured B200 numbers in §3.4(a) came from** — verbatim from
[`benchmarks/single_node/agentic/dsv41flash_fp4_vllm_mtp.sh`](https://github.com/SemiAnalysisAI/InferenceX/blob/main/benchmarks/single_node/agentic/dsv41flash_fp4_vllm_mtp.sh)

SPEC_CONFIG='{"method":"dspark","num_speculative_tokens":5,"draft_sample_method":"probabilistic","rejection_sample_method":"synthetic","synthetic_acceptance_length":3.51,"enable_adaptive_verification":false}'

serving use `"rejection_sample_method":"block"` and `"enable_adaptive_verification":true`
(the NVIDIA default in the vLLM recipe schema, [architecture.md §7.4](architecture.md)).

**SGLang** — B200 is `verified: true` at TP4/EP4, but the published verbatim command in this
repo's engine document is the GB300 Low-Latency cell

```bash
sglang serve --trust-remote-code --model-path deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash \
  --tp 4 --ep-size 4 --mem-fraction-static 0.8 \
  --speculative-algorithm DSPARK --speculative-dspark-block-size 5 \
  --cuda-graph-max-bs-decode 64 \

```
**Do not override `--attention-backend`, `--moe-runner-backend` or `--fp8-gemm-backend`**:
SGLang selects them automatically and *"overriding them is the most common cause of a
disappointing measurement"* [inference-engines.md §6.1](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md).
Given §3.4(a), prefer vLLM on B200 regardless.

### 5.3 Warm-up and load time

510.29 GB of weights from NVMe (`est.`, `bytes / bandwidth`):

| NVMe read bandwidth | Weight load |
|---|---:|
| 5 GB/s | **102 s** |
| 12 GB/s | 43 s |
| 20 GB/s | **26 s** |

**Measured, and the storage path matters more than the disk:**

- vLLM PR #56686, 8× B200, weights on a shared mount under `HF_HOME`: **weights 47–51 s**,
  then **engine init 111–114 s** with a warm DeepGEMM/FlashInfer cache — and **190 s on the
  first start from a new checkout path**, of which the o-projection DeepGEMM warm-up alone is
  **22.3 s for 27 kernels**. Total cold start ≈ **4 minutes**
  [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/56686).
- The InferenceX launcher documents the failure mode: vLLM auto-prefetches only on NFS or
  Lustre; on other filesystems it memory-maps lazily, and *"on cluster:h200-dgxc the HF cache
  is a VIRTIOFS mount … the lazy path read this 475 GiB checkpoint at ~170 s/shard (19/48
  shards when the 3600 s readiness deadline fired) against ~12.5 s/shard for the same files
  on the `deepseekv41-flash-0909` build."* The fix is
  **`--safetensors-load-strategy prefetch`**
  [src](https://github.com/SemiAnalysisAI/InferenceX/blob/main/benchmarks/single_node/agentic/dsv41flash_fp4_vllm_mtp.sh).
- `VLLM_ENGINE_READY_TIMEOUT_S=3600` is in the base environment for a reason:
  *"Expect a long first load"* [architecture.md §8.3](architecture.md).

Budget **4 minutes warm, 10+ minutes cold, and hours if the checkpoint sits on a filesystem
vLLM does not recognise as a network FS.** If Engram is offloaded to host, size for ~300 GB
of free host RAM (the tables are pinned, ~190 GiB) [architecture.md §4.1](architecture.md).

## 6. Risks and open questions

### 6.1 Carried forward from the pinned inputs

1. **⚠️ B200 capacity: 180 GB (METHODOLOGY §8, Lenovo SKU sheet, vLLM's own `vram_gb: 1440`)
   vs ~183 GiB ≈ 192 GB observed on the node in vLLM PR #56686** (§1.4). This document plans
   with 180 GB. If the higher figure is general, every KV budget here gains ≈ 11 GB/GPU.
2. **⚠️ HBM bandwidth 7.7 TB/s (Lenovo SKU sheet, used here) vs 8.0 TB/s (NVIDIA DGX B200
   page, and the figure cloud-pricing.md §2.1 tabulates)** [b200.md §2](../../gpus/b200.md).
   Every decode figure here rises 3.9 % at 8.0 TB/s.
3. **⚠️ SWA ring-buffer dtype.** 2.77 MiB/seq assumes true FP8 + E8M0 scales; DeepSeek's
   reference implementation stores fp8-rounded values in a **BF16** container = 5.38 MiB
   [architecture.md §5.3](architecture.md). No vLLM or SGLang document states which. Below
   ~3–6K context this term dominates the per-sequence cost.
4. **⚠️ Engine state-slot count `S`.** All figures assume `S = 1`. If a future build
   allocates slots for the 5 DSpark draft positions, multiply the fixed state by `S`.

### 6.2 New to this pair, B200-specific

5. **⚠️ The engine-realised KV cost is ≈ 2.3 KB/token, not 890 B/token** (§1.3), consistent
   across two independent same-image pairs and a third block-count check. The decomposition is
   unknown; it could be an FP8 main cache by default, SWA inside the paged pool, or block
   padding. **This is the single largest uncertainty in this document** — it moves every
   max-concurrency figure by 2.6×. Resolve by reading `vllm:kv_cache_*` and the resolved
   attention backend name on a real B200 boot.
6. **⚠️ Every published B200 throughput number assumes DSpark acceptance 3.51 synthetically.**
   `"rejection_sample_method":"synthetic","synthetic_acceptance_length":3.51` is a value the
   harness is *told*, not one it observed (§4.2). The only measurement of this checkpoint's
   acceptance anywhere is the DGX Spark community mean of 3.57 with a 1.88–5.92 range;
   SGLang's GB300 figures simulate 5.5. **Measure acceptance on your own traffic before
   sizing anything around it** — vLLM says so explicitly.
7. **⚠️ MBU 0.18 in the 8 < batch ≤ 128 range is interpolated**, between an anchor at batch 1
   (0.10) and one at batch ~640 (0.23). Every S1/S2/S3 row at batch 32–128 rests on it.
8. **⚠️ The concurrency-128 collapse is characterised but not diagnosed.** KV usage 67.6 %
   and TTFT 48.18 s say the pool ran out, but whether the binding resource is the pool, the
   scheduler's preemption behaviour, or PCIe pressure from the host-resident Engram tables is
   unknown. B300 at identical settings with 2.3× the pool does not collapse.
9. **⚠️ No vision measurement on B200.** Every published B200 run uses
   `--language-model-only`. A max-resolution image costs ≈ 18.86 TFLOP — as much as ~1,200
   prefill tokens — and the ViT's attention is dense bidirectional over 9,216 patches
   [architecture.md §6.4](architecture.md). Encoder placement (`--mm-encoder-tp-mode data`)
   and EPD disaggregation are unmeasured here.
10. **⚠️ DP-attention / DCP on B200 for this model is unverified.** It is the obvious fix for
    the replicated KV of §1.1, and SGLang has measured DCP keeping 128K decode scaling past
    TP's ~680 tok/s saturation on 8× B200 — **for a DeepSeek-V3.2-class model, not V4.1**
    [b200.md §5](../../gpus/b200.md). Both B200 V4.1 sweeps ran `dcp_size: 1`.
11. **⚠️ PD disaggregation on B200 is documented but not verified** for this model; the
    verified 1P1D shape is GB200 NVL4 and was text-only (§5.1).
12. **⚠️ TP2 with Engram→host misses by 0.1 GB at util 0.90 and opens to +3.5 GB at 0.92.**
    Whether a 2-GPU B200 server is actually bootable at 0.92 with 1M context is untested and
    would be a curiosity, not a deployment.

### 6.3 Known engine issues that bite on B200

13. **DeepGEMM compiles inside serving steps.** A first-seen o-projection token count triggers
    a ~3.4 s `sm100_fp8_gemm_1d1d` compile *in the request path*; `jit_monitor` covers Triton
    and CuTeDSL only so it leaves **no log line**, and `VLLM_DEEP_GEMM_WARMUP=full` does not
    reach it. It landed in the timed window in **three of eight** baseline runs
    [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/56686). PR #56686 buckets and pre-warms it;
    until that merges, expect unexplained multi-second latency spikes on a cold cache.
14. **Sparse-attention bookkeeping is sized by `max_model_len`, not by the batch** on
    pre-PR#56686 builds: a `[requests, 1,048,576]` fp32 indexer buffer (3.5 GB at 830
    requests, four allocations per step) and candidate-block kernels that write `−inf` into a
    million columns every decode step — **5.8 ms of a 35 ms step**. Setting a smaller
    `--max-model-len` is the mitigation on an unpatched build. PR #56686 was still **open** as
    of 2026-09-19 and Mergify reported it conflicting with `main`.
15. **FlashInfer autotune has crashed on SM100 NVFP4 MoE**; the 26.08 NGC container re-enabled
    it after a cycle with it off. Workaround `--moe-backend flashinfer_cutlass`, or
    `--no-enable-flashinfer-autotune` [b200.md §11](../../gpus/b200.md). The GB200 PD recipe
    disables autotune, JIT and CuTeDSL warm-up wholesale via `--kernel-config`.
16. **Nothing is in a numbered release.** vLLM's recipe declares `min_vllm_version: 0.30.0`
    with `nightly_required: true`, and **0.30.0 does not exist** — 0.29.0 (2026-09-09) is the
    latest. SGLang's B200 support is a preview image (`dev-dsv41`), absent from 0.5.20.
    **TensorRT-LLM has no V4.1 support at all** (1.2.1 stable / 1.3.0rc27), and NVIDIA's own
    NVFP4 card lists only vLLM and SGLang as runtimes
    [inference-engines.md §6.1](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md).
17. **`sm_100a` vs `sm_100`.** Every tcgen05-accelerated kernel needs `sm_100a`, which is not
    forward compatible; a wheel built `-arch=sm_100` loads and runs *slowly*. **CUDA 12.9 is
    the real floor**, not 12.8 — FlashMLA's SM100 kernels require it
    [b200.md §11](../../gpus/b200.md). The measured runs used CUDA 13.0.
18. **Determinism and bounded replay.** SGLang refuses `--enable-deterministic-inference` on
    the `dsv4` backend, and `--enable-decoder-swa-bounded-replay` *"refuses prompt logprobs by
    design, is not numerically equivalent to full prefill, and is excluded at launch with the
    prefill CUDA graph and with DP attention"*
    [inference-engines.md §6.1](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md).
19. **Reasoning-effort forks between engines.** vLLM maps `low/high/xhigh/max → 25/50/75/100`
    and defaults thinking **on at effort 50**; SGLang defaults thinking **off**; DeepSeek's
    API maps `low/high/max → 50/75/100`. Output-token volume — and therefore cost — differs
    by up to **2.5×** between effort 25 and 100 [architecture.md §10.5](architecture.md).
    **Effort 60–80 is the cost-efficient operating point.**

### 6.4 Accuracy caveats of the precision actually executed

20. **The executed precision is the checkpoint's own.** The routed experts are MXFP4 as
    DeepSeek trained and shipped them, and B200 executes them natively as W4A8 — there is no
    dequantisation, requantisation or emulation anywhere in the B200 path, so **no
    B200-specific accuracy delta exists**. That is the cleanest thing about this pair. The
    published evals (GPQA Diamond 91.0, Terminal-Bench 2.1 90.6, DeepSWE 74.2) are for exactly
    this artifact [architecture.md §10.5](architecture.md).
21. **⚠️ SWA Bounded Replay's accuracy cost is unpublished by DeepSeek** (*"barely compromises
    response quality"*, no number) — but LMSYS measured **identical AIME 2026 pass@1 with
    replay off and on** (453/480 both) on 4× GB300, which is the best evidence available
    [src](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-09-10-deepseek-v41).
22. **The NVFP4 question, answered as far as the evidence allows.** On B200 the base MXFP4
    checkpoint executes **W4A8** → the 4,500 TFLOPS FP8 line. NVIDIA's NVFP4 build executes
    **W4A4** → the 9,000 TFLOPS FP4 line, at +16.98 GB of checkpoint (527.27 vs 510.29 GB) and
    accuracy parity (+0.40 points mean across six benchmarks, i.e. within noise)
    [quantization-formats.md §9.1–9.2](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md).
    **Does the doubled compute line help on B200? For decode, almost not at all**: B200's FP4
    ridge point is batch ~1,169 and the measured decode step here is bandwidth-bound at
    MBU 0.10–0.23, nowhere near it [b200.md §9.1](../../gpus/b200.md). **For prefill it should
    help — but prefill on this model is measured at 1.6 % of the FP4 peak** (§3.1), so the
    binding constraint is the indexer, top-k and Engram gathers, not the GEMM line. Against
    that, NVFP4 costs 17 GB more weights on a part whose measured failure mode is *running out
    of memory* (§3.4(b)). **⚠️ No head-to-head throughput comparison exists for any GPU, and
    NVIDIA publishes none. On the evidence available, the NVFP4 build is not the default
    choice on B200; the base MXFP4 checkpoint is.** (A deeper treatment of the NVFP4 build
    belongs in `research/models/deepseek41fnvfp4/b200.md`.)
23. **⚠️ Whether the Engram tables can be quantised below FP8** is unstudied; two community
    repos do INT4 (GSM8K reported lossless) and Ascend does INT8, but no systematic study
    exists [architecture.md §12](architecture.md). On a 180 GB part this is worth 100 GB.
