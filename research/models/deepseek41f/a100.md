## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today — community only.** Engine: `lazymio/vllm-backport` image
   `sha256:34969032…` (branch [`master-v013`](https://github.com/wtdcode/vllm-backport/tree/master-v013), whose own README still
   marks DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash **"WIP!"**) plus the 24-file SM80 patch set in
   [`patches/manifest.json`](https://github.com/Tokha233/deepseek-v4.1-flash-a100-turbo/blob/main/patches/manifest.json).
   Upstream vLLM does **not** support it — [issue #57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144)
   (opened 2026-09-16) is still open; SGLang, TensorRT-LLM, TokenSpeed and Dynamo have no SM80 path.
2. **Min 8 GPUs, recommended 8 GPUs, one HGX A100 node**, `TP4 × DP2` with `EP8`
   (attention data-parallel, experts expert-parallel). 4 GPUs is arithmetically 0.4 GB
   short of usable even with the Engram tables on the host, so it is not an option (§1).
3. **Weight format actually executed: MXFP4 → Marlin W4A16 (dequant-to-BF16), FP8 32×32 → FP8-Marlin W8A16.**
   Nothing on this GPU executes 4-bit or 8-bit math; A100 keeps the *memory* win of the
   checkpoint and none of the *FLOPs* win. KV runs **FP8 (`fp8_ds_mla`, 1,650 B/token)**,
   not the architecture's native FP4 890 B/token.
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms), 8 GPUs, concurrency 128: $1.16 – $2.51 per 1M output
   tokens** ($0.99 at Hyperstack 1-yr reserved). **Max throughput (S4): identical —
   $1.16 – $2.51**, because the published recipe saturates at 128 running requests and
   C256 measures 3,040.71 tok/s against C128's 3,039.88.
4a. **One throughput basis here, not two: 380 out tok/s/GPU is a decode-only rate.** §3's
   grid is measured as *output token IDs received ÷ 180 s* on an 80-in / 1,024-out workload,
   so prefill is ~7 % of the token work and the figure is the decode rate
   [METHODOLOGY §4](../../METHODOLOGY.md) defines — **not** a sustained prefill+decode rate.
   It is C128's 42.11 ms TPOT that reproduces it (`128 / 0.04211 s / 8 = 380.0`). ⚠️ The
   `pairs.json` S4 block pairs it with **concurrency 256 and the 45.58 ms P95** instead, and
   at C256 this engine admits only ~128 (§3 records `running 127.55 / waiting 127.14`), so
   `256 / 0.0456 / 8` overstates the rate by 1.85×. The rate and the costs are correct and
   unchanged; the concurrency and `tpot_ms` fields are the ones to re-cut. **TO BE VERIFIED.**
5. **Confidence: `measured`** for throughput/latency/cost at short context and for the
   long-context aggregate — on **A800**, not A100, and with a 24-patch community stack.
   Everything reported as a *scenario* (S1–S3 grid, prefill rate, TTFT at 4K/32K/128K) is
   `est.`, calibrated against those measurements.

## 1. Fit

Hardware inputs, all from METHODOLOGY §8 and [`gpus/a100.md`](../../gpus/a100.md) §2–§4:
**80 GB decimal** per GPU, `usable_hbm = 80 GB × 0.90 = 72.0 GB = 67.06 GiB` (METHODOLOGY §3).
**Basis resolved 2026-09-19 to decimal GB tree-wide** ([METHODOLOGY §8](../../METHODOLOGY.md#gpus),
[gpus/a100.md §2](../../gpus/a100.md)); `nvidia-smi`'s 81,920 MiB = 80 GiB = 85.90 GB is an
unsourced binary reading kept only as a ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED +7.4 % sensitivity. **This document
previously planned on that GiB reading; §1.1–§1.3 and §3.2's concurrency headers were recomputed
onto the decimal budget in that pass** (see the audit log). HBM 2.039 TB/s, BF16 dense
312 TFLOPS, **no FP8, no FP4, no MX**.
Topology: HGX A100 8-GPU baseboard, 6× 2nd-gen NVSwitch, 600 GB/s all-to-all per GPU;
**no NVLink domain larger than one baseboard** — every cross-node hop is 200 Gb/s HDR IB
(DGX A100) or 4×100 Gb/s EFA (AWS p4de), i.e. **24× less than intra-node NVLink**.

Model inputs from [`architecture.md`](./architecture.md) §3.1/§4/§5:
checkpoint `510,286,023,000 B = 510.29 GB = 475.24 GiB`; Engram tables (FP8 payload +
E8M0 scales) `202.76 GB = 188.83 GiB`; routed + DSpark MXFP4 experts `296.00 GB`.

`*` **N = 4 does not fit at all on the decimal basis** (it was a marginal false positive on the
demoted GiB one): `307.53 / 4 = 76.88 GB` against `72.0 GB` usable is **4.88 GB short** before
any activation workspace, CUDA graph, NCCL buffer or KV byte. (On the old 77.3 GB figure it
"fitted" with 0.42 GB to spare.) `gpus/a100.md` gotcha 11 warns that CUDA context + NCCL +
CUDA graphs "routinely eat 3–5 GiB before the first token" on A100, so the verdict was already
the same. **Minimum viable GPU count is 8.**

An independent measurement on the same shape, from the upstream feature request, agrees
with the Engram-resident row to within a GB:

> "MoE experts (fp4): 296 GB total · Engram tables: 203 GB total · backbone/embedding/head:
> ~11 GB total · **per-GPU ~64 GB**, leaving **roughly 15 GB/rank for KV cache, activations
> and graphs**" — [vLLM #57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144), 8×80 GB.

`475.24 GiB / 8 = 59.4 GiB = 63.8 GB` ✓. Their "~15 GB/rank" is measured against **raw**
capacity, not a planning budget: `80 − 63.8 = 16.2 GB` ✓. On METHODOLOGY §3's 0.90 planning
factor the same rank has `72.0 − 63.8 = 8.2 GB` — which is why the Engram-resident row in T2
below is nearly empty once the 6 GiB workspace is taken out.

### 1.2 Tensors that cannot be sharded

Per-GPU replication cost, from [`architecture.md`](./architecture.md) §3.1/§3.4:

| Tensor group | Shape / dtype | Bytes | Why it is replicated |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `layers.N.ffn.gate.weight` (routers) | (384, 5120) BF16 × 40 | 157 MB | every EP rank must score all 384 experts to route |
| `layers.N.hc_*_fn / _base / _scale` (mHC) | (24, 20480) F32 etc. | 157 MB | 24-row coefficient projections; splitting costs more in comm than it saves |
| `layers.N.attn.compressor.wkv/wgate/norm` | (512, 5120) BF16 × 4 | 37 MB | one per DP-attention group |
| Engram `wkv` projections | (25600, 6144) × 2 | 310 MB | each rank holding Engram rows needs the projection ⚠️ engine-specific |
| `ffn.gate.bias`, `bias_vl`, `attn.attn_sink` | F32 | 0.04 MB | per-layer scalars |
| All RMSNorm weights | BF16 | 0.6 MB | trivially replicated |
| **Total** | | **662 MB = 631 MiB / GPU** | |

Additionally, under `TP4 × DP2` **the whole attention block (5.07 GB) and its 128-slot SWA
ring buffers are replicated once per DP group**, not sharded across all 8 GPUs. The 2.77 MiB
(FP8) / 5.38 MiB (BF16) per-sequence fixed state of §5.3 is held by the TP group that owns
the request; whether a TP4 group replicates it ×4 (the KV head count is **1**, so it cannot
be split head-wise) is **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** — no engine document states it, and at
`max-num-seqs 64` a ×4 replication would cost 0.7 GiB/GPU, not a plan-breaker.

### 1.3 KV budget and max concurrency

Per METHODOLOGY §3, `kv_budget = usable_hbm − per_gpu_weights − activation_workspace`,
activation workspace **6 GiB** (upper end of METHODOLOGY's 2–6 GB band, because this model
carries the residual stream as **4 parallel copies** — `hc_mult: 4`, §2.7 — and the recipe
runs `--max-num-batched-tokens 8192` with 14 CUDA-graph capture sizes).

**KV bytes per token on this GPU are 1,650, not 890.** The 890 B/token figure is the
FP4-KV figure; `--kv-cache-dtype nvfp4`/`nvfp4_ds_mla` is Blackwell-only
([quantization-formats.md §8.1](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md),
[flash-attention.md §16.1](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)), and the only working
A100/A800 recipe sets **`--kv-cache-dtype fp8_ds_mla`**
([`config/serve.json`](https://github.com/Tokha233/deepseek-v4.1-flash-a100-turbo/blob/main/config/serve.json)).
⚠️ **Stated disagreement, per METHODOLOGY §8:** [`architecture.md`](./architecture.md) §5.2
argues the FP4 KV *"works identically on Hopper and Ampere — no FP4 tensor cores needed"*
because the cache is dequantised in software, and METHODOLOGY §8's model row echoes that.
Against it: three cross-cutting docs scope the 890 B figure to sm_10x, and the one shipped
SM80 deployment uses FP8. **Plan with 1,650 B/token**; if an engine ever exposes a software
FP4-KV path on sm_80, max concurrency roughly doubles (the `maxC FP4` column below sizes the
prize).

Three consequences:

- **Capacity is set by weights, not by KV** — exactly as vLLM says of this model
  (*"Weights and batch size, not cache, set the capacity limit"*,
  [vLLM recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)). Even the
  Engram-resident 8-GPU row holds 540 sequences at 8K.
- **`--engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}'` buys 24× the KV budget** (1.03 → 24.64 GiB/GPU)
  and is what makes the advertised 1M context reachable: 122 concurrent 1M-token sequences
  at FP8 KV versus 5 with the tables resident. The published recipe uses it. *(On the demoted
  GiB basis the same comparison read 5× — 6.0 → 29.6 GiB/GPU, 146 vs 29; the offload flag
  became far more load-bearing when the budget tightened.)*
- **Multi-node is a fit answer, not a performance answer.** N=16 removes every capacity
  constraint, but EP16 for a top-6-of-384 MoE across a 200 Gb/s IB boundary is the exact
  case `gpus/a100.md` §4 calls out: *"Multi-node TP is therefore almost always wrong on
  A100; use PP or replicate… EP on A100 is EP≤8 inside one baseboard, or EP over IB with a
  large penalty."* **Fabric needed for N ≥ 16: HDR IB / EFA. Do not do it.** Replicate
  8-GPU nodes instead.

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.0 Assumptions (METHODOLOGY §4)

- **MBU 0.65** for the roofline (METHODOLOGY says 0.6–0.8 on H100/H200;
  [`gpus/a100.md`](../../gpus/a100.md) §9 says *"no published A100 MBU figure found… use
  0.6–0.75"* ⚠️). **MFU 0.40** for the BF16 decode compute branch, **0.40 GEMM / 0.30
  attention** for prefill. **Comm overhead ×1.10** for TP/EP inside one NVSwitch baseboard.
- **Aggregate bandwidth 8 × 2.039 = 16.312 TB/s; aggregate dense BF16 8 × 312 = 2.496 PFLOPS.**
  There is no FP8 or FP4 branch on this GPU, so the prefill ceiling is the BF16 ceiling.
- `weights_read(batch)` from [`architecture.md`](./architecture.md) §6.3
  (`distinct_experts(b) = 384 × (1 − (1 − 6/384)^b)`, one MXFP4 expert = 18,800,640 B,
  static non-expert 18.75 GB). Engram tables are on the host, so they are **not** in the
  HBM read.
- KV read per sequence per step is the §6.3 access pattern **doubled** on the two FP4 terms,
  because this GPU stores KV at FP8: SWA 2.77 MiB + `38 × 512 × 576 B` + `4 × min(16384,ctx) × 136 B`
  + `340 B × ctx`.
- **Measured anchor**: 8× A800-SXM4-80GB, which is an A100 with NVLink cut from 12 links
  (600 GB/s) to 8 links (**400 GB/s**) — identical GA100 die, identical 80 GB HBM2e at
  2.039 TB/s, identical 312 TFLOPS
  ([NADDOD topology writeup](https://www.naddod.com/blog/high-performance-gpu-server-hardware-topology-and-cluster-networking-2)).
  **A100 should therefore be equal or slightly better than every measured number below**,
  since the only degraded axis is the collective bandwidth that TP4/EP8 leans on. The
  recipe's own words: *"A100 uses the same SM80 execution path but has not been
  independently re-measured here."* ⚠️

**Explaining the gap (this is the load-bearing part of §3).**

1. **At batch 1 the roofline over-predicts by 3.6×; at batch 128 by only 1.5×.** That shape
   — a large fixed cost per step that amortises with batch — is the signature of
   *launch-count and latency*, not bandwidth. This model runs 40 layers × 11 kernels in
   decode plus the DSpark draft, the mHC Sinkhorn (20 iterations), the indexer, and the
   EP8 all-to-all. SGLang found exactly this on Kimi-K3: at bs=1 a giant MoE is *"not
   compute-bound — it is a launch-count and latency problem"*
   ([serving-optimizations.md §4.4](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
2. **The Engram host-offload adds a per-step PCIe round trip.** §3.4 below: 48 random
   264-byte gathers per token, 6,144 per step at batch 128. Bytes are trivial; the
   *latency* of scattered PCIe reads is not. The DGX Spark study isolated exactly this term
   and measured **2.8 ms/step node-local versus 5.9–7.8 ms over NFS**, and moving Engram
   from NVMe to pinned RAM was worth **2.16×** on RTX PRO 6000 (31.5 → 68.0 tok/s at 46K)
   ([architecture.md §10.2/§10.4](./architecture.md)).
3. **My k is far smaller than the MI355X k.** [`architecture.md`](./architecture.md) §10.1
   records the same roofline under-predicting measured MI355X step time by **≈12.6×** at
   batch 1. Here it is 3.6×. The likely reason is that MI355X was measured at **131K
   context** where the indexer scan dominates, whereas the A800 sweep is at ~600 tokens.
   Consistent with that, my long-context calibration is **1.55× worse** than the short one.
4. **Implied MBU rises from 0.164 to 0.409** — i.e. even at the best measured point the
   machine achieves **~41 % of HBM peak**, against METHODOLOGY's 0.6–0.8 planning band.
   **This is the number to carry forward for A100 + this architecture.** ⚠️ It is one
   workload on one stack; it is not a characterisation of the GPU.
5. **DSpark is inside these numbers.** `k` is a *net* factor: it absorbs every overhead
   **and** whatever speedup DSpark5 delivers. That DSpark is on and `k` is still > 1
   everywhere means the drafter is not recovering the launch overhead at any batch. At
   batch 128 the verify step widens the batch to `128 × 6 = 768` rows, whose pure compute
   term is `2 × 16.13e9 × 768 / (2.496e15 × 0.40) = 24.8 ms` — **comparable to the 25.6 ms
   memory term**, so on A100 the model crosses into compute-bound during verification. That
   is the mechanism [serving-optimizations.md §2.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)
   describes: *"at concurrency 256, draft tokens compete with real tokens for the same compute."*

### 4.3 Effect of the two big levers

- **Speculative decoding (DSpark5).** Already inside every measured number — it cannot be
  added on top. The recipe publishes no with/without arm, so **its isolated contribution on
  A100 is ⚠️ unknown**. What §3.1 shows is that with DSpark on, `k` is still 1.5–3.6, so the
  drafter is not paying for the launch overhead. At batch 128 the verify widens the batch to
  768 rows whose compute term (24.8 ms) matches the memory term (25.6 ms), which is the
  textbook point at which speculation stops helping. **Do not budget a DSpark speedup on
  A100; budget its risk.**
- **The SM80 kernel work.** Measured and isolated: `2,842.48 → 2,970.50 tok/s` (+4.5 %
  total) from dense BF16 dispatch plus custom EP8 AG/RS, i.e. **$1.243 → $1.189 per 1M
  output tokens** at $1.59/GPU-hr. Small.
- **Prefix caching.** At a 90 % hit rate the input term falls to
  `(1−0.90) + 0.90 × 0.10 = 0.190×`, i.e. **5.3× cheaper input**, taking the blended low-tier
  figure from **$0.361 to $0.315** (`0.75 × 0.190 × 0.1696 + 0.25 × 1.162`). Because this model's cached prefix is only 1,650 B/token
  (A100) and a hit replays just the 128-token SWA window (SWA Bounded Replay), the hit is
  almost pure I/O — [serving-optimizations.md §1.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)
  computes the 128K reload at **1.4 ms** against a 2.097 PFLOP recompute. The real agentic
  evidence: the RTX PRO 6000 40-minute run served **87 % of 18.5M prompt tokens from prefix
  cache** ([architecture.md §10.2](./architecture.md)). **⚠️ Counter-evidence for unstructured
  traffic, measured on A100**: leaving APC on with no shared prefix cost **−36.7 % throughput
  / +25.0 % TPOT** (2024-era vLLM, [serving-optimizations.md §1.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

**Single node, full stop.** One HGX A100 8-GPU baseboard is simultaneously the minimum that
fits (§1) and the maximum that should be used, because the next allowed step is a 200 Gb/s
IB boundary and a 384-expert top-6 MoE all-to-all is the worst possible thing to put across
it. To serve more traffic, **replicate 8-GPU nodes behind a router**; do not widen EP.

**PD disaggregation does not pay here, and there is no recipe.** The measured TTFT profile
says why: TTFT P95 is **0.406 s at C1 with an 85-token prompt**, i.e. the fixed overhead
already dwarfs the prefill of anything short, and it only rises to **0.770 s at C128**.
Prefill is not starving decode on this shape — the model is CED (8 B active per prompt
token versus 16 B per output token), and the recipe's chunked prefill at 8,192 tokens plus
GPU prefix caching already keeps the interference bounded. Splitting an 8-GPU node into
1P1D would give each half 4 GPUs, and **4 GPUs cannot hold the model** (§1). PD would
require ≥ 16 GPUs and a cross-node KV transfer over the same 200 Gb/s fabric. **Not
recommended; ⚠️ no A100 PD-disaggregation data exists for this or any DeepSeek-V4.1 build.**

**Wide-EP is unavailable** — no NVLink-C2C, no NVL72 domain (`gpus/a100.md` §4).

**expect single-digit to tens of minutes on a cold cache, a few minutes warm.** ⚠️ No

## 6. Risks and open questions

**Blocking / structural**

1. ⚠️ **Every measurement is on A800, not A100.** *"A100 uses the same SM80 execution path
   but has not been independently re-measured here."* A800 differs only in NVLink
   (8 links / 400 GB/s vs 12 / 600 GB/s), so A100 should be **≥** A800 — but that direction
   is an inference, not a measurement.
2. ⚠️ **No vendor-verified engine supports this pair.** vLLM's own hardware map for this
   model is `{"h100":"verified","h200":"verified","b200":"verified","gb200":"verified","gb300":"verified","b300":"verified","mi350x":"verified"}`
   — **A100 absent**; `HTTP 404` on `recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash/hw/a100.json`;
   SGLang's `supportedHardware` array excludes it; TensorRT-LLM's Ampere row is FP32/FP16/BF16/INT8/INT4
   with **no FP8**; TokenSpeed and Dynamo publish no SM80 path. [#57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144)
   is open with no maintainer response, and [`wtdcode/vllm-backport`](https://github.com/wtdcode/vllm-backport)'s
   own status table still reads **`DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash | Native FP4 with Engram | WIP!`**.
   **You are running a WIP fork plus a third-party 24-file patch set with no support
   contract and no upgrade path.**
3. ⚠️ **Upstream vLLM actively fails here.** [#35922](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35922):
   MXFP4/NVFP4 MoE on A100 → `no kernel image is available for execution on the device`;
   INT4 W4A16 MoE → `illegal memory access` in `fused_marlin_moe` during `profile_run()`.
   Opened 2026-03-03, **closed "not planned" / stale, no fix**. If the community patches
   regress, you land back on that.
4. ⚠️ **vLLM's own quantization matrix contradicts itself here** and says Marlin covers
   `GPTQ/AWQ/FP8/FP4` on Ampere with a footnote excluding only Turing, while #35922 says
   the MoE path dies ([quantization-formats.md §5](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md),
   open question 9). Both were true on different code paths; the patch set is the
   reconciliation.

**Precision and accuracy**

5. **The executed precision is BF16 math on 4-bit and 8-bit storage** — Marlin W4A16 and
   FP8-Marlin W8A16. Accuracy is therefore governed by the *checkpoint's* MXFP4/FP8
   quantization, which DeepSeek trained for, not by any A100-side requantisation. The
   measured evals on this exact stack (SWE-bench Verified 87.0 %, GPQA Diamond 88.89 %,
   GSM8K 95.53 %) are consistent with the model card's own figures, so **no accuracy loss is
   attributable to the Ampere path**. ⚠️ But they were produced by the same party that
   produced the throughput numbers, with no independent reproduction.
6. ⚠️ **`--hf-overrides '{"head_dtype":"float32"}'` is unexplained.** Forcing the lm_head to
   FP32 costs +1.33 GB and smells like a numerics workaround for the SM80 path. Nobody says why.
7. ⚠️ **SWA Bounded Replay has no published accuracy delta** on any hardware — the tech
   report says only that it *"barely compromises response quality"* ([architecture.md §2.3](./architecture.md)).
8. ⚠️ **FP8 KV is storage-only on A100.** Without FA3 the K/V are dequantised to FP16 before
   the MMA, and per-head FP8 scales are unavailable (`supports_per_head_quant_scales()`
   requires FA ≥ 3), leaving only per-tensor scales. Calibrate them; the vLLM default of
   all-ones is the accuracy-risky option (`gpus/a100.md` §6).

**Numbers I could not close**

9. ⚠️ **No DSpark acceptance rate on A100/A800.** The recipe runs DSpark5 in every
   measurement and publishes nothing. The only real measurement of this checkpoint's
   acceptance anywhere is the DGX Spark community **mean 3.57 tok/step**; the widely quoted
   **3.51 is a synthetic benchmark constant** (`"rejection_sample_method":"synthetic"`), not
   an observation. My §3 `k` therefore silently contains whatever DSpark contributes.
10. ⚠️ **No prefill throughput measurement.** §4's `$/1M input` rests on a roofline and a
    decode-derived haircut; the band is 2.9× wide ($0.057–$0.170 at the low tier).
11. ⚠️ **No 1M-context decode step time on any hardware**, so the `1024K` rows of §1.3 are a
    capacity statement only. The recipe advertises 1M and the KV budget supports 146
    sequences at FP8 KV, but nobody has published a step time.
12. ⚠️ **The FP4-KV-on-Ampere question is open.** [`architecture.md`](./architecture.md) §5.2
    and METHODOLOGY §8 say the FP4 KV is software-dequantised and therefore not
    Blackwell-only; three cross-cutting docs and the working recipe say otherwise. If §5.2
    is right, an engine could halve KV bytes here and roughly double every concurrency
    figure in §1.3. **Nobody has built it.**
13. ⚠️ **Engram contribution to the step time is unquantified on this stack.** The recipe
    offloads to host and does not isolate the term; my §3.4 PCIe arithmetic is a lower bound
    on bytes, not on latency. The one isolated measurement anywhere is DGX Spark's 2.8 ms.
14. ⚠️ **Concurrency above 128 is untested.** KV allows 12,886 at 8K. The recipe's
    `max-num-seqs 64 × DP2` and its 384-row CUDA-graph ceiling both stop at 128, and C256
    measured **zero** additional throughput. Whether a larger `max-num-seqs` with recaptured
    graphs unlocks more is unknown.
15. ⚠️ **Vision is disabled in every published run** (`--language-model-only`). #57144 lists
    "Vision tower + DeepSelect" as an unresolved blocker. **Assume multimodal does not work
    on A100 until someone shows it does.**
16. ⚠️ **Implied MBU 0.164–0.409** is far below METHODOLOGY §4's 0.6–0.8 planning band. I
    have propagated the measured value rather than the default, but it comes from one
    workload on one patched stack.
17. ⚠️ **A100 supply is thinning.** Vast.ai showed **zero** A100 SXM4 and **zero** A100 PCIe
    offers on 2026-09-19; the used market is described as *"high supply as data centers cycle
    from Ampere to Hopper and Blackwell"* (`gpus/a100.md` gotcha 10). Price a migration path.
18. **Power envelope.** The HGX A100-80GB **CTS** SKU runs at 500 W versus the standard
    400 W, and MLPerf v3.1 showed that difference worth **1.56×** on GPT-J offline. When
    renting you usually cannot tell which you got — `nvidia-smi -q -d POWER` before
    benchmarking (`gpus/a100.md` gotcha 1).

**If you decide this is too much risk**, the nearest runnable alternatives, in order:
(a) **8× H200** — vLLM-*verified*, 1,128 GB holds the checkpoint with the Engram tables
resident and no offload, TP4 default, `MXFP4 → Marlin W4A16` still (no FP4 math) but with
FA3, FlashMLA sparse, real FP8 attention and an optional `VLLM_DSV4_FP4_DEQUANT=1`
re-encode to block-FP8 for 1.4–1.5× prefill; (b) **8× H100** with the same mandatory
`--engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}'`; (c) staying on the **DeepSeek API**, which §4.4
shows is cheaper than any rented A100 configuration at every utilisation.
There is no smaller-checkpoint escape on A100: the 2.0 bpw EXL3 pack is 358 GB (4 GPUs of
usable memory is 288 GB), the AutoRound INT4 build is 451.7 GB, and MXFP4-everything is
411.69 GB — **all still need 8 GPUs**, so shrinking the checkpoint buys KV headroom, not a
cheaper node.
