## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, yes** — vLLM `main`/nightly (recipe declares `min_vllm_version: 0.30.0`,
   `nightly_required: true`; 0.30.0 does not exist, so any nightly ≥ 2026-09-10; InferenceX ran
   image `vllm/vllm-openai:deepseekv41-flash-0909`), or SGLang `main` preview
   `lmsysorg/sglang:dev-dsv41`. Both vendors mark `b300: verified`. TensorRT-LLM does **not**
   support V4.1 [inference-engines.md §3.6](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md).
2. **Min 2 GPUs (TP2, Engram tables host-offloaded — arithmetically required, not optional);
   recommended 4 (TP4)** for interactivity, or **four independent TP2 replicas per 8-GPU node**
   for throughput. TP2 is vLLM's default; the recipe itself says *"For high interactivity,
   deploy the model using TP4."*
3. **Weight format actually executed: MXFP4 routed experts as W4A8** (MXFP4 weights × MXFP8
   activations → PTX `.kind::mxf8f6f4`, i.e. the **4,500 TFLOPS FP8 rate, not the 13,500 TFLOPS
   FP4 rate**), non-expert tensors FP8 E4M3 on 32×32 UE8M0 tiles, FP4 compressed KV at
   890 B/token. **B300's entire 1.5× FP4 advantage over B200 is therefore unavailable on the
   default path** — and the measured B300-vs-B200 rows confirm it: identical output tok/s/GPU at
   every concurrency B200 can hold (§3.2). B300 wins on *capacity*, which is worth +245 % at
   concurrency 128.
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $1.50–$3.04 per 1M output tokens** (Hyperstack $7.40 →
   OCI $15.00 per GPU-hour), $0.46 at SemiAnalysis's owned-at-scale TCO. **Max throughput (S4):
   $0.77–$1.57 per 1M output tokens.** Blended (METHODOLOGY §6): **$0.48–$0.97 per 1M**.
5. **Confidence: `measured`** for throughput, latency, KV-pool size and cost; `est.` for the fit
   table at GPU counts nobody has published, and ⚠️ for the W4A4/NVFP4 upside (§6).

## 1. Fit

so the allowed GPU counts are {1, 2, 4, 8} in one node and {16, 32, …} across nodes over
Quantum-X800 InfiniBand / Spectrum-X at 1.6 TB/s per node
([gpus/b300.md §4](../../gpus/b300.md)). The 18/36/72 counts are a **GB300 NVL72** property and
do not exist here — that part is a different SKU at 288 GB/GPU (≈ 279 usable) and must never be
merged with this one (METHODOLOGY §8; [gpus/b300.md §2](../../gpus/b300.md)).

### 1.1 Which tensors cannot be sharded

| Tensor group | Bytes (whole model) | Why it cannot shard | Cost per GPU |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| **The compressed KV cache itself** | 890 B/token | `num_key_value_heads: 1` — one MLA-style latent per cache-owning layer. TP shards the 64 *query* heads; every rank needs the whole latent. | full KV, on every rank |
| mHC coefficient projections `hc_*_fn/_base/_scale` (F32) | 0.157 GB | produce 24 scalars per sublayer from the flattened 4×5120 stream; no reduction dimension to split | 0.157 GB |
| Routers `ffn.gate.weight` (BF16) | 0.157 GB | every rank must score all 384 experts to route | 0.157 GB |
| `attn.compressor.wkv/wgate/norm` (BF16) | 0.037 GB | feeds the single shared latent | 0.037 GB |
| Indexer `wk`, `weights_proj`, `k_norm` | 0.003 GB | same | 0.003 GB |
| Norms, `ffn.gate.bias`/`bias_vl`, `attn_sink` | 0.0004 GB | elementwise | 0.0004 GB |
| Vision tower + aligner | 0.97 GB | replicated when `--mm-encoder-tp-mode data` (the recipe's default) | 0.97 GB if multimodal |
| **Engram hash tables** | 202.76 GB | 24 random row gathers/token/module; a column-parallel `engram.wkv` needs the *whole* 6,144-wide concatenation, so naive TP replicates them ⚠️ | see §1.3 |

Total unconditionally-replicated weight = **0.355 GB/GPU** (+0.97 GB with the ViT resident).
Tiny in bytes; the load-bearing item is the **KV cache**, which is replicated and therefore
does *not* get cheaper per sequence as you add TP ranks.

**⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED — but now strongly constrained by measurement.** The replicated-vs-sharded
KV question is flagged open in
[serving-optimizations.md §7.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md) ("*If CSA2's latent
KV shards across TP ranks rather than replicating, ≈ 3,668 — ⚠️ which; the replicated reading is
the binding one*"). §1.4 below shows the **measured** KV pools on B300 reconcile with the
**replicated, per-rank** reading and not with the aggregate one.

### 1.2 Fit table

`usable_hbm = 268 × 0.90 = 241.2 GB`; `per_gpu_weights = (W − R)/n + R` with `R = 0.355 GB`;
`kv_budget = usable_hbm − per_gpu_weights − activation_ws` (METHODOLOGY §3).

Two workspace figures are shown. METHODOLOGY §3 suggests **2–6 GB**; **the measured B300 KV
pools imply 44–58 GB** for this model (§1.4) — hyper-connections carry the residual stream in
**4 parallel copies** ([architecture.md §2.7](architecture.md)), the B300 profile batches 8,192
tokens, and it captures a 26-size CUDA-graph ladder up to 8,190. **Plan with 50 GB on this
pair.** The 12 GB column is kept because it is what [architecture.md §12 q13](architecture.md)
used, and because it is the right order for a `--max-num-batched-tokens 2048`, eager
deployment.

**The one line that decides the deployment:** at **TP2 with the Engram tables resident,
per-GPU weights are 255.3 GB against a 241.2 GB budget — 14.1 GB over before a single byte of
KV.** That is why `--engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}'` appears in vLLM's B300 profile and
nowhere in its B200, GB200 or GB300 profiles: on this SKU at TP2 it is *arithmetically
required*. ([inference-engines.md §3.6](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md) reaches the
same conclusion from GiB: *"at 268 GB/GPU, TP2 puts 237.6 GiB of weights against a 224.6 GiB
0.90-utilization budget"*.) At **TP4 the tables fit resident** (127.8 GB of 241.2), and §1.4
shows the measured TP4 runs did keep them resident.

If the latent KV does shard across ranks (the optimistic reading; METHODOLOGY §3's literal
`kv_budget × n_gpus`), multiply the (c) rows by `n`: TP4 at 128 K becomes 3,812 rather than 953
and TP8 becomes 10,200. **Do not plan on it** until someone prints `num_gpu_blocks` per rank —
§1.4 says the measurement does not support it.

**KV is never the binding constraint on this GPU.** At 128 K and TP4 the budget holds ~950
sequences of full-length context, and the measured runs used **2–26 % of the pool**
(`gpu_kv_cache_usage_pct` 0.008–0.258). vLLM's own summary is right: *"Weights and batch size,
not cache, set the capacity limit"* ([vLLM recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)).

### 1.4 The fit model validated against measured KV pools

InferenceX reports `kv_cache_pool_tokens` for every run. Converting at 890 B/token and solving
for the workspace term:

| TP | measured pool | = GB | implied ws, Engram **resident+sharded** | implied ws, Engram **host** |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 4 | 78.11 M tok | 69.52 | **43.8 GB** | 94.5 GB (implausible) |
| 4 | 63.09 M tok | 56.15 | **57.2 GB** | 107.9 GB (implausible) |
| 2 | 50.13 M tok | 44.62 | −58.7 GB (impossible) | **42.6 GB** |
| 2 | 42.64 M tok | 37.95 | −52.1 GB (impossible) | **49.3 GB** |
| 2 | 32.58 M tok | 29.00 | −43.1 GB (impossible) | **58.3 GB** |

Three things fall out, and they are mutually consistent:

1. **The pool is a per-rank figure and the KV is replicated.** Under the aggregate reading the
   implied per-GPU pool would be 14–17 GB against a 114 GB budget, i.e. vLLM would be leaving
   85 % of free HBM unused — which it does not do.
2. **TP4 keeps the Engram tables resident and shards them; TP2 must offload them.** The TP2
   rows are arithmetically impossible with the tables resident, and the TP4 rows are
   implausible with them offloaded. Same conclusion as §1.2, now from the other direction. It
   also answers the ⚠️ in the table above: **on B300 at TP4 the tables do shard.**
3. **The workspace term is 44–58 GB/GPU, not 2–6 GB.** Both branches converge on the same band
   from independent arithmetic. `est.`, but doubly derived.

### 2.1 The finding that matters most

B300's headline over B200 is **1.5× dense FP4** and nothing else — FP8, BF16, TF32 and HBM
bandwidth are identical ([gpus/b300.md §12b](../../gpus/b300.md),
[quantization-formats.md §4.3](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)). Collecting that
1.5× requires the **`.kind::mxf4` / `.kind::mxf4nvf4` W4A4 path**. This checkpoint's default
execution is **W4A8**: MXFP4 weights against MXFP8 activations, which is `.kind::mxf8f6f4` —
the pipe NVIDIA lists on the **FP8 row** (it is the same pipe that makes FP6 run at FP8 rate,
[gpus/b300.md §3](../../gpus/b300.md)). So the default path sees 4,500 TFLOPS on a B300 exactly
as it does on a B200.

This is an **inference from the PTX kinds plus the engine's own precision labelling**, not a
vendor statement, so it is **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** with `cuobjdump -sass` on the expert GEMM.
But it predicts something falsifiable, and the prediction holds: §3.2 shows B200 and B300
posting output tok/s/GPU within 1.5 % of each other at concurrency 1–64 on the same harness on
the same day.

### 3.5 Why estimate and measurement differ

| Observation | Size of gap | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| At batch 1, roofline says 0.65 ms TPOT; measured 2.42 ms | **3.7×** | decode at batch 1 is **launch-count bound, not bandwidth bound**. | At batch 128 TP4, roofline says 4.59 ms; measured 23.31 ms | **5.1×** | the step is neither bandwidth- nor GEMM-bound. The candidates: the **CSA2 indexer's full scan** of the whole causally-visible range in the 4 Full-mode layers, the gather-based `sparse_attn` over 640 entries in 30 Reuse layers, the 48 random Engram gathers per token, and kernel launch overhead. Nobody has published a profile ⚠️ |
| Measured decode MFU 0.0217, prefill MFU 0.0307 vs METHODOLOGY's 0.25–0.40 | **8–18×** | METHODOLOGY's bands are for dense/GQA transformers. **They do not transfer to this architecture.** The same discrepancy is recorded on MI355X, where the roofline under-predicts step time by ~12.6× ([architecture.md §12 q9](architecture.md)) — so this is an architecture property, not a B300 property |

### 4.4 Effect of speculative decoding and prefix caching

**DSpark.** At the S1 point, γ = 5 raises bytes/GPU/step from 69.0 GB (no speculation) to
78.6 GB, but yields 3.57 output tokens instead of 1 — **3.13× output per byte** `est.`
Removing DSpark therefore costs ~68 % of throughput and takes $/1M output at $7.40 from
**$1.50 to $4.69**. Caveats: (a) a = 3.57 is a community measurement on DGX Spark, not on B300
⚠️; (b) the 3.51 figure everyone quotes is a *synthetic benchmark constant*; (c) the benefit
shrinks as batch grows and the verify step becomes compute-bound — per
[architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md) that crossover lands late here because expert reads do
not saturate until ~batch 128, which is exactly where the measured curve flattens.

**Prefix caching.** Effective $/1M input at $7.40, assuming a hit costs 10 % of a miss for our
own serving (METHODOLOGY §6):

| prefix hit rate | effective $/1M input |
|---|--:|
| 0 % | $0.2583 |
| 50 % | $0.1420 |
| 90 % | $0.0491 |
| **95.6 % (the measured B300 rate)** | **$0.0361** |

For comparison the DeepSeek API charges **$0.0030/M** for a cache hit — 12× less than our
best self-hosted cached-input cost. METHODOLOGY §6 and
[architecture.md §11.1](architecture.md) are explicit that the vendor ratio (≈ 2.1 % for
DeepSeek, not a generic 10 %) is the one to use when comparing *against the API*; the 10 %
figure above is our own serving cost.

Prefix caching does **not** remove the whole prefill on this architecture: the four Full-mode
indexer layers still scan the entire cached range for every new token
([architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md), tech report §2.3.2). That term is folded into the
TTFT-90 % column of §3.4 and is why TTFT at 90 % hit is 10 % of cold at 4 K but only 10.8 % at
128 K.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

**Stay on one node.** A 2,144 GB HGX B300 node holds the 510.29 GB checkpoint four times over,
and §1.2 shows the KV budget is comfortable from TP4 up. The only reasons to cross nodes for
this pair would be wide-EP or PD disaggregation, and neither pays here:

- **Wide-EP** (DeepEP, EPLB) was designed for the 72-GPU NVL72 domain; on an 8-GPU domain it
  *"runs badly"*, and everything wider than EP8 pays 20–40 % comm overhead over InfiniBand
  ([gpus/b300.md §11.11](../../gpus/b300.md), METHODOLOGY §4). With 384 experts and top-6 the
  model *would* reward wide EP in principle — but the measured curve already flattens at batch
  64–128 for other reasons (§3.5), so there is nothing for wide-EP to unlock until the kernel
  gap closes.
- **PD disaggregation** *"is a thousand-GPU problem"*; below that, rounding losses dominate and
  Modular reports a **20–30 % performance drop** on small or untuned workloads
  ([serving-optimizations.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). A 1P1D split
  of one 8-GPU node is 4 GPUs per role — exactly the regime that piece warns about. It becomes
  interesting at ≥ 4 nodes, and vLLM's verified 1P1D layout for this model is on **GB200 NVL4,
  text-only**, not on B300 ⚠️.
- **What *does* pay on one node:** run **four independent TP2 replicas** rather than one TP8
  deployment. The measured TP2 rows deliver 2,656 out tok/s/GPU against TP4's 1,798 — a **1.48×
  throughput-per-GPU win** — at the cost of interactivity (41.5 vs 42.9 tok/s/user at c128 is a
  wash, but TP2's TTFT collapsed to 35.2 s on 72 K prompts, so cap prompt length or fall back
  to TP4 for long-context traffic).

### 5.2 Launch commands

**vLLM, B300, the vendor default (TP2).** Quoted from vLLM's own hardware profile
(<https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash/hw/b300.json>, fetched 2026-09-19) and
the recipe page (<https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash>):

```bash
# image: vllm/vllm-openai:nightly
VLLM_ENGINE_READY_TIMEOUT_S=3600 VLLM_USE_RUST_FRONTEND=1 \
vllm serve deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash \
  --tokenizer-mode deepseek_v41 \
  --tensor-parallel-size 2 \
  --engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}' \
  --max-cudagraph-capture-size 8190 \
  --max-num-batched-tokens 8192 \
  --max-num-seqs 256 \
  --tool-call-parser deepseek_v41 --enable-auto-tool-choice \
  --reasoning-parser deepseek_v41 \
  --mm-encoder-tp-mode data
# compilation: VLLM_COMPILE, FULL_AND_PIECEWISE cudagraph capture across 26 sizes, 6..8190
```

> *"The command builder defaults to TP2 for B300 (2 of the 8 GPUs). **For high interactivity,
> deploy the model using TP4: pass `--tensor-parallel-size 4`.**"*
> — [vLLM recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash), verbatim

For TP4, drop `--engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}'` — §1.4 shows the tables fit resident and
shard at TP4, and keeping them in HBM avoids the PCIe gather path that cost the DGX Spark
deployment 5.9–7.8 ms per step ([architecture.md §4.1](architecture.md)). Add
`--speculative-config` from [architecture.md §7.4](architecture.md):

```bash
--speculative-config '{"method":"dspark","num_speculative_tokens":5,
  "draft_sample_method":"probabilistic","rejection_sample_method":"block",
  "enable_adaptive_verification":true}'
# size the graph ladder for the widened batch: 128 * (1 + 5) = 768 -> round up to 1024
```

**SGLang** — B300 is `verified: true` at TP4/EP4. The closest quoted command is the GB300
Low-Latency cell ([inference-engines.md §6.1](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md),
<https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/DeepSeek/DeepSeek-V4_1.md>); the B300 cell
differs only in GPU count:

```bash
# image: lmsysorg/sglang:dev-dsv41
sglang serve --trust-remote-code --model-path deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash \
  --tp 4 --ep-size 4 --mem-fraction-static 0.8 \
  --speculative-algorithm DSPARK --speculative-dspark-block-size 5 \
  --cuda-graph-max-bs-decode 64 \
  --reasoning-parser auto --tool-call-parser auto \
  --host 0.0.0.0 --port 30000
```

> *"`--attention-backend`, `--moe-runner-backend` and `--fp8-gemm-backend` are selected
> automatically … **Overriding them is the most common cause of a disappointing measurement**:
> it leaves the 32-wide ue8m0 blocks on the Triton `_w8a8_block_fp8_matmul` fallback, which
> dominates the decode step and costs most of the model's bs=1 throughput."*
> — SGLang cookbook, quoted in [inference-engines.md §6.1](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)

**Do not run TensorRT-LLM** — it has no V4.1 support (V4 only), and NVIDIA's own NVFP4 card
lists only vLLM and SGLang ([inference-engines.md §2.3](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)).

### 5.3 Warm-up and load time

| what | GB | @ 5 GB/s | @ 10 GB/s | @ 20 GB/s |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| full checkpoint | 510.3 | 1.7 min | 0.9 min | 0.4 min |
| resident weights, Engram host-offloaded | 307.5 | 1.0 min | 0.5 min | 0.3 min |
| Engram tables only, into host RAM | 202.8 | 0.7 min | 0.3 min | 0.2 min |

Per-GPU share at TP4 with Engram on host: **76.9 GB**. `p6-b300.48xlarge` carries **8 × 3.84 TB
= 30.72 TB of local NVMe** and 4 TB of system RAM ([gpus/b300.md §11.13](../../gpus/b300.md)),
so the whole checkpoint plus the Engram tables stage locally with room to spare.

**Streaming is not what makes the first boot slow.** vLLM's base env sets
`VLLM_ENGINE_READY_TIMEOUT_S=3600` and the recipe says *"Expect a long first load"*; the time
goes into FlashInfer autotune/JIT and DeepGEMM warm-up, not I/O. Use
`--model-loader-extra-config '{"enable_multithread_load": true, "num_threads": 128}'` (from the
GB300 command in [architecture.md §8.6](architecture.md)) and, if startup fails or drags,
`--no-enable-flashinfer-autotune` / `VLLM_DEEP_GEMM_WARMUP=skip`. A comparable SGLang
deployment reports *"5–10 min (model loading + JIT kernel compilation)"*
([Yotta Labs](https://docs.yottalabs.ai/tutorials/inference-and-serving/run-deepseek-v4-flash-pro-on-b300)).

## 6. Risks and open questions

**Precision / accuracy**

1. **⚠️ Is the expert GEMM really W4A8 at the FP8 rate?** §2.1's argument is an inference from
   the PTX `.kind` families plus vLLM's and SGLang's own "W4A8" labelling
   ([quantization-formats.md §9.1](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)), not a vendor
   statement. It is corroborated by the B300 ≡ B200 measurement but should be settled with
   `cuobjdump -sass` on the expert GEMM. If it is wrong, B300 should already be 1.5× B200 —
   and it is not.
2. **⚠️ Does `--enable-w4a4-mxfp4-megamoe` actually help on B300?** It is the only documented way
   to reach 13,500 TFLOPS with this checkpoint, and nobody has published a measurement of it on
   any GPU for this model.
3. **⚠️ Is the NVFP4 build faster than the base on B300?** NVIDIA publishes accuracy parity and a
   **larger** checkpoint (527.27 vs 510.29 GB) and **no speedup number at all**; its only tested
   hardware is GB300 ([gpus/b300.md §6](../../gpus/b300.md),
   [quantization-formats.md §9.2](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)). The pair in this
   document is the base MXFP4 build, which is the right default; do not switch on format
   reasoning alone.
4. **⚠️ SWA Bounded Replay accuracy delta is unpublished.** The tech report says it *"barely
   compromises response quality"* and gives no number ([architecture.md §2.3](architecture.md)).
   It is off by default in vLLM and opt-in in SGLang; it also refuses prompt logprobs.
5. **⚠️ FP4 KV accuracy under long context.** The 890 B/token figure presumes the Blackwell FP4-KV
   kernel, which B300 has. `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers sliding_window` exists precisely because
   *"sliding-window layers are more sensitive to KV-cache quantization"*
   ([quantization-formats.md §8.3](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)) — and every layer
   of this model carries a 128-token SWA component. No eval isolates it.

**Performance model**

6. **⚠️ The 5× gap between roofline and measurement at batch 128 is unexplained** (§3.5). No
   vendor or engine has published a kernel-level profile of CSA2 decode on any GPU. Until one
   exists, plan from the measured ceiling (1,798 tok/s/GPU at TP4, 2,656 at TP2), not from
   METHODOLOGY's MBU/MFU bands, which over-predict this architecture by 8–18×.
7. **⚠️ DSpark acceptance on B300 is unknown.** Every number in §4.4 rides on a = 3.57, a
   community measurement on **GB10 DGX Sparks**. The implied-MBU rows above 1.0 in §3.3 are
   direct evidence that either acceptance or the effective draft width differs on these traces.
8. **⚠️ Whether the measured runs used FP4 or FP8 KV.** InferenceX's `precision: fp4` labels the
   weights. The KV-pool arithmetic reconciles at 890 B/token, which supports FP4 KV, but that is
   circumstantial.
9. **⚠️ No measurement at S1/S2/S3 shapes.** All 15 B300 rows are agentic traces with 72 K–335 K
   prompts and 90–97 % cache hit. The decode-rate transfer to 4 K prompts is justified in §4.2
   (the KV term is 2–4 % of the step) but has not been measured.
10. **⚠️ No 1M-context measurement on any hardware** for this model
    ([architecture.md §12 q7](architecture.md)). §3.4's 1 M prefill figure (24.9 PFLOP, 41 % of
    which is the indexer's quadratic term) is `est.`
11. **⚠️ No multimodal measurement on B300.** Every published run is `--language-model-only`.
    A max-resolution image costs ≈ 18.9 TFLOP; the encoder-DP flag is untested for this pair.

**Engine / platform**

12. **Nothing is in a numbered release.** vLLM `main` only (the recipe demands a version, 0.30.0,
    that does not exist); SGLang `main` preview image only. Pin the digest, re-test on every bump.
    [inference-engines.md §0](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)
13. **vLLM has no Blackwell CI** — its own RFC says CUDA CI covers only L4 and H100, and that
    *"importability does not prove that an optional extension contains a kernel for the active
    GPU"*; FA4 and full NVFP4 native paths are *"not yet qualified"*
    ([gpus/b300.md §11.7](../../gpus/b300.md)).
14. **FlashInfer is the default attention backend on Blackwell and is *"architecture-gated but
    not yet signoff-qualified"* on SM103a** ([gpus/b300.md §11.6](../../gpus/b300.md)). Default +
    unqualified: regression-test before production.
15. **`sm_100a` binaries do not load on B300.** Verify with
    `cuobjdump --list-elf lib*.so | grep -c sm_103` before blaming the model
    ([gpus/b300.md §11.2](../../gpus/b300.md)). FlashMLA does ship `sm_103a` (PR #221); other
    projects may not.
16. **FP4 MoE needs an environment variable** on some paths: `VLLM_USE_FLASHINFER_MOE_FP4=1`.
    Without it you silently get a slower MoE path ([gpus/b300.md §11.8](../../gpus/b300.md)).
17. **Output is not bitwise stable.** `--enable-deterministic-inference` is refused on SGLang's
    `dsv4` backend; *"two default kernels are shape-guarded to a single token"*.
18. **PD and speculative decoding cannot be combined** on SGLang; on vLLM, DSpark must run in
    both pools ([serving-optimizations.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
19. **Reasoning-effort mapping forks between engines.** vLLM maps `low/high/xhigh/max` to
    25/50/75/100 and defaults thinking **on** at effort 50; SGLang defaults it **off**; the
    DeepSeek API maps `low/high/max` to 50/75/100. Since effort 25 → 100 multiplies output
    tokens ~2.5× ([architecture.md §10.5](architecture.md)), **this silently changes your
    $/request by 2.5× between engines.** Effort 60–80 is the cost-efficient operating point.
20. **⚠️ No public data on HGX B300 sustained-clock behaviour** under long FP4 prefill bursts —
    the air-cooled bin is the derated one (13,500 vs GB300's 15,000 dense FP4). Watch
    `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=clocks_throttle_reasons.active`
    ([gpus/b300.md §11.10](../../gpus/b300.md)). The measured runs drew 323–1,051 W average
    against an ~1,100 W limit, so the top of the curve is close to the cap.
21. **Cloud availability is a sales conversation.** AWS routes real on-demand through account
    managers, P6-B300 exists in six regions, Azure publishes no price, Lambda and Crusoe do not
    list B300 at all ([gpus/b300.md §11.12](../../gpus/b300.md)).
22. **⚠️ B300's "2× attention" claim is unqualified and unmeasured.** Its only footnote is the
    comparison baseline. This model's attention is a gather over 640 selected entries, not a
    FlashAttention softmax loop, so the SFU doubling may not even apply
    ([gpus/b300.md §3](../../gpus/b300.md), [quantization-formats.md §4.3](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)).
23. **⚠️ The workspace figure (44–58 GB/GPU) is derived, not reported.** It is inferred from
    measured KV pools in §1.4 by two independent routes that agree; it has never been printed by
    an engine log in public.

**Total ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED items in this document: 23.**
