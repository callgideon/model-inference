# nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4 on NVIDIA H200 SXM 141GB (HGX 8-GPU)

> Research date **2026-09-19**. Formulas, legend and markers per
> [`research/METHODOLOGY.md`](../../METHODOLOGY.md). Model facts are taken from
> [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) and GPU facts from
> [`research/gpus/h200.md`](../../gpus/h200.md); both are fact-checked and are
> cited by section rather than re-derived. Every number here is `meas.` with a
> source link, `est.` with the formula shown, or **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED**.
> All arithmetic was run in `python3`; the script's output is pasted verbatim
> into the tables below.

## 0. Verdict

1. **Not supported today.** The bytes fit on 8×H200 and vLLM/SGLang will *load*
   an NVFP4 checkpoint on `sm_90` via an explicit Marlin W4A16 opt-in, but no
   vendor, engine recipe or published run covers **this** checkpoint on Hopper:
   NVIDIA's card states *"Supported Hardware Microarchitecture Compatibility:
   NVIDIA Blackwell"* and nothing else [src](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4/raw/main/README.md).
2. **Engine + version if you try anyway:** vLLM `v0.29.0+` / `vllm/vllm-openai:nightly`
   (≥ 2026‑09‑10) with `--moe-backend marlin`, `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_ds_mla`,
   `--language-model-only`; or SGLang `v0.5.20` with `--fp4-gemm-backend marlin`.
   Both are unvalidated on this pair; see §2 and §6.
3. **Min 8 GPUs, recommended 8 GPUs, TP8 on one HGX H200 node.** TP1/2/4 are
   `infeasible (KV)` — 4 × 141 GB × 0.90 = 507.6 GB against 527.27 GB of weights
   (§1). TP4 becomes feasible only with `--engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}'`.
4. **Weight format actually executed: BF16.** `sm_90` has no FP4 tensor core, so
   the NVFP4 routed experts are dequantised in-register by Marlin and the MMA is
   issued at the BF16 rate. You keep the 4-bit HBM footprint; you lose the FP4
   math entirely. KV runs at **FP8 1,650 B/token**, not the architectural
   890 B/token — `nvfp4_ds_mla` needs `FLASHMLA_MEGA_ATTN_DSV41`, which is
   `major == 10` only.
5. **Cost (8×H200, calibrated est.):** interactive S1 **$1.01–$2.01 per 1M output
   tokens**; max-throughput S4 **$0.25–$0.49**. Blended (75 % in / 25 % out)
   **$0.28–$0.56** at S1. **Confidence: `estimate`** — decode is calibrated
   against a measured 8×H200 sweep of the *base* checkpoint; nothing has been
   measured for the NVFP4 build on any Hopper part.

> **The one-line answer to the question this document exists to ask:** on H200
> the NVFP4 build is **~4–5 % slower and ~4 % smaller in KV pool** than the base
> MXFP4 checkpoint, for zero accuracy benefit that Hopper can realise, because
> both are dequantised to BF16 by the same kernel and the NVFP4 one carries
> 16.99 GB more scale bytes through HBM on every expert read (§3.5). **Use the
> base `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` checkpoint on H200.**

> reading as primary** — which moves the numbers by more than the ring does.

> **Activation workspace: 4 GiB is wrong for this model.** vLLM's recipe states
> that the sparse-attention indexer allocates a
> `[max-num-batched-tokens × max-model-len]` logits buffer at start-up, and that
> *"8192 x 1M x 2 B is exactly 16 GiB and does not fit next to the weights, so
> batched tokens are capped at 4096 (8 GiB)"*
> [src](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/recipes/main/models/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.yaml).
> This document therefore plans with **`activation_ws = 8 GiB`** at 1 M context
> and shows the METHODOLOGY 4 GiB default alongside.

### 1.3 Fit over the GPU counts this topology allows

HGX H200 is an 8-GPU NVSwitch domain; there is **no NVL72-equivalent on Hopper**
and the largest single NVLink domain is 8 GPUs / 1,128 GB
([h200.md §4](../../gpus/h200.md)). The allowed set is therefore {1, 2, 4, 8}
single-node and {16, 32, …} multi-node.

```

    1         TP1        525.63   1.64     527.27     -409.0         0.0           no    INFEASIBLE
    2         TP2        262.82   1.64     264.46     -146.1         0.0           no    INFEASIBLE
    4         TP4        131.41   1.64     133.05      -14.7         0.0           no    INFEASIBLE
    8   TP8 / EP8         65.70   1.64      67.34       51.0       407.7           no            OK
   16     2 nodes         32.85   1.64      34.49       83.8     1,341.1  YES 400G IB/EFA        OK
   32     4 nodes         16.43   1.64      18.07      100.2     3,207.8  YES 400G IB/EFA        OK
```
`est.`, `kv_budget = 126.90 GB − wt/GPU − 8 GiB`. At METHODOLOGY's 4 GiB
workspace the TP4 row is still negative (−10.4 GB), so **TP4 does not fit under
any workspace assumption**; TP8 is the floor.

> **The vLLM recipe's own TP default for H200 is 4**, because `single_node_tp`
> sets `tp: {default: 4, h100: 8, b300: 2}` with no `h200` override, while the
> same file declares `vram_minimum_gb: 614`
> [src](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/recipes/main/models/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.yaml).
> 4 × 141 = 564 GB < 614 GB even for the *base* checkpoint. The recipe's prose
> is the correct guidance — *"It fits … one 8-GPU H200 node (1128 GB) with room
> for KV cache"* — and its `tp` default is not. **Use TP8.** ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED
> whether the recipe's command builder warns on this.

**Engram CPU offload** — the lever the recipe applies on H100
(`--engram-config '{"cpu_offload":true}'`, moving the 202.76 GB FP8/MXFP8 Engram
tables to pinned host DRAM behind UVA):

```
   n=2   wt/GPU  163.08 GB  kv/GPU  -44.8 GB  INFEASIBLE
   n=4   wt/GPU   82.36 GB  kv/GPU   36.0 GB  node   143.8 GB  OK
   n=8   wt/GPU   42.00 GB  kv/GPU   76.3 GB  node   610.5 GB  OK
   n=16  wt/GPU   21.82 GB  kv/GPU   96.5 GB  node 1,543.9 GB  OK
```
TP4 becomes feasible, at the price of routing 12,672 B/token of scattered Engram
gather across PCIe Gen5 (128 GB/s bidirectional) instead of HBM
([h200.md §4](../../gpus/h200.md)). **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** — no measurement of the
Engram-offload decode penalty exists on any GPU.

```

   FP8 KV, REPLICATED, 16 GPUs (2 nodes)                      5,103       1,471         382          48
   FP8 KV, REPLICATED, 8 GPUs, Engram CPU-offloaded           4,646       1,339         348          44
```
`est.`, `floor(pool / (ctx × B + 2,906,112))`.

**Which reading is right — settled by measurement.** The InferenceX 8×H200 run of
DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash reports a KV pool of **31.0 M tokens**
[src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/api/v1/benchmarks?model=DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash).
Against the *base* checkpoint's per-GPU weights (65.22 GB) at
`activation_ws = 8 GiB` the per-GPU KV budget is 53.09 GB:

| Hypothesis | Predicted pool | vs measured 31.0 M |
|---|---|---|
| FP8 1,650 B/tok, **replicated per rank** | **32.2 M tokens** | **+3.8 %** ✅ |
| FP4 890 B/tok, replicated | 59.7 M | +93 % ✗ |
| FP8 1,650 B/tok, aggregated ×8 | 257.4 M | +730 % ✗ |

The replicated-FP8 hypothesis is the only one that closes. **This single number
simultaneously confirms (a) that H200 runs this architecture at FP8 KV, not the
architectural FP4 KV, and (b) that the compressed latent cache is replicated on
every TP rank.** The same arithmetic for the NVFP4 build gives **30.9 M tokens**
— 4.0 % less pool for the same hardware, purely from the extra 16.99 GB of
weights.

### 1.5 Does it need multi-node?

**No.** 8 × H200 in one HGX chassis holds the model with 51 GB/GPU of KV budget,
and TP8 all-reduce runs over NVSwitch at 900 GB/s/GPU, i.e. METHODOLOGY's
5–15 % comm band ([h200.md §4](../../gpus/h200.md)). 16 GPUs buys 1.6× the
replicated-KV concurrency but crosses a node boundary (400G IB/EFA ≈ 50 GB/s per
GPU, an 18× drop) and lands in the 20–40 % overhead band. **Do not go
multi-node for this model on H200** unless you need >232 concurrent 128K
sessions, and even then prefer a second independent 8-GPU replica.

### 2.1 The precision story, stated precisely

`sm_90` has no FP4 tensor-core instruction; NVIDIA's own TensorRT-LLM matrix
lists Hopper as `FP32, FP16, BF16, FP8, INT8, INT4` with NVFP4 absent
[src](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/reference/support-matrix.html), and
FlashInfer states *"FP4 GEMM: NVFP4 and MXFP4 matrix multiplication **for
Blackwell GPUs**"* [src](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer#gemm--linear-operations).
So on this pair:

```
NVFP4 checkpoint  --load-->  4-bit weights resident in HBM (the memory win, kept)
                  --Marlin-> unpack E2M1 + E4M3/16 scales to BF16 in registers
                  --MMA---->  989.5 TFLOPS dense BF16   (the compute win, lost)
```

A community report quantifies the framing on the other non-SM100 Blackwell part:
Marlin is *"a fallback path that wastes half the theoretical throughput"* by
dequantising FP4 to FP16 rather than using native FP4 compute — and yet it still
beat every native NVFP4 attempt on SM120 (50.5 tok/s vs 5–41 tok/s)
[src](https://discuss.vllm.ai/t/sm120-rtx-pro-6000-nvfp4-moe-performance-report-qwen3-5-397b/2536).
On `sm_90` there is no native path to lose to, so Marlin is simply the path.

Independent confirmation that this is architectural, not a packaging gap: a
project that shipped a 4-bit DeepSeek-V4-Flash serving recipe for **8 × H200
(Hopper SM 9.0a)** states plainly that the NVFP4-expert approach *"requires
SM 10.0+ instructions unavailable on Hopper/early Blackwell"* and instead built
a **W4A16 INT4 + FP8-block-attention** checkpoint
[src](https://github.com/canada-quant/dsv4-flash-w4a16-fp8). That is the
engineering answer the market reached for this exact GPU: not NVFP4.

### 3.5 Gaps between est. and meas., explained

| Gap | Size | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Roofline TPOT at MBU 0.70 vs measured | **2.2–5.5×** too fast | METHODOLOGY's Hopper MBU band assumes dense FP8 decode with FA3 and CUDA graphs. the expert byte count differs: MXFP4 18,800,640 B/expert vs NVFP4 19,906,584 B,
a ratio of 1.0588):

```
 batch  NVFP4 GB/step/GPU  NVFP4 TPOT ms  base GB/step/GPU  base TPOT ms  NVFP4 penalty
     1               1.74           1.14              1.71          1.12         +1.94%
     8               6.13           4.01              5.88          3.85         +4.28%
    32              18.31          11.99             17.47         11.44         +4.81%
    64              29.56          19.35             28.21         18.47         +4.78%
   128              42.63          27.91             40.79         26.70         +4.51%
   256              55.46          36.31             53.37         34.95         +3.91%
```

Prefill and decode FLOPs are **identical** — same shapes, both dequantised to
BF16 by the same Marlin kernel. The only difference Hopper can see is the extra
scale byte per 32 weights that NVFP4's group-16 layout costs, and on a
bandwidth-bound decode that is a straight **~4–5 % tax**. NVFP4's accuracy
argument (group-16 vs group-32 dynamic range, a **wash** in NVIDIA's own evals —
4 wins, 2 losses, all within ±1.5 points, [architecture.md §10.1](architecture.md))
is about the *executed* numerics; on H200 both checkpoints execute in BF16, so
even that argument does not apply. **On this GPU the NVFP4 build is strictly
dominated by the base checkpoint.**

**Speculative decoding (MTP / DSpark), at S4:**

```

```
`est.`, assuming a 15 % draft-plus-verify overhead for acc > 1.

> **The 3.51 row is not a measurement.** It is the InferenceX MI355X server
> config's `"rejection_sample_method":"synthetic","synthetic_acceptance_length":3.51`
> — a benchmark constant
> ([serving-optimizations.md §2.3](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
> The only *measured* acceptance figure for a DeepSeek model with MTP is **2.37
> at MTP3** on GB300 [src](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-02-19-gb300-longctx/),
> and it is for R1, not V4.1. **NVIDIA did not exercise speculation for this
> checkpoint at all**: *"DSpark tensors are preserved, but speculative decoding
> was not exercised in the reported validation."*
> [src](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4/raw/main/README.md)
> Against that, every InferenceX H200 row carries `spec_method: "mtp"`, so the
> measured H200 numbers in §3.4 **already include** whatever MTP delivered — you
> cannot stack this multiplier on top of them. Note also that at batch ≥ 64 the
> distinct-expert count is already 244/384, so drafting buys little and costs a
> full verify pass ([architecture.md §7.3](architecture.md)).

**Prefix caching**, 128 K prompts. A hit is **not free**: METHODOLOGY §6 costs it at
10 % of the uncached prefill (KV load / reuse), so **input cost scales with
`1 − 0.9h`**. The TTFT column keeps `1 − h` — a hit genuinely skips the prefill
wall-clock — and floors at the 267 ms the non-prefill path costs.

```
  h=0.0    effective prefill    123,668 tok/s/node | TTFT 1,060 ms | $/1M in $0.0717 low / $0.1422 high
  h=0.5    effective prefill    224,851 tok/s/node | TTFT   530 ms | $/1M in $0.0394 / $0.0782
  h=0.9    effective prefill    650,884 tok/s/node | TTFT   267 ms | $/1M in $0.0136 / $0.0270
  h=0.975  effective prefill  1,009,535 tok/s/node | TTFT   267 ms | $/1M in $0.0088 / $0.0174
```
At the 97.6 % hit rate of the target agentic workload, prefix caching is worth
**~8× on input cost** (`1 ÷ (1 − 0.9 × 0.976) = 8.2`) and takes blended cost at
S4/low from $0.0907 to about **$0.068/1M** — the input leg of the blend shrinks
from $0.0285 to $0.0063 while the output leg ($0.0622) is untouched, which is
also why no hit rate can take this blend below ~$0.062.
**It is off in NVIDIA's own recipe** (`--no-enable-prefix-caching`).
Turning it on is the single largest cost lever available on this pair, and the
one thing that is entirely under your control. Note the counter-risk: on traffic
with *no* shared prefix, vLLM's APC cost −36.7 % throughput in a 2024-era
measurement ([serving-optimizations.md §1.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md))
— measure on your own traffic.

**Read the >100 % rows as "never breaks even".** At the interactive operating
point on rented H200s, self-hosting this checkpoint costs **1.4–3.1× DeepSeek's
own off-peak API price even at 100 % utilisation** — there is no utilisation at
which it wins. Only the batch-oriented S4 point, on reserved capacity, clears the
bar, and it needs **31 % utilisation off-peak / 15 % peak** to do so. And S4 at
batch 2,048 is 32× beyond what the published engine has demonstrated on this
hardware.

For contrast, the same exercise on Blackwell: the B200 row in
[architecture.md §11.3](architecture.md) delivers **$0.026/1M all tokens** on 4
GPUs versus H200's **$0.077** — a 3× gap that is exactly the FP4-tensor-core
gap ([h200.md §12(b)](../../gpus/h200.md): B200/H200 is 1.7–3.0× on FP8 workloads
and **4–6× where FP4 is in play**). **If you are paying for a 4-bit checkpoint,
pay for a GPU that can execute it.**

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

One 8-GPU HGX H200 node, TP8, Engram resident. That is the whole answer:
8 × 141 GB = 1,128 GB holds 527.27 GB of weights with 407.7 GB of node-wide KV
budget, all-reduce stays on NVSwitch, and **there is no Hopper rack domain to
scale into** ([h200.md §4](../../gpus/h200.md)). Scale out with independent
8-GPU replicas, not with TP16.

The one case for 16 GPUs is 128 K-context fleets: replicated KV caps a TP8 node
at **232** concurrent 128 K sessions, and 2 nodes lift that to 382 — a poor
return for a 20–40 % comm tax. **EP8 + DP-attention on a single node is the
better lever** (232 → 1,860), and it is unpublished for this architecture ⚠️.

### 5.2 When PD disaggregation or wide-EP pays here

**PD disaggregation: not on H200, for this pair.** CED makes the case
structurally attractive — prefill activates 7.55 B parameters and decode 15.75 B,
so the two phases want different shapes — and the vLLM recipe says the verified
GB200 NVL4 1P1D layout *"becomes TP8 per role"* on 8-GPU nodes
[src](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/recipes/main/models/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.yaml).
But TP8-per-role means **16 H200s for one model copy**, i.e. 2× the capital for a
workload whose measured bottleneck (§3.4) is neither prefill nor decode in
isolation but engine overhead and KV thrash at concurrency ≥ 128. Revisit if and
when a tuned H200 config demonstrates concurrency > 128.

**Wide-EP: not available.** DeepEP/EPLB wide-EP is an NVL72 story; the largest
Hopper NVLink domain is 8 GPUs. EP8 within the node is worth trying purely for
the DP-attention KV split (§5.1), not for expert bandwidth.

The **H100 override** in vLLM's own recipe is the closest thing to Hopper
guidance that exists, and its levers apply unchanged to H200 (quoted verbatim)
[src](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/recipes/main/models/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.yaml):

```yaml
h100:
  extra_args:
    - "--engram-config"
    - '{"cpu_offload":true}'
    - "--max-num-batched-tokens"
    - "4096"
    - "--gpu-memory-utilization"
    - "0.92"
  extra_env:
    VLLM_USE_V2_MODEL_RUNNER: "1"
    PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF: "expandable_segments:True"
```

with the recipe's own reasoning: *"Engram tables (183 GiB FP8) move to pinned
host DRAM behind UVA, leaving ~36 GiB/GPU of resident weights at TP8. The
sparse-attention indexer then allocates a `[max-num-batched-tokens,
max-model-len]` logits buffer at startup: 8192 x 1M x 2 B is exactly 16 GiB and
does not fit next to the weights, so batched tokens are capped at 4096 (8 GiB)…
The V2 model runner and expandable segments keep the KV pool from fragmenting
around that buffer."*

**Synthesised H200 command — untested, every deviation from a published source is
marked.** Do not treat this as a recipe:

### 5.4 Warm-up and loading

```
   5 GB/s: NVFP4    105 s (1.8 min) | base    102 s | the two 101.5 GB Engram shards alone   41 s | NVFP4 penalty +3.4 s
  10 GB/s: NVFP4     53 s (0.9 min) | base     51 s | the two 101.5 GB Engram shards alone   20 s | NVFP4 penalty +1.7 s
  20 GB/s: NVFP4     26 s (0.4 min) | base     26 s | the two 101.5 GB Engram shards alone   10 s | NVFP4 penalty +0.8 s
```
Per-GPU share at TP8 is 65.9 GB; 8 concurrent readers saturate a typical NVMe
array long before the GPUs do. **The 41–102 s figure is the floor, not the
warm-up.** Real start-up is dominated by:

1. **Two 101.54 GB Engram shards** (shards 47–48). A naive single-threaded
   `safetensors` load stalls on them — this is exactly why the card passes
   `enable_multithread_load` with 128 threads
   ([architecture.md §1.1](architecture.md)).
2. **Marlin repack of 46,080 expert projections** — `prepare_nvfp4_moe_layer_for_marlin`
   runs at load and has an open **~0.66 GiB/layer leak** (§6).
3. The recipe's own `VLLM_ENGINE_READY_TIMEOUT_S=3600`.
4. **Gotcha:** `model.safetensors.index.json` still carries the *base*
   checkpoint's `metadata.total_size = 510,286,023,000`. Anything sizing an
   allocation from that field under-allocates by 16.99 GB
   ([architecture.md §1.1](architecture.md)).

## 6. Risks and open questions

### 6.1 Blockers — things that may simply not work

1. ⚠️ **FlashMLA V4.1 sparse *decode* on SM90.** FlashMLA's own support matrix
   says *"Sparse Decoding for DeepSeek V4.1 is only available on SM100"*
   [src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA), while vLLM's
   `FLASHMLA_SPARSE_DSV41` gate accepts `capability.major in [9, 10]`. One of
   them is wrong, or vLLM carries its own SM90 instantiation. The InferenceX
   H200 rows prove decode happens; **which kernel, at what quality, is
   unresolved** ([flash-attention.md §15](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)).
2. ⚠️ **NVFP4 backbone experts + MXFP4 MTP/DSpark experts in one process.**
   `ignore: ["mtp.*"]` leaves the 3×128 MTP experts MXFP4 while the 40×384
   backbone experts are NVFP4. On Blackwell both have native kernels; on Hopper
   they take **two different Marlin fallback paths in the same engine**. No
   source confirms any engine has validated this
   ([architecture.md §7.3](architecture.md)). It gets worse at TP8 — see next.
3. 🔴 **Marlin tile alignment breaks the MXFP4 half at TP8.** Marlin GEMM/repack
   requires tile-aligned dimensions (multiples of 64 or 128):

   ```
     TP1  per-rank intermediate  2304  mod64=0    64-aligned
     TP2                         1152  mod64=0    64-aligned
     TP4                          576  mod64=0    64-aligned
     TP8                          288  mod64=32   NOT 64-aligned -> repack needs zero-padding
     TP16                         144  mod64=16   NOT 64-aligned
   ```

   vLLM PR #45295 added zero-padding to `prepare_fp4_layer_for_marlin` and
   `prepare_nvfp4_moe_layer_for_marlin` — **but overlooked the MXFP4 MoE
   variant**, which still assumes pre-aligned weights and crashes with *"CUDA
   error: an illegal memory access was encountered"* in
   `prepare_moe_mxfp4_layer_for_marlin` on Hopper under TP+EP
   [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/47769). `moe_intermediate_size`
   is **2304 for both the backbone and the DSpark MoE**
   ([config.json](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4/blob/main/config.json)),
   so **TP8 — the only GPU count that fits this checkpoint on H200 — is exactly
   the configuration that trips the unfixed path.** This is the concrete form of
   risk 2 and the most likely way a first attempt fails.
4. ⚠️ **Marlin MoE may cap TP at 2.** The one project that shipped a 4-bit
   DeepSeek-V4-Flash on 8×H200 reports *"TP≥4 hits upstream Marlin MoE
   scale-sharding bugs"* and pins TP = 2 on all three SKUs it validated
   [src](https://github.com/canada-quant/dsv4-flash-w4a16-fp8). TP2 cannot hold
   527.27 GB on H200 (§1.3). If this limitation still applies, **the pair is not
   runnable at all** on stock vLLM.
5. 🔴 **NVFP4 Marlin repack leaks ~0.66 GiB per layer.** SGLang issue #38074:
   `prepare_moe_nvfp4_layer_for_marlin` in `marlin_utils_fp4.py` retains old
   parameter tensors via a `{fqn: tensor}` registry; *"torch.cuda.memory_allocated()
   grows +0.66 GiB per layer, and the server OOMs at layer ~9/48"*. **Open**, and
   the reporter notes it *"specifically targets SM80–SM90x deployments where
   model weights approach VRAM capacity limits"*
   [src](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/38074) — i.e. precisely
   this deployment. 40 backbone layers × 0.66 GiB = 26.4 GiB of transient leak
   against a 51 GB/GPU KV budget.
6. ⚠️ **DSpark speculative decoding failed on H200/SM90 for the V4 sibling**,
   with the issue recording that no working attention-backend combination
   existed for that pairing [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/47648).
   V4.1 MTP evidently works (every InferenceX H200 row has `spec_method: "mtp"`),
   but the V4.1 **DSpark** path on SM90 is unverified.
7. ⚠️ **`moe_wna16_marlin_gemm` applies wrong per-row topk weights at NVFP4 MoE
   shapes**, producing corrupt output
   [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/48895). Status and whether
   this model's shapes are affected: unverified. **Run an accuracy smoke test
   (`17*19` → `323`, plus a real eval) before trusting any output from this
   configuration.**

### 6.2 Performance and configuration risks

8. ⚠️ **Concurrency ceiling of 64.** The published stack goes from TPOT 29.6 ms
   at concurrency 64 to 130 ms and TTFT 200 s at 128 (§3.4). Everything in §3.3
   and §4 above batch 64 is a capacity-model figure, not a demonstrated one.
9. ⚠️ **No isolated H200 prefill measurement** for this model; §4's input prices
   rest on a roofline that the decode calibration suggests may be 2× optimistic.
10. ⚠️ **The 267 ms TTFT floor is unexplained.** It is 9–17× the arithmetic at
    97.6 % cache hit and is the dominant term for any prompt under ~30 K tokens.
    No published H200 profile isolates scheduling vs indexer vs kernel time.
11. ⚠️ **EP8 + DP-attention is unpublished for this architecture** — and it is
    the lever that would multiply 128 K concurrency by 8 (§1.4). SGLang's
    EP = TP InferenceX arm was 14× behind vLLM's EP1 arm on H200, which is
    evidence of nothing except that the configuration is untuned.
12. ⚠️ **Engram CPU-offload decode penalty is unmeasured** on any GPU. It is the
    only way to fit TP4, and it routes 12,672 B/token of *random* gather across
    PCIe.
13. ⚠️ **`VLLM_DSV4_FP4_DEQUANT=1` is documented for MXFP4 only.** If it has no
    NVFP4 equivalent, the base checkpoint gets a 1.38–1.53× prefill option on
    Hopper that this checkpoint does not — widening the §3.5 gap well beyond the
    4–5 % byte tax.
14. ⚠️ **Vision on H200 is entirely untested.** Every published recipe passes
    `--language-model-only`.
15. ⚠️ **The recipe's `tp` default for h200 is 4**, which contradicts its own
    `vram_minimum_gb: 614` and its own prose. Anyone who takes the generated
    command at face value will OOM.
16. ⚠️ **`S` (engine state slots per request) is unknown for V4.1-Flash.** Every
    fixed-state figure assumes `S = 1`
    ([architecture.md §5.3](architecture.md)). An MTP-speculating runtime
    plausibly allocates extra ring slots for the 5-token drafted block. It moves
    `max_concurrency` by <1 % at 8 K and not at all at 1 M.

### 6.3 Accuracy caveats of the executed precision

17. **The executed precision on H200 is BF16, not NVFP4.** Marlin's W4A16 path
    dequantises E2M1 + E4M3/16 to BF16 exactly, so the *weights* are the same
    numbers NVIDIA calibrated. NVIDIA's accuracy table
    ([architecture.md §10.1](architecture.md)) was measured **on vLLM on GB300
    with native W4A4**, where activations are also FP4. On H200 activations stay
    BF16, so the H200 numerics are **different from — and strictly no worse
    than — the evaluated configuration**. The card's own caveat applies:
    *"Lossless weight conversion does not imply identical inference outputs."*
18. **18 of 46,080 projection entries used fallback activation scales** (0.04 %)
    during calibration
    [src](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4/raw/main/README.md).
    Under W4A16 the activation scales are not used at all, so this particular
    defect is inert on H200 — one of the very few things Hopper gets *right* here.
19. ⚠️ **The checkpoint's own config contradicts itself on activation scaling**:
    `config_groups.group_0.input_activations.dynamic = false` versus the
    top-level `activation_scheme: "dynamic"`
    ([architecture.md §1.2](architecture.md)). Moot under W4A16; live if anyone
    ever ships an sm_90 W4A8 path.
20. **FP8 KV accuracy.** The H200 path forces FP8 KV where the architecture was
    trained for FP4. Uncalibrated FP8 KV gives *"1–2 % accuracy recovery or
    better"* on most models, but *"Kimi-K2.5 with FlashMLA showed consistent
    modest degradation"* [src](https://vllm-project.github.io/2026/04/22/fp8-kvcache.html).
    Calibrate with llm-compressor for MLA models. ⚠️ No V4.1-specific FP8-KV
    accuracy data exists.

### 6.4 The nearest runnable alternatives, in order

| Rank | Option | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` (base MXFP4) on 8×H200, TP8** | `h200: verified` in the vLLM recipe; 16.99 GB smaller; ~4–5 % faster decode (§3.5); 4 % larger KV pool; has documented Hopper paths the NVFP4 build does not (FlashInfer SM90 CUTLASS W4A16, Humming W4A8 via `--flashinfer-mxfp4-moe-precision fp8`, and `VLLM_DSV4_FP4_DEQUANT=1` for 1.38–1.53× prefill); it is the checkpoint the measured InferenceX H200 rows almost certainly served |
| **2** | **`lvkaokao/…-W4A16-Engram-AutoRound` (INT4 GPTQ, 451.7 GB)** | The purpose-built W4A16 route for Hopper/Ampere ([architecture.md §9.2](architecture.md)). Machete (Hopper-tuned, CUTLASS 3.5.1) beats Marlin by +29–42 % on mixed-input GEMM [src](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2024/10/14/introducing-machete-mixed-input-gemm-kernel). 75 GB smaller than the NVFP4 build. ⚠️ No published evals |
| **3** | **A W4A16-INT4 + FP8-block-attention build** | The only 4-bit DeepSeek-V4-Flash recipe actually validated on 8×H200 [src](https://github.com/canada-quant/dsv4-flash-w4a16-fp8) — but TP=2 only, on the V4 (not V4.1) checkpoint |
| **4** | **Move the workload to Blackwell** | 4 × B200 or 4 × B300 runs this exact checkpoint natively, with `nvfp4_ds_mla` KV at 890 B/token and the fused Mega-Attn kernel. Measured B200 TP4 at conc 64: **92,073 tok/s/GPU total** vs H200 TP8's 22,638 — 4× the per-GPU throughput on half the GPUs (§3.4) |

**Do not** reach for this checkpoint on H200 to save memory: it is *larger* than
the base. The only scenario in which it is the right artifact on Hopper is a
fleet that is otherwise Blackwell and wants one checkpoint everywhere — and even
then the base MXFP4 build is the artifact that runs everywhere, including on
MI355X where NVFP4 must be converted back.
