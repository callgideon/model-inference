## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today: yes.** vLLM `main`/nightly and SGLang `main` both execute
   `DeepseekV41ForCausalLM` with NVFP4 routed experts natively on `sm_100`
   (vLLM v0.29.0 / nightly; SGLang v0.5.20, commit `da64c5cb…` in NVIDIA's own
   recipe). But **B200 is not a validated target for *this* checkpoint** — NVIDIA
   tested only GB300, and `nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4` is absent from the
   vLLM recipes catalogue (only `nvidia/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-NVFP4`, the V4 build, is listed).
2. **Min 4 GPUs (TP4); recommended 8 (one HGX B200 node).** TP2 and TP1 are
   `infeasible (KV)` — 527.27 GB does not fit in 2 × 162 GB usable. At TP4 the KV
   budget is **24.9 GB/GPU** (208 concurrent at 128K); at TP8 it is **90.6 GB/GPU**
   (757 at 128K). TP16 crosses the 400 Gb/s scale-out fabric and is not worth it.
3. **Weight format actually executed: NVFP4 E2M1 gs16 W4A4** on the 384 routed
   experts of 40 backbone layers (58 % of bytes), **MXFP4** on the MTP/DSpark
   experts, **FP8/MXFP8** on attention, shared experts and the 188.8 GiB Engram
   tables, **BF16** on embeddings/head/vision. All native on `sm_100`; nothing is
   dequantised.
4. **Cost per 1M output tokens.** S1 interactive (4K in / 512 out, TPOT ≤ 50 ms) at
   concurrency 256, TP4: **$0.57 – $1.32** (Hyperstack $6.00 → OCI $14.00/GPU-h),
   TPOT 21.7 ms `est.`. S4 max-throughput at the KV ceiling (3,557 concurrent):
   **$0.048 – $0.112** `est.` — a roofline, 14× beyond any published batch.
5. **Confidence: estimate.** Throughput and cost are roofline-derived. The only
   measurement for *any* DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash build on B200 is InferenceX
   (`precision: "fp4"`, checkpoint id not exposed) plus a vLLM PR's TP=8 offline run.
   **Verdict on the format question: on B200 this build has no measured advantage
   over the 510.29 GB base and costs 5.5 % more decode bytes and 17 % of the TP4
   KV budget. Use the base MXFP4 checkpoint unless you need NVIDIA's calibration
   audit.**

## 1. Fit

### 1.1 Method and inputs

```
usable_hbm       = 180 GB × 0.90 = 162.0 GB / GPU          (METHODOLOGY §3, §8)
per_gpu_weights  = (W_total − R_repl)/n + R_repl
activation_ws    = 4 GB / GPU                              (METHODOLOGY §3 band 2–6 GB)
kv_budget        = usable_hbm − per_gpu_weights − activation_ws
max_concurrency  = floor( kv_budget / (ctx × kv_bytes_per_token + fixed_state) )
```

Pinned inputs, reconciled (`python3` output, verbatim):

```
checkpoint payload        527,273,322,840 B = 527.27 GB = 491.06 GiB
base MXFP4 checkpoint     510,286,023,000 B = 510.29 GB  -> NVFP4 is +16.99 GB (+3.33%)
replicated tensors        1,675,655,616 B = 1.676 GB (embed 1.324 + mHC 0.157 + routers 0.157 + KV compressor 0.037 + norms 0.0004)
B200 as deployed          180 GB  usable(x0.90) 162.0 GB  act_ws 4 GB  BW 7.7 TB/s
fixed state/seq           METHODOLOGY §8 2.77 MiB = 2,906,112 B ; arch §5.3 40-layer = 2,703,360 B (+7.5%)
expert bytes              NVFP4 19,906,584  vs base MXFP4 18,800,640  -> +5.88% per expert read
```

**Two documented disagreements, stated per the inputs-discipline rule:**

| Quantity | METHODOLOGY §8 | Other doc | What this doc uses |
|---|---|---|---|

MTP/DSpark layers ship in this checkpoint and speculation is on in every published run, so the 43-layer ring is the right one. The difference is 7.5 % of a per-sequence constant — it moves `max_concurrency` by 2.0 % at 8K and 0.02 % at 1M. |

**DP+EP (`single_node_dep`, `--data-parallel-size 8 --enable-expert-parallel`) is
`infeasible` on B200 and the recipe catalogue lists it anyway.** Under DP-attention
only the routed experts shard; everything else is replicated per DP rank. Routed
experts (backbone NVFP4 305.77 GB + MTP MXFP4 7.22 GB) = 312.99 GB; the remainder
— dominated by the **202.76 GB Engram tables** — is 214.28 GB **per rank**, which
exceeds 162 GB of usable HBM before a single expert or KV byte. `est.`, from
[`architecture.md` §3.3](architecture.md). This is the single biggest structural
difference between this model and an ordinary MoE: its largest tensor group is not
expert weights and does not follow expert parallelism.

### 1.3 Max concurrency — and which reading is binding

`num_key_value_heads = 1`: there is one shared latent KV stream, so under tensor
parallelism the compressed KV cache, the indexer K cache and the 128-slot SWA ring
are **replicated on every rank**, exactly as MLA caches are. The per-GPU KV budget,
not the node aggregate, is therefore what bounds concurrency.

```
max_concurrency(ctx) -- KV REPLICATED per TP rank (n_kv_heads=1 latent MLA). BINDING reading.
  n   KV dtype         8K        32K       128K         1M
  4 FP4 shipped      2,444        777        208         26
  4        FP8      1,517        437        113         14
  4       BF16        782        225         58          7
  8 FP4 shipped      8,887      2,825        757         96
  8        FP8      5,518      1,590        413         52
  8       BF16      2,845        820        213         26
 16 FP4 shipped     12,108      3,850      1,032        131
 16        FP8      7,518      2,167        563         71
 16       BF16      3,876      1,117        290         36

ALTERNATIVE reading (latent KV sharded across TP ranks -> aggregate budget). arch §5.6 uses this.
  n         8K        32K       128K         1M
  4      9,777      3,108        833        106
  8     71,099     22,606      6,063        774
 16    193,742     61,603     16,523      2,110
```

> **The FP8 and BF16 rows are counterfactuals, not options.** This model's KV format
> is architectural, not an engine flag: `kv_cache_quant_algo` is `null` in the
> checkpoint and `fp4_act_quant(latent, 16, scale_dtype=float8_e4m3fn)` is written
> into `inference/model.py` [[`architecture.md` §5.2](architecture.md)]. There is
> nothing to turn on and nothing to turn off. The 890 B/token FP4 layout requires a
> Blackwell kernel (`nvfp4_ds_mla` → `FLASHMLA_MEGA_ATTN_DSV41`), which B200 has.
> The BF16 rows additionally use the 5.375 MiB BF16 SWA ring rather than the FP8 one.

**This document's §1.3 table differs from [`architecture.md` §5.6](architecture.md)
by a factor of `n_gpus`**, and the disagreement is stated rather than resolved
silently. §5.6 computes an aggregate KV budget (`kv_budget × n_gpus`) and reports
10,478 / 3,286 / 877 / 111 for 4 × B200; the same formula reproduces exactly here.
Three independent reasons favour the replicated reading on B200:

1. **Architecture.** One KV head cannot be split head-wise across four ranks.
2. **Measurement.** Every InferenceX B200 KV pool lands on the *per-GPU* budget
   (§3.3 below), never near 4× it.
3. **The repo already flagged it.**
   [`cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md` §7.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)
   carries the same open question for the base checkpoint on B300 and says "the
   replicated reading is the binding one".

⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED** — no engine source was found that states, in words, whether
vLLM's `FLASHMLA_MEGA_ATTN_DSV41` block table is per-rank or sharded. The
measurement in §3.3 is strong circumstantial evidence, not a code citation.

### 1.4 Tensors that cannot be sharded

Replicated on every rank regardless of TP degree — **1.676 GB/GPU**, which is why
`per_gpu_weights` is `(W − R)/n + R` and not `W/n`:

| Tensor group | Bytes | Why it cannot shard |
|---|---|---|
| `embed.weight` (BF16) | 1.324 GB | vocab-parallel is possible (vLLM's `VocabParallelEmbedding`) but is **not** guaranteed for this model class; counted as replicated for a conservative budget. The measured pools in §3.3 sit slightly above this reading, i.e. the engine probably *does* shard it. |
| MoE routers `ffn.gate.weight` + FP32 `bias` + `bias_vl` | 0.157 GB | routing must score **all 384** experts on every rank before dispatch |
| mHC `hc_attn_fn` / `hc_ffn_fn` / bases / scales (**FP32**) | 0.157 GB | a Sinkhorn-normalised 24 × 20,480 mixing matrix over the 4 parallel residual copies — mixes across the hidden dim, so there is no shard axis |
| KV compressor `wkv` (BF16) + `wgate` (FP32), 4 layers | 0.037 GB | feeds the single shared KV head |
| RMSNorms, attention sinks, image tokens | 0.0004 GB | per-element, replicated by construction |

Also unsharded, but as *cache* rather than weights: the compressed KV cache, the
indexer K cache and the SWA ring (§1.3).

Shardable: Engram tables by row (`ParallelEngramEmbedding` in
[`inference/model.py`](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4/raw/main/inference/model.py)),
routed experts by expert (EP) or by intermediate dim (TP), attention `wq_b` by
head (64), `wo_b` by group (**8** — this is the hard ceiling that makes TP16
questionable), the CSA2 indexer by head (32), the LM head by vocab.

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.1 MBU / MFU assumptions (METHODOLOGY §4)

| Quantity | Value used | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| HBM bandwidth | **7.7 TB/s** | [`b200.md` §2](../../gpus/b200.md) plans with the Lenovo SKU sheet; NVIDIA's DGX B200 page implies 8.0. Multiply every decode tok/s below by **1.039** to re-derive at 8.0 |
| **MBU (decode)** | **0.50** | [`b200.md` §9.4](../../gpus/b200.md) back-solves **MBU ≈ 46–47 %** from NVIDIA's measured Llama-3.3-70B NVFP4 point on B200. METHODOLOGY's Blackwell band is 0.5–0.7; 0.50 is its floor and the one a real measurement supports. Scattered Engram gathers (12,672 B/token of random access) argue against the upper half |
| **MFU (decode compute branch)** | **0.13** | [`b200.md` §6](../../gpus/b200.md): measured grouped-MoE NVFP4 GEMMs on B200 reach **11–14 % of the 9 PFLOPS dense FP4 peak** (FlashInfer 1,132 / SGLang 993 / vLLM 968 TFLOPS on a DeepSeek-scaled 256-expert shape). *"Never size a MoE deployment off peak FP4."* |
| **MFU (prefill)** | **0.18** | Blend: 0.12–0.15 for the expert GEMM, 0.65 on the FlashMLA attention kernel. [`b200.md` §9.3](../../gpus/b200.md) used 0.22 for this model family; 0.18 is deliberately more conservative |
| Comm overhead | **+7 % at TP4, +12 % at TP8** | METHODOLOGY §4 band 5–15 % for TP ≤ 8 over NVLink |
| Speculative decoding | **off** | No measured acceptance rate exists for this checkpoint. Every number below is per real decode step |

Decode-byte model (METHODOLOGY §4, MoE distinct-expert form), from
[`architecture.md` §6.3](architecture.md):

```
distinct_experts(b)   = 384 × (1 − (1 − 6/384)^b)
weights_read(b)       = 40 × (distinct_experts(b) × 19,906,584 + 174,931,968) + 315,043,840 + 1,323,827,200
kv_read(b, ctx)       = b × (ctx × 170 + 13,058,048)        <- scales with batch × ctx, per METHODOLOGY §3
engram_gather(b)      = b × 12,672
decode_step_time      = max( bytes / (n_gpus × 7.7e12 × MBU),
                             2 × 15,753,543,680 × b / (n_gpus × 9e15 × 0.13) ) × (1 + comm)
```

Prefill model — note the **causal encoder-decoder inversion**: prefill runs only the
20 encoder layers (7.55 B active), so it costs **half** a normal 40-layer model's
FLOPs per token, while decode runs all 40 plus the LM head (15.75 B active).

```
prefill_flops/token(ctx) = 2 × 7,545,815,040        (dense, encoder only)
                         + 1.544e9                  (encoder SWA + encoder sparse attention; context-INDEPENDENT)
                         + 20,480 × ctx             (the 4 full-pool indexers — the only term that grows)
TTFT ≈ prompt × (1 − hit) / (n_gpus × prefill_tok_s_per_gpu) + one decode step
```

```

  ctx     4,608:  16.73 GFLOP/token ->    96,835 tok/s/GPU (   387,340 on TP4,    774,679 on TP8)
  ctx    33,792:  17.33 GFLOP/token ->    93,495 tok/s/GPU (   373,979 on TP4,    747,957 on TP8)
```

### 3.2 Estimated matrix — concurrency × scenario

`est.` throughout. Rows above `max_concurrency(ctx)` are printed `infeasible (KV)`

Three things fall out of these tables and none of them is obvious:

* **TPOT ≤ 50 ms is never the binding constraint at 4K context on B200 — KV capacity
  is.** At TP4 the KV ceiling (3,557) arrives at 25.6 ms; at TP8 the ceiling (12,933)

* **Per-GPU output throughput is essentially flat in TP degree** at a fixed batch
  (TP8 is 4 % *worse* per GPU than TP4 because of the extra comm), but TP8 buys
  **3.6× the KV budget** and therefore 3.6× the feasible concurrency. TP8 wins
  exactly where KV binds: ≥32K context.
* **TTFT is context-dominated and cache-dominated, not batch-dominated.** At 128K,
  TP4 TTFT goes 393 ms → 40 ms as the prefix-cache hit rate goes 0 → 90 %. The
  decode step contributes under 22 ms everywhere in these tables.

### 3.4 Why estimate and measurement differ

```
 conc  mean in tok  meas TPOT ms  roofline bytes GB  roofline TPOT ms  implied accept
    1      306,772           2.2               13.5              0.94            0.43
    2      200,017           2.2               18.2              1.27            0.59
    4      188,907           2.2               27.5              1.91            0.85
    8      160,126           2.5               45.2              3.14            1.25
   16      154,548           3.1               77.4              5.38            1.76
   32      177,875           4.4              131.1              9.11            2.06
   64      135,018          10.2              205.1             14.25            1.40
  128       86,009         105.5              277.2             19.26            0.18
```

The last column is `roofline TPOT ÷ measured TPOT` at MBU = 0.50 — i.e. how many
tokens each real decode step must have emitted for the measurement to be physical.

* **Concurrency 1–4: the roofline is 2.3× too fast.** At batch 1 a decode step
  reads 13.5 GB across 4 GPUs = 0.94 ms of pure bandwidth, but the measurement is
  2.2 ms. Small-batch kernel-launch and scheduling overhead dominates; effective
  MBU is ~0.21. [`b200.md` §6](../../gpus/b200.md) sees the same thing at the kernel
  level — per-layer FP4 MoE latency at batch 1 spans **1.78×** between SGLang and
  vLLM and **2.33×** to FlashInfer on identical hardware.
* **Concurrency 8–64: the roofline is 1.25–2.06× too slow, and MTP explains it
  exactly.** Every InferenceX row has `spec_method: "mtp"`, so measured TPOT is per
  **accepted** token while the roofline is per decode **step**. An accepted-tokens-
  per-step of 1.3–2.1 is entirely ordinary for DSpark γ=5 on agentic traffic, and it
  is the *only* free parameter needed to reconcile the two. This is also why the
  §3.2 tables, which assume no speculation, should be read as the **pessimistic**
  bound for a stack with MTP on. ⚠️ acceptance is not exposed by the API, so this is
  an inference, not a measurement.
* **Concurrency 128: the roofline is 5.5× too fast and the run has collapsed.**
  TTFT 48.2 s and a KV pool shrunk to 20.4 GB say the engine is thrashing between
  chunked prefill and decode, not that the hardware got slower. §1.3 predicts a
  TP4 KV ceiling of 208 sequences at 128K; at 128 concurrent × 86K mean input the
  run is at 44 % of the pool and the scheduler, not the memory, gives way first.
  **B200 TP4 is the configuration where this model runs out of road; B300 TP4 and
  GB300 TP4 hold TPOT at 23–25 ms at the same concurrency** because they carry
  1.5–1.6× the memory [[`architecture.md` §10.2](architecture.md)].
* **Structural gaps that remain in the roofline's favour** (i.e. reality will be
  worse than §3.2 in these respects): routing skew is ignored (uniform-routing
  `distinct_experts` is an upper bound on distinct experts, so real weight traffic
  is somewhat lower — this one favours reality); the 12,672 B/token Engram gather is
  counted at its byte value although it is *random* access, which on HBM3e costs far
  more than streaming; and the four full-pool indexers are assumed to scan
  `ctx × 170 B` every step, which the reference implementation does but production
  kernels may bound ⚠️.

### 4.5 Effect of speculative decoding and prefix caching on cost

* **Prefix caching is worth 10× on the input half.** At the 97.6 % median hit rate of
  the InferenceX agentic traces, blended input drops from $0.0172 to
  $0.0172 × (0.024 + 0.976 × 0.1) = **$0.00209/1M** at the low price. For a
  155K-in / 1.1K-out agentic request that is the difference between a $0.0027 and a
  $0.00032 prefill bill. **NVIDIA's own vLLM command for this checkpoint disables
  it** (`--no-enable-prefix-caching`), which on this workload is the most expensive
  single line in the recipe. ⚠️ the reason is unstated — most likely an interaction
  with SWA Bounded Replay, whose windowed state is replayed from the last 128 tokens
  rather than restored.
* **Speculative decoding cuts output cost by the accepted-tokens-per-step, at low
  concurrency only.** §3.4 back-solves 1.3–2.1 accepted tokens/step from the
  InferenceX B200 rows at concurrency 8–64; taking 1.5× as a planning figure moves
  the concurrency-256 output cost from $0.565 → **$0.377/1M** at low price. Above
  ~batch 64 the distinct-expert count is already 244/384, so drafting buys little
  and costs a full verify pass [[`architecture.md` §7.3](architecture.md)], and on
  Blackwell Ultra it has already gone negative on throughput (GB300, 128K/8K:
  **−0.9 %** peak tok/s/GPU with MTP, while TPS/user went **+87 %**)
  [[`serving-optimizations.md` §2.4](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)].
  **Treat MTP on B200 as a latency feature, not a cost feature**, and note that no
  acceptance rate has ever been published for this checkpoint — the 3.51 in the
  MI355X recipe is `"rejection_sample_method":"synthetic"`, and SGLang's 5.5 is
  explicitly simulated.

```
  S1 KV ceiling   TP8 @high: $0.1173/1M out -> break-even utilisation vs $0.60 =  19.5%, vs $1.20 =   9.8%
```

At the reported (concurrency-256) operating point the picture inverts:

| Operating point | $/1M out, low | vs $0.60 API | $/1M out, high | vs $0.60 API |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| conc 32 (NVIDIA's cap) | $1.883 | **3.1× worse than the API** | $4.395 | 7.3× worse |
| conc 64 | $1.474 | 2.5× worse | $3.440 | 5.7× worse |
| conc 128 | $0.997 | 1.7× worse | $2.326 | 3.9× worse |
| **conc 256 (reported S1)** | **$0.565** | **break-even at 94 % utilisation** | $1.319 | 2.2× worse |
| conc 3,557 (KV ceiling, S4) | $0.048 | break-even at 8.0 % utilisation | $0.112 | 18.7 % |

**Read this as a tuning statement, not a hardware statement.** Self-hosting this
model on rented B200 beats DeepSeek's own list price only if you can sustain
concurrency above ~256 at near-100 % fleet utilisation on the cheapest neocloud —
and nobody has published a run at that concurrency. On an OCI or AWS B200 you do not
beat the API on output tokens at any concurrency anyone has demonstrated. On the
*blended* metric the answer flips: $0.148/1M blended (conc 256, low price) against a
DeepSeek blended price of roughly $0.0067–$0.0134/1M at 97.5 % cache hit
[[`architecture.md` §11.1](architecture.md)] — the API is still 10× cheaper on
agentic traffic, which is consistent with DeepSeek running owned silicon at high
utilisation rather than with any B200 inefficiency.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

**Stay inside one HGX B200 node.** TP ≤ 8 runs at 1.8 TB/s per GPU over NVLink-5
with 4 NVSwitches and no oversubscription; TP > 8 crosses a 400 Gb/s-per-GPU fabric
— *"a 36× bandwidth cliff (1.8 TB/s → 50 GB/s per GPU)"*
[[`b200.md` §4](../../gpus/b200.md)]. HGX B200's scale-out fabric is 0.8 TB/s per
node, **half** HGX B300's, so B200 is the worse of the two parts to scale out from.
Beyond that, `o_groups = 8` caps clean output-projection sharding at 8 ways ⚠️.

Two node layouts are worth considering, and which one wins depends entirely on
context length:

| Layout | Best for | KV budget | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2 × TP4 replicas on one node** | ≤ 8K context, max throughput | 24.92 GB/GPU, 2,444 seq/replica @8K | Twice the independent scheduling domains, no 8-way all-reduce, matches the only verified B200 shape. `2 × 138,821 = 277,642` agg out tok/s at the ceiling vs TP8's 265,246 |
| **1 × TP8 replica** | ≥ 32K context, and 128K/1M | **90.62 GB/GPU**, 757 seq @128K vs TP4's 208 | 3.6× the KV budget, half the TPOT at fixed batch. At 128K, TP4 is the configuration that collapsed in the InferenceX conc-128 run |

### 5.2 When PD disaggregation and wide-EP pay for this pair

* **Wide-EP: never on B200.** DeepEP/EPLB across 32–72 GPUs is a GB200/GB300 NVL72
  play; B200's NVLink domain stops at 8 [[`b200.md` §4](../../gpus/b200.md)]. Keep
  EP ≤ 8 inside the node, or EP=1. The one published B200 EP data point for this
  model is SGLang TP4/EP4 at 8.4× behind vLLM's TP4/EP1 — configuration, probably,
  but it is the only evidence there is.
* **PD disaggregation: architecturally ideal for this model, unproven on B200.**
  The causal encoder-decoder makes prefill (7.55 B active) and decode (15.75 B
  active) genuinely different workloads — a 2:1 FLOP asymmetry that is exactly what
  disaggregation exists to exploit. vLLM's verified 1P1D layout is GB200 NVL4
  (4 GPUs per role, TP4 each, NIXL, `vllm-router --vllm-pd-disaggregation`); on an
  8-GPU B200 node it becomes 4+4 [src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash).
  **But 4+4 is exactly the size at which the literature says disaggregation stops
  paying**: *"disaggregation is a thousand-GPU problem"*, with a reported 20–30 %
  drop on small or untuned workloads
  [[`serving-optimizations.md` §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)].
  Do not disaggregate a single B200 node for this model; consider it at 4+ nodes.
  If you do enable speculation, **DSpark must run in both pools** so the transferred
  KV stays compatible.

```bash
# vLLM, image vllm/vllm-openai:deepseekv41-flash-0909
vllm serve nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4 \
```

**Changes to make when moving either command from GB300 to B200:**

1. **`--max-model-len 1048576` is not affordable at TP4 on B200.** 1M context costs
   892.6 MiB/sequence; the TP4 KV budget holds **26** of them (§1.3) against GB300
   TP4's ~527. Either drop to TP8 (96 at 1M) or cap `--max-model-len` at 128K.
2. **Raise `--max-num-seqs` / `--max-running-requests`.** 16–32 is 1–2 % of the KV
   budget and leaves 15–70× of throughput on the floor (§4.2). Start at 256.
3. **Turn prefix caching back on** unless you are reproducing NVIDIA's benchmark
   (§4.5).
4. **Add `--moe-backend flashinfer_cutlass`** (vLLM) if you are on a container older
   than NGC 26.08, to dodge the SM100 NVFP4 MoE autotune crashes
   [[`b200.md` §11](../../gpus/b200.md)].
5. **Prefer `--attention-backend trtllm_mla`** on SGLang: 1.2× prefill / 1.45× decode
   over FlashMLA at the kernel level on B200.
6. Keep `--model-loader-extra-config '{"enable_multithread_load": true, "num_threads": 128}'`
   — see §5.4.

### 5.4 Warm-up and load time

```
   5 GB/s:  105.5 s for 527.27 GB; each 101.5 GB Engram shard =  20.3 s
  10 GB/s:   52.7 s for 527.27 GB; each 101.5 GB Engram shard =  10.2 s
  20 GB/s:   26.4 s for 527.27 GB; each 101.5 GB Engram shard =   5.1 s
  base checkpoint for comparison: 51.0 s at 10 GB/s (1.7 s less)
```

`est.`, `bytes / rate`. Three things make the real number worse:

* **Shards 47 and 48 are 101.54 GB each** — the two Engram tables
  [[`architecture.md` §1.1](architecture.md)]. A naive single-threaded `safetensors`
  load serialises on them, which is precisely why NVIDIA's command passes
  `num_threads: 128`. Without it, budget the two shards' time on top of everything else.
* **`model.safetensors.index.json` still reports the base checkpoint's
  `metadata.total_size = 510,286,023,000`** — it was not updated after
  requantisation. Any loader that sizes an allocation from that field
  **under-allocates by 17 GB** [[`architecture.md` §1.1](architecture.md)].
* **Engine warm-up is not free.** The vLLM recipe sets
  `VLLM_ENGINE_READY_TIMEOUT_S=3600` — a one-hour readiness timeout — and the PD
  variant disables FlashInfer autotune, JIT and CuTeDSL warm-up via `--kernel-config`
  and skips DeepGEMM warm-up (`VLLM_DEEP_GEMM_WARMUP=skip`). Plan on **2–10 minutes**
  from process start to first token on a cold container, not the 26–106 s of pure I/O.
  ⚠️ no measured end-to-end B200 startup time was found.

## 6. Risks and open questions

### 6.1 The central question: is the NVFP4 build worth using on B200?

```
 batch  E[distinct experts]   NVFP4 GB/step  base MXFP4 GB/step  NVFP4 decode penalty
     1                  6.0           13.45               13.18                 2.01%
     8                 45.5           45.11               43.10                 4.67%
    32                152.0          130.81              124.08                 5.42%
    64                243.8          205.06              194.28                 5.55%
   128                332.8          278.19              263.47                 5.59%
   256                377.2          318.03              301.34                 5.54%
   384                383.1          327.25              310.31                 5.46%

per-GPU weights TP4: NVFP4 133.08 GB vs base 128.83 GB
KV budget TP4:       NVFP4 24.92 GB vs base 29.17 GB -> base has 17.0% more KV
KV budget TP8:       NVFP4 90.62 GB vs base 92.75 GB -> base has  2.3% more KV
     ctx   TP4 NVFP4   TP4 base   TP8 NVFP4   TP8 base
   8,192       2,444      2,860       8,887      9,095
  32,768         777        909       2,825      2,892
 131,072         208        243         757        775
1,048,576          26         31          96         99
```

**Measured costs of choosing this build on B200, all of them certain:**

1. **+5.5 % decode bytes per step** at any batch ≥ 32 (one extra E4M3 scale byte per
   16 weights instead of one E8M0 per 32). Decode on B200 is bandwidth-bound below
   batch ~1,169 [[`b200.md` §9.1](../../gpus/b200.md)], so this is a direct ~5.5 %
   TPOT penalty in the regime that matters.
2. **−17 % TP4 KV budget** and therefore −14 % concurrency at every context length.
   At TP8 the penalty shrinks to −2.3 % because the 16.99 GB is divided by 8.
3. **You lose the `deep_gemm_mega_moe` kernel**, which is FP8-only and therefore
   unavailable to NVFP4 experts [src](https://huggingface.co/LibertAIDAI/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4).
4. **+1.7 s of load time** and 17 GB more storage and network per node.
5. **A mixed-quantisation process** — NVFP4 backbone experts alongside MXFP4
   MTP/DSpark experts — that no source confirms any engine has validated.

**The only possible benefit is W4A4, and it is unverified.** The card is explicit
that this is *"NVFP4 weights **and activations** (W4A4), with group size 16"*. If
Blackwell's `nvf4` MMA path (9,000 TFLOPS dense) requires both operands to be 4-bit
while the base checkpoint's MXFP4 × MXFP8 **W4A8** path falls on the mixed-input
`mxf8f6f4` line (4,500 TFLOPS), then the NVFP4 build has up to a **2× prefill
advantage on the expert GEMM**:

```
  ctx    4,608: W4A4     96,835 vs W4A8     48,417 tok/s/GPU  (2.00x ceiling, expert GEMM only)
  ctx   33,792: W4A4     93,495 vs W4A8     46,747 tok/s/GPU
  ctx  133,120: W4A4     83,671 vs W4A8     41,836 tok/s/GPU
```

⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED, and this is the single most consequential open question in this
document.** [`b200.md` §3](../../gpus/b200.md) lists "FP4 / NVFP4 / MXFP4" on one
9,000 TFLOPS row and [`b200.md` §6](../../gpus/b200.md) says MXFP4 is *"Same
tensor-core rate as NVFP4"* — under which reading the 2× above is zero and only the
five costs remain. No source was found that states the Blackwell mixed-input
(`f8f6f4`) rate versus the pure-FP4 (`nvf4`/`mxf4`) rate for a *grouped MoE* GEMM.

**Three pieces of counter-evidence against the W4A4 thesis:**

* On MI355X, `AITER_SITUV2_A8W4` (the shipped default) beats `AITER_SITUV2_A4W4`
  on Kimi-K3 — **537.3 vs 530.8 tok/s**, a 1.2 % deficit for the 4-bit-activation
  path [[`quantization-formats.md` §6.3](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)].
  Different vendor and different kernel family, but the lesson transfers: *4-bit
  activations are not automatically faster; layout and quantise/dequantise overhead
  around the GEMM dominate at MoE shapes.*
* Measured grouped-MoE FP4 GEMMs on B200 reach **11–14 % of the 9 PFLOPS peak**.
  At 12 % MFU the tensor core is not the bottleneck, so doubling its theoretical rate
  cannot double the observed throughput.
* **NVIDIA published no speedup.** Re-read 2026-09-19, the card has zero occurrences
  of "speedup", "throughput", "tokens per second" or "performance"
  [[`architecture.md` §0](architecture.md)]. A vendor that had a 2× prefill result in
  hand would print it.

**Verdict for this GPU:** on B200, use `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` (the base
MXFP4 checkpoint). It is smaller, gives 17 % more KV at TP4, is the checkpoint every
B200 recipe and every InferenceX row names, keeps the `deep_gemm_mega_moe` path, and
is accuracy-indistinguishable (NVIDIA's own table: NVFP4 wins 4 of 6 benchmarks by
≤1.5 points, loses 2 — a wash, presented as a wash). Use the NVFP4 build only if you
specifically want NVIDIA's calibration and its *"all 16,986,931,200 weight blocks
passed lossless conversion"* audit, or if a paired B200 benchmark eventually shows
the W4A4 prefill win.

### 6.3 Everything marked ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED in this document

1. **Whether Blackwell's mixed-input `f8f6f4` MMA runs at half the pure-FP4 rate for
   grouped MoE** — determines whether this checkpoint has any compute advantage at
   all (§6.1). *The single most important unknown here.*
2. **Whether vLLM's `FLASHMLA_MEGA_ATTN_DSV41` block table replicates the compressed
   KV per TP rank or shards it** (§1.3). Every concurrency number in §1 and every
   `infeasible (KV)` verdict in §3.2 turns on this. The measurement in §3.3 supports
   replication to 0.15 %, but no code citation was found.
3. **Which HF checkpoint the InferenceX B200 rows served.** The API exposes
   `precision: "fp4"` but not the repo id. The KV-pool arithmetic in §3.3 favours the
   NVFP4 build by a hair; `act_ws` and `W` are degenerate, so it is not proof.
4. **MTP/DSpark acceptance rate on this checkpoint** — never published. §3.4's
   1.3–2.1 accepted tokens/step is inferred from the roofline gap, not measured.
   The 3.51 in the MI355X recipe is `"rejection_sample_method":"synthetic"` and
   SGLang's 5.5 is explicitly *"the simulated accept length"*.
5. **Whether NVFP4 backbone experts + MXFP4 MTP experts have ever been validated in
   one process** — two MoE quantisation methods in one model. The most likely place
   for this checkpoint to break, and SGLang issue #25704 (NaN without speculation on
   B200 NVFP4) is uncomfortably adjacent.
6. **Whether the SGLang #25704 NaN bug reproduces on V4.1** as it does on V4-Pro and
   V4-Flash.
7. **Why NVIDIA's vLLM command sets `--no-enable-prefix-caching`** — the single most
   expensive flag in the recipe on the workload this model targets.
8. **B200 as-deployed capacity: 180 GB (METHODOLOGY §8) vs "183 GB each" reported on
   a real 8 × B200 node** in vLLM PR #56686. A 1.7 % difference that is 11 % of the
   TP4 KV budget.
9. **Activation workspace.** METHODOLOGY §3's 2–6 GB band; the measured KV pools
   imply 4.0 GB at concurrency 1 rising to **9.8 GB at concurrency 128** (§3.3). The
   4 GB used throughout §1 is optimistic at high concurrency.
10. **Whether production kernels bound the four full-pool indexers** the way the
    reference implementation does not. At 1M context they cost 21.5 GFLOP/token —
    more than the entire encoder forward pass — which would make the §3.2 1M-context
    prefill figures roughly 2× optimistic.
11. **`o_groups = 8` divisibility at TP16** — the block-diagonal grouped output
    projection has only 8 groups, so TP16 must split within a group. Nothing
    confirms an engine handles this.
12. **No B200 PD-disaggregation, wide-EP or multi-node run has been published for
    this checkpoint.** Both official commands are `--tp 4`, EP off, on GB300.
13. **Cold-start time on B200** — no measured end-to-end figure; §5.4 is I/O
    arithmetic plus an inferred warm-up band.
14. **Engram `NgramHashState` per-sequence size** is not stated anywhere, so the
    `fixed_state_per_seq` in §1 omits it. It is small enough not to move the 8K
    numbers, but it is genuinely unknown.

### 6.4 Accuracy caveats of the executed precision

NVIDIA's own eval, on vLLM, GB300
[src](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4/raw/main/README.md):

| Precision | GPQA Diamond | AA-LCR | SciCode | IFBench | MMMU-Pro | Terminal-Bench 2.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MXFP4 (source / base) | 91.035 | **78.563** | 54.401 | 76.667 | **74.046** | 81.60 |
| **NVFP4 (this repo)** | **91.288** | 78.438 | **55.843** | **77.267** | 73.699 | **82.16** |

* **This is a wash, and NVIDIA presents it as one.** Four wins, two losses, all
  within ±1.5 points on benchmarks with 5–16 repeats. The defensible claim is
  "group-16 NVFP4 does not cost accuracy versus group-32 MXFP4", not "NVFP4 is more
  accurate".
* *"Lossless weight conversion does not imply identical inference outputs"* — the
  card's own words. The weights round-trip exactly; the activations do not, because
  this is **W4A4**: activations are quantised to FP4 at group 16 at runtime.
* **18 of 46,080 projection entries used fallback activation scales** — 0.04 % of the
  calibration failed and was defaulted. Which projections, and whether they sit on
  hot layers, is not stated ⚠️.
* **The config contradicts itself on activation scaling.**
  `config_groups.group_0.input_activations.dynamic = false` (static/calibrated) sits
  beside a top-level `"activation_scheme": "dynamic"`. Which one an engine honours —
  and therefore whether activation scales are recomputed per batch — is
  **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** [[`architecture.md` §1.2](architecture.md)].
* Calibration was **1,024 samples at sequence length 512** (512 each from
  cnn_dailymail and Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset-v2). For a model whose marquee
  feature is a 1M-token context, calibrating activation ranges at 512 tokens is a
  real extrapolation ⚠️.
* The base card's own instruct numbers (GPQA 90.9, Terminal-Bench 90.6) do **not**
  match NVIDIA's MXFP4 baseline (91.035 / 81.60), so the two harnesses are not
  comparable. Use NVIDIA's table only for the MXFP4-vs-NVFP4 delta.
