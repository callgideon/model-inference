# nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4 on NVIDIA A100 SXM 80GB (HGX 8-GPU; no FP8/FP4 tensor cores)

> Research date **2026-09-19**. Formulas, legend and markers per
> [`research/METHODOLOGY.md`](../../METHODOLOGY.md). Checkpoint facts come from
> [`models/deepseek41fnvfp4/architecture.md`](architecture.md); hardware facts from
> [`gpus/a100.md`](../../gpus/a100.md). Both are fact-checked; their numbers are
> inputs here and are cited by path + section rather than re-derived.
> Every arithmetic table below was produced by a `python3` script and pasted verbatim.

## 0. Verdict

1. **Not runnable today.** No engine serves `DeepseekV41ForCausalLM` on `sm_80` at all — every DSV4.1 attention backend in vLLM `main` rejects compute capability 8 (`sparse_mla.py:125` → `major in [9,10]`, `flashinfer_sparse.py:137` → `major in [10,12]`, `flash_mla_mega_attn` → `major == 10`), and the only tracking issue ([vLLM #57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144), opened 2026-09-16, open, no maintainer reply) targets the **base MXFP4** repo, not this NVFP4 one. The *nearest runnable alternative* is the base `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` on the community fork [`wtdcode/vllm-backport` v0.13.0 `-sm80`](https://github.com/wtdcode/vllm-backport), which **is** measured on 8×A800-SXM4-80GB.
2. **Min 8 GPUs, recommended 8 GPUs, TP8+EP8 (or TP4×DP2+EP8), one HGX A100 baseboard, Engram tables CPU-offloaded.** 4 GPUs is `infeasible (KV)` even with Engram offloaded (324.52 GB > 4 × 68.0 GB); 2 and 1 are not close.
3. **Weight format actually executed: NVFP4 → Marlin W4A16, BF16 math, activation scales discarded.** `MarlinExpertsBase.__init__` asserts `use_nvfp4_w4a16` and the oracle routes MARLIN to `nvfp4_w4a16_moe_quant_config` with no `a1_gscale`/`a2_gscale` — so the W4A4 group-16 calibration that is this build's *entire* reason to exist is thrown away on Ampere.
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $0.99 – $2.51 per 1M output tokens; max-throughput (S4): $0.99 – $2.51** (identical, because the measured proxy plateaus at a scheduler cap, not a hardware one). Blended 75/25 with 50 % cached input: **$0.28 – $0.76 / 1M**.
5. **Confidence: not-runnable.** Fit is `est.` from hardware; the throughput/cost points are the *base-checkpoint* 8×A800 measurement, which this checkpoint would under-perform by ≈ 6 % (it reads 5.88 % more expert bytes per step for zero compute benefit — §2, §3.4). **On A100 this checkpoint is strictly worse than the base in every dimension.** 22 items are ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED (§6).

### 1.2 KV per token — A100 cannot have the 890 B/token cache

The headline **890 B/token** is the FP4-KV figure and it is **not reachable on `sm_80`**.
Realising it requires KV dtype `nvfp4_ds_mla`, which exists only in
`FLASHMLA_MEGA_ATTN_DSV41`, which is gated `capability.major == 10`
([cross-cutting/flash-attention.md §11](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md), §6.2, §16.1).
A100's two reachable options are the ones METHODOLOGY asks for:

| KV scheme on A100 | main KV | indexer K | **B/token** | fixed SWA ring / seq | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FP8** (`--kv-cache-dtype fp8_ds_mla`) | 1,320 | 330 | **1,650** | 2,906,112 B = 2.77 MiB | [architecture.md §5.2/§5.3](architecture.md) |
| **BF16** (no KV quantisation) | 2,560 | 640 | **3,200** | 5,636,096 B = 5.38 MiB | [architecture.md §5.2](architecture.md), [METHODOLOGY §8](../../METHODOLOGY.md#models) |
| *(FP4, 890 B/token — Blackwell only)* | *720* | *170* | *— unreachable —* | *2.77 MiB* | [flash-attention.md §11](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md) |

FP8 KV on `sm_80` has a second catch: Triton on SM80 cannot emit `fp8e4nv`, so the
stock vLLM FP8 KV **write** kernels do not compile. The fork replaces them with
hand-rolled E4M3 bit manipulation; DeepSeek's own TileLang `act_quant` also works on
`sm_80` ([vLLM #57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144) blocker 4,
[#50576](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/50576) blocker 1).

**Independent corroboration of row A at n=8.** The author of [vLLM #57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144),
measuring the *base* 510 GB checkpoint on 8×A800 80GB, reports "MoE experts (fp4) 296 GB
→ 37 GB/rank, Engram tables 203 GB → 25.4 GB sharded, backbone/embed/head/vision/mtp
~11 GB → ~1.5 GB, **total 510 GB ≈ 64 GB [per rank]** … that leaves roughly
**15 GB/rank for KV cache, activations and graphs**". Our row A gives 65.9 GB/GPU for
the 17 GB-larger NVFP4 build and 2.09 GB of KV after a 4 GB workspace — i.e. the same
15 GB pool, split differently between workspace and KV. Two independent derivations,
one of them from a real box.

**Multi-node.** 8 GPUs is one HGX A100 baseboard, 600 GB/s NVLink all-to-all through
6× 2nd-gen NVSwitch — **no multi-node fabric needed**. 16 GPUs means either the rare
HGX A100 16-GPU baseboard or (in practice) two nodes over 8×200 Gb/s HDR IB (DGX) or
4×100 Gb/s EFA (p4de) = **25 GB/s per GPU, 24× less than intra-node NVLink**;
`gpus/a100.md` §4 states flatly that "multi-node TP is therefore almost always wrong on
A100". For this model the 16-GPU row is a *capacity* escape hatch, not a throughput one.

### 1.4 What cannot be sharded — explicitly

| Tensor group | Bytes | Shardable? |
|---|---|---|
| Routed experts (NVFP4) | 305.77 GB | **Yes**, by expert under EP (384 experts / 40 layers; EP8 → 48 experts/rank/layer, whole experts, no block-scale straddling) |
| Engram lookup tables | 202.76 GB | **Yes**, by row (`ParallelEngramEmbedding`, [architecture.md §4.2](architecture.md)) — or moved to host entirely |
| Attention projections + shared experts (FP8) | 6.49 GB | Yes, by head / intermediate |
| **`embed.weight` (BF16)** | **1.32 GB** | **No — replicated on every rank** ([architecture.md §4.2](architecture.md)) |
| **LM head `head.weight` (BF16)** | **1.32 GB** | Column-parallel in principle; the sm_80 recipe forces `--hf-overrides '{"head_dtype":"float32"}'`, which **doubles it to 2.65 GB** per replica |
| **mHC (`hc_attn_fn`, `hc_ffn_fn`, bases, scales) — FP32** | **0.157 GB** | **No — replicated**; and `rms_norm_eps = 1e-20` forces fp32 accumulation |
| **MoE routers (`gate.weight` BF16 + fp32 `bias`, `bias_vl`)** | **0.157 GB** | **No — replicated** (every rank must score all 384 experts) |
| Norms, attention sinks, image tokens | 0.42 MB | **No — replicated** |
| **The compressed KV cache itself** | — | **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED.** `num_key_value_heads = 1`: this is MQA-mode MLA, so there is nothing to split across TP ranks. Unless the engine runs DP-attention, the latent KV is **replicated per TP rank**, which divides every `max_concurrency` above by the TP degree. [serving-optimizations.md §7.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md) flags the same ambiguity for the base checkpoint on B300 and calls the replicated reading "the binding one". The measured sm_80 recipe hedges by running **TP4 × DP2**, which gives 2 KV replicas instead of 8. |

Replicated total ≈ **1.64 GB/rank** (2.97 GB with the fp32 head), i.e. ~2.5–4.5 % of the
65.9 GB/rank no-offload footprint and ~4–7 % of the 40.6 GB/rank offloaded one. Small in
bytes; not small relative to a 2.09 GB KV budget.

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.1 MBU / MFU assumptions — and why the METHODOLOGY defaults are wrong here

METHODOLOGY §4's planning band is MBU 0.6–0.8 (H100/H200 analogue) and
`gpus/a100.md` §9 says "no published A100 MBU figure found … use 0.6–0.75".
**Both are far too optimistic for this model on this GPU**, and for once there is a
measurement to prove it. Back-computing MBU from the 8×A800-SM80 run (§3.4), with
`bytes_per_decode_step` from [architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md) and A100's
2,039 GB/s × 8:

```
  C=  1 tps=  115.01 TPOT=  8.69 ms  tokens/step=2.0 -> step=  17.39 ms; bytes/step=  13.45 GiB; ideal(MBU=1)=  0.89 ms -> achieved MBU=0.051
  C=  1 tps=  115.01 TPOT=  8.69 ms  tokens/step=2.9 -> step=  25.22 ms; bytes/step=  13.45 GiB; ideal(MBU=1)=  0.89 ms -> achieved MBU=0.035
  C= 32 tps= 1459.17 TPOT= 21.93 ms  tokens/step=2.0 -> step=  43.86 ms; bytes/step= 122.41 GiB; ideal(MBU=1)=  8.06 ms -> achieved MBU=0.184
  C= 32 tps= 1459.17 TPOT= 21.93 ms  tokens/step=2.9 -> step=  63.60 ms; bytes/step= 122.41 GiB; ideal(MBU=1)=  8.06 ms -> achieved MBU=0.127
  C=128 tps= 3039.88 TPOT= 42.11 ms  tokens/step=2.0 -> step=  84.21 ms; bytes/step= 258.62 GiB; ideal(MBU=1)= 17.02 ms -> achieved MBU=0.202
  C=128 tps= 3039.88 TPOT= 42.11 ms  tokens/step=2.9 -> step= 122.11 ms; bytes/step= 258.62 GiB; ideal(MBU=1)= 17.02 ms -> achieved MBU=0.139
```

(`tokens/step` = 1 + 5 × DSpark acceptance; 2.0 / 2.9 / 3.4 bracket the measured
acceptance range in §3.5. ⚠️ the acceptance rate for *this* workload is not published,
so the MBU estimate inherits that uncertainty.)

**Planning values used below:**

Two things fall out. (a) **There is no T² term** — a 128K prefill costs 3.6× a 32K one,
not 16×, because every layer sees a 128-token window plus ≤512 compressed positions.

with 0 % attention blow-up. CED + CSA2 is worth more on A100 than on Blackwell,
precisely because A100 has no FA3/FA4 to hide a quadratic term behind.

more than the entire encoder forward pass, and TTFT hits 97 s. ⚠️ whether production
kernels bound those four the way the Hierarchical Sparse Indexer bounds the deeper four
is unverified ([architecture.md §6.2](architecture.md)).

### 3.6 The central question: does the NVFP4 build beat the base on A100?

**No, and the arithmetic is unambiguous.** Neither format executes natively on `sm_80`;
both go through the same Marlin W4A16 dequant-to-BF16 path, so there is no compute
difference to trade against the size difference. What is left is bytes:

```
  MXFP4 (base)  experts = 288,777,830,400 B = 288.78 GB
  NVFP4 (this)  experts = 305,765,130,240 B = 305.77 GB
  ratio NVFP4/MXFP4 = 1.0588  -> +5.88% expert bytes per decode step, 0 compute benefit on sm_80
  checkpoint delta = 16,987,299,840 B = +16.99 GB (+3.33%)
```

The packed 4-bit payload is byte-identical (271,790,899,200 B in both); the NVFP4 build
pays **one extra scale byte per 32 weights** to halve the block from 32 to 16
([architecture.md §3.4](architecture.md)). On Blackwell that buys W4A4 accuracy
neutrality at group-16. On A100 it buys **nothing at all**, because:

1. Marlin drops the activation scales (`nvfp4_w4a16_moe_quant_config` takes no
   `a1_gscale`/`a2_gscale`), so the W4A4 calibration — *"Activation scales were
   calibrated; 18 of 46,080 projection entries used fallback scales"* — is inert.
   NVIDIA's accuracy table (§6) was produced at W4A4 on GB300 and **does not transfer**
   to a W4A16 Ampere run.
2. The finer E4M3 scales are read per step regardless: at batch ≥ 128 nearly all 384
   experts per layer are touched, so decode traffic is ~+5.9 % and, decode being
   memory-bound at MBU 0.18, so is TPOT.
3. It costs 16.99 GB more HBM, which at 8 GPUs is 2.12 GB/rank — **larger than the
   entire 2.09 GB/rank KV budget** in the no-offload row of §1.3.

Applying −5.6 % to the measured proxy gives this checkpoint ≈ **2,870 tok/s at C128**
(TPOT ≈ 44.6 ms) — still inside the 50 ms SLO, but strictly behind the base for a
strictly larger download. **If you must run a DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on A100, run the base
`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`.** The community repos that go the other way —
`lvkaokao/…-W4A16-Engram-AutoRound` (INT4 GPTQ, 451.7 GB) and
`lvkaokao/…-MXFP4-Engram-AutoRound` (412.0 GB) — are the Ampere-shaped artefacts,
because they also shrink the 202.76 GB Engram table that A100 has the least room for
([architecture.md §9.2](architecture.md)). ⚠️ Neither publishes evals.

| Both together (they are already both on in the measured proxy) | blended **$0.313 (low) / $0.674 (high) / $0.267 (res1y)** | — |
| Engram CPU offload | no direct throughput cost measured ⚠️; it is what makes 8 GPUs usable at all (§1.3) | 12,672 B/token of host-side random gather; latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound |

### 4.5 Versus the vendor API — and the break-even

Vendor prices from [architecture.md §11](architecture.md): DeepSeek first-party
`deepseek-flash` **$0.60 (off-peak) – $1.20 (peak)** per 1M output, **$0.15 – $0.30**
uncached input, **$0.003 – $0.006** cached input. Cheapest third-party output on
OpenRouter: **DeepInfra $0.42** (FP8), then Relace $0.52 (FP4).

```
    low   $1.59 RunPod Secure     cost $1.162 vs DeepSeek off-peak $0.60    -> break-even utilisation  193.7%
    low   $1.59 RunPod Secure     cost $1.162 vs DeepSeek peak $1.20        -> break-even utilisation   96.9%
    low   $1.59 RunPod Secure     cost $1.162 vs DeepInfra $0.42            -> break-even utilisation  276.7%
    high  $3.431 AWS p4de         cost $2.508 vs DeepSeek off-peak $0.60    -> break-even utilisation  418.0%
    high  $3.431 AWS p4de         cost $2.508 vs DeepSeek peak $1.20        -> break-even utilisation  209.0%
    high  $3.431 AWS p4de         cost $2.508 vs DeepInfra $0.42            -> break-even utilisation  597.2%
    res1y $1.36 Hyperstack        cost $0.994 vs DeepSeek off-peak $0.60    -> break-even utilisation  165.7%
    res1y $1.36 Hyperstack        cost $0.994 vs DeepSeek peak $1.20        -> break-even utilisation   82.8%
    res1y $1.36 Hyperstack        cost $0.994 vs DeepInfra $0.42            -> break-even utilisation  236.7%
```

Read "break-even utilisation" as: *the fraction of the wall-clock hours you must keep the
8-GPU node saturated at the S1 operating point for your cost-of-goods to equal the API
price.* Anything **above 100 % is impossible** — you cannot run a node more than
full-time.

**So: self-hosting this model on rented A100s never beats the API.** Only one row is
even theoretically reachable (res1y $1.36 vs DeepSeek's *peak* $1.20, at 82.8 %
utilisation), and it requires a one-year commitment, 24/7 saturation at 128 concurrent
requests, and a comparison against DeepSeek's *expensive* hours. Against DeepInfra's
$0.42 the worst case needs **597 %** of a node. Add the input side and it gets slightly
better — DeepSeek charges $0.15–$0.30/1M for uncached input against our $0.154 at
MFU 0.20 — but input is the cheap half, and DeepSeek's cached-input rate of
**$0.003 (2.0 % of uncached)** is far below the 10 % METHODOLOGY assigns to our own
KV reuse, which is the whole ballgame on a 97.6 %-cache-hit agentic workload.

The owned/colo tier is the only one that changes the answer: at `gpus/a100.md` §8's
**$0.58/GPU-hour** `est.` ⚠️ the S1 cost is **$0.42/1M output** — exactly DeepInfra's
price, at 100 % utilisation. That is the honest ceiling of the A100-for-this-model case:
*break even with the cheapest reseller, if you already own the silicon and never let it
idle.* And you would run the **base** checkpoint to get there, not this one.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

**One HGX A100 8-GPU baseboard, and nothing else.** 8 GPUs is simultaneously the minimum
that holds the weights and the maximum NVLink domain Ampere offers. Going to 16 means
crossing to 200 Gb/s HDR IB (DGX) or 4×100 Gb/s EFA (p4de) — 25 GB/s per GPU against
600 GB/s intra-node, a 24× cliff, on a model whose decode step already moves 250+ GiB and
whose EP all-to-all is on the critical path. `gpus/a100.md` §4: *"Multi-node TP is
therefore almost always wrong on A100; use PP or replicate."* The correct 16-GPU shape is
**two independent 8-GPU replicas behind a router**, not TP16. Do not deploy on **PCIe**
A100s: no NVSwitch, 64 GB/s peer-to-peer, ~9.4× worse, and several marketplaces do not
disclose the form factor ([gpus/a100.md §11](../../gpus/a100.md) gotcha 9).

### 5.2 When PD disaggregation or wide-EP pays

**Wide-EP: never on this hardware.** There is no DeepEP-over-NVLink rack, no EPLB across
72 ranks, no NVLink-C2C. EP ≤ 8 inside the baseboard is the whole envelope — and it is
mandatory, not optional (§2).

**PD disaggregation: the theory is excellent, the software does not exist.** CED makes
this model the best PD candidate in the repo — prefill activates 7.55 B params over 20
encoder layers, decode activates 15.75 B over 40 plus the LM head, so prefill and decode
want genuinely different shapes. TokenSpeed already runs a per-commit **1P1D** (TP4
prefill + TP4 decode) Slurm job — **on GB300, against the base MXFP4 repo**
([inference-engines.md §2.5](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md),
[architecture.md §12](architecture.md)). Nothing equivalent exists for `sm_80`, and the
prerequisite (a working sm_80 attention backend) is still an open feature request.
⚠️ On A100 specifically, PD would also have to survive the 200 Gb/s KV transfer between
pools, which the 1,650 B/token FP8 cache makes cheap: a 128K prefix is 216 MB, ~9 ms at
25 GB/s. Worth building; nobody has.

### 5.3 Launch commands

**There is no vendor recipe for this pair.** For contrast, NVIDIA's own commands for this
checkpoint are both `--tp 4` on **GB300**, quoted verbatim in
[architecture.md §8.2](architecture.md) and
[inference-engines.md §6.2](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md); neither runs on Ampere.

The nearest runnable command is the community fork's, for the **base** checkpoint,
quoted verbatim from [`wtdcode/vllm-backport`](https://github.com/wtdcode/vllm-backport)
("`DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` | Native FP4 with Engram | **Fully Supported (v0.13.0+)**";
images `lazymio/vllm-backport:v0.13.0-sm80`):

And the exact argv behind the §3.4 measurements
([`config/serve.json`](https://github.com/Tokha233/deepseek-v4.1-flash-a100-turbo/blob/main/config/serve.json)),

  --speculative-config '{"method": "dspark", "num_speculative_tokens": 5, "use_local_argmax_reduction": true}' \

Two environment switches the ablation in §3.4 toggles:
`VLLM_AMPERE_DENSE_BF16_MIN_TOKENS=32` (route dense GEMMs of ≥32 rows to BF16 instead of

worth **+4.5 %**.

**To point any of these at `nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4` instead, you would need:**
(i) the fork's sm_80 attention/indexer/KV path to accept `moe_quant_algo: "NVFP4"` and
route to `ModelOptNvFp4FusedMoE` → `MarlinExperts` rather than `Mxfp4MoEMethod`;
(ii) NVFP4-group-16 Marlin MoE to actually launch on sm_80 (⚠️ the open question from
[#35922](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35922)); (iii) the backbone-NVFP4 /
MTP-MXFP4 mix to coexist. Nobody has published any of the three.

cosmetic.

## 6. Risks and open questions

### 6.1 Blocking — this pair does not run

1. ⚠️ **No `sm_80` attention backend for `DeepseekV41ForCausalLM` in any upstream engine.**
   vLLM `main`: `sparse_mla.py:125` → `major in [9,10]`; `flashinfer_sparse.py:137` →
   `major in [10,12]`; `flash_mla_mega_attn` → `major == 10`. SGLang's supported-hardware
   array for DS-V4.1 is `["h200","b200","b300","gb300","mi350x"]`
   ([inference-engines.md §3.2](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)). TensorRT-LLM has
   no V4.1 row at all, and its Ampere precision list stops at INT4/INT8.
2. ⚠️ **The one tracking issue targets the base checkpoint, is open, and has no maintainer
   reply.** [#57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144) (2026-09-16) lists
   eight blockers; the only human comment is another user pointing at a fork. Its
   predecessor [#50576](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/50576) records the
   upstream sentiment that *"SM<90 for DSv4 'better lives in a fork'"*.
3. ⚠️ **No published run of `nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4` on any Ampere device.**
   The card says "Supported Hardware Microarchitecture Compatibility: NVIDIA Blackwell"
   and nothing else. The community fork lists the **base** repo as supported and never
   mentions NVFP4.
4. ⚠️ **NVFP4 MoE on `sm_80` may not launch at all.**
   [#35922](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35922) (2026-03-03, vLLM 0.16.0,
   Qwen3-30B-A3B): `NVFP4`, `NVFP4A16`, `MXFP4A16` MoE all fail with
   `cudaErrorNoKernelImageForDevice`; INT4 W4A16 MoE fails with
   `cudaErrorIllegalAddress` in `fused_marlin_moe` during `profile_run()`. **Closed "not
   planned"/stale, no fix documented.** Current `main` claims SM75+ for the same path and
   `ModelOptMixedPrecisionConfig` claims A100 validation — the contradiction is
   unresolved and was not testable in this pass.
5. ⚠️ **Which quant config class wins.** The checkpoint declares `quant_method: "fp8"`
   *and* `quant_algo: "MIXED_PRECISION"`. `DeepseekV4FP8Config` and
   `ModelOptMixedPrecisionConfig` both claim it through `override_quantization_method`,
   and they take different code paths (the former builds `ModelOptNvFp4Config(group_size=16)`
   and routes to `ModelOptNvFp4FusedMoE`; the latter is the one with the A100-validated
   comment). Untested.

### 6.2 Would-break-the-fit

6. ⚠️ **MXFP8 dequant-to-BF16 at load.** `EmulationMxfp8LinearKernel` "dequantizes to BF16
   at load" and sits behind `MarlinMxfp8LinearKernel`, which
   [#57144](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57144) says "needs an sm_80
   verdict". If emulation is reached, the checkpoint's **204.0 GB of FP8/MXFP8 tensors
   become ~408 GB of BF16** and 8 GPUs stop being enough at any KV budget. This is the
   single most consequential unknown in this document.
7. ⚠️ **KV replication across TP ranks.** `num_key_value_heads = 1` — MQA-mode MLA has
   nothing to shard. If the latent KV replicates per rank rather than sharding, every
   `max_concurrency` in §1.3 divides by the TP degree (8 → 1,671 @8K instead of 13,364).
   [serving-optimizations.md §7.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md) flags the
   same ambiguity for B300 and treats replication as binding.
8. ⚠️ **HBM capacity disagreement, 80 GB vs 85.90 GB** (§1.1), worth 47.2 GB per node —
   2.8× the entire no-offload KV budget. METHODOLOGY §8 vs `gpus/a100.md` §2; the latter
   marks its own figure ⚠️.
9. ⚠️ **Engram host-gather cost is unmeasured.** 12,672 B/token of random rows pulled over
   PCIe per token per sequence. The byte count is trivial; the *latency* on a
   random-access pattern is not, and it is inside the decode critical path.
   `NgramHashState` per-sequence size is also unstated ([architecture.md §5.3](architecture.md)).
10. ⚠️ **`S` (engine state slots per request) is unknown for V4.1.** All fixed-state
    figures assume `S = 1` ([architecture.md §5.3](architecture.md)). An MTP-speculating
    runtime plausibly allocates extra SWA ring slots for the 5-token drafted block.

### 6.3 Accuracy caveats of the precision actually executed

11. ⚠️ **NVIDIA's accuracy table does not transfer.** GPQA 91.288 / SciCode 55.843 /
    Terminal-Bench 82.16 were measured **W4A4 on GB300 with vLLM**
    ([architecture.md §10.1](architecture.md)). On A100 the experts run **W4A16 with the
    activation scales discarded** (§2), so the evaluated numerical path and the executed
    one are different. The *weights* are bit-identical after dequant ("All 16,986,931,200
    weight blocks passed lossless conversion"), so BF16-activation inference should be at
    least as accurate — but the card itself warns *"Lossless weight conversion does not
    imply identical inference outputs"*, and nobody has evaluated this path.
12. ⚠️ **18 of 46,080 projection entries used fallback activation scales.** Irrelevant on
    A100 (the scales are dropped) — but it means the checkpoint's own calibration was not
    clean, and it will matter if an A4 path ever lands.
13. ⚠️ **`rms_norm_eps = 1e-20`** forces fp32 norm accumulation; the sm_80 recipe
    additionally forces `head_dtype: float32`, with the fork's own hedge: *"once helped
    reduce garbage outputs by improving precisions but might be not compulsory."* A
    numerics-sensitive model on a fallback-kernel stack.
14. ⚠️ **Per-tensor-only FP8 KV scales** (`supports_per_head_quant_scales()` needs FA ≥ 3).
    `gpus/a100.md` §6: the default all-1.0 scales are "the accuracy-risky option" —
    calibrate them.
15. ⚠️ **Backbone NVFP4 + MTP/DSpark MXFP4 in one process** is unvalidated on *any* GPU
    ([architecture.md §7.3](architecture.md)); on a Marlin-fallback GPU the two halves take
    different fallback paths.

### 6.4 Measurement and engine risks

16. ⚠️ **Every number in §3.4 is community-measured on A800, for the base checkpoint,**
    by an author who discloses AI assistance. No independent replay exists. A800 differs
    from A100 only in NVLink (400 vs 600 GB/s), which makes it pessimistic for collectives.
17. ⚠️ **DSpark acceptance for this workload is not published.** The 2.6× reconciliation in
    §3.5 and the ~29 %/48 % acceptance from the vision port are the only anchors.
    **The DSpark 3.51 that appears in the MI355X recipe is a synthetic benchmark constant,
    not a measured acceptance rate** — do not carry it into an A100 plan.
18. ⚠️ **The measured plateau is a scheduler cap, not a ceiling** (§3.5). `--max-num-seqs 64
    × DP2` against a 13,364 KV capacity. Nobody has raised it, so the true A100
    max-throughput point is unknown.
19. ⚠️ **Prefill MFU on `sm_80` for this model has no anchor at all.** `gpus/a100.md` §9
    records that no published A100 LLM-serving MFU figure exists; I halved METHODOLOGY's
    BF16 band to 0.20 by analogy with the decode MBU shortfall. That is an assumption.
20. ⚠️ **Whether production kernels bound the four full-pool indexers.** At 1M context they
    cost 21.47 GFLOP/token, more than the whole encoder forward pass; the reference
    implementation does not bound them ([architecture.md §6.2](architecture.md)).
21. ⚠️ **Multimodal on `sm_80` for V4.1 is unattempted.** Every published sm_80 command
    passes `--language-model-only`. The sibling V4-Vision-Exp port succeeded on SM80, so it
    is probably tractable — but the vision path also drops DSpark unless the draft head is
    wired explicitly, and that failure is silent (near-zero acceptance, no error).
22. ⚠️ **Engine churn.** `sgl_kernel` has shipped wheels without SM80 cubins
    ([SGLang #11421](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/11421)); Dynamo requires
    driver 580.xx+ / CUDA 13.x, which an old Ampere cluster on R535 cannot run
    ([gpus/a100.md §7, §11](../../gpus/a100.md)). Pin everything, and verify an sm_80 cubin
    exists before rollout.

### 6.5 The non-technical risk

A100 rental supply is thinning: Vast.ai listed **zero** A100 SXM4 and **zero** A100 PCIe
offers on 2026-09-19, Lambda raised A100 pricing during 2026, and the used market is
described as "high supply as data centers cycle from Ampere to Hopper and Blackwell"
([gpus/a100.md §8, §11](../../gpus/a100.md)). A plan that depends on renting 8 A100s
on demand should price a migration path.
