## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, but not as shipped.** `MarlinForConditionalGeneration` is in no engine registry; vLLM serves it with one documented flag, `--hf-overrides '{"architectures": ["Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration"]}'`, on **vLLM `main`/nightly** (≥ the v0.29.0 line, CUDA 13.0; [architecture.md §8.1](architecture.md), [gpus/gb300.md §7](../../gpus/gb300.md)). SGLang `main` needs the same remap and its **video** ingestion is unconfirmed ⚠️.
2. **Min GPUs = 1, recommended = 1 per replica, parallelism = TP1 × DP-N.** 4.426 GB of weights against ~279 GB of HBM. A 4-GPU tray is 4 replicas; the 72-GPU rack is 72 replicas. **Wide-EP is inapplicable — the model is dense, it has no experts**, and the NVLink domain that justifies GB300's price buys this pair nothing.
3. **Weight format actually executed: BF16, natively, 2,500 TFLOPS dense.** No FP8/NVFP4/MXFP4 Marlin checkpoint exists; the published INT8 one is **dead on sm_103** (~167 TOPS, 1/30 rate) and the published GPTQ-INT4 one runs W4A16 dequant only.
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $0.226 per 1M output tokens / $0.068 blended** at OCI's $18.00 — the only published GB300 rental rate. **Max-throughput (S4): $0.214 / $0.065.** Per video: **$0.0011 for a 2-minute clip, $1.09 per 1,000 clips, $0.00054 per video-minute.**
4a. **Two throughput bases, and item 4 quotes the sustained one.** §3.5's **sustained end-to-end** rate charges prefill against every output token (`gpu_seconds = prefill_time + osl × TPOT / batch`, and every scenario here is prefill-bound): **22,164 out tok/s/GPU at S1, 23,353 at S4 → $0.226 / $0.214 per 1M output, $0.068 blended**. The **decode-only** rate METHODOLOGY §4 defines (`batch / decode_step_time` = 256 / 3.64 ms and 1,024 / 12.19 ms on 1 GPU) is **70,330 / 84,003 out tok/s/GPU → $0.0711 / $0.0595 per 1M output, $0.0294 blended**. `pairs.json` and [matrix/cost-matrix.md](../../matrix/cost-matrix.md) carry the **decode-only** pair, because that is the only basis on which the 40 pairs are comparable; the sustained pair is the one to plan a video fleet with, and the 3.2–3.6× gap between them is the prefill bill.
5. **Confidence: estimate.** Zero measurements of Marlin-2B exist on any hardware; the nearest published proxy on this exact GPU is Qwen-3.5-397B-A17B (same Qwen3.5 hybrid-GDN family, 180× the active params) in InferenceX. **GB300 is the wrong GPU for this model** — it is 1.0× a B300 on the metric that binds and 2.4× the price.

## 1. Fit

### 1.1 Pinned inputs

```
weights BF16 resident         4,426,483,328 B = 4.426 GB = 4.122 GiB   (2,213,241,664 unique params, tie honoured)
checkpoint on disk            5,443,677,224 B = 5.444 GB              (lm_head duplicated, +1.017 GB)
decode weight read            3,763,650,176 B = 3.764 GB              (language layers + lm_head; ViT idle after prefill)
HBM as deployed               279 GB ; usable x0.90 = 251.10 GB = 233.86 GiB
KV budget per GPU             242.38 GB = 225.73 GiB   (usable - weights - 4 GiB act ws)
KV bytes/token                BF16 12,288  FP8 6,144  FP4 3,072
GDN fixed state / seq         19,537,920 B = 18.63 MiB  (S=1)
```

**Capacity basis, and a disagreement worth stating.** METHODOLOGY §8 pins GB300 at
"288 GB (≈ 279 usable)"; [`gpus/gb300.md` §1.1/§2.2](../../gpus/gb300.md) resolves that to
**279 GB as the planning basis** (NVIDIA publishes only the rack figure, 20 TB ÷ 72 = 277.8 GB
`est.`; SemiAnalysis says 278 GB usable; Lambda and CoreWeave list 279 GB). This document uses
**279 GB × 0.90 = 251.10 GB**, identical to [`architecture.md` §8.5](architecture.md), which is
why the 23,520-token column below reproduces its 785. Sizing from the 288 GB nominal die instead
would over-commit ~3.2 % per GPU. A second, engine-specific basis exists and is **not** mixed in
here: SGLang's published GB300 config holds back 25 % (`mem_fraction_static = 0.75`), which would
cut every concurrency figure below by ~17 % ([gpus/gb300.md §2.2](../../gpus/gb300.md)).

### 1.2 Fit vs GPU count — the topology ladder

Because 4.426 GB fits in one GPU with 246 GB to spare, **every GPU count is a replica count, not a
shard count.** Weights are replicated on each GPU; nothing is sharded. `max_concurrency` therefore
scales exactly linearly and the fabric question never arises inside one rack.

`est.` per METHODOLOGY §3: `max_concurrency(ctx) = floor(kv_budget × n_gpus / (ctx × kv_bytes_per_token + fixed_state))`,
`kv_budget = 279 GB × 0.90 − 4.426 GB − 4 GiB`.

Per-GPU, the numbers behind that table:

| ctx | BF16 KV | FP8 KV |
|---|---:|---:|
| 8 K | **2,016** | **3,469** |
| **23,520 (2-min video, the only ctx a video fleet sees)** | **785** | **1,477** |
| 32 K | 574 | 1,097 |
| 128 K | 148 | 293 |
| 262,144 (native max) | 74 | 148 |
| 1 M | 18 | 37 |

The 23,520 row is the operating point: **785 concurrent 2-minute videos per GPU, 56,520 per rack**
at BF16 KV; **1,477 / 106,344** at FP8 KV. The 785 reproduces
[`architecture.md` §8.5](architecture.md) exactly.

**⚠️ 1 M is out of spec**, not merely optimistic: `max_position_embeddings` is 262,144 and
`rope_parameters.rope_type` is `default` with no YaRN/NTK entry ([architecture.md §5.3](architecture.md)).
The 1 M column is arithmetic. It is also unreachable by video: the 240-frame cap bounds any video
request at ~23.5 K tokens no matter how long the clip is ([architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)).

**Multi-node fabric needed? No, at any count in the table.** All 72 GPUs sit in one NVLink-5 domain
(1.8 TB/s/GPU, 130 TB/s aggregate, one switched hop — [gpus/gb300.md §4.1](../../gpus/gb300.md)),
and this model never sends a byte across it anyway. Crossing racks (800 Gb/s/GPU ConnectX-8,
an ~18× cliff) is irrelevant for the same reason: independent replicas need no collective at all,
only a load balancer.

### 1.3 What cannot be sharded — and why TP > 1 is a trap here

If someone reaches for TP anyway (there is no reason to):

```
 TP  W/GPU GB  KV heads/GPU  KV B/tok/GPU  GDN state/GPU MiB verdict
  1     4.426          2.00        12,288              18.63 optimal
  2     2.213          1.00         6,144               9.32 KV shards
  4     1.107          2.00        12,288               4.66 KV heads REPLICATED - no KV win
  8     0.553          2.00        12,288               2.33 KV heads REPLICATED - no KV win
 16     0.277          2.00        12,288               1.16 KV heads REPLICATED - no KV win
```

Explicitly non-shardable, or shardable only to a hard ceiling:

| Tensor group | Params | Sharding limit | Why |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `self_attn.k_proj` / `v_proj` KV heads | 6 × 2 heads | **TP ≤ 2** | `num_key_value_heads: 2`. Above TP=2 engines **replicate** KV heads, so per-GPU KV cache stops shrinking — the whole point of TP for a KV-bound model evaporates at TP=4. |
| GDN heads (`in_proj_*`, `conv1d`, `out_proj`, recurrent state) | 18 × 16 heads | **TP ≤ 16** | `linear_num_key_heads` / `linear_num_value_heads` = 16 |
| ViT attention | 24 × 16 heads | TP ≤ 16, but **use DP** | `--mm-encoder-tp-mode data`: at hidden 1024 TP costs more than it saves ([serving-optimizations.md §6.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)) |
| **Fully replicated, cannot be sharded at all** | **548,672** | — | `input_layernorm` + `post_attention_layernorm` (48 × 2048), `linear_attn.norm` (18 × 128), `model.norm` (2048), `self_attn.q_norm`/`k_norm` (12 × 256), `linear_attn.A_log` + `dt_bias` (36 × 16), and the depthwise `linear_attn.conv1d.weight` (18 × 24,576 = 442,368) which shards by channel with the heads but never by hidden dim ([architecture.md §3.6](architecture.md)) |
| Embedding / `lm_head` | 508,559,360 | vocab-shardable | `tie_word_embeddings: true`, so the on-disk duplicate is dropped at load — **or not**, ⚠️ (§6) |

**Conclusion: TP1, always.** TP=2 halves KV per GPU but halves nothing else worth having; TP≥4 pays
NVLink collectives on a 3.76 GB weight read for zero KV benefit.

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.1 Assumptions (METHODOLOGY §4)

| Parameter | Value | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| **MBU (decode)** | **0.60** | METHODOLOGY §4 gives 0.5–0.7 for Blackwell-generation software; [gpus/gb300.md §9.4](../../gpus/gb300.md) defends **0.60–0.75** given FA4's measured 71 %-of-peak on B200 and 93 % NVLink efficiency. 0.60 is the conservative end and is what [`architecture.md` §10.3](architecture.md) uses for every GPU, so these rows stay comparable across the repo. |
| **MFU (BF16 prefill)** | **0.40** | METHODOLOGY §4 band 0.35–0.50 |
| Peak | **2,500 TFLOPS BF16 dense**, 8.0 TB/s HBM | METHODOLOGY §8 / [gpus/gb300.md §3](../../gpus/gb300.md). **Dense, never sparse.** Note GB300's BF16 is 2,500 vs HGX B300's 2,250 — the +11 % is the *only* compute difference that reaches a BF16 model |
| Decode bytes | `3.764 GB + batch × ctx × 12,288` | Dense model: **all language weights read every step regardless of batch**; the 331 M-param ViT is idle after prefill |
| Prefill FLOPs | `3.764 GFLOP × T + 24,576 · T² (+ 0.2722 TFLOP × frames)` | [architecture.md §6.1, §6.4](architecture.md) |
| Comm overhead | **0 %** | TP1. No collective on the critical path |

**Machine balance, for context.** At BF16 a GEMM must reuse every byte **312.5 times** to be
compute-bound on GB300 ([gpus/gb300.md §9.1](../../gpus/gb300.md)). Every decode row below is
bandwidth-bound; the compute term never binds, even at batch 256.

`est.` `decode_step_time = max(bytes_per_step/(8 TB/s × 0.60), 2 × 1.8818e9 × batch/(2.5 PFLOPS × 0.40))`;
`TTFT = prefill_time(uncached) + one decode step`. Per METHODOLOGY §3's consistency rule, **S3 at
batch 256 exceeds `max_concurrency(132,096) = 147` and is printed `infeasible (KV)`**, not as a number.

Four things to read off it:

1. **TPOT ≤ 50 ms is met at every feasible row.** This model has no latency problem on this GPU. The
   SLO never binds; **KV capacity and prefill throughput** do.
2. **The 90 %-prefix-cache TTFT column is a fantasy for this workload** (§2) and is included only
   because METHODOLOGY §5.4 asks for it. Read the 0 % column.
3. **S3's 128 K rows are text-only.** The 240-frame cap means no video request ever exceeds
   ~23.5 K tokens ([architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)).
4. **These are roofline ceilings a 2 B model will not reach.** At batch 8 the roofline says a
   0.87 ms decode step — comfortably *below* an engine's per-step scheduler + sampler overhead.
   Treat everything below batch ~32 as overhead-bound in practice, not bandwidth-bound. CUDA graphs
   are what close part of that gap, and the assertion in §2 is what breaks them.

### 3.5 Sustained end-to-end, per GPU — prefill is not free

The §3.2 tables charge nothing for prefill. Per METHODOLOGY §6 the cost model must, so:
`gpu_seconds_per_request = prefill_time + osl × TPOT / batch`, one GPU doing both serially. A real
engine with chunked prefill and continuous batching overlaps them and lands somewhat better.

**Every scenario is prefill-bound on GB300.** That inverts the batch-1 intuition (where 71–79 % of
the time is decode) and it is the single most consequential result in this document: the levers that
matter are **FLOPs and frames**, not bandwidth. It also means GB300's 8 TB/s — identical to
GB200's and to HGX B300's — buys nothing, while its +11 % BF16 dense rate buys exactly +11 %.

**Why the estimates and the proxies differ, and by how much.** They are not comparable directly —
different model, precision and parameter count — but three structural gaps are worth naming:

1. **Active-parameter ratio.** Qwen-3.5-397B-A17B reads ~17 B active params per token against
   Marlin's 1.88 B — roughly 9×. That alone predicts Marlin landing about an order of magnitude
   above the proxy's per-GPU decode throughput, which is what §3.2 shows (57,914 vs 1,641 at batch
   128). The proxy therefore **validates the shape of the curve, not the level**.
2. **The proxy's TPOT is 4.5 ms at concurrency 1**; the roofline for a 397B-A17B FP8 model at TP4
   would be well under that. The residual is engine overhead — scheduler, sampler, graph replay,
   Dynamo routing. **That residual is a near-constant per step, so on a 2 B model whose roofline
   step is 0.8 ms it dominates completely.** Expect measured Marlin batch-1 TPOT of several ms on
   GB300, not 0.8 ms, and expect the §3.2 low-batch rows to be optimistic by 3–5×.
3. **The proxy is prefill-limited in the same direction Marlin is.** Its TTFT at 8 K input is
   177–194 ms across concurrency 1–16 — the same order as §3.5's 167.6 ms prefill for a
   23,560-token video request at ~3× the tokens but ~9× fewer active params per token. The two land
   in the same place for opposite reasons, which is a mild independent sanity check on the MFU 0.40
   assumption.

### 4.4 Effect of the optimisations on cost

| Lever | Effect on $/1M | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **FP8 KV cache** | **~0 % on cost, +88 % on concurrency** | Every scenario is prefill-bound (§3.5), so doubling KV capacity raises the *ceiling* without raising the *rate*. It matters if you are memory-constrained; on 279 GB you are not |
| **Prefix caching** | **~0 %** | Hit rate ≈ 0 % for video. The METHODOLOGY §6 "cached input at 10 % cost" term in the blended formula above is therefore **charged but never earned** on this workload — the realistic blended figure is the 0 %-cache one, `0.75 × C_in + 0.25 × C_out`, which for S1 at $18.00 is **$0.0776** rather than $0.0680, i.e. **+14 %** |
| **Speculative decoding** | **0 % — unavailable** | No MTP weights, no EAGLE drafter. And it could not help: every scenario is prefill-bound, and speculation cannot touch prefill |
| **FP8 weights (if someone built the checkpoint)** | **−16 %** | LLM prefill 102.3 → 68.2 ms at 5,000 TFLOPS dense, MFU 0.30; ViT stays BF16. $/1k videos 1.085 → **0.911** `est.` ⚠️ no FP8 Marlin checkpoint exists |
| **NVFP4 weights (if someone built the checkpoint)** | **−35 %** | LLM prefill 102.3 → **27.3 ms** at 15,000 TFLOPS dense, MFU 0.25 (METHODOLOGY §4's FP4 band 0.20–0.35). Total video prefill 167.6 → **92.6 ms**, now **ViT-dominated** (65.3 of 92.6 ms — no vision-tower quantisation recipe exists). $/1k videos 1.085 → **0.706** `est.` **Real, and nobody has built it** ⚠️ |
| **Cutting frames / resolution** | **linear** | 120 frames instead of 240 halves both the ViT and the LLM prefill term — the largest available lever, at a quality cost the cap is already imposing on long video |
| **NVDEC video decode** | ⚠️ unquantified, possibly the largest | vLLM measured >2× at 8×H100 on CPU-bound video captioning. GB300 trays are 4 GPUs : 2 Grace CPUs |

### 4.5 Break-even vs a vendor API

**There is no vendor API to compare to.** [`architecture.md` §11.1](architecture.md) establishes it:
HF `inferenceProviderMapping` for `NemoStation/Marlin-2B` is **`{}`** (empty); the base
`Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B` has one provider, `featherless-ai`, **text only, not video**; the author runs a
Gradio demo with no documented token-priced API and monetises through custom fine-tuning by email.
⚠️ Whether Fireworks / Together / Replicate / Baseten / DeepInfra / SiliconFlow host it is unverified.

So METHODOLOGY §6's API sanity check cannot be run for this pair. What can be stated is the
break-even against a hypothetical price, at the S1 blended figure of **$0.0680/1M** on OCI's $18.00:

```
  vs API $0.05/1M : break-even utilisation =  136.1 %  (API always cheaper)
  vs API $0.10/1M : break-even utilisation =   68.0 %  (self-host wins above that)
  vs API $0.20/1M : break-even utilisation =   34.0 %  (self-host wins above that)
  vs API $0.50/1M : break-even utilisation =   13.6 %  (self-host wins above that)
```

`est.` **On GB300 specifically, self-hosting needs 68 % sustained utilisation to beat a $0.10/1M
API.** The same calculation on an `h100` at its `low` $3.20 needs a small fraction of that — which
is the real verdict, and it is about the GPU choice, not about self-hosting.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

**Neither, in the usual sense.** The deployment unit is **one GPU = one replica**, and everything
above that is a stateless load balancer. Concretely:

| Shape | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|
| **1 GPU** | one vLLM process, TP1 | development, and the correct unit of capacity |
| **4-GPU slice** (`BM.GPU.GB300.4`, `Standard_ND128isr_GB300_v6`) | **4 independent replicas**, 2 Grace CPUs, 16 TiB NVMe | the smallest rentable GB300 unit. 3,140 concurrent 2-min videos, ~18.4 videos/s sustained |
| **8-GPU** | 8 replicas across 2 trays | no engine-level meaning for this model; it is 2 slices |
| **72-GPU rack** | **72 replicas.** NVLink carries nothing | only if you already have the rack |

**Wide-EP on the full rack: inapplicable, not merely unhelpful.** The model is dense —
`mlp_only_layers: []`, no `num_experts`, no router, no `moe_intermediate_size`
([architecture.md §2.2](architecture.md)). DeepEP, EPLB and the 130 TB/s all-to-all have nothing to
carry. The argument that justifies NVL72 — *"DeepSeek-R1 has 256 routed experts, at ~4 experts/GPU
you need 64 GPUs to hold them once, which fits inside one NVL72 and does not fit inside an 8-GPU
node"* ([gpus/gb300.md §10.6](../../gpus/gb300.md)) — has no analogue here.

**PD disaggregation: possible, rarely worth it.** The KV to ship per 2-minute video is 275.6 MiB,
trivial over 1.8 TB/s NVLink, and Dynamo/NIXL/SGLang-PD all work on GB300. But both phases fit on
one GPU with 246 GB spare, the prefill:decode GPU-time ratio at the video operating point is
167.6 ms : 48.6 ms ≈ **3.4 : 1**, and a 1P/1D split would strand a third of the decode GPU. It pays
only if (a) you run a mixed fleet where long `max_new_tokens=2048` captions and short `.find()`
calls have different SLOs, or (b) **encode-prefill-decode (EPD)** separation lets a CPU-starved tray
hand video decode to dedicated nodes — which is the real reason to look at it here
([serving-optimizations.md §6.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).

1.1 s even at the slow end of the 5–20 GB/s band, and the `Standard_ND128isr_GB300_v6` tray carries
16 TiB of local NVMe. What actually costs warm-up time on this model is everything else: CUDA-graph
capture across the `--max-num-seqs` ladder, `torch.compile` on first call (`marlin.compile()` in the
card's own snippet), FlashInfer **JIT warm-up for the GDN prefill kernel** (vLLM warns about it —
[flash-attention.md §14.3](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)), and the Mamba/GDN state
allocator. ⚠️ None of those is measured for this model; budget minutes, not the 1.1 s.

## 6. Risks and open questions

### Blocking for this pair

1. **⚠️ The cross-cutting docs and the architecture doc disagree about whether this model runs at
   all, and both are cited here.** [`inference-engines.md` §6.5](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)
   is titled *"`NemoStation/Marlin-2B` — no engine supports it"* and was written **without gate
   access**; [`architecture.md` §8.1](architecture.md) was written **with** gate access and
   establishes that `modeling_marlin.py` is a pure subclass of `Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration`
   with an unmodified forward pass, so `--hf-overrides` suffices. **This document follows the
   architecture doc**, which has the stronger evidence, and flags the cross-cutting section as
   stale. Both are cited so the disagreement is visible rather than silently resolved.
2. **⚠️ That remap is not verified end to end.** Nobody in this repo has loaded all 618 tensors into
   vLLM's `Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration` loader. Sub-question: how vLLM handles the materialised
   `lm_head.weight` against `tie_word_embeddings: true` (most likely benign).
3. **⚠️ Video token budget: Path A (23,520 tokens) vs Path B (12,288).** `qwen-vl-utils` runs its own
   `smart_resize` and bypasses the HF processor's `size`; the gated `processor_config.json`
   **confirms the conflict** (`max_frames: 768`, `longest_edge: 25165824`) rather than resolving it.
   Serving through vLLM/SGLang likely gives the model **half the frames or half the resolution** the
   training path did — a silent quality regression, and a **1.91× error in every prefill, TTFT and
   cost number in §3–§4** if Path B is what your engine actually does
   ([architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)).
4. **⚠️ SGLang video ingestion for `qwen3_5` is unconfirmed.** The class is exported and subclasses
   `Qwen3VLForConditionalGeneration`, but the SGLang multimodal docs page 404'd on both domains.

### GB300-specific

5. **⚠️ flash-attn-4 rejects sm_103 in some builds** — `Only SM 10.x and 11.x are supported` at
   warmup, because `sm_110f` resolves numerically below `sm_103` on non-CUDA-13 CuTeDSL builds.
   Verify your pin before standing up a fleet ([gpus/gb300.md §5.1](../../gpus/gb300.md)).
6. **⚠️ No published FA4 or FlashMLA TFLOPS figure exists for B300 at all.** The FA4 paper explicitly
   declines to benchmark B300. The GPU doc's planning guidance is to scale B200's 1,613 TFLOPS by
   2500/2250 = 1.11× for a **GB300 NVL72** B300 and by 1.0× for an air-cooled HGX B300
   ([flash-attention.md §9.4](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)) — and that only covers the
   6 full-attention layers, which are 13.3 % of prefill FLOPs at the video operating point.
7. **⚠️ No published end-to-end MBU or MFU figure for GB300 specifically.** MBU 0.60 here is the
   conservative end of a defended band, not a measurement ([gpus/gb300.md §9.4](../../gpus/gb300.md)).
8. **⚠️ CUDA-graph / scheduler overhead dominates a 2 B model's decode step on this hardware.** The
   roofline says 0.80 ms at batch 1; the nearest measured GB300 proxy runs 4.5 ms at concurrency 1
   on a model with 9× the active params. **§3.2's rows below batch ~32 should be read as ceilings
   that will not be reached**, and nobody has measured the actual floor for a model this small on
   sm_103.
9. **⚠️ NVFP4 KV cache at `head_dim 256`** — FlashInfer exposes `nvfp4_kv_cache_full_dim` and its
   NVFP4 paged-KV sparse decode/prefill merged for SM100/SM103 on 2026-09-18, but hd256 support is
   unconfirmed.
10. **⚠️ FlashQLA dispatch.** SM103 is on its supported list and it claims 2–3× over FLA Triton, but
    it is unconfirmed that vLLM or SGLang dispatch to it for this head config rather than their own
    FlashInfer/Triton GDN kernels. SGLang carries `triton_gdn_fused_proj` and an
    `SGLANG_ENABLE_GDN_DECODE_FUSED_PROJ_CONV` flag, so it may not use FlashQLA at all.
11. **⚠️ GDN conv-state width** (`kernel` vs `kernel − 1`) and **`--mamba-ssm-cache-dtype bfloat16`**
    — the first is +1.1 % on the state (immaterial), the second would halve it to 9.63 MiB/seq.
    Neither is verified for this model.
12. **⚠️ Engines reserve more than METHODOLOGY's 10 %.** SGLang's published GB300 config holds back
    25 %. Every §1.2 concurrency figure would fall ~17 % under that config.
13. **⚠️ GB300 rests on one published price ($18.00, OCI) with no published reserved tier.** Every
    §4 conclusion is one price list away from changing, and the `low`/`high` band is degenerate.

### Accuracy caveats of the executed precision

14. **BF16 is the checkpoint's native precision, so there is no quantisation accuracy question for
    the recommended path.** That is the good news and it is unusual in this repo.
15. **⚠️ Every quantised Marlin variant is unevaluated.** Not one of the GPTQ-INT4, SDNQ-INT8,
    MLX-8bit or GGUF repos publishes a CaReBench / DREAM-1K / TimeLens score, a perplexity delta or
    any accuracy measurement. **For a model whose product is second-precise timestamps, INT4 numeric
    error landing on the timestamp digits is a specific, plausible and entirely unmeasured risk**
    ([architecture.md §9](architecture.md)). On sm_103 the INT8 one is a non-starter for a second
    reason (§2).
16. **⚠️ The 240-frame cap is an accuracy cliff, not a capacity feature.** A 10-minute clip is
    sampled at 0.40 fps and an hour at 0.067 fps for identical cost. Whether "second-precise
    timestamps" survive frames 2.5 s apart is unmeasured. Chunk client-side.
17. **⚠️ Benchmark contamination.** ActivityNet, Charades and TimeLens appear in both the card's
    training-source list and its evaluation list; the card does not say how contamination was
    controlled. All quality claims are vendor marketing with no numeric table and `model-index: null`.

### Workload risks the GPU roofline does not see

18. **⚠️ torchcodec / video-decode throughput for 240 frames at 448×448 is unmeasured** and is
    plausibly the real production bottleneck. A GB300 tray is **4 GPUs to 2 Grace CPUs** — an
    Arm CPU:GPU ratio that makes CPU-side decode more likely to bind here than on an x86 HGX node.
    vLLM's NVDEC path is the mitigation and its own post reports >2× on 8×H100; **no GB300 number
    exists** ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-09-18-pynvvideocodec)).
19. **⚠️ `.multi_find` cost.** N sequential ffmpeg `libx264` re-encodes plus N full prefills, all CPU
    work outside the roofline. On a Grace-CPU tray, budget generously
    ([architecture.md §1.4](architecture.md)).
20. **Two EOS ids and a `<think>` prefix.** `generation_config.json` lists `[248044, 248046]` while
    `config.eos_token_id` is 248046 alone; an engine reading only the config misses a stop id. The
    model emits `<think>` at the start of every response, which `modeling_marlin.py` strips and a
    raw engine does not. Both must be re-supplied after the `--hf-overrides` remap
    ([architecture.md §1.3, §8.1](architecture.md)).
21. **Name collision.** "Marlin" is also vLLM's INT4 W4A16 GEMM kernel, and it appears in this
    document in both senses. Searching issue trackers for "vllm marlin" returns the kernel.

### The question this document exists to answer

22. **Is GB300 the right GPU for Marlin-2B? No.** It fits 63× over, uses none of the rack's
    distinguishing features (EP, NVLink domain, 279 GB, FP4 tensor cores), is bandwidth-identical to
    GB200 and to HGX B300, and costs **$18.00/GPU-h against `b200` at $6.00 and `h100` at $3.20**.
    [`architecture.md` §11.2](architecture.md) ranks it **last of eight pinned GPUs on $/1k videos**.
    The only reasons to run this pair are: you already own the rack and have spare GPUs; or you are
    co-locating Marlin as a preprocessing stage beside a frontier model that genuinely needs NVL72,
    in which case the marginal cost is whatever a spare GPU-hour is worth to you, and the answer in
    §4 at the $2.31–$2.57 colo/volume anchors ($0.0087–$0.0097 blended) is the relevant one.
