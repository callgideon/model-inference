Research date: **2026-09-19**. Formulas, legend (`[src]` / `est.` / `meas.` / ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED**)
and the pinned inputs are [`research/METHODOLOGY.md`](../../METHODOLOGY.md) §1–§8. Model facts come
from [`models/marlin2b/architecture.md`](architecture.md); hardware facts from
[`gpus/b200.md`](../../gpus/b200.md); kernel, format, engine, serving and price facts from the five

## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, with one flag and no port.** vLLM **v0.29.0** (or the NGC 26.08 container) via
   `--hf-overrides '{"architectures":["Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration"]}'` — the checkpoint is a pure
   subclass of `Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration` with an unmodified forward
   ([architecture.md §8.1](architecture.md)); as-shipped, `MarlinForConditionalGeneration` is in no engine's registry
   ([inference-engines.md §6.5](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)). `transformers ≥ 5.7.0` is the only first-class path.
2. **Min = recommended = 1 GPU, TP1.** 4.426 GB of resident weights on a 180 GB part; extra GPUs are
   **data-parallel replicas**, never tensor-parallel shards. 8×B200 = 8 independent replicas.
3. **Weight format executed: BF16.** It is the only checkpoint that exists; B200's NVFP4 tensor cores
   go **unused**, and buying B200 for FP4 on this model is buying nothing (§2, §4).
4. **Interactive (S1, 4K/512, TPOT ≤ 50 ms) at concurrency 256: $0.0235–$0.0547 per 1M output tokens**
   (`low`–`high`); **max-throughput (S4, concurrency 1,024): $0.0195–$0.0455**. A 2-minute video costs
   **$0.00043–$0.00101**, i.e. **$0.43–$1.01 per 1,000 videos**.
5. **Confidence: `estimate`.** No published throughput measurement of Marlin-2B, or of any Qwen3.5-2B
   derivative, on B200 exists (§3). One genuinely new measured input is used: FlashQLA's
   `benchmark_results_GB200.txt` contains a **`2B/0.8B TP1` row at exactly this GDN geometry**
   (`h_qk = h_v = 16`, `d = 128`) — the file the architecture doc could not reach.

## 1. Fit

**Capacity is not the question for this pair, and this section exists to say so with numbers.**
Per METHODOLOGY §3 and §8, using B200 **as deployed: 180 GB** (not 192 — see
[gpus/b200.md §2](../../gpus/b200.md), "The 180 GB vs 192 GB question — resolved").

```
usable_hbm      = 180e9 × 0.90                   = 162.0 GB  = 150.87 GiB
per_gpu_weights = 4.426 GB (BF16 resident, tie honoured; 5.444 GB on disk, §1.2 below)
activation_ws   = 4 GiB  (METHODOLOGY §3 band 2–6 GB; 4 GiB matches architecture.md §8.5)
kv_budget       = 162.0 − 4.426 − 4.295          = 153.28 GB = 142.75 GiB per GPU
max_concurrency(ctx) = floor( kv_budget / (ctx × kv_bytes_per_token + 19,537,920) )
```

`kv_bytes_per_token` = **12,288 B BF16** / **6,144 B FP8** / **3,072 B NVFP4** — only the 6 gated-GQA
layers cache anything; the 18 Gated-DeltaNet layers cache **zero per token** and instead hold a
**18.63 MiB fixed state per sequence** (`S = 1` slot, no MTP draft to allocate for)
([architecture.md §5.1–§5.2](architecture.md)).

**Multi-node fabric needed? No.** Not at any GPU count, for any purpose. A 16-GPU deployment is two
HGX nodes running 16 unrelated replicas; nothing crosses the 0.8 TB/s scale-out fabric except the
load balancer's HTTP. NVLink 5's 1.8 TB/s per GPU is **entirely idle** for this pair — which is a
cost argument against B200-class silicon, not for it (§4).

### 1.2 Per-GPU concurrency at every context this model can reach

`est.` One replica, one B200:

```
  5,920 = 30s video        BF16 KV  1,660   FP8 KV  2,741   NVFP4 KV  4,063
  8K                       BF16 KV  1,275   FP8 KV  2,193   NVFP4 KV  3,428
  23,520 = 2-min+ video    BF16 KV    496   FP8 KV    934   NVFP4 KV  1,669
  32K                      BF16 KV    363   FP8 KV    693   NVFP4 KV  1,275
  128K                     BF16 KV     94   FP8 KV    185   NVFP4 KV    363
  262,144 native max       BF16 KV     47   FP8 KV     94   NVFP4 KV    185
  1M (out of spec)         BF16 KV     11   FP8 KV     23   NVFP4 KV     47
```

The BF16 column reproduces [architecture.md §8.5](architecture.md)'s `b200` row **exactly** at 23,520 (**496**), 32K
(363), 128K (94) and 262,144 (47). The 5,920 cell differs — **1,660 here vs 1,734 there** — and the
difference is *systematic*: re-deriving every GPU in that table's 5,920 column from its own stated
`kv_budget` and the same `ctx × 12,288 + 19,537,920` gives a uniform **+4.4 %** in the published
column (e.g. `a100` 685 derived vs 715 printed, ratio 1.044; `b200` 1,661 vs 1,734, ratio 1.044). So
that one column was produced with a slightly different context length, not with a per-row error.
Both are cited; **use 1,660**, which follows from METHODOLOGY §3 applied to ctx = 5,920. Immaterial
to every conclusion, since a video fleet lives in the 23,520 column where the two agree.

**Two context caveats that bound what these numbers mean.**

- **1M is out of spec.** `max_position_embeddings` is 262,144 with `rope_type: default` and no YaRN
  entry ([architecture.md §5.3](architecture.md)). The 1M row is arithmetic, not a deployable context.
- **Video never reaches 128K.** The 240-frame cap fixes every video request at **~23,560 prefill
  tokens** regardless of clip length ([architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)). The 128K and 262K rows are reachable
  only with text or image prompts. For a video fleet, **max concurrency is a constant, 496 (BF16 KV)
  or 934 (FP8 KV), per GPU** — not a distribution.

### 1.3 Which tensors cannot be sharded — stated explicitly, as required

At **TP1 the question is moot: nothing is sharded, everything is replicated.** For completeness, if
someone forced TP > 1 on this model, these are the hard limits (shapes from
[architecture.md §2.2/§2.4](architecture.md), read from the gated `config.json`):

| Tensor group | Shape fact | Shardable to |
|---|---|---|
| Full-attention K/V projections | `num_key_value_heads = 2` | **TP ≤ 2** before KV heads must be replicated |
| Full-attention Q / output gate | 8 heads × 256, `attn_output_gate: true` (q_proj emits 4096) | TP ≤ 8 |
| GDN `in_proj_qkv` / `in_proj_z` / `out_proj` | 16 key heads, 16 value heads × 128 | TP ≤ 16 |
| GDN `conv1d.weight` `[6144,1,4]` | depthwise conv, sharded **with** the channel split, not replicated |
| GDN `A_log`, `dt_bias` (16 each), `linear_attn.norm` (128) | per-head SSM scalars | **replicated on every rank** |
| `input_layernorm`, `post_attention_layernorm`, `model.norm`, `q_norm`, `k_norm` | 548,672 params total | **replicated on every rank** |
| `embed_tokens` / `lm_head` `[248320, 2048]` | 508.6 M params; `tie_word_embeddings: true` but **both tensors are materialised on disk** | vocab-parallel in principle; **1.017 GB of the 5.444 GB download is a duplicate** |
| Entire ViT (24 blocks, 331.4 M params) | vLLM's recommendation for every multimodal model here is **data-parallel encoder**, `--mm-encoder-tp-mode data` ([serving-optimizations.md §6.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)) | not TP |

The replicated tail (548,672 language params + whatever a rank keeps of the embedding) is
**0.02 % of the model**. Sharding a 4.4 GB model across a 180 GB GPU buys nothing and costs a
NCCL all-reduce on every one of 24 layers. **TP1. Always.**

### 3.3 Estimated TTFT — with a *measured* GDN kernel term

TTFT here = GEMM + full-attention roofline **plus the measured Gated-DeltaNet prefill kernel time**,
which a pure roofline misses entirely ([architecture.md §6.1](architecture.md): GDN is 0.5 % of the FLOPs but is
"bandwidth- and kernel-bound … not a roofline one"). The GDN term comes from
`benchmark/benchmark_results_GB200.txt` §3.4 below, 18 layers, linearly interpolated in `T`.
The **FlashInfer column is the one that matters** — it is what vLLM dispatches to on `sm_100`.

```
  scenario                  T tok  GEMM+attn ms  GDN FI ms  GDN FQLA ms  TTFT 0% ms  TTFT 90% ms
  S1 4K text                4,096          17.6        3.8          2.4        21.4          2.1
  S2 32K text              32,768         166.4       27.9          8.4       194.2         17.1
  S3 128K text            131,072        1017.2      111.5         33.7      1128.7         70.8
  30 s video (LLM only)     5,920          25.7        5.3          3.4        31.0          3.1
  2-min video (LLM only)   23,520         113.5       20.1          6.3       133.6         12.3
```

`est.` "TTFT 90 %" is a 90 % prefix-cache hit (10 % of tokens recomputed). **Print it, then ignore
it**: for Marlin the realistic hit rate is **~0 %** ([architecture.md §5.5](architecture.md)), because every request
carries a different video and only the ~40-token scaffold is shared. The 90 % column is what the
methodology asks for, not what this workload gets.

### 3.6 Sensitivities and the two levers that actually move this pair

```
  MBU 0.60 (plan)                                      out    71,040 tok/s/GPU  $/1M out low $0.0235

    batch  128: BF16 KV        14509 tok/s   FP8 KV        26565 tok/s   speedup 1.83x
    batch  256: BF16 KV        15212 tok/s   FP8 KV        29019 tok/s   speedup 1.91x
    batch  512: BF16 KV    infeasible (KV)   FP8 KV        30423 tok/s   speedup 1.95x

  Weight-format effect on decode (batch 1, video ctx 23,520) — bandwidth only:
    BF16 (the only shipped checkpoint)     TPOT 0.877 ms  (1,140 tok/s)
    FP8 lang linears + BF16 lm_head        TPOT 0.580 ms  (1,724 tok/s)
    INT4 W4A16 g128 (GPTQ ckpt exists)     TPOT 0.436 ms  (2,293 tok/s)
    NVFP4 (no checkpoint)                  TPOT 0.436 ms  (2,293 tok/s)
```

**FP8 KV is worth 1.83–1.95× at the video operating point and doubles concurrency. Weight
quantisation is worth 1.5–2.0× at batch 1 and ~nothing at batch ≥ 14**, where KV traffic overtakes
weight traffic ([architecture.md §6.2](architecture.md): the crossover is `3.7637 / 0.289014 = 13.02`).

**So the FA4-hd256-vs-FP8-KV trade resolves decisively.** Quantized KV forces the hd256 kernel back
to FA2 ([flash-attention.md §2.3](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)). But the hd256 kernel only touches **6 of 24 layers**, which
carry **13.3 % of prefill FLOPs at the video context** ([architecture.md §6.1](architecture.md)) and contribute nothing
to decode beyond the KV read that FP8 halves. **Take the FP8 KV; give up the kernel B200 was the
only GPU to offer.** ⚠️ Unmeasured, and the one experiment worth running first (§6).

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

**Single GPU is the unit. The node is a billing artefact.** One HGX B200 node = **8 independent
replicas** behind a load balancer. Multi-node means more replicas, never a larger model. The NVLink
5 domain, the 0.8 TB/s scale-out fabric and the four NVSwitch chips ([gpus/b200.md §4](../../gpus/b200.md)) are **100 %
idle** for this pair — which is the strongest argument that B200 is over-specified for Marlin-2B and
that the interesting comparison is per-replica $/video, not per-node capability.

Practical note: OCI, AWS and GCP sell B200 only in **8-GPU shapes** ([cloud-pricing.md §5.6](../../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md)); **Lambda
($6.99), RunPod Secure ($6.79) and Hyperstack ($6.00) sell single B200s**, so a 1–2 replica
deployment should go to a neocloud, not a hyperscaler, on availability grounds as well as price.

### 5.2 When PD disaggregation or wide-EP pays — it does not

| Technique | Verdict for this pair |
|---|---|
| **Wide-EP / DeepEP / EPLB** | **Never.** Dense model, zero experts ([architecture.md §2.2](architecture.md)). |
| **PD disaggregation** | **Never at this scale.** *"out above ~1000 GPUs; inward below — −20–30 % on small/untuned"* ([serving-optimizations.md §4.3](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). A 1-GPU replica has no prefill/decode pools to split, and the KV handover would cross PCIe to transfer a 0.287 GiB cache that is already resident. |
| **EPD (encoder/prefill/decode) disaggregation** | **The only one worth a thought, and still no.** SGLang splits encode from prefill with Mooncake, and NVIDIA measures *"30 % lower TTFT and 25 % higher throughput"* on image requests for Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B on GB200 under PD ([serving-optimizations.md §6.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). The ViT is 35 % of Marlin's prefill (§3.3), so the shape is right — but that is a *different model on a different topology*, and at 1 GPU per replica there is nowhere to send the encoder. ⚠️ Revisit only if a multi-GPU replica ever becomes necessary. |
| **DCP (decode context parallelism)** | **Never.** It exists for 128K+ MLA models where *"plain TP stops scaling around 680 tokens per second on eight B200s"* ([gpus/b200.md §5](../../gpus/b200.md)). TP1, and video context is 23.5K. |
| **Scale-out that does pay** | **More replicas, and GPU video decode.** M5's "more than double" is a bigger multiplier than any parallelism technique on this list. |

### 5.3 Launch commands

**vLLM** — the architecture remap is mandatory and documented API; `--hf-overrides` is a first-class
`ModelConfig` field and the supported-models page gives this exact shape for other models
[src](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models.html). B200-specific additions are
`--block-size 128` (the FA4 hd256 kernel requirement, [flash-attention.md §2.3](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)) and CUDA 13 for the
FlashInfer GDN prefill path ([flash-attention.md §14.3](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)):

```shell
vllm serve NemoStation/Marlin-2B --port 8000 \
  --hf-overrides '{"architectures": ["Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration"]}' \
  --tensor-parallel-size 1 \
  --max-model-len 32768 \
  --block-size 128 \
  --mamba-cache-mode=align \
  --mm-encoder-tp-mode data \
  --mm-processor-cache-type shm \
  --media-io-kwargs '{"video": {"num_frames": -1}}'
```

The four multimodal/Mamba flags are quoted from the vLLM Qwen3.5 recipe
[src](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/recipes/en/latest/Qwen/Qwen3.5.html); the remap and
`--media-io-kwargs` from [architecture.md §8.1/§8.6](architecture.md). **Add both EOS ids** (248044 **and** 248046 —
`config.eos_token_id` alone misses one, [architecture.md §1.3](architecture.md)) and strip the leading `<think>` yourself;
the remap discards `modeling_marlin.py`'s wrappers.

**GPU video decode** (the highest-value addition on B200, since the model work per clip is only
206 ms) — verbatim from the vLLM blog [src](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-09-18-pynvvideocodec), plus a
running CUDA MPS daemon:

```shell
  --mm-ipc-gpu-memory-gb 2 \
  --media-io-kwargs '{"video":{"backend":"pynvvideocodec","min_frames":16,"max_frames":16,"hw_decoders":2}}'
```

⚠️ Those `min_frames`/`max_frames: 16` are the blog's values for an 8 B captioner, **not** Marlin's
Path A budget (`FPS 2.0`, `FPS_MAX_FRAMES 240`, `VIDEO_MAX_PIXELS 200704`, [architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)).
Substituting them would silently give the model 16 frames instead of 240. **Note also
CVE-2026-69147** — *"Request-selected PyNvVideoCodec GPU decode bypasses static VRAM reservation"*
[src](https://advisories.gitlab.com/pypi/vllm/CVE-2026-69147/) — so pin a patched vLLM before
exposing `media_io_kwargs` to untrusted callers.

**transformers** (the only first-class path, and what the author ships) — verbatim from
[architecture.md §8.6](architecture.md) / `MODEL_CARD.md`:

```python
marlin = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(
    "NemoStation/Marlin-2B", trust_remote_code=True,
    dtype=torch.bfloat16, device_map={"": "cuda"})
marlin.compile()
result = marlin.caption("video.mp4")   # max_new_tokens=2048
```

**Container:** NVIDIA NGC **vLLM release 26.08** is the current NVIDIA-packaged vLLM
[src](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/frameworks/vllm-release-notes/rel-26-08.html); vLLM
stable is **v0.29.0** (2026-09-09), SGLang **v0.5.20** (2026-09-18), FlashInfer **v0.6.18.post1**
([gpus/b200.md §7](../../gpus/b200.md)). *"NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs (B200, GB200) require a minimum of CUDA 12.8"*
[src](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/getting_started/installation/gpu.html) — but **CUDA 13 is what
you want**, for the FlashInfer GDN prefill kernel.

### 5.4 Warm-up and loading

`est.` Weights are **5.444 GB on disk** (including the 1.017 GB duplicated `lm_head`,
[architecture.md §1.2](architecture.md)):

| Stage | Time | Note |
|---|---|---|
| NVMe → host, at 5 GB/s | **1.09 s** | cold page cache |
| NVMe → host, at 20 GB/s | **0.27 s** | Gen5 NVMe array; DGX B200 carries 8× NICs and local NVMe ([gpus/b200.md §4](../../gpus/b200.md)) |
| Host → HBM over PCIe Gen5 ×16 (128 GB/s) | **0.04 s** | never the bottleneck |
| **Weight load subtotal** | **0.3–1.2 s** | trivially small; this model is not a loading-time problem |
| CUDA context + engine init | ~10–30 s ⚠️ | not measured for this pair |
| **FlashInfer GDN JIT warm-up** | ⚠️ unquantified | vLLM's GDN prefill resolver emits a **JIT warm-up warning** on the FlashInfer path ([flash-attention.md §14.3](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md)); DS-V4.1's B200 recipe goes as far as disabling FlashInfer autotune, JIT and CuTeDSL warmup via `--kernel-config` ([inference-engines.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)) |
| CUDA graph capture | ~10–60 s ⚠️ | and the `causal_conv1d_update` / mamba-cache-size assertion lives here ([architecture.md §8.7](architecture.md)) |

**Loading is 0.3–1.2 s; warm-up is one to two orders of magnitude more.** Size replica churn against
JIT and graph capture, not against the 5.4 GB download. If a third party's dequantised or remapped
copy is used, prefer `prasannaJagadesh/marlin-2B-GPTQ-4BITS` **only** for its already-correct
`architectures` field — its INT4 weights are the wrong choice on B200 ([gpus/b200.md §6](../../gpus/b200.md)) and its
accuracy is unmeasured ([architecture.md §9](architecture.md)).

## 6. Risks and open questions

### 6.1 Blocking — resolve before committing B200 capacity

1. ⚠️ **Does Blackwell buy anything at all for this model?** §3.4 measures the GDN prefill kernel —
   **18 of 24 layers** — at **1.00–1.16× of H200**, and one row at **0.69× (slower)**. M6 independently
   shows B200 ≈ H200 on QwQ-32B in vLLM. Against `h100` at `$3.20` and `h200` at `$3.99`
   ([cloud-pricing.md §5.14](../../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md)) versus `b200` at `$6.00`, a 1.0–1.2× kernel speedup on most of the
   model is **a cost regression**. **Method:** benchmark one 2-minute clip end-to-end on B200 and
   H200 with identical vLLM builds before signing anything.
2. ⚠️ **FA4 hd256 vs FP8 KV — which wins?** §3.5 argues FP8 KV by a wide margin (1.91× decode,
   +88 % concurrency, against a kernel that touches 6 of 24 layers and 13.3 % of prefill FLOPs) but
   **neither side is measured**. **Method:** two runs, `--kv-cache-dtype auto --block-size 128` vs
   `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3`, and compare end-to-end videos/hour.
3. ⚠️ **Path A vs Path B video token budget — 23,520 vs 12,288 tokens, a 1.914× gap.**
   `qwen-vl-utils` bypasses the HF processor's `size`, so serving through vLLM/SGLang likely gives
   the model **half the frames or half the resolution** it was trained on — a silent quality
   regression, not an error ([architecture.md §6.3](architecture.md)). **Every throughput and cost number in this
   document assumes Path A.** At Path B they roughly halve, and the model gets worse. This is the
   highest-leverage open question in the whole pair and B200 does not change it.
4. ⚠️ **Does the checkpoint actually load under `--hf-overrides`?** All 618 tensor names must map
   onto vLLM's `Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration` loader, and vLLM must handle the materialised
   `lm_head.weight` against `tie_word_embeddings: true` ([architecture.md §8.1](architecture.md), open question 2).
   Evidence is strong (pure subclass, unmodified forward, third party already ships the remap) but
   nobody has run it.
5. ⚠️ **Video decode may bottleneck before the GPU.** torchcodec throughput for 240 frames at
   448×448 is unmeasured everywhere ([architecture.md §10.3](architecture.md), [serving-optimizations.md §6.3](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). On B200 the
   *model* work is 206 ms per clip, so the decode side has **less** headroom than on the H100 where
   M5 was measured, not more.

### 6.2 Unmeasured inputs carried into the numbers

6. ⚠️ **No FA4 hd256 TFLOPS figure exists** on any GPU. M2's 1,613 TFLOPS is hd128. The dedicated
   `BlackwellFusedMultiHeadAttentionForward` kernel is B200's one Marlin-specific advantage and its
   performance is entirely unpublished.
7. ⚠️ **MBU 0.60 is a plan.** The only B200 measurement (M3) is 46–47 %, on a 70 B model with a
   39.4 GB step. A 2 B model's 0.83 ms step across ~170 kernels is a regime nobody has measured;
   it could be materially worse.
8. ⚠️ **MFU 0.40 for `hidden 2048` prefill GEMMs on B200** is unsupported by any measurement.
9. ⚠️ **7.7 vs 8.0 TB/s** — Lenovo SKU sheet vs NVIDIA DGX page, unretracted on both sides
   ([gpus/b200.md §2](../../gpus/b200.md)). 7.7 used; +3.9 % at 8.0.
10. ⚠️ **GB200 ≠ HGX B200.** M1's GDN numbers are from a GB200 superchip (2,500 TFLOPS BF16, 186 GB).
    Scaling to HGX B200 by 2250/2500 = **1.11×** is a planning assumption, not a measurement.
11. ⚠️ **NVFP4 KV at `head_dim 256`** — FlashInfer exposes `nvfp4_kv_cache_full_dim` and vLLM lists
    `nvfp4` as a first-class KV dtype, but nothing establishes support at 256-wide heads
    ([architecture.md §5.4](architecture.md), [quantization-formats.md §8.1](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)).
12. ⚠️ **vLLM's actual GDN dispatch.** Whether vLLM/SGLang call FlashQLA, FlashInfer or their own
    Triton kernels for this head config is unconfirmed; SGLang carries `triton_gdn_fused_proj` and an
    `SGLANG_ENABLE_GDN_DECODE_FUSED_PROJ_CONV` env flag and may use neither
    ([architecture.md §8.3](architecture.md)). §3.3 assumes FlashInfer.
13. ⚠️ **Whether any provider hosts Marlin-2B**, and at what price — no vendor API anchor exists
    (§4.6).
14. ⚠️ **vLLM conv-state width.** Whether vLLM stores the GDN conv state at `kernel` or `kernel − 1`
    (18.84 vs 18.63 MiB/seq, +1.1 %) and whether `--mamba-ssm-cache-dtype bfloat16` is honoured
    (would halve the state to 9.63 MiB) ([architecture.md §5.2](architecture.md)). Immaterial to every conclusion here.
15. ⚠️ **No B200 numbers for GPU video decode** (M5 is 8×H100).
16. ⚠️ **SGLang video ingestion for this class is unconfirmed** ([architecture.md §8.4](architecture.md)); TensorRT-LLM
    has no evidence of Qwen3.5-hybrid support at all.

### 6.4 Accuracy caveats of the executed precision

**BF16 is executed, so there is no quantisation accuracy risk in the recommended configuration** —
the one genuinely comfortable statement in this document. Two adjacent caveats:

- **FP8 KV** (§3.5's recommended lever) carries *"at most 1-2 points of accuracy degradation"* on
  reasoning tasks and 94–98 % of baseline long-context AUC ([quantization-formats.md §8.2](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)) — measured on
  *other* models. For a model whose product is **second-precise timestamps**, numeric error landing
  on the timestamp digits is a specific, plausible, **entirely unmeasured** risk. Calibrate scales
  with `llm-compressor` rather than accepting the default all-1.0 scales
  ([quantization-formats.md §8.3](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)), and run TimeLens-Bench before and after.
- **Every quantised Marlin variant is unvalidated.** *"Not one repo reports a CaReBench / DREAM-1K /
  TimeLens score, a perplexity delta or any accuracy measurement"* ([architecture.md §9](architecture.md)). The INT4
  GPTQ build is the wrong choice on B200 anyway.
- The model's own quality claims are **vendor marketing with no numeric table**, `model-index: null`,
  no third-party reproduction, and ActivityNet/Charades/TimeLens appearing in **both** the training
  and evaluation lists with no stated contamination control ([architecture.md §10.1](architecture.md)).
