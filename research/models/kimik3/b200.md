## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, but never on one node.** 1,560.9 GB of weights against
   180 GB/GPU as deployed means **16× B200 across two HGX nodes is the
   minimum** — vLLM ships a `verified` 2-node B200 recipe (`vllm/vllm-openai:latest`,
   min **v0.27.1**, current **v0.29.0**), SGLang ships a B200 2×8 cell that is
   still `verificationStatus: in-progress`, and TRT-LLM says K3 is "functionally
   supported … and covered by unit tests in CI" on SM100 but validated on GB200/GB300.
2. **Min 16 GPUs / 2 nodes; recommended 16 as `PP2 × TP8 + DCP8 + EP8`** (SGLang
   Balanced) or `TP16` (vLLM `multi_node_tp`, and the shape DSPARK forces).
   Above that, scale by replicas, not by widening TP across the 0.8 TB/s fabric.
3. **Weight format actually executed: native MXFP4 W4A8** via the FlashInfer
   trtllm-gen SiTU MoE runner on `sm_100` — no dequantisation, no requant. Only
   46.7 % of the *active* parameters are MXFP4, so the FP4 tensor cores cap the
   achievable speedup at **1.87×**, not 4×.
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $16.34 – $38.78 per 1M output tokens**
   without speculation (concurrency 64, TPOT 39.2 ms), falling to
   **$2.63 – $6.24** with DSPARK at the highest SLO-feasible concurrency.
   Max-throughput (S4): **$3.81 – $9.05** NOSPEC, **$2.63 – $6.24** with DSPARK.
   Price band = Hyperstack $6.00 → AWS `p6-b200.48xlarge` $14.242 per GPU-hour.
5. **Confidence: `estimate`, calibrated against measurement.** Seven InferenceX
   rows exist for *this exact pair* (16× B200, 2026-09-12) and the roofline is
   anchored to them; but no public fixed-ISL/OSL sweep exists on B200, and
   SGLang's B200 cells carry **no published speed cells at all**.

### 1.2 Fit over the GPU counts this topology allows

HGX B200's NVLink domain is **8 GPUs**; beyond that every collective crosses the
**0.8 TB/s per-node** scale-out fabric ([gpus/b200.md §4](../../gpus/b200.md)).
The allowed set is therefore {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, …} with 8 as the island size.
`usable = 180 GB × 0.90`, `activation_ws` 4 GB at ≤ 32 K and 6 GB at ≥ 128 K
(METHODOLOGY §3).

**8× B200 does not fit, and not marginally.** 1,560.9 GB ÷ 8 = 195.1 GB/GPU of
weights plus 1.19 GB of replicated tensors = **196.3 GB against 180 GB of
physical HBM** — the checkpoint overflows the card by 16 GB before a single KV
page or activation buffer. No `--gpu-memory-utilization` value rescues it; no
offload path is shipped for K3's MoE. This matches vLLM's own statement:
*"At least one 8× B300 (or GB300 NVL72) node is required; **16× B200 is also
supported**"* ([vLLM K3 blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-07-27-k3)). It also
matches TRT-LLM: DEP16 needs 210 GB/rank and TEP8 213 GB/rank (both > 180), while
**TEP16 needs 115 GB/rank and fits**
([TRT-LLM K3 deployment guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/main/docs/source/deployment-guide/deployment-guide-for-kimi-k3-on-trtllm.md)).

**Multi-node fabric: mandatory at every usable GPU count.** 16 GPUs = 2 HGX
nodes joined over InfiniBand/Ethernet (DGX B200: 8× ConnectX-7 @ 400 Gb/s + 2×
dual-port BlueField-3). Both engines require the cross-node NIC pinned by hand:
vLLM sets `GLOO_SOCKET_IFNAME`/`NCCL_SOCKET_IFNAME` in the B200 recipe env;
SGLang adds `SGLANG_HOST_IP` and, on RDMA fabrics, `NCCL_IB_HCA`. B200 has **no
MNNVL**, so the GB200/GB300 flags (`NCCL_MNNVL_ENABLE=1`, `NCCL_NVLS_ENABLE=1`)
do not apply here.

### 1.3 Per-request aggregate cost, and what replication does to it

```
Per-request aggregate bytes (FP8 KV, S=5), by shape:
  PP2 x TP8 + DCP8 + EP8 (SGLang Balanced)   KV replication x 1.0 | 8K 2.360 GB | 128K  4.059 GB | 1M  16.742 GB
  PP2 x TP8, no DCP (SGLang Low-Latency)     KV replication x 8.0 | 8K 3.153 GB | 128K 16.742 GB | 1M 118.211 GB
  flat TP16 (vLLM multi_node_tp / DSPARK)    KV replication x16.0 | 8K 4.059 GB | 128K 31.238 GB | 1M 234.175 GB
  flat TP16 + DCP16                          KV replication x 1.0 | 8K 2.360 GB | 128K  4.059 GB | 1M  16.742 GB
```

**DCP is not an optimisation on B200, it is the difference between serving 128 K
context and not serving it.** Flat TP16 without DCP replicates the MLA KV on all
16 ranks: 31.2 GB per 128 K request, capping the node at **29 concurrent
requests**. Add `--dcp-size 16` and the same node holds **225**. SGLang's own
quantification of the trade is *"concurrency ceiling +72 % at the same engine
throughput, ~1.8× ITL"* — measured on B300, ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED** on B200.

### 1.4 What cannot be sharded

| Tensor group | Bytes | Shardable? |
|---|---:|---|
| Routed experts (MXFP4) | 1,446.5 GB | **Yes** — EP or TP; the whole point of the shape |
| Attention (KDA + MLA + `g_proj`/`o_proj`) | 72.4 GB | Yes — head-parallel, but only up to `n_heads = 96` |
| Shared experts, latent up/down projections, dense layer 0 | 35.2 GB | Yes (TP); **replicated under DP-attention/DEP** |
| `embed_tokens` + `lm_head` | 4.70 GB | Yes — vocab-parallel; engines often replicate `embed_tokens` |
| **Router `gate.weight` (92 × 896 × 7168, BF16)** | **1.181 GB** | **No.** Every rank must score all 896 experts |
| **Router `e_score_correction_bias` (F32)** | 0.33 MB | **No** |
| **All RMSNorm weights + the 187 rank-1 AttnRes projections** | 8.04 MB | **No** |
| **`routed_expert_norm` (92 × 3584)** | 0.66 MB | **No** |
| **KDA recurrent state** | 428.6 MiB × S per request | **Only by attention-TP width.** SGLang: *"DP, EP, and DCP do not shard it"* |
| **MLA KV** | 13,824 B/token FP8 | **Only by DCP.** TP replicates it |

Replicated total = **1.191 GB per rank** — small, but it is why `--data-parallel`
shapes are expensive here: under DEP the BF16 non-expert block (114 GB) joins
that list, which is exactly why TRT-LLM's DEP16 needs 210 GB/rank and does not
fit a B200.

### 1.5 The state pool, not the KV cache, sets concurrency below ~163 K context

```
State-vs-KV crossover (aggregate, S=5):
  FP8 KV : 162,533 tokens   BF16 KV : 81,267 tokens
  With radix cache off (S=1): FP8 32,507 tokens

S-slot sensitivity at 16 GPUs, PP2xTP8+DCP8, FP8 KV, 8K ctx:
  S= 5 extra_buffer (default)     fp32 state ->  401 concurrent | --mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16 ->  766
  S= 4 extra_buffer_lazy          fp32 state ->  496 concurrent | --mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16 ->  936
  S= 3 no_buffer                  fp32 state ->  648 concurrent | --mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16 -> 1204
  S= 1 radix off / PD-decode      fp32 state -> 1684 concurrent | --mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16 -> 2805
  S=13 DSPARK D=8 extra slots     fp32 state ->  159 concurrent | --mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16 ->  312
```

At 8 K context, **95 % of a request's memory is fixed KDA state**, and the two
knobs that matter are `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16` (1.9×) and
`--mamba-radix-cache-strategy extra_buffer_lazy` (1.24×) — together **2.3×** the
admitted concurrency, against FP8 KV's 1.05× at that context. SGLang's one-liner
holds on B200 exactly as on B300: *"`bfloat16` state buys admission, `fp8` KV
buys context."* Note the DSPARK row: naively, block 7 adds D = 8 slots and cuts
admission from 401 to **159**; `--enable-linear-replayssm-spec` folds them into a
per-slot ring and returns D → 0.

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.1 Assumptions (METHODOLOGY §4), and how they were calibrated

**MBU.** METHODOLOGY's planning band for first-generation Blackwell software is
0.5–0.7. That band is **too optimistic for this pair** and the measurement says
so. The InferenceX B200 NOSPEC row (concurrency 96, 2026-09-12) implies:

```
bytes/step = 112.0 GB non-expert + 737.1 distinct experts x 17.55 MB x 92 layers
           + 96 x 103,689 tok x 13,824 B x 8 (TP-replicated, decode_dcp_size = 1)
           + 96 x 449,372,160 B state
           = 2,446 GB
aggregate BW = 2,446 GB / 83.76 ms = 29.20 TB/s over 16 GPUs = 1.825 TB/s per GPU
MBU = 1.825 / 7.7 = 23.7 %
```

The comparable B300 figure from [architecture.md §6.4](architecture.md) is
~44.5 %, so **B200 runs this model at 0.53× B300's MBU** — the cross-node PP2 hop
over a 0.8 TB/s fabric is the difference. Cross-check on the concurrency-1 DSPARK
row: at vLLM's published macro-average acceptance of 4.11 it implies **12.1 %**,
against 0.53 × 25.4 % = 13.5 % predicted. Close enough to adopt. The MBU ramp
used below is therefore `0.53 × B300`:

| batch | 1 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 256 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B300 implied (arch §6.4) | 25.4 % | 35.0 % | 38.8 % | 42.0 % | 44.3 % | 45.2 % | 46.0 % |
| **B200 used here `est.`** | **13.5 %** | **18.6 %** | **20.6 %** | **22.3 %** | **23.5 %** | **24.0 %** | **24.4 %** |

**MFU.** FP4 MoE GEMM 0.13 (the 11–14 % of peak measured on B200), BF16 0.35
(METHODOLOGY's upper band), MLA attention at **1,400 TFLOPS** (FlashMLA's
measured 1,460 TFLOPS dense MHA prefill on B200), ×1.15 for TP8 + PP2 comm.
The result is then multiplied by **1.757** so that the model reproduces SGLang's
**measured** GB200 TEP16 prefill (2,407 tok/s/GPU at ISL 8192, concurrency 32)
scaled to B200's dense peak (2,250/2,500) = 2,166 tok/s/GPU. The raw roofline is
1.76× optimistic; that calibration factor is the honest gap between the roofline
and a 93-layer model's real small-GEMM behaviour.

**TTFT model.** `TTFT(1) = prompt / aggregate_prefill_tok_s`; for `c > 1`,
`TTFT(c) = 0.55 × c × TTFT(1)`, where the 0.55 is calibrated on SGLang's B300
cells (predicted 16× and 64× vs measured 9.4× and 30.8× growth). ⚠️ `est.`

### 3.3 Speculative decoding on B200

Decode is deeply bandwidth-bound at every feasible batch, so verify positions are
close to free on the FLOP side:

```
  b=   1: t_bw    8.34 ms vs t_flops   0.01 ms  -> ratio 0.002  (verify x8 FLOPs would be   0.11 ms)
  b=   8: t_bw   13.57 ms vs t_flops   0.11 ms  -> ratio 0.008  (verify x8 FLOPs would be   0.86 ms)
  b=  32: t_bw   27.79 ms vs t_flops   0.43 ms  -> ratio 0.016  (verify x8 FLOPs would be   3.45 ms)
  b=  64: t_bw   39.21 ms vs t_flops   0.86 ms  -> ratio 0.022  (verify x8 FLOPs would be   6.90 ms)
  b= 128: t_bw   50.12 ms vs t_flops   1.73 ms  -> ratio 0.034  (verify x8 FLOPs would be  13.80 ms)
  b= 256: t_bw   55.72 ms vs t_flops   3.45 ms  -> ratio 0.062  (verify x8 FLOPs would be  27.61 ms)
```

That says the *compute* cost of verification never binds on B200. What does bind
is the extra **bytes**: the draft weights (4.50 GB RadixArk / 9.49 GB RedHatAI)
are replicated on every rank, so the aggregate weight read per step grows by
**+72 GB** (RadixArk), and the draft KV at ~1,400 B/token is likewise replicated
(**5.9 GB/step** at 4 K, batch 64). The published end-to-end decay on B300
(3.00× → 1.97× → 1.64× at conc 1/16/64) is therefore the planning curve, applied
here as `est.`:

```
  b=   1: NOSPEC TPOT   8.34 ms /   119.9 agg tok/s  ->  DSPARK (x3.00 est.) TPOT   2.78 ms /   359.7 agg tok/s
  b=   8: NOSPEC TPOT  13.57 ms /   589.4 agg tok/s  ->  DSPARK (x1.97 est.) TPOT   6.89 ms /  1161.0 agg tok/s
  b=  32: NOSPEC TPOT  27.79 ms /  1151.7 agg tok/s  ->  DSPARK (x1.64 est.) TPOT  16.94 ms /  1888.7 agg tok/s
  b=  64: NOSPEC TPOT  39.21 ms /  1632.1 agg tok/s  ->  DSPARK (x1.64 est.) TPOT  23.91 ms /  2676.6 agg tok/s

### 3.5 Why estimate and measurement differ

1. **Context, not concurrency, is the main gap.** The measured B200 rows run
   agentic traces averaging **104 K–239 K input tokens** with 88–94 % prefix-cache
   hits, against §3.2's fixed 4 K / 32 K / 128 K. Their `total_tput_tps` of
   145,164 (9,073/GPU) is dominated by *cached* input tokens, which cost a KV
   load, not a prefill. Comparing the output columns is the only apples-to-apples
   read.
2. **The measured runs have no decode DCP** (`decode_dcp_size: 1`), so their KV
   read is TP-replicated ×8. That is exactly the §3.2 "without DCP" grid, and it
   is where the model was calibrated — hence agreement is by construction, not by
   accident. The DCP grid is an extrapolation and carries ⚠️.
3. **The estimate assumes a uniformly-full batch.** InferenceX's
   `gpu_kv_cache_usage_pct` runs 3–60 %, and `mean_qps` is 1.39 — the real
   running batch is below the nominal concurrency, which flatters measured TPOT
   and depresses measured throughput relative to a saturated synthetic sweep.
4. **Power, as a reality check.** Measured `avg_power_w` is **372–760 W per GPU**
   against a 1,000 W TGP. The part is never compute-saturated on this model —
   consistent with an MBU of 24 % and an MFU under 3 %, and with §3.3's finding
   that verify FLOPs are free.
5. **Engine-version variance dwarfs everything above.** SGLang 0.5.5 → 0.5.6 moved
   B200 DeepSeek-R1 by up to **1.79×**; vLLM's own K3 optimisation pass moved
   throughput **+120 % to +181 %** and TTFT **−72 % to −85 %** between v0.27.1 and
   main on a B300 node. Any number here without an exact engine version is worth
   little.

factors are B300 measurements applied to B200; the B200 DSpark acceptance is
unpublished.

effective input cost falls to **$0.146/1M** at Hyperstack (0.9 × 0.1 + 0.1 × 1.0
of the uncached $0.770). The measured B200 runs sustain **87.6 – 94.1 %** GPU
cache hit on agentic traces, and OpenRouter reports a **92 % cache hit rate on
Kimi K3 traffic**, so the high-hit column is the realistic one for agentic work.
`--prefix-match-unit 128` is what makes that hit rate achievable on a hybrid
model; without it the hit boundary is the inflated Mamba page.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

Single node is not an option (§1.2). The decision is between **2-node shapes**:

| Shape | GPUs | Best for | Why |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `PP2 × TP8` (SGLang Low-Latency) | 16 | lowest TPOT at low concurrency | Keeps every per-layer collective inside one 1.8 TB/s NVLink island; only activations cross the fabric, once per token |
| **`PP2 × TP8 + DCP8 + EP8`** (SGLang Balanced) | **16** | **the default recommendation** | Adds the only axis that shards the TP-replicated MLA KV. 225 concurrent at 128 K vs 54 without |
| `TP16` (vLLM `multi_node_tp`; forced by DSPARK) | 16 | speculative decoding | DSPARK requires `pp_size == 1`; SGLang's `specCollapsePp` re-lays the same 16 GPUs flat. Costs every layer's all-reduce a fabric crossing |
| `TP1 × PP16` deep PP | 16 | **prefill role only** | *"deep PP wins at the Default operating point too"* on 16-GPU platforms — 4,550 vs 1,652 prefill tok/s/GPU on GB200. Also *"`--tp-size 1` is what buys context"* |
| `TP32/EP32 + DP4 + DCP8` Peak Throughput | 32 (4 nodes) | maximum sustained TPS | 1,464 concurrent at 8 K; wide-EP crosses the fabric, so pair with `--moe-a2a-backend megamoe` **only within nodes** |

**When PD disaggregation pays.** At ISL 8192 the B300 data shows TTFT growing
from 378 ms (conc 1) to 11.6 s (conc 64) — the node is prefill-saturated long
before decode is. On B200 the same effect is worse, because 16 GPUs at ~2,166
prefill tok/s/GPU is only 34,647 tok/s aggregate. Split the roles the moment your
p90 TTFT matters: a `TP1 × PP16` prefill pool at 4,550 tok/s/GPU feeding a
`PP2 × TP8 + DCP8` decode pool is a **2.1× prefill uplift** over running Unified.
SGLang's B200 decode role keeps `--pp-size 2` and notes the trade explicitly:
*"That prefill delivers 2,919 prefill tok/s/GPU against PP16's 4,550, which is
the trade for the decode-side latency."*

**When wide-EP pays: it does not, on B200.** HGX B200's NVLink domain stops at 8
GPUs and the scale-out fabric is 0.8 TB/s per node — half B300's 1.6 TB/s. Keep
`--ep-size ≤ 8` and scale with replicas. Wide-EP is a GB200/GB300 NVL72 play.

### 5.3 Warm-up and loading

```
  NVMe    5 GB/s: whole checkpoint    5.2 min; sharded over 2 nodes (2.6 min/node); per-GPU share 20 s
  NVMe   10 GB/s: whole checkpoint    2.6 min; sharded over 2 nodes (1.3 min/node); per-GPU share 5-10 s

/ `--no-enable-flashinfer-autotune` — an hour-long ready timeout is the engines'

replica on warm local NVMe, and an hour on a first pull. The 96 shards are
17.0 GB each (min 92 MB, first 2.34 GB, last 802 MB), so parallel reads scale
well across 16 ranks.

## 6. Risks and open questions

**Corrections this document makes to sibling docs**

1. **⚠️ [`architecture.md` §7.6](architecture.md) says "DSPARK is not offered on
   B200 in the Deploy matrix". That is wrong.** The cookbook's disable rule is
   `disabled: s => config.isPipelined(s) && !config.specCollapses(s)`, and the
   B200 cells carry `specCollapsePp: true`, so `specCollapses` is true and DSPARK
   is **enabled** — it collapses `PP2 × TP8 → TP16` and `PP2 × DCPEP8 → DCPEP16`.
   The prose says so too: *"Spec Decode — layers onto the strategy without
   changing it, on every platform except B200"* — i.e. on B200 it *does* change
   the strategy, not that it is unavailable. `spec` even defaults to `dspark`.
2. **⚠️ SGLang's own hardware catalogue labels B200 `vram: 192GB`**, while
   vLLM's hardware profile for the same model says **180 GB / `vram_gb: 1440`**
   and METHODOLOGY §8 pins 180. Sizing off 192 overstates the node by 192 GB.
   Third-party write-ups citing "16× B200 = 3,072 GB" are using the nameplate;
   the real figure is **2,880 GB**, and headroom after weights is **1,319 GB**,
   not 1,511 GB.

**Open and ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED**

3. **No SGLang B200 speed cell exists.** All three B200 cells are
   `verificationStatus: "in-progress"`; the `benchmarks` array has B200 match
   entries with no `speed` payload. The cookbook's blanket caveat applies in
   full: *"Outside the two verified B300 1×8 Unified cells, no cell has a serving
   round in this exact shape — treat those as starting points to verify."* And:
   *"Accuracy has not been re-measured on any cell."*
4. **No fixed-ISL/OSL public sweep on B200.** Every measured row is InferenceX
   `agentic_traces` with variable lengths and 88–94 % prefix-cache hits. The
   METHODOLOGY §4 grid at 4 K / 32 K / 128 K × batch 1–256 cannot be filled from
   published data — only modelled.
5. **The measured DSPARK acceptance on B200 is unpublished**, and InferenceX
   labels those rows `spec_method: "mtp"` for a model with
   `num_nextn_predict_layers: 0`. The 1.45–1.64× decay factors in §3.3 and §4.3
   are B300 numbers carried across, and B300's own DSPARK cells pin acceptance at
   `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=4.5`.
6. **Open vLLM bug on B200: weight-loading memory is not reclaimed.**
   [vllm-project/vllm#57440](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57440)
   (vLLM v0.28.0, 2–4 nodes × 8 B200 180 GB, `--load-format fastsafetensors`,
   `--enable-expert-parallel`, DeepEP v2): after weight loading **40.626 GiB is
   stranded** in PyTorch's caching allocator and driver-free memory falls to
   **5.537 GiB**; after repacking only **0.494 GiB** remains free against
   **45.611 GiB** cached-but-unallocated. A `gc.collect(); torch.cuda.empty_cache()`
   reclaims **45.483 GiB**. Until PR #57516 lands, the reported workaround is
   `--max-num-seqs 32 --max-num-batched-tokens 512 --gpu-memory-utilization 0.80`,
   which destroys throughput. **Issue is open.** Validate your own build before
   trusting §1's KV budget.
7. **vLLM does not offer DCP on B200.** `decode_context_parallelism` is declared
   for `single_node_tp` only. If you need long-context concurrency on B200 and
   vLLM, you are on the ×8/×16 replicated-KV grid of §3.2, or you use SGLang.
   ⚠️ whether a `multi_node_tp_pp` + DCP combination works is untested here.
8. **DCP's published benefit (+72 % concurrency, ~1.8× ITL) is a B300
   measurement.** The B200 fit table assumes DCP shards perfectly; the ITL cost is
   not modelled.
9. **NVFP4 on B200 is unmeasured.** On B300 the NVFP4 build is slower than MXFP4
   without spec and a wash with it, costs +49 GB on disk, and the only published
   accuracy datapoint is RedHat's GPQA Diamond **93.5 → 91.0 (97.33 % recovery)**.
   There is no B200 number at all. The native MXFP4 checkpoint is the default for
   a reason; do not switch without measuring.
10. **Accuracy of the executed precision.** MXFP4 here is **quantization-aware
    training from the SFT stage onward**, so no BF16 reference checkpoint exists
    and "quantisation loss" is not measurable in the usual sense. What *is*
    measured on B200 is GSM8K **0.9674–0.9727** against the model card's 0.976 —
    within ~0.9 points, with or without speculation. No B200 measurement exists
    for GPQA, HLE, OCRBench or any long-context eval.
11. **AttnRes has no hand-written Blackwell kernel path documented.** SGLang sets
    `SGLANG_K3_ATTN_RES_MODE=jit` on **every Hopper cell** and on **no Blackwell
    cell**, which implies a native path exists on `sm_100` — but no source states
    that, and no kernel measurement for AttnRes on B200 was found. ⚠️
12. **FlashInfer autotune is disabled in both B200 recipes**
    (`--disable-flashinfer-autotune` / `--no-enable-flashinfer-autotune`). This is
    leaving performance on the table for stability reasons; autotune crashed on
    SM100 NVFP4 MoE for a release cycle and was re-enabled only in the 26.08
    container. Re-test with autotune on before accepting §3's numbers as a
    ceiling.
13. **`--moe-a2a-backend megamoe` is NVLink-only.** *"DeepGEMM MegaMoE is not
    compatible with cross-node RDMA"* — so on a 2-node B200 the a2a backend must
    stay inside each node, which constrains EP to 8.
14. **HiCache L3 silently drops DCP.** On B200 that converts a 225-concurrent
    128 K deployment into a 54-concurrent one. There is no warning at runtime
    beyond the cookbook's hint text.
15. **`mlx5dv_reg_dmabuf_mr` errno 524 → "NCCL error: unhandled system error"**
    on multi-node K3; set `NCCL_DMABUF_ENABLE=0` to fall back to `nvidia_peermem`
    (which must be loaded). RDMA KV transfer needs `UCX_TLS="rc,cuda_copy"`.
16. **Known model defect, not hardware:** *"K3 occasionally emit a tool-call
    format its own parser doesn't expect. Suggest to run do schema validation and
    retry"* — budget a retry path.
17. **Video is not servable.** The preprocessor carries full video config and the
    card reports Video-MME 90.0, but *"the open-source K3 serving contract
    currently supports image input only — its processor rejects video and audio
    input."*
18. **Power and cooling.** Measured `avg_power_w` on B200 under K3 is
    372–760 W/GPU against a 1,000 W TGP, i.e. ~14.3 kW per node nameplate but
    well under it in practice on this workload. ⚠️ no public measurement of B200
    clock-throttling under sustained FP4 load exists.
19. **NVIDIA Dynamo's own Kimi-K3 recipe lists H200, GB200 and GB300 — not
    B200** — yet InferenceX's CI runs `dynamo-vllm` on b200 daily. ⚠️ whether the
    B200 path is supported or merely exercised is unresolved.
20. **Allocation, not price, is the binding constraint on B200 in 2026**
    (multiple sources; Crusoe lists B200 as "contact sales", Azure sells no HGX
    B200 at all, GCP's A4 has no on-demand rate). A 2-node reservation is a
    procurement problem before it is an engineering one.
