## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, and measured.** Both vLLM (`0.27.1+`, `vllm/vllm-openai:latest`, `multi_node_tp`) and SGLang (`v0.5.17+`; the published H200 measurements are on the `lmsysorg/sglang:kimi-k3` image ≈ v0.5.16) serve `moonshotai/Kimi-K3` on H200; TensorRT-LLM does **not** (SM100 only).
2. **Minimum 16 GPUs (2 × HGX 8-GPU nodes), recommended 16 at TP16 + EP16**; 32 (TP32/EP32) only for the high-throughput profile. TP is hard-capped at 32 because `gcd(96 heads, 7168 hidden) = 32`. Multi-node 400G-class fabric (IB NDR / EFA) is mandatory — 1,561 GB of weights cannot fit the 1,128 GB NVLink domain.
3. **Weight format actually executed: MXFP4 dequantised in-kernel to BF16 via Marlin W4A16** — sm_90 has no FP4 tensor core. Measured cost: **+5.3 % weight bytes** (102.75 GB/GPU at TP16 vs 97.56 GB theoretical) and a decode step that is latency-, not bandwidth-bound (implied MBU 2–20 %).
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms) is reachable only with DSpark speculative decoding**: measured 23.21 ms TPOT at concurrency 8 on 16×H200 → **$63.86 – $126.63 per 1M output tokens** ($44.65 reserved). Max-throughput (S4) is **$27 – $228 / 1M output** — a bracket, because no H200 measurement combines high concurrency with FP8 KV *and* DSpark.
5. **Confidence: estimate** (four independent H200 measurements exist but disagree by 7× on per-user decode rate; no vendor benchmark, no MLPerf, and every SGLang H200 cell is `verified: false`).

**The one-line economic finding:** break-even against Moonshot's own API is **$2.44 – $3.51 / GPU-hour** at 0 % cache-hit and **$1.22 – $1.76** at 90 % — below the cheapest published H200 on-demand rate ($3.99, Hyperstack) and below the cheapest published 1-year reserved rate ($2.79). **Self-hosting Kimi-K3 on rented H200 loses to the vendor API at every published price.**

## 1. Fit

### 1.1 Inputs

```
usable_hbm/GPU  = 141e9 × 0.90                      = 126.90 GB     (METHODOLOGY §3)
activation_ws   = 4 GiB                             =   4.295 GB    (gpus/h200.md §2 convention)
weights (ckpt)  = 1,560,936,091,448 B               = 1560.94 GB    (arch §1.2; METHODOLOGY §8)
  experts MXFP4                                     = 1446.53 GB
  non-expert BF16/F32                               =  114.40 GB
MLA KV/token    = 24 × (512+64) × B  = 27,648 B BF16 / 13,824 B FP8  (arch §5.2)
KDA state/slot  = 69 × (96/attnTp × 128×128 × Bs + 9 × (96/attnTp) × 128 × 2)
                = 428.55 MiB (attnTp 1, fp32) → 26.78 MiB (attnTp 16) → 13.39 MiB (attnTp 32)
S (slots/req)   = 5 default · 4 extra_buffer_lazy · 3 no_buffer · 1 PD-decode   (arch §5.3)
```

**The H200-specific correction: Marlin repack.** On sm_90 the MXFP4 experts are
repacked into Marlin's W4A16 layout at load. The published community deployment
measured **102.75 GB/GPU at TP16** against `1,560.94 / 16 = 97.56 GB`
theoretical — a **×1.0532 (+5.3 %)** factor, giving an effective
**1,644.0 GB** of resident weights
([HF discussion 136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136), `meas.`).
Every row below uses the Marlin-adjusted figure; a plan built on 97.56 GB/GPU
over-states free HBM by 5.2 GB per GPU, which is a quarter of the whole KV
budget at TP16.

{16,32,64} cross a 400G-class fabric.

**Minimum GPU count = 16.** An 8-GPU HGX H200 node holds 1,128 GB of HBM
against 1,644 GB of Marlin-resident weights; it is short by 516 GB, i.e. not a
tuning problem. This reproduces the published topology exactly: vLLM's H200
recipe is `node_count: 2`, `--tensor-parallel-size 16`
([hw/h200.json](https://recipes.vllm.ai/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/hw/h200.json)),
SGLang's H200 low-latency and balanced cells are `nnodes: 2`
([cookbook](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Moonshotai/Kimi-K3)),
and the independent derivation in
[cross-cutting/inference-engines.md §6.3](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)
(`⌈1680/126.9⌉ = 14 → next power of two = 16`) lands on the same answer.

### 1.3 Max concurrency

Per-GPU accounting, because **MLA KV is replicated on every TP rank** and DCP
(the only lever that shards it) is in no published H200 recipe:

```
max_concurrency(ctx) = floor( (141e9 × mem_frac − 1644.0e9/n − 4 GiB)
                              / ( S × state_slot(attnTp) + ctx × kv_bytes_per_token ) )
```

**TP16 (2 × 8), `--mem-fraction-static 0.85` — the published SGLang profile:**

| ctx | S=5, fp32 state (default) BF16 KV / FP8 KV | S=4, bf16 state (`extra_buffer_lazy` + `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16`) BF16 KV / FP8 KV |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 K | **34 / 50** | **44 / 74** |
| 32 K | 12 / 21 | 13 / 25 |
| 128 K | 3 / 6 | 3 / 6 |
| 1 M | **0 / 0** | **0 / 0** |

**TP32 (4 × 8), `--mem-fraction-static 0.90` — the SGLang high-throughput profile, S=4 bf16:**

| ctx | BF16 KV | FP8 KV |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 K | 278 | **500** |
| 32 K | 76 | 147 |
| 128 K | 19 | 38 |
| 1 M | 2 | **4** |

**vLLM's H200 profile** (`--gpu-memory-utilization 0.97 --max-num-seqs 5
--max-model-len 32768`) leaves 29.73 GB/GPU, which the memory model says would
hold 117 sequences at 8 K FP8 KV — **but the recipe caps `--max-num-seqs` at
5**. That cap is a *scheduler* decision, not a memory one, and it is the single
loudest statement in the whole H200 story: NVIDIA/vLLM's own verified H200
profile admits five concurrent requests at 32 K on sixteen $30,000 GPUs
([hw/h200.json](https://recipes.vllm.ai/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/hw/h200.json)).

> **Correction to [`gpus/h200.md` §2](../../gpus/h200.md).** That table gives
> Kimi-K3 on 16×H200 as **169 / 148 / 98** concurrent at 8 K / 32 K / 128 K. It
> computed with the *aggregate* budget (`126.9 × 16 − weights − 16 × 4 GiB =
> 401 GB`) divided by `ctx × 13,824 + 2.25 GB`, i.e. it treated MLA KV as if it
> were sharded across all sixteen ranks and the KDA state as if it were not
> sharded at all. Both are backwards: MLA KV is **replicated** per rank without
> DCP, and the KDA state **is** divided by attention-TP width
> ([architecture.md §5.3](./architecture.md), quoting SGLang: "DP, EP, and DCP
> do not shard it; only attention-TP width, SSM dtype, and cache strategy
> change the per-GPU bill"). Recomputed per-GPU above: **50 / 21 / 6** at FP8 KV
> and the SGLang default S=5, i.e. **3.4× / 7.0× / 16× lower**. The 128 K figure
> matters most — 98 vs 6 is the difference between a service and a demo.

**Validation against a measurement.** The HF deployment report ran TP16+EP16 at
`--mem-fraction-static 0.85` with BF16 KV and reported a KV pool of
**308,608 tokens = 7.95 GiB** and CUDA graphs captured up to **bs = 54**
([HF discussion 136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136)).
Reproducing:

```
static  = 0.85 × 141e9                     = 119.85 GB
weights (measured)                         = 102.75 GB
free                                       =  17.10 GB
KV pool 308,608 × 27,648 B                 =   8.53 GB = 7.95 GiB   ← matches the report exactly ✔
remaining for KDA state pool + graphs      =   8.57 GB
state/slot @ attnTp16, fp32 = 26.78 MiB ; × S=5 = 140.4 MB/request
=> implied admission ceiling               = 61 requests            (report captured graphs to bs=54) ✔
```

Both the reported pool size and the reported CUDA-graph ceiling fall out of the
model to within 13 %. That is the strongest cross-check available for this pair
on this GPU.

### 1.4 Which tensors cannot be sharded — stated explicitly

1. **Tensor-parallel width is capped at 32.** TP must divide both the 96
   attention heads and the 7,168 hidden size; `gcd(96, 7168) = 32`. TP64 gives
   1.5 heads per rank and is illegal. Legal TP ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32}. Any
   64-GPU H200 shape therefore needs DP-attention on top of TP≤32 — a preset
   SGLang publishes for Blackwell but **not** for H200.
2. **The KDA recurrent + conv state shards by attention-TP width and by nothing
   else.** DP, EP and DCP leave it untouched. At TP16 it is 26.78 MiB per slot
   per GPU; at S=5 that is 140.4 MB per request per GPU before one KV token is
   stored. This is the concurrency ceiling at short context — at 8 K with FP8 KV
   the state is **55 %** of the per-request bill (140.4 MB state vs 113.2 MB KV)
   at S=5/fp32. The crossover where KV finally overtakes state is **10,157
   tokens** at FP8 KV and **5,078** at BF16 KV — so all of S1, and most chat
   traffic, is **state-bound, not KV-bound**, and `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16`
   buys more admission there than `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3` does.
3. **MLA KV is replicated across every TP rank.** Only `--dcp-size` /
   `--decode-context-parallel-size` deduplicates it, and DCP appears in **no**
   published H200 recipe — vLLM scopes `decode_context_parallelism` to the TP8
   single-node Blackwell profile, and SGLang's H200 cells carry no `--dcp-size`
   ([cookbook](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Moonshotai/Kimi-K3)).
   The ~+72 % concurrency that DCP8 buys on B300 is not available here.
4. **Under DP-attention** (`multi_node_tp_dp`, listed `supported` for h200)
   each DP replica holds a *complete* KV and state pool. DP multiplies the
   reservation; it does not divide it.
5. **Pure DEP is not an option**: vLLM marks `multi_node_dep` explicitly
   **`unsupported`** on h200 (and h100)
   ([Kimi-K3.json `strategy_hardware`](https://recipes.vllm.ai/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.json)).
   That is fortunate — under DEP the 114.4 GB of BF16 non-expert weights are
   replicated per rank (TRT-LLM quotes 210 GB/rank for DEP16), which would not
   fit a 141 GB card at all.
6. **Small replicated tensors**, present on every rank regardless of
   parallelism: the 506 F32 tensors (`{q,k,v}_conv1d`, `A_log`, `dt_bias`,
   `o_norm`, router `e_score_correction_bias`) totalling 44.5 MB; the 4.02 M
   AttnRes rank-1 projections and norms; the router gates. Individually
   negligible, collectively the reason `1,560.9/n` is a floor, not a value.
7. **Under DSpark**: the draft model is replicated on every rank — 4.50 GB
   (`RadixArk/Kimi-K3-DSpark`) or 9.49 GB
   (`RedHatAI/Kimi-K3-speculator.dspark`) — and its ~1,400 B/token KV is
   replicated too, along with **D = block+1 = 8 extra KDA state slots per
   request** unless `--enable-linear-replayssm-spec` folds them into a per-slot
   ring ([architecture.md §7.5](./architecture.md)). On B300 that cost was
   measured as a drop in admission from 101 → 68 concurrent (−33 %); the
   equivalent H200 measurement does not exist ⚠️.
8. **Multi-node fabric is required, not optional.** 16 GPUs = 2 HGX nodes.
   Cross-node all-reduce runs at ~50 GB/s per GPU over 400G IB/EFA against
   900 GB/s on NVLink — an 18× drop, and METHODOLOGY §4's 20–40 % multi-node
   overhead band applies to every one of the 93 layers' collectives. The
   published H200 deployment ran on AWS `p5e` with `FI_PROVIDER=efa`
   ([HF discussion 136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136)).

**Read this plainly: Kimi-K3 decode on H200 is not bandwidth-bound.** At batch 1
the step is 59.5 ms across 93 layers = 640 µs per layer, against an 8.74 GB/GPU
read that 4.8 TB/s should retire in 1.8 ms. The binding costs are (a) Marlin's
in-kernel dequantisation, which turns every expert GEMM into a BF16 MMA with a
4-bit unpack in front of it, (b) 93 layers × two collectives each, of which the
cross-node hop runs at ~50 GB/s per GPU rather than 900, and (c) a per-GPU shard
so thin at TP16 (6 attention heads, 448 hidden) that the GEMMs are
launch-latency dominated. A plan built on METHODOLOGY's default 0.6–0.8 MBU
band will be wrong by 20× on this pair; that band is for kernels that exist.

### 3.5 Explaining the gap between estimate and measurement — and between the measurements

**Estimate vs measurement (5–65×).** Three causes, in order of size:

1. **Marlin W4A16 is a dequantisation tax, and the roofline does not model it.**
   vLLM's own Hopper measurement for the analogous DeepSeek-V4 MXFP4 case found
   that moving the MoE GEMM off Marlin onto FP8 tensor cores is worth **1.38–1.53×
   on prefill and only 1.12–1.13× on decode**, with the PR stating "Hopper has no
   FP8-tensor-core MoE path for these checkpoints today"
   ([quantization-formats.md §6.2](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)).
   The independent H200 measurement for *this* model agrees in direction and
   magnitude: requantising to W4A8 buys **+17.9 % throughput, −33.9 % TTFT**.
2. **Cross-node TP16 pays METHODOLOGY §4's 20–40 % band on all 93 layers**, and
   the per-rank GEMMs are tiny (6 heads, 448 hidden) — a launch-latency regime
   the bandwidth model does not represent.
3. **93 sequential layers with two attention stacks.** Each layer runs a KDA
   recurrent update *or* an MLA attention, plus a LatentMoE hidden→latent
   projection, 16 expert GEMMs, a shared-expert GEMM, and an AttnRes cross-depth
   read that SGLang JIT-compiles on Hopper. The fixed per-layer cost dominates
   at every batch this GPU can hold.

**Measurement vs measurement (7× on per-user decode rate: 5.4 → 39.95 tok/s/user).**
The four runs differ in four dimensions at once, and no two of them isolate a
variable:

- **Speculative decoding.** M1 has DSpark at acceptance ≈ 3.4; M2 and M4 do not.
  That alone is ~3×. (M3 lists "Speculative decoding: None" but the page also
  says "Benchmarking uses a synthetic acceptance length with the SpeedBench
  coding AL" — ⚠️ the two statements are not obviously compatible, and M3's
  39.95 tok/s/user is the outlier.)
- **KV dtype.** M1 is FP8 with `flashmla` on both sides; M2/M3 are BF16.
- **Topology.** M3 uses **TP8 × PP4** — four pipeline stages of intra-node TP8 —
  which keeps every all-reduce on NVLink and puts only activations on the
  fabric. M1/M2 use cross-node TP16; M4 uses TP8/EP32 with DP-attention across
  four nodes. This is the most likely explanation for M3 being 2.4× better than
  M2 with no spec and no FP8 KV, and it is an actionable finding: **on H200,
  pipeline-parallel-across-nodes beats tensor-parallel-across-nodes for latency.**
  vLLM ships `multi_node_tp_pp` as a strategy, but the H200 `alternatives` map
  does **not** list it ⚠️ — and the community report found PP crashed on the K3
  branch with `RuntimeError: cp_world_size must be positive` in CUDA-graph
  capture.
- **Image vintage.** M4 runs the superseded `vllm/vllm-openai:kimi-k3` branch
  image. vLLM's own optimisation post reports **+120 % to +181 % throughput and
  −57 % to −60 % latency** between v0.27.1 and the 13 Sept 2026 main branch
  ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-09-13-kimi-k3-performance-optimization)) —
  measured on B300 TP8, not H200 ⚠️, but it means M4 is almost certainly a floor
  rather than a current figure.
- **Workload.** M3 and M4 replay agentic traces with ~64 K median input and a few
  hundred output tokens, so they are prefill-dominated (M4's input:output token
  ratio is ~245:1); M1 and M2 are short-prompt generation benchmarks. These are
  not comparable throughput numbers and should never be placed in one column
  without the ISL/OSL beside them.

**One internal inconsistency to flag in M2.** The report's own columns do not
close: at conc 1, 16.8 tok/s × 28.3 s = 475 tokens (consistent with 512-token
completions), but at conc 54, 146.9 tok/s × 60 s = 8,814 tokens over 54 requests
= 163 tokens each. Either the completions were much shorter at high concurrency
(plausible with `reasoning_effort=low`) or the aggregate is windowed over a
ramp. **Do not derive per-user TPOT from M2's aggregate/concurrency**; I use it
only for the shape of the throughput curve and for the memory figures, which are
self-consistent (§1.3).

### 4.5 Effect of speculative decoding and prefix caching on cost

- **DSpark is the difference between a service and a science project.** At
  acceptance 3.4, removing it multiplies S1's output cost from **$63.86 to
  ~$217 / 1M** at $3.99 (`est.`, scaling M1's 277.7 tok/s down by 3.4), and
  pushes TPOT from 23 ms to ~79 ms, which **fails the 50 ms SLO outright**. On
  H200, DSpark is not an optimisation; it is the only path to S1.
  ⚠️ The acceptance figure is the vessl card's own measurement on its own
  traffic (~3.4), not a synthetic constant — unlike SGLang's B300 DSPARK cells,
  which pin `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=4.5`, and unlike the DSpark 3.51 in the
  MI355X recipe, which METHODOLOGY flags as synthetic.
- **Prefix caching moves input cost, not output cost.** At 90 % hit and 10 % of
  uncached prefill cost, S1's effective input cost falls $7.98 → **$1.52/1M**
  (`7.98 × (0.1 + 0.9 × 0.1)`) and the **blended figure falls from $19.26 to
  $17.10/1M at $3.99** — an 11 % improvement, because
  the blend is already output-dominated (output is 83 % of the blended cost).
  The bigger effect is on TTFT: 2,643 → 280 ms at 32 K. ⚠️ **But neither H200
  recipe enables it**: vLLM's H200 profile carries neither
  `--enable-prefix-caching` nor `--prefix-match-unit 128`, and without the
  latter a hybrid model's hit boundary lands on the whole Mamba state page.
- **The W4AFP8 requant is the cheapest available win**: +17.9 % throughput for a
  patch and a different checkpoint download → S1 output cost $63.86 → **$54.16/1M**
  at $3.99 (`est.`, 277.7 → 327.5 tok/s), with accuracy inside 2σ on six
  benchmarks.

### 4.6 Versus the vendor API, and break-even

Moonshot list prices ([architecture.md §11](./architecture.md),
[platform.kimi.ai](https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/pricing)): **$3.00 input
(miss) / $0.30 input (hit, 10×) / $15.00 output** per 1M tokens. Blended on the
same 75/25 mix with the vendor's own hit discount: **$4.987/1M**.

| | Self-host H200 (low $3.99) | Self-host H200 (high $7.912) | Moonshot API |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $/1M output, S1 | $63.86 | $126.63 | **$15.00** |
| $/1M output, S4 mid | $44.33 | $87.91 | **$15.00** |
| $/1M blended | $19.26 (S1) / $13.37 (S4) | $38.19 / $26.51 | **$4.99** |

**Break-even $/GPU-hour** (100 % utilisation, list price, no margin, 8:1 in:out
per the scenario):

```
   S1 conc 8 DSpark   hit   0% : $  38.99/h over 16 GPU -> $ 2.44/GPU-h
   S1 conc 8 DSpark   hit  90% : $  19.55/h over 16 GPU -> $ 1.22/GPU-h
   S4 mid conc 74     hit   0% : $  56.16/h over 16 GPU -> $ 3.51/GPU-h
   S4 mid conc 74     hit  90% : $  28.17/h over 16 GPU -> $ 1.76/GPU-h
   S4 upper conc 74   hit   0% : $  92.10/h over 16 GPU -> $ 5.76/GPU-h
   S4 upper conc 74   hit  90% : $  46.19/h over 16 GPU -> $ 2.89/GPU-h
   cheapest published on-demand $3.99 | 1-yr reserved $2.79 | owned/colo $1.52-$2.31
```

**Break-even utilisation.** Required utilisation = (price per GPU-hour) ÷
(break-even $/GPU-hour at 100 %). Anything above 100 % means the configuration
cannot match the API at that price at *any* utilisation.

| Operating point, cache hit | break-even @100 % | @ $3.99 on-demand | @ $2.79 reserved 1y | @ $1.52 owned |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S1 conc 8 DSpark, 0 % hit | $2.44 | **163 % — impossible** | 114 % — impossible | 62 % ✅ |
| S1 conc 8 DSpark, 90 % hit | $1.22 | 327 % — impossible | 229 % — impossible | 125 % — impossible |
| S4 midpoint, 0 % hit | $3.51 | **114 % — impossible** | 79 % ✅ | 43 % ✅ |
| S4 midpoint, 90 % hit | $1.76 | 227 % — impossible | 159 % — impossible | 86 % ✅ |
| S4 upper bracket, 0 % hit | $5.76 | **69 % ✅** | 48 % ✅ | 26 % ✅ |
| S4 upper bracket, 90 % hit | $2.89 | 138 % — impossible | 97 % ✅ (marginal) | 53 % ✅ |

Exactly one cell in the on-demand column clears 100 %, and it is the optimistic
end of the S4 bracket with no prefix caching at all. Once the traffic is as
cacheable as Moonshot's own (">90 % cache hit rate in coding workloads",
[blog](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3)), nothing on rented H200 closes.

The verdict, stated once: **there is no published H200 rental price at which
self-hosting Kimi-K3 beats buying it from Moonshot.** Only owned hardware at
$1.52/GPU-h (100 % utilised) clears the S4-midpoint break-even of $1.76 at 90 %
cache hit — and that ignores the capital cost of the idle half of a 16-GPU
replica. By contrast, [architecture.md §11.1](./architecture.md) shows the same
model on 8×B300 breaking even at **$21.79–$31.01/GPU-h** against published B300
rates of $7.40–$17.80. **The correct H200 decision for Kimi-K3 is to use the
API, or to buy Blackwell.**

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

Single node is not an option (§1.2). The real choice is **2 nodes (TP16) vs 4
nodes (TP32)**, and the trade is sharp:

| | 2 × 8 (TP16+EP16) | 4 × 8 (TP32+EP32) |
|---|---|---|
| Weights/GPU (Marlin) | 102.75 GB | 51.37 GB |
| KV budget/GPU | 19.86 GB @0.90 (12.81 @0.85) | 71.23 GB @0.90 |
| Max concurrency @8 K FP8 KV, S=4 bf16 | **74** | **500** |
| Max concurrency @128 K FP8 KV | 6 | 38 |
| Max concurrency @1 M FP8 KV | **0** | 4 |
| TTFT @128 K, 0 % hit | 12.7 s | 6.4 s |
| $/hr (low / high) | $63.84 / $126.59 | $127.68 / $253.18 |
| Published status | vLLM `verified`; SGLang low-latency + balanced, `verified:false` | SGLang high-throughput only, `verified:false` |

Doubling to 4 nodes multiplies capacity **6.8×** at 8 K and unlocks 1 M at
concurrency 4, for 2× the money. If you are going to run this model on H200 at
all, **TP32 is the shape that makes the cost per token competitive with itself**
— but note that nobody has published a throughput number for it, so the 6.8×
concurrency does not come with a 6.8× throughput promise (§3.3's b^0.532 says
expect ~2.7×).

### 5.2 When PD disaggregation or wide-EP pays

- **Wide-EP: never, on H200.** vLLM marks `multi_node_dep` **unsupported** on
  h200, and TP is capped at 32 by `gcd(96, 7168)`. `multi_node_tep`
  (`--enable-expert-parallel` on top of TP16) is available and is what the
  SGLang cells already do with `--ep-size 16`.
- **PD disaggregation: not at this scale.** The `pd_cluster` strategy exists for
  h200, but a minimum replica is 16 GPUs, and PD forces you into at least one
  prefill pool plus one decode pool, i.e. ≥ 32 GPUs before any specialisation
  pays. Modular's measured **20–30 % regression** on small or untuned
  disaggregated workloads and the "disaggregation is a thousand-GPU problem"
  rule both apply
  ([serving-optimizations.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
  K3's PD is additionally the hardest case there is — the connector must ship
  the recurrent KDA state, the paged MLA KV and the block tables, with
  untransferred tail regions zeroed. **⚠️ No H200 PD measurement exists.**
- **What *does* pay, in order**: (1) DSpark — 3.4× acceptance, measured on this
  GPU; (2) the W4AFP8 requant — +17.9 %, measured on this GPU; (3) FP8 KV +
  `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16` — +47 % and +48 % concurrency respectively;
  (4) TP8 × PP4 instead of cross-node TP16, if M3's 39.95 tok/s/user holds up
  ⚠️; (5) TP32.

### 5.4 Warm-up and loading

1,560.9 GB of safetensors, 96 shards (median 17.0 GB), **per node** — both nodes
pull the full checkpoint unless it is on shared storage.

| Source | Time | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| NVMe @ 20 GB/s | **78 s** | `est.`, sequential, one reader |
| NVMe @ 10 GB/s | **156 s** | `est.` |
| NVMe @ 5 GB/s | **312 s** | `est.` |
| **Measured**, shared FS with warm cache, `load_format=auto` | **669 s = 11.2 min** | = 2.33 GB/s effective ([HF disc. 136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136)) |
| **Measured full cold start** | **13 min 02 s** | 25 s distributed init + model build, 669 s weight load, 39 s CUDA-graph capture (11 batch sizes up to bs=54), warm-up |

`VLLM_ENGINE_READY_TIMEOUT_S=3600` is in both H200 env blocks for this reason.
`--load-format fastsafetensors` (which the Blackwell profile pins) is **absent
from the H200 profile**, and the community report found it stages one full
~16 GB shard per rank with double-buffering — ~33 GB transient per GPU, which
OOMs on a 141 GB card already holding 103 GB of weights. **Do not add
`fastsafetensors` on H200.** Also note the 10 Gbit/s download figure: 1.56 TB is
~21 minutes of pure wire time before any of the above.

## 6. Risks and open questions

### 6.1 Everything marked ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED in this document

1. **Which kernel serves K3's MLA *prefill* on sm_90.** FlashMLA's dense-prefill
   (MHA) path is documented SM100-only and K3 has no sparse path, yet the vessl
   H200 recipe sets `--prefill-attention-backend flashmla`. Method to close:
   read the SGLang backend-resolution log on a live H200 run.
2. **vLLM FP8 KV on H200 for this model.** The recipe's FP8-KV note pairs
   `--kv-cache-dtype fp8` with `mla_prefill_backend: TOKENSPEED_MLA`, which is
   SM100-only. SGLang demonstrably does FP8 KV on H200; whether vLLM's FLASHMLA
   path does is unstated.
3. **Prefix caching on H200.** Neither H200 recipe enables it, and
   `--prefix-match-unit 128` — the flag that makes hybrid-model prefix hits
   usable — is Blackwell-only in the published profiles. Whether it is safe and
   effective on the FLASHMLA path is untested.
4. **Why `SGLANG_K3_ATTN_RES_MODE=jit` is set on the H200 *high-throughput*
   cell and on every H100 cell, but not on the H200 low-latency/balanced
   cells.** AttnRes needs a JIT path where there is no hand-written kernel; the
   asymmetry suggests either an oversight or a TP32-specific requirement.
5. **M3's 39.95 tok/s/user.** NVIDIA Dynamo's H200 row reports 2.4× better
   per-user decode than any other H200 measurement, with no spec and BF16 KV,
   while the same page says "Benchmarking uses a synthetic acceptance length
   with the SpeedBench coding AL" and the H200 target's own status reads "No
   recipe available for this combination". The deploy.yaml it links lives on a
   fork branch (`milesial/dynamo`, `docs/kimi-k3-framework-selector`), not on
   `ai-dynamo/dynamo` main. Treat it as provisional.
6. **Whether TP8 × PP4 is actually the better H200 shape.** It is the only
   H200 topology that keeps all-reduce on NVLink, and M3 is the fastest
   per-user H200 number — but vLLM's H200 `alternatives` map does **not** list
   `multi_node_tp_pp`, and the community report hit
   `RuntimeError: cp_world_size must be positive` from the MLA path during
   CUDA-graph capture with TP8+PP4 on the K3 branch.
7. **DSpark acceptance on real H200 traffic.** M1's ~3.4 is measured on the
   vessl harness, which is better than SGLang's simulated 4.5 on B300, but it is
   one workload (1,530-token prompts). Acceptance is strongly workload-dependent:
   RadixArk measured 5.51 on HumanEval and **2.99 on AIME26** — and hard-maths
   reasoning is exactly the `reasoning_effort=max` traffic K3 is built for.
8. **No H200 measurement at high concurrency with FP8 KV and DSpark together.**
   This is the single biggest hole; it is why §4.3's S4 is a 4.3×-wide bracket.
9. **No H200 measurement at 32 K, 128 K or 1 M context.** Every number in §3.4
   is either short-prompt (M1, M2) or an agentic trace with unstated per-request
   lengths (M3, M4). METHODOLOGY §4's ctx grid cannot be filled from published
   data for this pair.
10. **The vessl card's quoted serve command contains no `--speculative-*`
    flags**, yet its performance note reports an acceptance length of ~3.4 on
    both sides. The benchmark must have used a different command than the one
    published. The MXFP4-vs-W4AFP8 *delta* is still a clean A/B (checkpoint is
    "the only changed variable"), but the absolute 277.7 tok/s / 23.21 ms
    figures rest on an unpublished configuration.
11. **The Marlin repack factor (+5.3 %) is one measurement on one build.** It
    is load-bearing for §1's min-GPU and concurrency figures. A build that
    repacks differently would move every number in §1.3.
12. **What `--moe-backend marlin` actually costs on Hopper for MXFP4 g32
    specifically** (as opposed to Marlin's usual INT4 g128) is unmeasured
    anywhere — carried over from
    [architecture.md §12 Q12](./architecture.md).

### 6.3 Accuracy caveats of the precision actually executed

- **The MXFP4 weights are not a quantisation artefact — they are the model.**
  Kimi-K3 was quantisation-aware-trained at MXFP4 from the SFT stage onward and
  **no BF16 checkpoint exists**. So Marlin's dequant-to-BF16 on Hopper is
  numerically the *reference* path, not a lossy one: it computes the same values
  Blackwell's W4A8 kernel approximates, at BF16 accumulate. **If anything, H200
  is the more accurate of the two** — it is only slower.
- **FP8 KV is the one real accuracy risk on H200.** The checkpoint ships
  `kv_cache_scheme: null`, so FP8 KV scales are runtime-calibrated. vLLM's own
  FP8-KV study reports "consistent modest degradation" for Kimi-K2.5 on the
  FlashMLA path without calibration, and recommends llm-compressor calibration
  for MLA models ([gpus/h200.md §6](../../gpus/h200.md)). Since FP8 KV is what
  buys +47 % concurrency here, this trade is not optional if you want throughput
  — calibrate it.
- **`--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16`** halves the KDA recurrent state's precision.
  The F32 tensors in the KDA path (`A_log`, `dt_bias`, `{q,k,v}_conv1d`,
  `o_norm`) are numerically sensitive by design. ⚠️ No published accuracy
  measurement for bf16 SSM state on K3 on any GPU.
- **Requantisation to W4AFP8** (the recommended Hopper speed path) is the one
  precision change with published evals: six benchmarks all within 2σ of the
  Kimi-K3 card (GPQA-Diamond 92.9 vs 93.5, MMMU-Pro-vision 80.3 vs 81.6,
  SciCode 56.9 vs 58.7, OCRBench 89.5 vs 89.0, Terminal-Bench 2.1 86.5 vs 88.3,
  AA-LCR 82.0 vs 74.7), with the card's own caveat that its harness is not
  identical to Moonshot's.
- **NVFP4 builds are the one thing to avoid on H200**: RedHatAI's NVFP4 measures
  GPQA Diamond **91.0 vs 93.5 (97.33 % recovery)** — a 2.5-point drop — and on
  sm_90 you pay it for a Marlin W4A16 path that is no faster than the native
  MXFP4 one, on a checkpoint that is 49–85 GB larger.

### 6.4 The honest summary of risk

The measured evidence for this pair on this GPU is thin and inconsistent: one
third-party model card, one community deployment report, one provisional NVIDIA
Dynamo row on a fork branch, and twelve InferenceX rows on a superseded image.
No vendor has published an H200 benchmark for Kimi-K3, no SGLang H200 cell is
verified, no MLPerf round contains it, and the four numbers that do exist
disagree by 7× on the metric that matters most. Everything in §3.3 and §4.3 that
is not one of those four measurements is `est.`, and the S4 bracket is 4.3× wide
for exactly that reason. **Confidence: estimate**, and the recommendation in §0
(use the API, or buy Blackwell) is robust across the whole bracket — which is
the only reason the thin evidence is tolerable.
