# moonshotai/Kimi-K3 on NVIDIA H100 SXM 80GB (HGX 8-GPU)

> Research date **2026-09-19**. Formulas, legend and rules per
> [`research/METHODOLOGY.md`](../../METHODOLOGY.md). Model facts are taken from
> [`models/kimik3/architecture.md`](./architecture.md); GPU facts from
> [`gpus/h100.md`](../../gpus/h100.md); both are already fact-checked and are
> cited by relative path + section rather than re-derived.
>
> **What is new in this document.** Two end-to-end Kimi-K3-on-Hopper serving
> runs were located that neither the architecture doc nor the GPU doc had
> (both listed H100/H200 throughput as "none published", architecture.md §12
> Q10): a 32× H100 80GB run published by Hyperstack with the full SGLang log,
> and a 16× H200 141GB run published as a Hugging Face discussion. They are the
> spine of §3 and they change the §1 fit table materially — the resident weight
> footprint on the Marlin path is **1.31× the checkpoint share**, not 1.00×.

## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today — barely, and only at 32 GPUs.** SGLang ≥ v0.5.17 (image
   `lmsysorg/sglang:kimi-k3`, cell `verified: false / in-progress`) and vLLM
   ≥ 0.27.1 both publish an H100 profile; TensorRT-LLM does **not** (SM100 only).
2. **Minimum = recommended = 32× H100 (4 HGX nodes), TP32 + EP32**, over
   InfiniBand/RoCE. 8 and 16 GPUs do not fit; 24 does not fit once the Marlin
   resident multiplier is counted. TP is hard-capped at 32 (gcd(96 heads, 7168
   hidden) = 32), so >32 GPUs means replicas or DP-attention, not a wider TP.
3. **Weight format actually executed: MXFP4 dequantised in-kernel to BF16
   (Marlin W4A16).** H100 has no FP4/MX tensor cores; the 4 bits buy capacity,
   the math runs at BF16 rates.
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): not achievable at any concurrency.** Best
   modelled TPOT is 113 ms at batch 1 (`est.`); the only published H100
   measurement is **172 ms** (5.8 tok/s single stream). Cost at that point is
   **$3,218–$6,918 per 1M output tokens** (`est.`, $3.20–$6.88/GPU-h) — versus
   Moonshot's $15.00 list.
5. **Max throughput: $32–$70 per 1M output tokens** (`est.`, conc 128, FP8 KV,
   876 tok/s aggregate). **Confidence: estimate** — fit and memory are
   measured-anchored, throughput on the SXM SKU is modelled from two Hopper
   measurements on adjacent SKUs (H100 PCIe-NVLink and H200 SXM).

### 1.2 The number the paper model misses: the Marlin resident multiplier

Two independent SGLang runs report per-GPU resident weights, and both are well
above `checkpoint / N`:

| Run | N | Engine | Resident weights/GPU | `1,560.9 / N` | Ratio |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hyperstack, 32× H100 80GB, TP32/EP32 | 32 | SGLang `kimi-k3`, marlin | **59.63 GiB = 64.03 GB** (`mem usage=59.63 GB` in the load log) | 48.78 GB | **1.313×** |
| HF discussion #136, 16× H200 141GB, TP16/EP16 | 16 | SGLang `kimi-k3`, marlin | **102.75 GiB = 110.33 GB** | 97.56 GB | **1.131×** |

Sources: [Hyperstack tutorial](https://www.hyperstack.cloud/technical-resources/tutorials/deploy-kimi-k3-on-gpu-cloud-for-multi-node-2.8t-inference)
(stage-2 log, verbatim: `Load weight end. elapsed=317.50 s, … avail mem=18.60 GB,
mem usage=59.63 GB`), [HF discussion #136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136).

SGLang's log prints GiB while labelling it "GB"; this is provable from the same
run's KV line — `#tokens: 140352, KV size: 3.62 GB` gives
`3.62 × 2³⁰ / 140,352 = 27,694 B/token` against the formula's 27,648 B (+0.17 %).
Read as decimal GB it would give 25,792 B/token, which matches nothing.

Fitting `w(N) = A/N + R` to the two points (`est.`, exactly determined, not
over-determined — treat as a two-point interpolation, ⚠️ not validated at a
third N):

```
A/16 + R = 110.33e9      A = 1,481.6e9 B   (bytes that shard with N)
A/32 + R =  64.03e9      R =    17.73e9 B  (bytes replicated on every rank)
```

`A` exceeds the 1,446.5 GB of routed experts by 35.1 GB, so a little of the
non-expert stack shards too; `R = 17.73 GB` is roughly `embed_tokens` +
`lm_head` + vision tower + router gates (6.8 GB, from
[architecture.md §3.2](./architecture.md#32-per-component-breakdown)) plus
~11 GB of Marlin repack buffers and per-rank workspace ⚠️ — the decomposition is
inferred, only the totals are measured. Spheron's independent write-up reaches
the same node total from the same log: *"each rank ends up holding roughly
59.6GB resident, about 1,908GB across all 32 GPUs rather than 1,561GB"*
([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/deploy-kimi-k3-gpu-cloud/)).

**This is the single largest correction this document makes.** It is what turns
24 GPUs from "fits" into "does not fit".

### 1.3 Fit over the GPU counts this topology allows

HGX H100 nodes are 8 GPUs with an 8-way NVSwitch domain
([gpus/h100.md §4](../../gpus/h100.md#4-interconnect-and-topology)), so the
allowed set is {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 64, …}. **METHODOLOGY §3 basis**:
`usable = 80 GB × 0.90 = 72.0 GB`, `activation_ws = 4 GB` (mid of the 2–6 GB
band). Parallelism is TP = min(N, 32) with EP = N.

| N | Parallelism | Weights/GPU (`est.`, §1.2) | KV budget/GPU | 8K bf16 / fp8 | 32K bf16 / fp8 | 128K bf16 / fp8 | 1M bf16 / fp8 | Multi-node fabric? |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | — | 1,499.3 GB | **−1,431.3** | — | — | — | — | does not fit |
| 2 | TP2 | 758.5 GB | **−690.5** | — | — | — | — | does not fit |
| 4 | TP4 | 388.1 GB | **−320.1** | — | — | — | — | does not fit |
| 8 | TP8/EP8 (1 node) | 202.9 GB | **−134.9** | — | — | — | — | does not fit |
| 16 | TP16/EP16 (2 nodes) | 110.3 GB | **−42.3** | — | — | — | — | does not fit (H200 does) |
| 24 | TP24/EP24 (3 nodes) | 79.5 GB | **−11.5** | — | — | — | — | **does not fit** — see note |
| **32** | **TP32/EP32 (4 nodes)** | **64.0 GB** | **3.97 GB** | **13 / 21** | 4 / 7 | 1 / 2 | 0 / 0 | **yes — IB/RoCE, 3 hops** |
| 64 | TP32 × DP2, EP64 (8 nodes) | 41.4 GB ⚠️ | 26.6 GB | 89 / 145 ×2 replicas | 27 / 50 | 7 / 14 | 0 / 1 | yes |

**The 24-GPU row is a correction to [gpus/h100.md §2](../../gpus/h100.md#the-repos-own-models-on-h100-per-methodology-234-and-8-est),**
which states Kimi-K3 "needs ≥ 24 H100 (3 nodes)". On the paper share
(1,560.9/24 = 65.0 GB) that is true; on the measured Marlin resident footprint
(79.5 GB/GPU against an 80 GB card) it is not. 24 is also not a TP size the
model permits — 96 attention heads / 24 = 4 ✓ but 7168 / 24 is not integral, so
TP24 is invalid on the hidden dimension. Both engines publish **32**, and the
independent arithmetic in
[cross-cutting/inference-engines.md §6.3](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md#63-moonshotaikimi-k3)
(`⌈1680/(80×0.9)⌉ = 24 → next power of two = 32`) lands there too.

**TP cannot exceed 32.** `gcd(96 heads, 7168 hidden) = 32`, so the valid TP set
is {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32}; TP64 would ask a rank for 1.5 attention heads. SGLang's
cookbook encodes this — the TP=64 knob value carries
`hide: {hw: [b300, gb300, b200, gb200, h200, h100, …]}`
([SGLang cookbook payload](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Moonshotai/Kimi-K3)),
and Hyperstack states it explicitly: *"The greatest common divisor of 96 and
7168 is 32, so the valid tensor-parallel sizes are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32, and no
more."* Above 32 GPUs you add **replicas** (DP) or widen **EP** under
DP-attention — never TP.

### 1.4 The same table on the engine's own memory basis

METHODOLOGY §3's `×0.90` is materially more conservative than what SGLang
actually sees on this part, and the two disagree; both are stated per the
inputs-discipline rule. The measured run reports **`avail mem=78.23 GB`
(= 78.23 GiB = 84.0e9 B) per card after CUDA context**, i.e. 98.8 % of the
81,559 MiB `nvidia-smi` total — then `--mem-fraction-static` carves the static
pool out of that. The resulting model,

```
static_pool(mf) = mf × 84.00e9 − resident_weights
max_concurrency(ctx) = floor( static_pool / (ctx × kv_bytes + S × state_slot) )
```

reproduces the measured run to −2.4 %: at `mf 0.85`, `0.85 × 84.00e9 − 64.03e9
= 7.37e9 B` against a measured `KV 3.62 + state 3.41 GiB = 7.55e9 B`.

| N | `--mem-fraction-static` | Static pool | 8K bf16 | 8K fp8 | 32K bf16 | 32K fp8 | 128K bf16 | 128K fp8 | 1M fp8 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 0.85 (SGLang cell) | 7.37 GB | 24 | 40 | 7 | 14 | 1 | 3 | **0** |
| 32 | 0.90 | 11.57 GB | 39 | 63 | 11 | 22 | 3 | 6 | **0** |
| 32 | **0.97** (vLLM recipe) | **17.45 GB** | **58** | **95** | 17 | 33 | 4 | 9 | **1** |
| 64 | 0.97 (TP32×DP2, EP64 ⚠️) | 40.60 GB | 136 | 221 | 41 | 77 | 10 | 21 | 2 |

**Validation against the measured run** (32× H100, `mf 0.85`, BF16 KV):

| Quantity | Model | Measured | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| KV bytes / token | 27,648 | 27,694 | +0.17 % |
| Static pool | 7.37 GB | 7.55 GB | −2.4 % |
| Admitted requests (state-pool bound) | 52 (`3.41 GiB / (5 × 13.39 MiB)`) | **49** (`max_running_requests=49`) | +6 % |

That +6 % is the same sign and magnitude as the architecture doc's B300
validation (+9 % vs SGLang's published 101-request cap,
[architecture.md §5.6](./architecture.md#56-does-it-fit-one-8-gpu-b300-node--the-briefs-question-checked)),
so the state-slot model is behaving consistently on a second platform.

### 1.5 What cannot be sharded

| Tensor group | Shards? | Why it matters on H100 |
|---|---|---|
| **KDA recurrent + conv state pool** | **No.** SGLang: *"DP, EP, and DCP do not shard it; only attention-TP width, SSM dtype, and cache strategy change the per-GPU bill"* ([architecture.md §5.3](./architecture.md#53-kda-per-sequence-state--the-real-constraint)) | At attn_tp 32 it is 13.39 MiB/slot × S=5 = 67 MiB/request — small in absolute terms, but it is what set `max_running_requests=49` in the measured run |
| **MLA KV cache under plain TP** | **No** — replicated on every rank unless DCP is on. The SGLang H100 cells carry **no `--dcp-size`**, and vLLM scopes `--decode-context-parallel-size` to the TP8 single-node Blackwell profile ([architecture.md §5.7](./architecture.md#57-dcp--the-only-lever-that-shards-mla-kv)) | Every one of the 32 ranks holds the full KV. This is why the 32-GPU KV budget is per-GPU, not aggregate, and why the 1M row is 0–1 |
| `embed_tokens`, `lm_head`, router gates, MoonViT-V2 + projector | Replicated per rank in the measured footprint (≈ 6.8 GB of the 17.73 GB `R`) | Fixed tax that grows as a *share* of the per-GPU budget as N rises — the reason `w(N)` flattens out at 41 GB rather than falling to 24 GB at N=64 |
| Marlin repack / workspace buffers | Per rank ⚠️ (inferred) | The rest of `R` |
| Routed experts (2,722.7 B params) | **Yes**, `--ep-size 32` | The only thing that genuinely divides |

**Consequence to read off the table: 32× H100 cannot serve the advertised 1 M
context.** The measured server printed `context_len=1048576` while its KV pool
held **140,352 tokens** — 0.13 of one 1M-token request. Even at `mf 0.97` with
FP8 KV the answer is one request, with nothing left for a second. The
1 M window on this GPU is a nominal capability, not an operating point.

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.1 Assumptions, stated

Per [METHODOLOGY §4](../../METHODOLOGY.md#4-throughput-and-latency-roofline).
Everything in §3.2 is `est.`; §3.3 is `meas.`

- **Decode is *not* bandwidth-bound in this deployment.** Both published Hopper
  runs sit at 5.7–7.6 % effective MBU at batch 1 — an order of magnitude below
  METHODOLOGY's 0.6–0.8 planning band for Hopper and below even
  [gpus/h100.md §9.3](../../gpus/h100.md#93-observed-mbu-on-h100-derived-from-measured-inferencex-runs)'s
  reduced 0.30–0.40 recommendation for TP8. The model therefore splits the step
  into a bandwidth term at **MBU 0.45** and a **launch/collective term**:

```
t_step(b, ctx) = t_launch + bytes_gpu(b, ctx) / (3.35e12 × 0.45)

bytes_gpu(b, ctx) = R_read + E_res × distinct_experts(b)/896
                  + b × ctx × kv_bytes_per_token          # MLA KV is REPLICATED on every rank
                  + b × 14,042,880                        # one live KDA slot per sequence
  R_read = 18.83e9 B (per-rank non-expert resident, read every step)
  E_res  = 45.20e9 B (this rank's share of the MXFP4 experts)
  distinct_experts(b) = 896 × (1 − (1 − 16/896)^b)        # METHODOLOGY §4
```

- **Calibration of `t_launch`** (`est.`, from the two published runs):

| Run | bytes/GPU @ b=1 | MBU-0.45 term | measured TPOT | residual `t_launch` | effective MBU |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32× H100 80GB **PCIe-NVLink**, TP32, 4 nodes, **TCP/Ethernet** | 19.65 GB | 21.8 ms | **172.4 ms** | **150.6 ms** | 5.70 % |
| 16× H200 141GB **SXM**, TP16, 2 nodes, **InfiniBand** | 21.57 GB | 10.0 ms | **59.5 ms** | **49.5 ms** | 7.55 % |
| → 32× H100 **SXM**, TP32, 4 nodes, IB (this pair) | 19.64 GB | 13.0 ms | — | **100 ms** ⚠️ `est.` | ≈ 5.2 % |

  The 100 ms is interpolated: twice the rank count and twice the node count of
  the H200 run (all-reduce latency scales roughly with rank count over 186
  collectives per step — 2 per layer × 93 layers), but on NVSwitch + IB rather
  than PCIe-bridged pairs + TCP. ⚠️ **This is the least defensible number in
  the document** and it sets every decode figure below.

- **Prefill: MFU 0.30 BF16**, a stated deviation below METHODOLOGY's 0.35–0.50
  band and below [gpus/h100.md §9.4](../../gpus/h100.md#94-prefill-mfu)'s
  0.35–0.50 for H100. Justification: TP32 across four nodes sits at the far end
  of METHODOLOGY §4's 20–40 % multi-node comm penalty, and *all* of the FLOPs
  run at BF16 rates because Marlin dequantises (there is no FP8 or FP4 path —
  [gpus/h100.md §3](../../gpus/h100.md#3-compute-per-data-type)). Peak used:
  **989.5 TFLOPS dense BF16 × 32 GPUs × 0.30 = 9.50 PFLOPS**.
- **Fixed TTFT overhead 0.35 s** ⚠️ `est.`, calibrated against the H200 run's
  measured 0.37–0.54 s TTFT on a 137-token prompt.
- `attn_flops(T) = 24 × 96 × 320 × T²` (24 MLA layers, causal;
  [architecture.md §6.2](./architecture.md#62-prefill-attention-flops--where-kda-pays)).
- **Feasibility** uses `--mem-fraction-static 0.97` (the value in vLLM's own
  H100 override), i.e. the §1.4 engine basis. On the stricter METHODOLOGY §3
  basis of §1.3 the caps are roughly 4× lower and only `conc 1` and `conc 8`
  survive anywhere; both bases are given so the disagreement is visible.

### 3.4 Where the estimate and the measurement diverge

| | est. (SXM, this doc) | meas. (PCIe-NVLink/Ethernet) | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| TPOT, batch 1 | 113 ms | 172 ms | 1.53× |
| Aggregate, batch 1 | 8.8 tok/s | 5.8 tok/s | 0.66× |
| Aggregate, batch 4 | 22.8 tok/s (same model run on the PCIe part) | 16.3 tok/s | **0.71×** |

Three separate effects, in descending order of size:

1. **Batch-1 decode is latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound.** 93 sequential
   layers × ~2 TP all-reduces each = ~186 collectives per token, each carrying
   only `7168 × b × 2 B` (14 KB at b=1). That is pure round-trip latency across
   a 32-rank, 4-node group. LMSYS say the same thing about this model
   generically — *"at bs=1 a 2.8T model is not compute-bound — it is a
   launch-count and latency problem", 93 attention + 92 latent-MoE layers per
   token* ([serving-optimizations.md §4.4](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md#44-mbu-and-mfu-achieved)).
   Effective MBU of 5.7 % is the symptom, not the cause.
2. **The measured part is the wrong SKU.** H100 PCIe is 2.0 TB/s vs SXM5's
   3.35 TB/s (0.60×), 1,600 vs 1,979 dense FP8 TFLOPS, NVLink in **pairs** at
   600 GB/s instead of an 8-way NVSwitch at 900 GB/s, and the four nodes are on
   TCP Ethernet rather than 3,200 Gb/s InfiniBand
   ([gpus/h100.md §1, §4](../../gpus/h100.md#1-identity-and-variants)).
3. **The model is optimistic at batch > 1 by ~40 %.** Re-running the same
   formula on the PCIe configuration predicts 22.8 tok/s at conc 4 against a
   measured 16.3 — i.e. the launch/collective term is not constant but grows
   with batch (scheduler work, per-request KDA state gather, larger
   all-reduce payloads). **Treat every aggregate column in §3.2 as an
   optimistic bound**, probably by 1.3–1.5× at conc ≥ 32. ⚠️ Only one
   multi-request H100 datapoint exists, so this correction is not calibrated.

A useful cross-check that the *direction* is right: the H200 run reaches
9.18 tok/s/GPU at conc 54 with 1.43× the bandwidth and half the ranks of the
H100 run. Scaling that back to H100 SXM (bandwidth 0.70×, twice the ranks)
lands in the 4–7 tok/s/GPU region at similar concurrency — which brackets this
document's 7.9 tok/s/GPU at conc 32 from below.

### 4.3 Effect of the two caching/speculation levers

- **Prefix caching** moves only the input side of the blend. Going from 0 % to
  90 % hit rate takes `C_in`'s contribution from `0.75 × $0.633 = $0.475` to
  `0.75 × (0.1 × $0.633 + 0.9 × 0.1 × $0.633) = $0.090` — i.e. the blended S4
  cost falls from **$8.59 to $8.21 at $3.20/GPU-h, a 4.4 % improvement**
  (the §4.2 table's $8.38 is the same blend at METHODOLOGY's 50 % hit rate).
  On a GPU where output costs 51× input, prefix caching is nearly irrelevant to
  self-hosting economics — and, as §4.4 shows, it actively *hurts* the case
  against the API, because it cuts the revenue you would otherwise be
  displacing. ⚠️ It is also not enabled in either shipped H100 cell (§2).
- **Speculative decoding** moves the 59 % that is output, and it is the only
  lever with real leverage here. DSPARK is worth **3.00× on TPOT at conc 1 and
  1.64× at conc 64** on B300 ([architecture.md §7.4](./architecture.md#74-measured-end-to-end-speedup)).
  Critically, those B300 numbers **pin acceptance with
  `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=4.5`** rather than measuring it — they are not
  end-to-end measured speedups, and the only real acceptance data
  (RadixArk's offline `acc_len`: 5.51 HumanEval, 4.13 MATH500, 2.99 AIME26)
  is static evaluation. On H100 the case is *stronger* than on B300, because
  §3.4 shows this deployment is latency-bound — a verified token costs almost
  nothing extra when the step is 90 % collective latency. If DSPARK delivered
  even 2.0× at conc 128, S4 output cost would fall $32.46 → **$16.23** and the
  blended figure $8.38 → **$4.32**, i.e. just below Moonshot's own blended list
  price of $4.99. **⚠️ No H100 DSPARK measurement exists; this is the
  highest-value experiment to run on this pair.**

Read against the price rows: the **only** cell that clears any published H100

Hyperstack on-demand row by 20 % and the $2.72 reserved row by 41 % — and it
collapses below both the moment the traffic is cacheable. Since OpenRouter
reports a **92 % cache-hit rate on Kimi K3 traffic**
([architecture.md §11](./architecture.md#11-vendor-api-pricing)), realistic
agentic traffic sits in the right-hand column, where break-even is **$1.93** —
below every H100 price in the survey including AWS 3-year RI ($2.972).

**Break-even utilisation vs the API.** The GPU bill is fixed; the displaced API
spend scales with how busy the node is. Solving `u × revenue/h = 32 × price`
for `u` at the S4 operating point:

| $/GPU-h (node $/h) | 0 % cache hits ($123.02/h) | 50 % hits ($88.95/h) | 90 % hits ($61.70/h) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **$2.72 res1y** ($87.04/h) | **70.8 %** | **97.9 %** | 141 % — impossible |
| **$3.20 low** ($102.40/h) | **83.2 %** | 115 % — impossible | 166 % — impossible |
| **$6.880 high** ($220.16/h) | 179 % — impossible | 247 % — impossible | 357 % — impossible |

Only two cells are reachable, and both demand near-saturation of a 32-GPU
cluster at concurrency 128 around the clock *with traffic that never hits the
cache*. Since OpenRouter reports a **92 % cache-hit rate on real Kimi K3
traffic**, the realistic column is the right-hand one, where no price tier
works. Note the counter-intuitive direction: **the more cacheable your traffic
is, the worse self-hosting looks**, because caching cuts the API bill you are
trying to avoid far more than it cuts your own serving cost (§4.3). Compare
B300, where the same arithmetic clears every published on-demand rate at
concurrency 64
([architecture.md §11.1](./architecture.md#111-cost-sanity-anchor)).

The honest one-liner: **on H100 you self-host Kimi-K3 for reasons other than
cost** — data residency, licence terms, an air-gapped environment, or evaluation
before committing to Blackwell.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node vs multi-node

There is no single-node option. 8× H100 = 640 GB raw against 1,560.9 GB of
weights; even at a hypothetical zero-overhead 8-way shard the checkpoint needs
195.1 GB/GPU (§1.3). **Four nodes is the floor and, for one replica, also the
ceiling** — TP stops at 32 (§1.3), and the two ways to spend more GPUs are:

- **More replicas** (N = 64, 96, …): linear throughput, no change to per-GPU
  economics, no change to max context. This is the safe option.
- **TP32 × DP-attention + EP64** (N = 64): would cut resident weights to
  ~41 GB/GPU (`est.`, §1.2) and roughly **5× the S1 concurrency ceiling** per
  replica. ⚠️ **Unvalidated on Hopper** — vLLM marks `multi_node_dep`
  "Unsupported on H100/H200/Ascend", and SGLang's DP-attention large-scale
  presets are gated to Blackwell ("MegaMoE a2a lane (SM100/SM103)").

Fabric: the NVLink domain is 8 GPUs
([gpus/h100.md §4](../../gpus/h100.md#4-interconnect-and-topology)), so a TP32
group crosses the node boundary on every one of ~186 collectives per decode
step. METHODOLOGY §4's 20–40 % multi-node comm penalty is the *optimistic*
reading here; §3.4 measures far worse on Ethernet. **Use InfiniBand or RoCE, not
TCP.** The measured run's `NCCL_IB_DISABLE=1` is the single biggest avoidable
cost in it.

### 5.2 When PD disaggregation pays

It pays here more than almost anywhere, for one specific reason: **the decode
role drops `S` from 5 to 1** (SGLang PD decode uses the chunk cache), which
roughly quintuples the state-pool admission cap on the decode tier
([architecture.md §5.3](./architecture.md#53-kda-per-sequence-state--the-real-constraint)).
Combined with FP8 KV that moves the S1 ceiling from 88 to well past 200 per
decode replica. The prefill tier, meanwhile, is compute-bound and tolerates the
TP32 collective cost far better than decode does (prefill amortises collectives
over thousands of tokens per step).

SGLang publishes `h100` `pdMode: prefill` and `pdMode: decode` cells with
`--disaggregation-mode {prefill,decode} --disaggregation-transfer-backend nixl`,
all `verified: false`
([cookbook payload](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Moonshotai/Kimi-K3)).
Two gotchas from [architecture.md §8.9](./architecture.md#89-known-issues-and-open-caveats):
the positional port after `--prefill` must equal `--disaggregation-bootstrap-port`
or only the decode worker registers, and
`--disaggregation-decode-extra-slots` must be pinned (unpinned it defaults to
twice the batch below 32 requests and **zero** above).

**Wide-EP does not pay on H100** because it is not available: the MegaMoE
all-to-all lane is SM100/SM103-only. ⚠️ Whether `--moe-a2a-backend deepep`
works for K3 on SM90 is untested in any published recipe.

### 5.3 Launch commands

**SGLang, 32× H100, 4 nodes — the cookbook's Balanced cell, quoted verbatim
from the served payload** ([SGLang cookbook](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Moonshotai/Kimi-K3)),
flags and env exactly as the `{hw: h100, pdMode: unified, strategy: balanced}`
cell emits them (`nnodes: 4`, `verified: false`, `verificationStatus:
in-progress`):

```bash
NCCL_CUMEM_ENABLE=1 \
PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True \
SGLANG_ENABLE_TP_MEMORY_INBALANCE_CHECK=0 \
SGLANG_K3_ATTN_RES_MODE=jit \
SGLANG_MOE_FUSED_GATE_RADIX=1 \
SGLANG_HOST_IP=<this-node-ip> \
NCCL_SOCKET_IFNAME=<nic> GLOO_SOCKET_IFNAME=<nic> \
sglang serve \
  --trust-remote-code \
  --model-path moonshotai/Kimi-K3 \
  --tp-size 32 \
  --ep-size 32 \
  --moe-runner-backend marlin \
  --decode-attention-backend flashmla \
  --mem-fraction-static 0.85 \
  --dist-timeout 3600 \
  --reasoning-parser kimi_k3 --tool-call-parser kimi_k3 \
  --host 0.0.0.0 --port 30000
```

The **High-Throughput** cell is the same plus
`--mamba-radix-cache-strategy extra_buffer_lazy` (S: 5 → 4). The cookbook's
only H100 note is: *"Set This node IP separately on each node; use the same
cross-node NIC name on all four nodes."*

Add on top, in order of measured value:

```bash
  --kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3            # 27,648 -> 13,824 B/token; S1 cap 88 -> 130
  --mamba-radix-cache-strategy extra_buffer_lazy   # S 5 -> 4; cap -> 145
  --mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16           # state slot 13.39 -> 6.70 MiB; cap -> 188
  --mem-fraction-static 0.97           # vLLM's own H100 value; 7.37 -> 17.45 GB pool
```

Concurrency ceiling at S1 (ctx 4,608), `mf 0.97`, computed in `python3`:

| Configuration | per-seq bytes | max concurrency |
|---|---:|---:|
| baseline: BF16 KV, S=5, fp32 SSM | 197.6 MB | **88** |
| `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3` | 133.9 MB | **130** |
| + `extra_buffer_lazy` (S=4) | 119.9 MB | **145** |
| + `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16` | 92.7 MB | **188** |
| at 128K ctx, all three on | 1.841 GB | **9** |
| at 1M ctx, all three on | 14.52 GB | **1** |

⚠️ `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16` changes numerics on the KDA recurrence, whose
conv kernels and decay parameterisation ship as F32 in the checkpoint
([architecture.md §3.5](./architecture.md#35-non-quantised-tensors-the-ignore-list-resolved-against-the-checkpoint)).
Re-measure quality before adopting.

**vLLM, 32× H100 = TP32 — head node, quoted verbatim** from
[the recipe JSON](https://recipes.vllm.ai/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/hw/h100.json)
(workers identical with `--node-rank {1,2,3} --headless`; env adds
`PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True`):

```bash
vllm serve moonshotai/Kimi-K3 \
  --trust-remote-code \
  --tensor-parallel-size 32 \
  --nnodes 4 \
  --node-rank 0 \
  --master-addr $HEAD_IP \
  --gpu-memory-utilization 0.97 \
  --max-num-seqs 5 \
  --max-model-len 32768 \
  --moe-backend marlin \
  --disable-custom-all-reduce \
  --no-enable-flashinfer-autotune \
  --max-num-batched-tokens 4096 \
  --attention-backend FLASHMLA \
  --enable-auto-tool-choice \
  --tool-call-parser kimi_k3 \
  --reasoning-parser kimi_k3
```

Note the two caps vLLM chooses for Hopper and SGLang does not:
**`--max-num-seqs 5`** and **`--max-model-len 32768`**. The 32K cap is a
reasonable reading of §1.4 (128K seats 4 requests at BF16 KV). The `5` is far
below both the model's 88-request capacity at `mf 0.97` and SGLang's measured
`max_running_requests=49` on a *more* constrained `mf 0.85` — so it looks like
an extreme conservatism rather than a hard limit ⚠️. Also: **do not set
`VLLM_ENABLE_K3_LATENT_MOE_TAIL_FUSION=1`**, which the recipe's Hopper profile
recommends but which raises `ValueError: K3 latent-MoE tail fusion requires
SM100.` on SM90 (measured on H200,
[HF #136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136)). The vLLM
image is CUDA 13 only and needs an **r580+** driver
([architecture.md §8.1](./architecture.md#81-minimum-versions-and-images)).

### 5.4 Warm-up and loading

1,560.9 GB must land on **every node's local NVMe** — TP shards at load time,
but each rank reads its slice of every tensor locally, so a 4-node deployment
pulls **6.24 TB** in total.

| NVMe read rate | Time to stream 1,560.9 GB |
|---:|---:|
| 5 GB/s | **5.20 min** |
| 10 GB/s | 2.60 min |
| 20 GB/s | 1.30 min |

Measured: SGLang's weight-load stage took **317.50 s = 5.29 min**
([Hyperstack log](https://www.hyperstack.cloud/technical-resources/tutorials/deploy-kimi-k3-on-gpu-cloud-for-multi-node-2.8t-inference)),
i.e. an effective **4.9 GB/s** — the bottom of the band, on a VM-flavour NVMe
with 32 ranks reading concurrently. Budget the 5 GB/s row, not the 20 GB/s row.

Full cold start, measured end to end:

| Stage | 32× H100 | 16× H200 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Distributed init / rendezvous | 41.5 s | — |
| Weight load | 317.5 s | ~670 s |
| Memory pools | ~2 s | — |
| CUDA-graph capture | ~254 s (to `startup complete`) | 39 s (11 batch sizes to bs 54) |
| **Total to ready** | **11 min 01 s** | **13 min 02 s** |

Plus a one-time **~32 min** to download 1.56 TB per node at ~0.8 GB/s average
(peak 1.8 GB/s), four nodes in parallel. `--load-format fastsafetensors` is
**not** recommended on Hopper: it stages a full ~16 GB shard per rank with
double-buffering (~33 GB transient per GPU) and OOMs against an 80 GB card
([HF #136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136), measured
on 141 GB H200 — it is worse on 80 GB). Use `--load-format auto`.

## 6. Risks and open questions

**⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED — numbers this document depends on**

1. **The 100 ms launch/collective term for H100 SXM TP32/4-node IB** (§3.1). It
   is interpolated between two measurements on different fabrics and sets every
   decode figure in §3.2. Method to close: run the SGLang H100 cell on an
   InfiniBand HGX cluster and read `TPOT` at bs=1.
2. **The `w(N) = A/N + R` resident-weight fit** (§1.2) is exactly determined by
   two points and is extrapolated to N=64. A third measurement (8×H200 would
   not fit; 32×H200 TP32 would) would validate or break it.
3. **The aggregate columns in §3.2 are optimistic by ~1.4×** at batch > 1,
   based on the single multi-request H100 datapoint (predicted 22.8 vs measured
   16.3 tok/s at conc 4). Not calibrated.
4. **Prefill MFU 0.30** is a stated deviation with no Kimi-K3 Hopper prefill
   measurement behind it. Neither published run reports a long-prompt TTFT.
5. **The 0.35 s fixed TTFT term** is calibrated on one short-prompt H200
   datapoint (0.37–0.54 s at 137 tokens).
6. **No DSPARK measurement on H100 at all** — and §4.3 shows it is the only
   lever that changes the economic answer. Even the B300 speedups it would be
   extrapolated from pin acceptance via `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=4.5`, which
   SGLang itself labels not-a-measured-acceptance
   ([architecture.md §7.4](./architecture.md#74-measured-end-to-end-speedup)).
   The draft weights (4.5 GB RadixArk / 9.5 GB RedHatAI, replicated per rank)
   against a measured 10.34 GB of free memory make this non-trivial to even try.
7. **FlashInfer's MXFP4 W4A8 "Humming" path on SM90** — documented for
   DeepSeek-V4, never demonstrated for K3's SiTU-GLU latent experts. If it
   works it is the largest single throughput lever available on Hopper.
8. **DP-attention / EP64 at N=64** is arithmetic only; both engines gate the
   shapes that would enable it to Blackwell.
9. **PD disaggregation on H100** — cells exist, `verified: false`, no
   measurement.
10. **Prefix-cache hit rate on a hybrid model with the KDA state pool.** The
    `--prefix-match-unit 128` fix is a vLLM Blackwell-profile flag; neither H100
    cell sets it, and no hit-rate measurement exists for a Hopper K3 deployment.
11. **FP8 KV quality on this checkpoint.** `kv_cache_scheme: null` means no
    shipped scales; scaling is runtime-calibrated and unmeasured for K3.
12. **`--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16`** halves the state pool but changes KDA
    numerics on tensors the checkpoint deliberately ships in F32.
13. **vLLM's `--max-num-seqs 5`** — is it a measured stability limit or
    conservatism? SGLang admits 49 on tighter memory.
14. **Spheron's "423 tok/s batch-1 on GB300 with DSpark"** is not traceable to a
    primary source and is quoted but unused here.

**Known engine issues that bite on this pair**

- `VLLM_ENABLE_K3_LATENT_MOE_TAIL_FUSION=1` is recommended by the vLLM Hopper
  profile and **fails on SM90** (§2, §5.3).
- vLLM's `kimi-k3` branch had a **weight-loading memory bug on H200** that made
  141 GB cards infeasible at any parallelism (130.2 GB/GPU at TP16 and 127.3 at
  TP32 — per-GPU memory did not fall when GPUs doubled). SGLang loading the same
  checkpoint landed at 102.75 GB. ⚠️ Unknown whether this affects the H100
  profile; SGLang is the better-trodden Hopper path today
  ([HF #136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136)).
- **Pipeline parallel crashes** on the vLLM K3 branch
  (`RuntimeError: cp_world_size must be positive` in the MLA path), and no PP
  strategy is published for K3 anyway.
- `--load-format fastsafetensors` OOMs during load on ≤141 GB cards (§5.4).
- `mlx5dv_reg_dmabuf_mr errno 524` → `NCCL error: unhandled system error`; set
  `NCCL_DMABUF_ENABLE=0` ([architecture.md §8.9](./architecture.md#89-known-issues-and-open-caveats)).
- Triton JIT cache races across 16–32 ranks on a shared filesystem →
  `FileNotFoundError` during CUDA-graph capture. Set
  `TRITON_CACHE_DIR=/tmp/triton-cache-rank$RANK`
  ([HF #136](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/discussions/136)).
- Cross-node NIC must be pinned on **every** rank
  (`NCCL_SOCKET_IFNAME`/`GLOO_SOCKET_IFNAME`), or Gloo bootstrap fails. Both
  measured runs hit this.
- *"K3 occasionally emit a tool-call format its own parser doesn't expect.
  Suggest to run do schema validation and retry."* (vLLM recipe notes.)
- SGLang: *"Accuracy has not been re-measured on any cell — re-measure before
  you rely on one."* No H100 cell is verified.

**Accuracy caveats of the executed precision**

- The executed precision *is* the checkpoint precision. Marlin W4A16 unpacks
  MXFP4 to BF16 inside the MMA and accumulates in FP32 — it is, if anything,
  numerically **more** generous than Blackwell's native W4A8 path, which
  quantises activations to FP8/MXFP8. There is no Hopper-specific accuracy
  penalty from the weight format.
- The checkpoint was **quantisation-aware-trained at MXFP4 from the SFT stage
  onward**, so no BF16 reference exists and no "quantisation loss vs original"
  can be quoted ([architecture.md §1.3](./architecture.md#13-checkpoint-dtype-and-quantisation-scheme)).
  The only published quantisation-accuracy datapoint for any K3 variant is
  RedHatAI NVFP4's GPQA Diamond **93.5 → 91.0 (97.33 % recovery)** — and 93.5 is
  itself the MXFP4 model.
- Risks that *are* Hopper-specific come from the things you turn on to make it
  fit: FP8 KV without shipped scales (⚠️ 11), BF16 SSM state (⚠️ 12), and the
  `SGLANG_K3_ATTN_RES_MODE=jit` AttnRes path, which is a JIT fallback rather
  than a validated kernel.

**The strategic caveat.** Hopper software for this model is finished improving,
while Blackwell's is not — *"H200 TRT single node: no performance changes over
4-month period (near theoretical peak)"* vs GB200 gaining up to 8× tokens/GPU in
under four months
([serving-optimizations.md §4.4](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md#44-mbu-and-mfu-achieved)).
The gap measured today (5.8 tok/s on 32 H100 vs 127 tok/s/GPU on 8 B300) is the
*narrowest* it will be.
