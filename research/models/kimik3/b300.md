## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, and this is the model's reference platform** — vLLM ≥ 0.27.1 (current **v0.29.0**, image `vllm/vllm-openai:latest`, CUDA 13 only, r580+ driver) and SGLang ≥ 0.5.17 (current **v0.5.20**); B300 1×8 is the **only** SGLang cell marked `verified: true` for Kimi-K3 anywhere, and vLLM's `recommended_command` is the B300 single-node one.
2. **Min = recommended = 8 GPUs = one HGX B300 node**, TP8 + DCP8 (`--dcp-size 8` / `--decode-context-parallel-size 8`); 1,560.9 GB of weights is 195.1 GB/GPU, 72.8 % of the 2,144 GB node; 1/2/4 GPUs cannot hold the weights at any quantisation that exists.
3. **Weight format actually executed: the native MXFP4 checkpoint at W4A8**, FlashInfer trtllm-gen SiTU kernels on the `sm_103` FP4 datapath — no dequant, no requant. NVFP4 is available and is **measurably slower** here (§2).
4. **Interactive (S1, 4K/512, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $10.34–$20.95 per 1M output tokens** (blended $3.12–$6.32); with DSpark at its published 1.64× that falls to **$6.30–$12.78** (blended $2.11–$4.27). **Max-throughput (S4): $6.39–$12.96 per 1M output** (blended $1.93–$3.91). Price band = `planning price · b300 · low` $7.40 → `· high` $15.00.
5. **Confidence: `estimate`, on measured foundations.** The fit and the format/kernel matrix are hard facts; the §3 grid is an interpolation of three published B300 cells and its high-batch tail (b ≥ 128) is an extrapolation; DSpark's speedups are measured *at a pinned synthetic acceptance*, never at a measured one.

~94 `est.` The **2,304 GB** figure (8 × 288) is the GB300 NVL72 die nameplate and
**does not exist as a shipping 8-GPU B300 product** — [gpus/b300.md §11 gotcha 1](../../gpus/b300.md).

**Take 101 / 64 as the planning pair at 8 K / 128 K on the default config.**

### 1.3 Which tensors cannot be sharded — stated explicitly

1. **The KDA per-sequence state pool.** SGLang, verbatim: *"The KDA state pool is the
   concurrency ceiling — DP, EP, and DCP do not shard it; only **attention-TP width**, **SSM
   dtype**, and **cache strategy** change the per-GPU bill."* At attnTp = 8 a slot is
   56,171,520 B (53.6 MiB) and `S = 5` makes it **280.9 MB/GPU per request** — 2.25 GB
   node-wide before one KV token is stored ([architecture.md §5.3](./architecture.md),
   [serving-optimizations.md §5.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). At 8 K
   context this is **95 % of the per-request bill**; the FP8-vs-BF16 KV choice is nearly
   irrelevant there. The crossover where KV finally exceeds state is **~163 K tokens at FP8
   KV / ~81 K at BF16** ([architecture.md §5.4](./architecture.md)).
2. **The MLA KV cache under plain TP.** MLA caches one latent + one rope key per layer —
   there is no `n_kv_heads` axis — so every TP rank holds a **full copy**
   ([serving-optimizations.md §3.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). Only
   **DCP** (position sharding with a log-sum-exp merge) shards it, which is the entire 1.7×–7.9×
   capacity story in the table above.
3. **All 114.4 GB of non-expert BF16/F32 weights, under EP-only (DEP) shapes.** TRT-LLM's own
   per-rank accounting: DEP16 = 114 GB replicated non-expert + 90 GB MXFP4 experts at EP16;
   TEP16 shards the non-expert weights to ~115 GB/rank total
   ([inference-engines.md §6.3](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)).
4. **Attention weights under DP-attention** — ~61 GB (KDA) + 11 GB (MLA) replicated per rank on K3
   ([serving-optimizations.md §3.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
5. **The DSpark draft model** — 4.50 GB (RadixArk) or 9.49 GB (RedHatAI) replicated on every rank,
   plus **~1,400 B/token of draft KV that is not DCP-sharded**, so under DCP8 it is the same order
   as the sharded MLA share (13,824/8 = 1,728 B) — DCP8 + DSPARK roughly **doubles** effective
   per-token KV ([architecture.md §7.5](./architecture.md)).
6. Norms, the 93 AttnRes rank-1 projections and the F32 router bias are replicated but total
   **4,021,248 params ≈ 8 MB** — negligible ([architecture.md §2.5](./architecture.md)).

### 1.4 Multi-node fabric

**Not needed.** 8 GPUs = 1 node = one NVLink domain, which is exactly what both engines ship as
the default. 16 and 32 GPUs cross **Quantum-X800 InfiniBand or Spectrum-X Ethernet** at 1.6 TB/s
per node ([gpus/b300.md §4](../../gpus/b300.md)) and pay METHODOLOGY §4's 20–40 % multi-node TP
overhead unless the shape is EP/PP (which hides it). The 8-GPU NVLink domain caps in-node
EP at 8; wide-EP designed for NVL72 runs badly here
([gpus/b300.md §11 gotcha 11](../../gpus/b300.md)).

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.0 The script

All numbers in §1, §3 and §4 come from one `python3` script; its pinned-input header,
reproduced from the run:

```
B300 as deployed 268 GB/GPU, node 2,144 GB; BW 8.0 TB/s; dense BF16/FP8/FP4 2250/4500/13500 TFLOPS
checkpoint 1560.94 GB (1453.74 GiB); active 104.19 B (46.7% MXFP4)
KV/token: BF16 27,648 B  FP8 13,824 B  (24 MLA layers x (512+64))
KDA state/slot attnTp=1 fp32 449,372,160 B = 428.6 MiB ; attnTp=8 56,171,520 B = 53.6 MiB
S=5 (SGLang extra_buffer) -> 2.25 GB/request node-wide, 280.9 MB/GPU at attnTp8
mixed-precision effective dense peak/GPU = 1/(0.533/2250 + 0.467/13500) = 3,682 TFLOPS
Amdahl FP4 cap = 1/(0.533+0.467/inf) = 1.87x
```

### 3.1 MBU / MFU assumptions — measured, not planning defaults

METHODOLOGY §4 says to prefer published measurements over the planning bands wherever they
exist. For this pair they exist, and **they are well below the METHODOLOGY Blackwell band of
MBU 0.5–0.7.** [architecture.md §6.4](./architecture.md) back-computes implied per-GPU
bandwidth from the measured SGLang B300 cells and the corrected decode-byte table:

| Cell | Concurrency | Bytes/step | TPOT | Per-GPU BW | **Implied MBU at 8.0 TB/s** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low-Latency, MXFP4 NOSPEC | 1 | 138.4 GB | 8.51 ms | 2.03 TB/s | **25 %** |
| Low-Latency, MXFP4 NOSPEC | 16 | 483.3 GB | 19.47 ms | 3.10 TB/s | **39 %** |
| Balanced DCP8, MXFP4 NOSPEC | 64 | 1,138.0 GB | 40.19 ms | 3.54 TB/s | **44 %** |

### 4.4 What speculative decoding and prefix caching do to cost

- **Speculative decoding (DSpark) is the single biggest output-cost lever, and it is
  batch-dependent in the wrong direction.** Measured on B300: **3.00× at c = 1, 1.97× at c = 16,
  1.64× at c = 64** ([architecture.md §7.4](./architecture.md)). It cuts $/1M output by exactly
  that factor and leaves $/1M input alone, so blended cost falls by ~32 % at S1. But it costs
  **admission capacity** (101 → 68 at 8 K, −33 %), which claws back throughput at the
  max-throughput end, and its gains decay to nothing — and past `vcr ≈ 0.53` to a net **loss** —
  as decode leaves the bandwidth-bound regime
  ([serving-optimizations.md §2.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
  `--enable-linear-replayssm-spec` returns most of the capacity (D → 0) and vLLM measures
  **+10.97 % effective capacity under TP8**. **⚠️ The 1.64× is measured at a pinned synthetic
  acceptance of 4.5, not a measured one** — §6.
- **Prefix caching moves cost by more than speculation does, and in the opposite direction from
  what a naive reading suggests.** For *our own serving cost* a hit skips prefill and costs ~10 %
  (METHODOLOGY §6), so it cuts the input side of blended cost. But it simultaneously **removes
  the revenue** you would have charged for uncached input, which is what decides the
  build-vs-buy case (§4.5). Concretely, re-running the blend at a 90 % hit rate instead of
  METHODOLOGY's 50 % — `0.75 × (0.1 × C_in + 0.9 × 0.1 × C_in) + 0.25 × C_out` — takes the S1
  blended cost from **$3.117 to $2.768** at the low tier `est.` (the input term falls 0.533 →
  0.184), a **−11 %** saving; over the same move, API-equivalent revenue falls from **$565 to
  $185 per node-hour, −67 %**. The asymmetry is the whole story: caching saves you a little and
  costs the API a lot, so it moves the break-even the wrong way.
  On this model the flag is **not** free: `--prefix-match-unit 128` is mandatory or the hit
  boundary is very coarse, and **KDA state cannot run backwards** — it must be replayed forward
  from a checkpoint, which is why vLLM's internal KDA prefix checkpoints are worth only
  **9–25 % of TTFT** on B300 rather than the 10× a pure-attention model would see
  ([architecture.md §8.8](./architecture.md),
  [serving-optimizations.md §1.3](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
- **Cross-replica caching is the bigger lever if you run more than one node**: 1.7 % → 92.2 %
  hit rate on real Codex traces via Mooncake ([serving-optimizations.md §1.4](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
  ⚠️ but L3 HiCache drops the DCP flags on K3, which costs you §1's capacity.

| S1 + DSpark 1.64× | $2.108 | 2.37× cheaper | $4.274 | 1.17× cheaper |

So: **at the cheapest published on-demand rate, self-hosting wins everywhere. At the hyperscaler
rate it loses at the interactive operating point** and only barely wins at max throughput — i.e.
**OCI-priced B300 loses to buying the tokens** for interactive traffic.

Break-even $/GPU-hour and the utilisation you need to justify a node:

```
 SGLang Balanced NOSPEC c=64    hit= 0% rev $   174.30/node-h -> break-even $   21.79/GPU-h | util needed: low   34.0%  hyper   68.8%  res1y   36.4%
 SGLang Balanced NOSPEC c=64    hit=90% rev $    87.42/node-h -> break-even $   10.93/GPU-h | util needed: low   67.7%  hyper  137.3%  res1y   72.7%

 est. S1 TPOT<=50ms             hit=90% rev $   112.04/node-h -> break-even $   14.00/GPU-h | util needed: low   52.8%  hyper  107.1%  res1y   56.7%

```

Read it as three findings:

1. **Prefix caching roughly halves the case for self-hosting**, every time: break-even falls
   ~2.0× going from 0 % to 90 % hit, because output is 5–50× the price of cached input while
   costing the same bytes to produce. OpenRouter reports a **92 % cache hit rate on Kimi-K3
   traffic** ([serving-optimizations.md §1.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)), and
   Moonshot claims *">90 % cache hit rate in coding workloads"* — so the 90 % row, not the 0 %
   row, is the realistic one for agentic traffic.
2. **At the low tier the node pays for itself at 48–53 % utilisation even at 90 % cache hit.**
   That is a comfortable margin.
3. **At the hyperscaler tier it does not.** OCI's $15.00 needs ~97 % utilisation at 90 % cache
   hit for the DSPARK cell, and both the NOSPEC row (**137 %**) and the interactive S1 row
   (**107 %**) need more than 100 % — i.e. impossible. If your only B300 access is
   OCI or AWS list, buy the API for agentic traffic and self-host only output-heavy
   (`reasoning_effort=max`) workloads.
4. **Single-stream is always a loss.** Low-Latency c = 1 NOSPEC costs $146.01/1M output at the
   cheapest published rate against a $15.00 list price — a **9.7× loss**. Nobody sells bs = 1 at
   list, and you should not plan to.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 Single node is the answer, and both engines say so

One HGX B300 node holds the whole model at **195.1 GB/GPU (72.8 % of 2,144 GB)** with
**32.68 GB/GPU free** at `--mem-fraction-static 0.85`. vLLM's `recommended_command` is
`hardware: b300, strategy: single_node_tp, node_count: 1, --tensor-parallel-size 8`, and
SGLang's **only two `verified: true` cells in the entire Kimi-K3 matrix are `b300 1×8 Unified`**
([architecture.md §5.6, §8.2](./architecture.md);
[inference-engines.md §6.3](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)). For contrast, RedHatAI
report *"Kimi-K3 does not fit on a single GB300 node"* — a GB300 NVL4 tray is 4 × 288 = 1,152 GB.

**When to leave one node:**

| Driver | Shape | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Context > ~128 K at real concurrency | 2–4 nodes, or PD | one node serves **15 concurrent at 1 M** (0.85) / 20 (METHODOLOGY 0.90); 1 M at meaningful concurrency needs more than one node ([architecture.md §5.6](./architecture.md)) |
| Prefill-bound agentic traffic | **PD: PP8 prefill node → DCP8/TP8 decode node** | a PP8 prefill node has **1.45–1.72× the prefill capacity of a TEP8 node**; 91 % of the stage hand-off hides behind the next chunk. Composed: **2,808 tok/s/GPU** ([serving-optimizations.md §3.2, §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)) |
| Throughput at scale | **32-GPU wide-EP preset**, TP32/EP32 + DP4 | the documented Peak-Throughput preset; needs `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3` (*"load-bearing — bf16 KV does not fit 128 requests per replica"*) |
| Nothing else | **stay on one node** | *"disaggregation is a thousand-GPU problem"*; Modular measures a **20–30 % drop** from PD on small or untuned workloads, and the 8-GPU NVLink domain makes wide-EP a net loss in-node ([serving-optimizations.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)) |

### 5.3 Warm-up and load time

```
  NVMe  5 GB/s aggregate:    312 s (5.2 min)
  NVMe 10 GB/s aggregate:    156 s (2.6 min)
  NVMe 20 GB/s aggregate:     78 s (1.3 min)
  8x3.84TB local NVMe on p6-b300 = 30.72 TB, holds the checkpoint 19x over
  10 GbE cold pull from HF/S3: 21 min ; 100 GbE: 2.1 min
```

Practical notes: the recipe pins `--load-format fastsafetensors` and raises
`VLLM_ENGINE_READY_TIMEOUT_S=3600` precisely because of this
([architecture.md §1.2](./architecture.md)); `p6-b300.48xlarge` ships
**8 × 3.84 TB NVMe = 30.72 TB** plus 4 TB host RAM
([gpus/b300.md §11 gotcha 13](../../gpus/b300.md)), so stage the 96 shards locally once and never
pay the 21-minute network pull again. Add CUDA-graph capture and FlashInfer autotune on top —
both B300 recipes pass `--no-enable-flashinfer-autotune` / `--disable-flashinfer-autotune`
to cut that tail. **Budget 5–10 minutes cold, 2–3 minutes warm, before first token.**

## 6. Risks and open questions

**Everything ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED, in rough order of how much money it moves.**

1. **⚠️ No measured DSpark acceptance rate exists for any real workload on this model.** SGLang
   states it outright: the B300 DSPARK cells *"pin the acceptance length via the serve env
   `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=4.5` — they report what block size 7 delivers at that acceptance, not
   a measured acceptance rate for this workload… measure against the same recipe running NOSPEC
   before adopting."* Every DSpark cost figure in §4 inherits that. RadixArk's offline `acc_len`
   table brackets 4.5 plausibly (MATH500 4.13, SWE-Rebench 4.66) and vLLM's own draft measures a
   **4.11 macro-average**, but the **worst** case is exactly the traffic K3 is built for —
   AIME26 at **2.99**, and 2.5–3.1 in the 0–4 K output buckets. This is a *measured* acceptance
   from an offline eval, **not** a synthetic constant like the DSpark 3.51 in the MI355X
   DeepSeek recipe — but it is also not a production number.
   [architecture.md §7.3, §7.4](./architecture.md)
2. **⚠️ SGLang: only the two B300 1×8 Unified cells are verified, and *"accuracy has not been
   re-measured on any cell — re-measure before you rely on one."*** Everything outside those two
   cells is a starting point, including the DCP8 Balanced cell that §3 and §4 lean on hardest.
3. **⚠️ The B300 InferenceX row is a month stale (2026-08-16) relative to GB300/GB200/H200
   (2026-09-19)**, in a window where vLLM published 2.2–2.8× gains on this exact pair. Any
   B300-vs-GB300 conclusion drawn from §3.6 is confounded. Re-run
   `GET /api/v1/benchmarks?model=Kimi-K3` before quoting it.
4. **⚠️ `spec_method: "mtp"` on the InferenceX Kimi-K3 rows is almost certainly mislabelled** —
   K3 has `num_nextn_predict_layers: 0` and ships no MTP head. It presumably means a DSpark
   draft. Unresolved.
5. **⚠️ FlashInfer is the default attention backend for both engines on Blackwell and is
   "architecture-gated but not yet signoff-qualified" on SM103a.** Default + unqualified is worth
   a regression suite before production. [gpus/b300.md §11 gotcha 6](../../gpus/b300.md)
6. **⚠️ vLLM has no Blackwell CI at all** — its own RFC says CUDA CI covers only L4 and H100 and
   that *"importability does not prove that an optional extension contains a kernel for the active
   GPU."* Pin versions; test your exact model on your exact node.
   [gpus/b300.md §11 gotcha 7](../../gpus/b300.md)
7. **⚠️ `sm_100a` binaries do not load on B300.** The #1 source of "works on B200, dies on B300".
   Verify with `cuobjdump --list-elf lib*.so | grep -c sm_103` before blaming the model.
   [gpus/b300.md §11 gotcha 2](../../gpus/b300.md)
8. **Known crash, B300-specific.** [sgl-project/sglang#34260](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/34260):
   crashes *"approximately twice daily"* on **8× B300 SXM6 (cc 10.3)**, SGLang **0.5.17**, with
   `--tensor-parallel-size 8 --dcp-size 8 --ep 8` plus hierarchical caching and priority
   scheduling. Opened 2026-08-10, now **closed**, but no workaround is recorded in the thread
   and no fix version is named. ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED** which release contains the fix. Note the
   config that crashed is DCP8 **and** EP8 together, which SGLang separately warns against
   (*"Don't use EP with an a2a backend"*).
9. **The vLLM Kimi-K3 tracking issue [#50001](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/50001)
   shows 0 of 20 checklist items complete** as of the fetch, with DCP (#50484), PCP, PP, KDA
   prefill, AttnRes kernel optimization and mamba-prefix-caching restrictions all still open.
   The features §2 marks "native" are shipping, but the surrounding work is not finished.
10. **⚠️ No published numbers at 32 K / 128 K / 1 M context for this pair on any hardware.**
    Every serving measurement found is ISL 8192 / OSL 1024 or an agentic trace. §3's S2 and S3
    rows are pure extrapolation on the prefill side. [architecture.md §12 Q24](./architecture.md)
11. **⚠️ Rows at batch ≥ 128 in §3.3 and the S4 batch-414 variant extrapolate MBU past the last
    measured anchor (c = 64).** If MBU falls rather than holding at 0.45, S4 throughput and cost
    both degrade proportionally.
12. **Accuracy caveats of the executed precision.** The MXFP4 checkpoint is
    **quantization-aware-trained from the SFT stage onward** — *"there is no BF16 Kimi-K3
    checkpoint"* — so **no unquantised reference exists to measure MXFP4 accuracy loss against.**
    The only published quantisation-accuracy datapoint for any K3 variant is RedHatAI NVFP4's
    **GPQA Diamond 93.5 → 91.0 (97.33 % recovery)**, and the 93.5 baseline is *itself* the MXFP4
    model. A 2.5-point GPQA drop is not small for a frontier model. **No evals at all** for
    `nvidia/Kimi-K3-NVFP4`, `RedHatAI/Kimi-K3-FP8-BLOCK` or the AMD Quark build.
    [architecture.md §9, §12 Q17–18](./architecture.md)
13. **FP8 KV is mandatory on the fast path and carries its own accuracy risk.** The checkpoint
    ships `kv_cache_scheme: null`, so scales are engine-calibrated; `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers`
    exists precisely for hybrid shapes like this one, and **no K3-specific guidance is published**.
    `TOKENSPEED_MLA` force-rewrites the KV dtype to fp8 whether you asked or not.
    [quantization-formats.md §8.3–§8.4](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)
14. **⚠️ The "2× attention" B300 claim is unqualified and unmeasured.** NVIDIA attaches no
    footnote to it beyond the comparison baseline, and no engine has published an `sm_103`
    attention-kernel TFLOPS figure. K3's KDA + softmax-heavy inner loops are exactly what the
    doubled SFU should help, and **no published measurement isolates it**.
    [gpus/b300.md §3, §5](../../gpus/b300.md); [quantization-formats.md §4.3](../../cross-cutting/quantization-formats.md)
15. **Operational gotchas that will bite on day one**, all sourced
    ([architecture.md §8.9](./architecture.md)):
    `mlx5dv_reg_dmabuf_mr` errno 524 → set `NCCL_DMABUF_ENABLE=0`;
    tool calling — *"K3 occasionally emit a tool-call format its own parser doesn't expect.
    Suggest to run do schema validation and retry"*;
    setting any one of the three attention knobs on Blackwell non-DCP recipes
    *"cancels the auto-resolution for the others"*;
    DCP is force-incompatible with `--enable-symm-mem`;
    L3 HiCache silently drops the DCP flags;
    the Rust frontend is default and you may need to fall back to Python.
16. **Thinking tokens accumulate in the prompt.** K3 *"requires the complete assistant message …
    be passed back to `messages` as-is — including `reasoning_content`"*, and
    `reasoning_effort` defaults to **`max`**. Effective input length per agentic turn grows much
    faster than for a model that discards thinking, and output volume — the expensive side — is
    dominated by that one knob. Budget prefill and $/1M output accordingly.
    [architecture.md §1.4](./architecture.md)
17. **⚠️ Sub-SKU capacity.** At DGX B300's 262.5 GB or OCI `BM.GPU.B300.8`'s 263 GB the 8 K
    admission ceiling drops from ~110 to ~94 `est.` (−14 %). Confirm `nvidia-smi` on the actual
    node before sizing.
18. **⚠️ No MLPerf or InferenceMAX *single-turn* entry exists for Kimi-K3.** MLPerf v6.0/v6.1
    Blackwell Ultra submissions cover DeepSeek-R1, gpt-oss-120b, Llama and (in Lambda's open
    division) **Kimi K2.6** — not K3. The InferenceX rows in §3.5 are agentic-trace runs only;
    `isl`/`osl` are `null` on every one of them.
