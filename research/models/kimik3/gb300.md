## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, and GB300 is the best-supported platform this model has.** vLLM
   ≥ 0.27.1 (current **v0.29.0**) marks `gb300` **`verified`**; SGLang ≥ v0.5.17
   (current **v0.5.20**) ships GB300 2×4 cells; **TensorRT-LLM's Kimi-K3 deployment
   guide was validated *on* GB300 NVL**; NVIDIA Dynamo publishes four GB300 recipes;
   TokenSpeed/LightSeek publishes a GB300 tuning series. Four engines, all with GB300
   evidence.
2. **Minimum 8 GPUs (2 NVL4 trays); recommended 8 per replica, 9 replicas per rack.**
   One 4-GPU tray is 1,152 GB against a 1,560.9 GB checkpoint — it does not fit.
   Parallelism: **TP8 + DCP8** per replica (attention-TP 8, decode-context-parallel 8),
   or **DEP16 / TEP8 wide-EP** on 16 GPUs when per-rank capacity is the goal.
3. **Weight format executed: native MXFP4 W4A8** (FlashInfer trtllm-gen SiTU kernels on
   the sm_103 block-scaled datapath), non-expert tensors BF16, **FP8 KV**. No dequant,
   no requant. The `nvidia/Kimi-K3-NVFP4` build also runs but measured *slower* than
   MXFP4 without speculation on the sibling B300.
4. **Interactive (S1, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $19.74 per 1M output tokens** at the only published
   GB300 rate ($18.00/GPU-h, OCI); **$12.04–$6.62 with DSpark** depending on batch; **$2.53**
   at SemiAnalysis's $2.31/chip-h ownership anchor. **Max-throughput (S4): $14.52**
   ($12.21 with a 90 % prefix-cache hit), **$1.86** at $2.31/GPU-h.
5. **Confidence: `measured`** for support, fit and the shape of the throughput curve
   (six independent GB300 measurement sets exist for this pair); **`estimate`** for the
   S1–S4 grid cells, which are roofline numbers calibrated to ±4 % against the published
   B300 cells and cross-checked against the GB300 measurements in §3.4.

## 1. Fit

### 1.0 Capacity basis, and the one place the pinned docs differ

| Source | GB300 per-GPU HBM | Note |
|---|---|---|
| [METHODOLOGY §8](../../METHODOLOGY.md) | **288 GB (≈ 279 usable)** | the pin |
| [gpus/gb300.md §1.1, §2.2](../../gpus/gb300.md) | **288 GB nominal, 279 GB usable**; plans with **279** throughout | SemiAnalysis says 278 usable; Lambda/CoreWeave list 279; NVIDIA publishes only "20 TB" ÷ 72 = 277.8 |
| [cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md §10.3](../../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md) | uses **288 GB nameplate** for $/GB-hour | a pricing-normalisation basis, not a sizing basis; the doc says so explicitly |
| [architecture.md §5.6](./architecture.md) | **288 GB physical / ≈ 279 usable** | agrees |

**These are not in conflict** — 288 is the die, 279 is as-deployed, and cloud-pricing.md
normalises on the nameplate on purpose. **This document sizes from 279 GB** and applies
METHODOLOGY §3's `usable_hbm = capacity × 0.90`:

```
usable_hbm = 279e9 × 0.90 = 251.1 GB per GPU        [METHODOLOGY §3]
```

Note this is deliberately *more* conservative than the engines: vLLM's GB300 recipe sets
`--gpu-memory-utilization 0.95` and SGLang's GB300 cell inherits `--mem-fraction-static`
0.85–0.92. Where a figure below is compared to an engine's own published cap, the engine
basis is stated alongside.

**Checkpoint, from [architecture.md §1.2/§4](./architecture.md):** 1,560,936,091,448 B =
**1,560.9 GB**, of which routed experts (MXFP4, 0.53125 B/param) = **1,446.5 GB** and the
BF16/F32 non-expert block = **114.4 GB**.

### 1.1 Fit over every GPU count this topology allows

Topology: 18 compute trays × (2 Grace + 4 B300), 72 GPUs in one 130 TB/s NVLink-5 domain
([gpus/gb300.md §4.3](../../gpus/gb300.md)). Allowed counts are therefore
{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 18, 36, 72} — 4 is one tray, 18 is 4.5 trays (the smallest count that
divides the rack into four), 36 is half a rack.

`per_gpu_weights = 1,560.9 GB / n`, `activation_ws = 6 GB` (top of METHODOLOGY §3's
2–6 GB band; a 93-layer model with 1 M `--max-model-len`, CUDA graphs and chunked prefill
sits at the top), `kv_budget = 251.1 − weights − replicated − 6`.

```
 GPUs topology                  wt GB/GPU  repl GB  act ws  KV budget GB  fits
    1 -                            1560.9     0.06     6.0       -1315.9    NO
    2 TP2 (half tray)               780.5     0.06     6.0        -535.4    NO
    4 TP4 (1 NVL4 tray)             390.2     0.06     6.0        -145.2    NO
    8 TP8 (2 trays)                 195.1     0.06     6.0          49.9   YES
   16 TP16/DEP16 (4 trays)           97.6     0.06     6.0         147.5   YES
   18 TP18 (4.5 trays)               86.7     0.06     6.0         158.3   YES
   36 EP36 (half rack)               43.4     0.06     6.0         201.7   YES
   72 EP72 (full rack)               21.7     0.06     6.0         223.4   YES
```

`repl GB` = the ~60 MB of per-layer norms, AttnRes rank-1 projections and the F32 router
bias that every rank holds regardless of sharding.

**Minimum = 8 GPUs = 2 NVL4 trays.** Independently confirmed three ways:
vLLM's GB300 profile is `node_count: 2` over a `gpu_count: 4, vram_gb: 1152` tray
([hw/gb300.json](https://recipes.vllm.ai/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/hw/gb300.json)); the vLLM blog
states *"At least one 8× B300 (or GB300 NVL72) node is required"*
([src](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-07-27-k3)); CoreWeave's managed deployment requires
*"at least 2 GB300 NVL72 nodes (8 GPUs)"*
([src](https://www.coreweave.com/blog/get-started-with-kimi-k3-dedicated-inference)).
RedHatAI's statement that *"Kimi-K3 does not fit on a single GB300 node"*
([architecture.md §5.6](./architecture.md)) is about the 4-GPU tray and is correct.

### 1.2 DEP / DP-attention: the non-expert block is replicated

Under data-parallel attention (`--enable-expert-parallel --data-parallel-size N`, vLLM's
`multi_node_dep`; TRT-LLM's DEP16) every rank holds a **full BF16 copy of the 114.4 GB
non-expert block** and only the experts shard.

```
 GPUs non-exp GB/rank experts GB/rank total GB/rank  KV budget GB  fits
    8           114.4           180.8         295.2         -50.1    NO
   16           114.4            90.4         204.8          40.3   YES
   18           114.4            80.4         194.8          50.3   YES
   36           114.4            40.2         154.6          90.5   YES
   72           114.4            20.1         134.5         110.6   YES
```

Cross-check: NVIDIA's TRT-LLM deployment guide says verbatim *"DEP16 replicates the BF16
non-expert weights on every rank (114 GB per rank) on top of the MXFP4 routed experts at
16-way expert parallelism (90 GB per rank)"* = **210 GB/rank**
([src](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/main/docs/source/deployment-guide/deployment-guide-for-kimi-k3-on-trtllm.md)).
Our 114 + 90 = **205 GB/rank**; the 5 GB delta is the guide's own activation reserve. **DEP
needs 16 GPUs minimum and is the reason the guide says K3 DEP16 "requires GB300-class
per-GPU memory"** — 210 GB/rank clears GB300's 279 GB by 69 GB but clears HGX B300's 268 GB
by only 58 GB ([cross-cutting/inference-engines.md §3.6–§3.7](../../cross-cutting/inference-engines.md)).

### 1.3 KV budget per GPU, and max concurrency

Per [architecture.md §5.2–§5.3](./architecture.md) and METHODOLOGY §2:

```
kv_bytes_per_token = 24 MLA layers × (512 + 64) × B   -> BF16 27,648 B  |  FP8 13,824 B
fixed_state_per_seq = S × 69 × (96/attnTp × 128 × 128 × ssmBytes + 9 × 96/attnTp × 128 × 2)
                    = 5 × 428.6 MiB = 2.25 GB at attnTp=1, fp32, S=5 (SGLang default)
                    = 5 × 53.6 MiB  = 268 MiB at attnTp=8
per_request_per_GPU = S × state_slot(attnTp) + ctx × kv_bytes_per_token / DCP
max_concurrency(ctx) = floor( kv_budget_per_GPU / per_request_per_GPU )
```

DCP8 is worth **+34 % at 8 K and +326 % at 128 K** on this pair. That is the same direction
as, and larger than, SGLang's published "+72 % concurrency ceiling at the same engine
throughput, ~1.8× ITL" for DCP on B300 ([architecture.md §5.7](./architecture.md)); the gap
is that SGLang's figure is measured end-to-end including the activation and CUDA-graph pools
this model omits.

**DEP16** (attention-TP = 1, so the KDA state is **not** sharded at all and MLA KV is
per-rank), FP8 KV:

| ctx | per rank | × 16 ranks | per-request GB |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 K | 17 | 272 | 2.36 |
| 32 K | 14 | 224 | 2.70 |
| 128 K | 9 | 144 | 4.06 |
| 1 M | 2 | 32 | 16.74 |

DEP16 gives **272 rack-slice concurrency at 8 K on 16 GPUs vs 2 × 169 = 338 for two
TP8+DCP8 replicas on the same 16 GPUs.** For this model DEP's replication of the 114.4 GB
BF16 block is not paid back by expert sharding, because the non-expert block is 7.3 % of
the checkpoint but 100 % replicated. **Wide-EP is a throughput/latency play here, not a
capacity play** — see §5.3.

### 1.4 Multi-node fabric

**None required at any count up to 72.** The whole rack is one NVLink-5 domain
(130 TB/s aggregate, 1.8 TB/s bidirectional per GPU, one switched hop,
[gpus/gb300.md §4.1](../../gpus/gb300.md)). The 8-GPU minimum spans two compute trays but
stays inside the NVLink domain — SGLang describes it as *"8 GPUs across two 4-GPU compute
trays, within one NVL72 NVLink domain"*
([LightSeek](https://lightseek.org/blog/kimi-k3-optimization-gb300-part-i.html)). vLLM's
GB300 recipe still emits `--nnodes 2` and `NCCL_SOCKET_IFNAME` because the two trays are
separate OS nodes, and the recipe notes require `NCCL_MNNVL_ENABLE=1 NCCL_CUMEM_ENABLE=1
NCCL_NVLS_ENABLE=1` for MNNVL ([architecture.md §8.9](./architecture.md)).

**Beyond 72 GPUs you cross racks and fall to 800 Gb/s per GPU over ConnectX-8 — a ~18×
bandwidth cliff** ([gpus/gb300.md §4.4](../../gpus/gb300.md)). For a model whose 8-GPU
replica already fits in a rack nine times over, there is no reason to cross.

### 1.5 Tensors that cannot be sharded — stated explicitly

| Tensor / pool | Why it cannot shard | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **KDA recurrent + conv state pool** | one fixed-size state matrix *per request*, not per token — there is no position axis to shard. SGLang: *"The KDA state pool is the concurrency ceiling — DP, EP, and DCP do not shard it; only attention-TP width, SSM dtype, and cache strategy change the per-GPU bill"* ([architecture.md §5.3](./architecture.md)) | 428.6 MiB/slot ÷ attnTp; × S=5 |
| **MLA KV under TP** | MLA has **one** KV head, so TP cannot split it by head — every TP rank holds a full copy unless DCP is on ([serving-optimizations.md §3.1](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)) | 8× replication at TP8 without DCP |
| **BF16 non-expert block under DP-attention** | DP-attention replicates attention weights per rank by construction | 114.4 GB/rank (61 GB KDA + 11 GB MLA + shared experts + latent projections + lm_head) |
| **Per-layer norms, AttnRes rank-1 projections, router `e_score_correction_bias`** | rank-1 / vector tensors, sharding them costs more comm than it saves | ~60 MB/GPU, negligible |
| **`embed_tokens` (2.35 GB)** | can be vocab-sharded, but it is a gather not a stream; engines commonly replicate | ≤ 2.35 GB/GPU if replicated |
| **Speculative draft weights** | a whole second model, replicated per rank | 4.5 GB (`RadixArk/Kimi-K3-DSpark`), 9.5 GB (`RedHatAI/Kimi-K3-speculator.dspark`), ~5 GB (`Inferact/Kimi-K3-DSpark`) |
| **Draft KV (~1,400 B/token)** | *"replicated on every rank (not DCP-sharded)"* ([architecture.md §7.5](./architecture.md)) | under DCP8 it roughly doubles effective per-token KV |

## 3. Throughput and latency

### 3.1 MBU / MFU assumptions — calibrated, not assumed

METHODOLOGY §4's generic band for Blackwell is MBU 0.5–0.7 / MFU 0.2–0.35 at FP4, and
[gpus/gb300.md §9.4](../../gpus/gb300.md) argues for MBU 0.60–0.75 on GB300. **Neither is
right for this model**, and we know that because SGLang publishes three measured B300 cells
for Kimi-K3 from which [architecture.md §6.4](./architecture.md) back-computes an implied
MBU of **25 % / 39 % / 44 %** at batch 1 / 16 / 64. The reasons are structural and carry to
GB300 unchanged (identical 8.0 TB/s): 93 sequential layers of small per-GPU GEMMs under
TP8, and a batch-1 step that is 81 % BF16 non-expert bytes.

So this document uses a **model-calibrated MBU ladder** anchored on those three points and
log-interpolated, with values beyond batch 64 extrapolated (+0.02 per doubling, capped
0.50) and marked ⚠️:

```
    batch   1 -> MBU 0.250     batch  32 -> MBU 0.415     batch 128 -> MBU 0.460 (extrapolated)
    batch   8 -> MBU 0.355     batch  64 -> MBU 0.440     batch 256 -> MBU 0.480 (extrapolated)
    batch  16 -> MBU 0.390
  decode check, 8x B300 ctx 8192: b=1 8.65 ms (meas 8.51) | b=16 19.36 ms (meas 19.47) | b=64 40.41 ms (meas 40.19)
```

Residual ≤ 1.6 % on all three. **Decode TPOT on GB300 ≈ decode TPOT on HGX B300**, because
the two parts share 8.0 TB/s per GPU and decode is bandwidth-bound; GB300's +11 % on math
rates does not show up until prefill. This is exactly what the cross-GPU measurement shows:
SGLang B300 conc-1 TPOT 8.51 ms vs vLLM **GB300** TP8 bs=1 at 111 tok/s/user = **9.01 ms**.

For prefill, the raw roofline (FP4 MFU 0.25 / BF16 0.40 / attn 0.50 per METHODOLOGY §4) is
calibrated against the same measurement set:

```
  raw roofline TTFT, 8x B300, ISL 8192, conc 1 : 161.5 ms
  SGLang measured (architecture.md §10.1)      : 378 ms
  => lambda = 0.427  -> effective MFU FP4 0.107 / BF16 0.171 / attn 0.214
  8x GB300 single-stream prefill: 3,010 tok/s/GPU (vs 8x B300 2,709 = +11.1%)
```

An effective BF16 MFU of 0.17 is low, and it is consistent with everything else known about
this pair: a 93-layer graph, half the active FLOPs stuck at BF16, and SGLang's own
"Pre-release / Final Verification In Progress" labelling. **Treat it as a 2026-09-19
software number, not a hardware ceiling** — GB300 software gained 2.77× on DeepSeek-R1 in
two quarters on identical silicon ([gpus/gb300.md §10.1](../../gpus/gb300.md)).

**End-to-end validation of the combined model** against the one fully-specified published
serving cell for this model family:

```
  SGLang B300 1x8 Balanced (DCP8), ISL 8192 / OSL 1024, conc 64:
    modelled median TTFT 12.10 s (meas 11.635) ; node output 1,231 tok/s (meas 1,241)
```

−0.8 % on throughput, +4.0 % on TTFT. The TTFT model is `median TTFT ≈ (b/2) × ISL × (1−h) /
aggregate_prefill_rate` — i.e. a burst arrival where all `b` requests queue for prefill,
which is what a fixed-concurrency benchmark harness produces.

### 3.5 Explaining the gaps between estimate and measurement

**(a) The InferenceX GB300 TPOT is *faster* than the no-spec roofline.** At conc 1 the
measured 3.85 ms beats the model's 8.86 ms; at conc 48 the measured 26.60 ms beats 38.61 ms.
This is not a modelling error — those rows have a draft model on (`spec_method` set), so
their TPOT is **per accepted token**. Dividing gives an implied acceptance length:

```
    conc   1: model_TPOT/meas_TPOT = 2.30
    conc   4: model_TPOT/meas_TPOT = 2.55
    conc  48: model_TPOT/meas_TPOT = 1.45
    conc  52: model_TPOT/meas_TPOT = 1.29
    conc  56: model_TPOT/meas_TPOT = 1.19
```

2.3–2.55 at low batch decaying to ~1.2 at batch 56 is exactly the DSpark batch-decay shape
SGLang measured on B300 (3.00× → 1.97× → 1.64×) and matches the published K3 DSpark
acceptance figures for non-code traffic (chat ~2.7, creative 2.61,
[serving-optimizations.md §2.3](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). The model
and the measurement agree once speculation is accounted for.

**(b) The InferenceX GB300 TTFT is far *below* the burst model.** Measured 1,193 ms at conc
48 vs a burst-model 43.5 s. Reconciled:

```
    aggregate prefill rate at ISL 136,747        :     15694 tok/s (1962/GPU)
    measured mean_qps 1.09246, GPU hit 0.7917 + CPU hit 0.1511 = 0.9428 combined
    uncached prefill demand = 1.09246 x 136747 x (1-0.9428) =      8545 tok/s = 54.4% of capacity
    => no prefill queue; single-request TTFT = 136747 x 0.0572 / 15694 = 0.50 s vs measured median 1.19 s
```

Agentic traces arrive at a rate the prefill engine sustains (54 % utilisation) *and* carry a
94 % combined cache hit, so there is no queue. The residual 0.50 → 1.19 s is chunked-prefill
scheduling and the Mooncake DRAM fetch. **The lesson for sizing: the burst column in §3.2 is
a worst case; a real agentic workload lands on the steady column.**

**(c) TP16 buys almost nothing over TP8 on decode.** Measured 111 → 118 tok/s/user is
**+6.3 % for 2× the GPUs**, i.e. a TP-scaling efficiency of **0.53** against the roofline's
implied 2×. A naive roofline that halves TPOT when you double the GPU count overstates 16-,
36- and 72-GPU decode by ~8×. The mechanism is METHODOLOGY §4's `comm_overhead` term plus
GEMM shapes: at TP16 each rank's slice of a 3072×3584 expert is 224 columns wide, and there
are 93 sequential layers of them. **Therefore this document does not model a wide TP
replica; it models the rack as N independent 8-GPU replicas:**

| shape | GPUs | replicas | S1 concurrency | S1 out tok/s | per GPU |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 replica (2 trays) | 8 | 1 | 96 | 2,026 | 253.3 |
| 2 replicas (4 trays) | 16 | 2 | 192 | 4,052 | 253.3 |
| 4 replicas (8 trays) | 32 | 4 | 384 | 8,105 | 253.3 |
| **9 replicas (18 trays = full rack)** | **72** | **9** | **864** | **18,234** | **253.3** |

This is also what every GB300 deployment in §3.4 actually does: CoreWeave runs 9 replicas
per rack, Dynamo's best GB300 cell is *3 replicas × 8 GPUs*, and InferenceX benchmarks
8-GPU replicas. **Per-GPU throughput on this pair is flat in rack size.**

**(d) DEP16 does not beat 8-GPU replicas on throughput per GPU.** TRT-LLM's measured
**239.0 out tok/s/GPU at conc 256** (TPOT 62.24 ms, TTFT 4.6 s) on 16 GPUs sits **below** the
modelled aggregated S4 point of **344.4 out tok/s/GPU** on 8 GPUs — and per-GPU throughput is
flat in replica count, so two 8-GPU replicas on those same 16 GPUs would also be at 344.4.
Its measured conc-64 point, 104.3 out tok/s/GPU, is further below still. **DEP16's case is
not aggregate throughput — it is that a single logical replica holds 256 concurrent requests
behind one queue and one prefix cache**, at 210 GB/rank. That is a scheduling and cache-hit
argument, not a FLOPs argument, and it is worth roughly the −30 % per-GPU throughput only if
your traffic's cross-request prefix sharing is high enough to pay it back.

**(e) The model omits the vision tower.** Correctly, per §2: the ViT is 1.2 % of the cost of
the tokens it produces, and both GB300 recipes run it data-parallel across ranks.

Measured energy, for what it is worth: the InferenceX GB300 rows report
**7.42 J per output token** at conc 48 (7,750 W average total GPU power across 8 GPUs = 969 W
per GPU under agentic load) — between the two TDP claims and closer to Lenovo's.

### 4.2 Cost at the operating points

`cost_per_1M = n_gpus × price / (aggregate_tokens_per_s × 3600) × 1e6` (METHODOLOGY §6).
Input rate is the 8-GPU replica's aggregate prefill **capacity**, 24,497 tok/s =
**3,062 tok/s/GPU** — per METHODOLOGY §6 the input cost is priced against the prefill rate,
i.e. it is the cost of a saturated prefill engine. At the S1 operating point the engine is
only 66 % prefill-loaded, so a deployment that never saturates prefill pays more per input
token than the table shows and correspondingly less per output token; the *blended* column is
the one that is insensitive to that split.

Blended = `0.75 × (0.5 × miss + 0.5 × 0.1 × miss) + 0.25 × out` = `0.4125 × $/1M-in + 0.25 ×
$/1M-out`, per METHODOLOGY §6 verbatim (own-serving cached tokens at 10 % of uncached).

| operating point | $/GPU-h tier | $/1M output | $/1M input | **blended $/1M** |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **S1 (TPOT ≤ 50 ms)**, batch 96, 253.3 out tok/s/GPU | **low = high = res1y $18.00 (OCI)** | **$19.74** | **$1.633** | **$5.61** |
| ″ | SA retail $5.00 | $5.48 | $0.454 | $1.56 |
| ″ | SA volume $2.31 | $2.53 | $0.210 | $0.72 |
| ″ | colo-amortised $2.569 ⚠️ | $2.82 | $0.233 | $0.80 |
| **S4 (max throughput, h=0)**, batch 147, 344.4 out tok/s/GPU | **low = high = res1y $18.00 (OCI)** | **$14.52** | **$1.633** | **$4.30** |
| ″ | SA retail $5.00 | $4.03 | $0.454 | $1.20 |
| ″ | SA volume $2.31 | $1.86 | $0.210 | $0.55 |
| ″ | colo-amortised $2.569 ⚠️ | $2.07 | $0.233 | $0.61 |

### 4.3 Effect of prefix caching and speculative decoding

**Prefix caching** does two separate things and they must not be conflated:

1. It reduces the *input* bill directly. Per METHODOLOGY §6's own-serving convention (a hit
   costs 10 % of an uncached prefill), the effective $/1M input at $18.00/GPU-h is:

   | hit rate | effective $/1M input |
   |---:|---:|
   | 0 % | $1.633 |
   | 50 % | $0.898 |
   | 90 % | $0.310 |
   | 92 % (OpenRouter's measured K3 rate) | $0.281 |

2. It **frees prefill capacity, which raises the batch you can run**, because §3.3 shows this
   replica hits the prefill wall before the decode wall. At a *fixed* batch caching barely
   moves output cost — the value is that it moves the wall:

   | hit rate | at fixed S1 batch 96 | | | max batch at ρ ≤ 0.90 | | |
   |---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | | prefill ρ | TTFT | $/1M out | batch | out tok/s/GPU | $/1M out |
   | **0 %** | 66.2 % | 331 ms | $19.74 | **147** | 344.4 | **$14.52** |
   | **50 %** | 33.4 % | 105 ms | $19.56 | **173** (KV cap) | 407.9 | **$12.26** |
   | **90 %** | 6.7 % | 17 ms | $19.49 | **173** (KV cap) | 409.6 | **$12.21** |

   So prefix caching is worth **−17 % on $/1M output** (via the raised batch) and **−81 % on
   $/1M input** (directly), plus a **19× better TTFT** — and it costs one flag. Moonshot
   reports *">90 % cache hit rate in coding workloads"* and OpenRouter measures **92 %** on
   real K3 traffic; the GB300 InferenceX rows measure **60–97 %** server GPU hit plus 15–32 %
   CPU-tier hit. **For agentic traffic the 90 % row is the realistic one.** Note the trap:
   prefix caching is **off by default for K3** in vLLM and is coarse without
   `--prefix-match-unit 128`.

**Speculative decoding** multiplies decode throughput, hence divides $/1M output — but the
multiplier decays with batch, and at the S1 batch of 96 the relevant multiplier is the
high-batch one:

| multiplier | provenance | $/1M out at $18.00 |
|---:|---|---:|
| ×2.98 | **measured**, vLLM GB300 TP8 bs=1: 111 → 331 tok/s | $19.74 → **$6.62** |
| ×1.97 | **measured**, SGLang B300 conc 16: TPOT 19.47 → 9.88 ms | $19.74 → **$10.02** |
| ×1.64 | **measured**, SGLang B300 conc 64: TPOT 40.19 → 24.47 ms | $19.74 → **$12.04** |
| ×~1.2–1.45 | **derived from the GB300 InferenceX rows** at conc 48–56 (§3.5a) | $19.74 → $13.6–16.5 |

⚠️ **Distinguish measured acceptance from benchmark constants.** SGLang's B300 DSPARK cells
pin `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=4.5` — the engine reports *what block size 7 delivers at that
acceptance*, not a measured acceptance for the workload, and SGLang says so
([architecture.md §7.4](./architecture.md)). The MI355X DeepSeek recipe's DSpark **3.51** is
likewise a `"rejection_sample_method":"synthetic"` constant
([serving-optimizations.md §2.7](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). The
*genuinely measured* K3 figures are RadixArk's offline `acc_len` table (HumanEval 5.51 →
AIME26 2.99), vLLM's macro-average **4.11**, SGLang's chat **~2.7** / few-shot-math **~5.0**,
and the 1.19–2.55 implied from the GB300 InferenceX rows in §3.5a. **Use 1.2–2.0 for a
batched GB300 deployment, not 4.5.**

### 4.4 Comparison to the vendor API, and break-even utilisation

Moonshot's published price for `kimi-k3`
([architecture.md §11](./architecture.md), [platform.kimi.ai](https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/pricing)):
**$3.00/1M cache-miss input · $0.30/1M cache-hit input · $15.00/1M output**, 1 M context.

| | Self-hosted, 8× GB300 @ $18.00/GPU-h | Moonshot API |
|---|---:|---:|
| $/1M output | **$19.74** (S1) / $14.52 (S4) / **$6.62–12.04 with spec** | **$15.00** |
| $/1M input | **$1.633** (0 % hit) / $0.310 (90 % hit) | $3.00 miss / $0.30 hit |
| Blended (75 % in, half cached / 25 % out) | **$5.61** (S1) / $4.30 (S4) | **$4.99** |

Break-even $/GPU-hour at 100 % utilisation, S1 token mix (4,096 in : 512 out):

| operating point | req/h | API revenue/h (8 GPUs) | break-even $/GPU-h | all-cache-hit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S1, batch 96 | 14,248 | $284.51 | **$35.56** | $15.87 |
| S4, batch 147 | 19,372 | $386.83 | **$48.35** | $21.57 |

Against the one published GB300 rate of $18.00/GPU-h:

| | utilisation needed to break even |
|---|---:|
| S1, all cache-miss traffic | **50.6 %** |
| S4, all cache-miss traffic | **37.2 %** |
| S1, Moonshot-like (all cache-hit) traffic | **113 %** — never breaks even |
| S4, Moonshot-like (all cache-hit) traffic | **83.4 %** — breaks even only at near-perfect utilisation |

**The honest reading.** On cache-miss-heavy traffic, self-hosting Kimi-K3 on a GB300 rack
beats the Moonshot API above roughly **37–51 %** utilisation, and the margin widens with
speculation. On the agentic, highly-cacheable traffic this model is actually built for —
where Moonshot charges $0.30 for input and OpenRouter measures a 92 % hit rate — **the API
wins unless you can keep the rack above ~92 % busy**, because a cache hit costs Moonshot
almost nothing and costs you almost nothing too, so the 10× discount transfers straight to
them. This mirrors the B300 conclusion in [architecture.md §11.1](./architecture.md), shifted
by GB300's higher price: B300 at $7.40 (Hyperstack) makes self-hosting easy; **GB300 at
$18.00 makes it a utilisation bet**, and GB300's advantage over B300 for this pair is
capacity and domain size, not tokens per dollar.

The obvious corollary: **if you can get GB300 at anything near the $2.31–$5.00 ownership
anchors, every one of these conclusions flips** — blended $0.72–$1.56 against the API's
$4.99. GB300 is quote-only from seven of eight providers precisely because that gap exists.

## 5. Scaling and deployment shape

### 5.1 The recommended shape

**One replica = 8 GPUs = 2 NVL4 trays, TP8 + DCP8, MXFP4 weights, FP8 KV, bf16 KDA state,
prefix caching on, DSpark on. Nine replicas per rack behind a KV-aware router.**

Rationale, in order of weight:
1. §3.5c — TP beyond 8 buys **+6.3 % for 2× the GPUs**. There is no throughput reason to go
   wider.
2. §1.1 — 8 is the minimum that fits, and it leaves 49.9 GB/GPU of KV budget, enough for 169
   concurrent 8 K requests (383 with bf16 state).
3. §1.2 — DEP needs 16 GPUs and replicates 114.4 GB/rank to save 90 GB of experts. A bad
   trade for a model that is only 7.3 % non-expert by bytes.
4. It is what every GB300 deployment in §3.4 converged on independently.

### 5.3 When wide-EP pays for this pair

**Rarely, and not for the usual reason.** The canonical NVL72 wide-EP argument is DeepSeek-R1's
256 routed experts needing 64 GPUs to hold them once during decode
([gpus/gb300.md §10.6](../../gpus/gb300.md)). Kimi-K3 inverts it: **896 experts but only
33.03 M params each** (the Stable LatentMoE trick — experts live in a 3584-d latent, so each
is half the size a 7168-d expert would be). At TP8 the entire expert bank is 180.8 GB/GPU and
already resident. **There is no "experts do not fit" problem to solve on GB300.**

Wide-EP pays in exactly three situations:

1. **You want one logical replica with very high concurrency.** DEP16 holds 256 concurrent
   requests in one queue at a measured 239 out tok/s/GPU (TRT-LLM). Nine independent 8-GPU
   replicas give more aggregate throughput but nine queues and nine cold prefix caches.
2. **Prefill.** SGLang measured on GB300 that for K3 prefill, **PP8 × TP1 reaches ~1.7× TEP8's
   ceiling** with lower TTFT, because `--tp-size 1` is also what buys context (above TP1 the
   MLA KV is replicated across TP ranks). A K3 prefill node running PP8 has
   **1.45–1.72× the prefill capacity of a TEP8 node**, so one prefill node feeds several
   decode nodes ([serving-optimizations.md §3.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)).
   Note this is *pipeline*, not expert, parallelism.
3. **Below concurrency ~8** the pipeline cannot fill and TEP leads instead (1,947 vs 1,227
   prefill tok/s/GPU on GB200) — *"use `--tp-size 16 --ep-size 16` there"*.

⚠️ **EP72 is a ceiling, not an operating point.** SGLang on GB300 used Wide-EP up to **32
GPUs** for 128 K decode, not 72 ([gpus/gb300.md §10.6](../../gpus/gb300.md)), and DeepEP
publishes **no SM103 row** at all. The 896-expert × EP72 = 12.4-experts-per-GPU shape is
inside SemiAnalysis's wide-EP band but **has no published measurement for Kimi-K3** ⚠️.

### 5.4 When PD disaggregation pays

§3.3 gives the quantitative trigger: **PD pays when the aggregated replica's prefill
utilisation ρ approaches 1.** For this pair on 8 GB300s that is:

| scenario | prefill ρ at the S1 batch, 0 % hit | verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| S1 (4 K in) | 66 % at batch 96, 99 % at batch 160 | aggregated is fine to batch ~128 |
| S2 (32 K in) | 160 % at batch 32 | **PD or prefix caching required** |
| S3 (128 K in) | 410 % at batch 32 | **PD required** |

The measured GB300 PD numbers confirm the trade rather than a free win:

| engine | shape | TPS/user | out tok/s/GPU |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| vLLM | aggregated, 16 GPU, DCP16+TEP16, conc 24 | 57.8 | **62.2** |
| vLLM | **1P1D**, 16 GPU, conc 16 | **90.0** (+56 %) | 50.3 (−19 %) |
| SGLang | aggregated ×3 replicas, 24 GPU, conc 54 | 51.3 | **84.4** |
| SGLang | **1P1D**, 16 GPU, conc 18 | **75.9** (+48 %) | 61.0 (−28 %) |

**PD buys ~50 % interactivity and costs ~20–28 % throughput at rack scale on this pair.** That
is consistent with the general finding that *"disaggregation is a thousand-GPU problem"* —
below that, rounding losses on whole GPUs eat the specialisation gain
([serving-optimizations.md §3.5](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)). Note also
the best per-GPU throughput cell in Dynamo's whole GB300 table is the **3-replica aggregated**
one — which is §5.1's recommendation.

⚠️ **PD on a hybrid model is unforgiving.** vLLM: the recurrent KDA state, the paged MLA KV
*and* the block tables all have to arrive correctly; the NIXL connector treats the shared
page as two logical views and **zeroes untransferred tail regions** to stop stale data
leaking through padding gaps. SGLang PD gotchas: the positional `8998` after `--prefill` must
match `--disaggregation-bootstrap-port` or *"only the decode worker registers"*, and
`--disaggregation-decode-extra-slots` must be pinned (unpinned it defaults to twice the batch
below 32 requests and **zero above**). With speculation on, **DSpark must run in both pools**.


Azure's `Standard_ND128isr_GB300_v6` ships **16 TiB of local NVMe per 4-GPU VM**
([gpus/gb300.md §8.1](../../gpus/gb300.md)), so a 1.56 TB checkpoint is 10 % of one tray's
scratch and both trays of a replica can hold a local copy. Budget:

| phase | time | note |
|---|---|---|
| NVMe → HBM, `--load-format fastsafetensors` | **1.3–5.2 min** | 96 shards, 17.0 GB median |

| KV pool + KDA state pool allocation | seconds | 195.1 GB weights + ~50 GB pools per GPU |

⚠️ **End-to-end time-to-ready on GB300 is not published.** Plan **10–20 minutes** from a warm

## 6. Risks and open questions

### 6.1 Everything marked ⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED in this document

1. **MXFP4 vs NVFP4 rate parity on sm_103.** Both use the block-scaled datapath and
   FlashInfer implements both, but **NVIDIA publishes no separate MXFP4 line**
   ([gpus/gb300.md §11 gotcha 13](../../gpus/gb300.md)). Every FP4 figure in §3 assumes
   parity at 15,000 TFLOPS dense. If MXFP4 runs slower, the 47 %-of-FLOPs expert term
   degrades — but since the calibration factor λ was fitted to a *measured* MXFP4 run, the
   §3 tables absorb any such penalty; only the "what if the software improves" ceiling moves.
2. **W4A8 execution semantics.** The engines describe the MoE path as **W4A8** (FP4 weights,
   FP8 activations). Which tensor-core rate that actually achieves on sm_103 — the 15,000
   FP4 line or the 5,000 FP8 line — is not documented anywhere fetched. Same absorption
   argument as (1), but it matters for projecting improvement.
3. **No measured GB300 cell at 32 K, 128 K or 1 M context with a stated ISL/OSL.** Every
   published K3 GB300 number is either 8K/1K or an agentic trace with `isl: null`. The S2/S3
   rows in §3.2 are pure model.
4. **No published MBU or MFU for GB300 specifically**, for any model
   ([gpus/gb300.md §9.4](../../gpus/gb300.md)). §3.1's ladder is a B300 transfer justified by
   identical 8.0 TB/s bandwidth and corroborated by the 111-vs-8.51 ms agreement, but it is a
   transfer.
5. **TP-scaling efficiency beyond 16.** §3.5c's 0.53 factor rests on **one** measured pair
   (111 → 118). Whether TP32 or TP72 is 0.3 or 0.05 efficient is unmeasured; this document
   sidesteps it by modelling replicas, which is also what every deployment does.
6. **EP72 / wide-EP on Kimi-K3 has no published measurement.** DeepEP has **no SM103 row**;
   SGLang's GB300 wide-EP work tops out at 32 GPUs; EPLB on GB300 is unmeasured.
7. **SGLang's GB300 cells are not `Verified`.** *"Outside the two verified B300 1×8 Unified
   cells, no cell has a serving round in this exact shape."* And: *"Accuracy has not been
   re-measured on any cell."*
8. **LightSeek's GB300 Part II on expert parallelism is "coming soon"** with no URL — the one
   source that would answer (6) does not exist yet.
9. **No MLPerf submission for Kimi-K3** on GB300 or anywhere. MLPerf v6.0 GB300 results cover
   DeepSeek-R1, Llama-3.1-405B, gpt-oss-120b and Qwen3-VL-235B.
10. **GB300 per-GPU power: 1,400 W (gpus/gb300.md §1.1) vs 1,100 W (cloud-pricing.md §8.1,

    largest capacity lever (+127 % concurrency at 8 K) and SGLang recommends it in the
    32-GPU preset, but the F32 conv kernels and decay parameterisation are the numerically
    sensitive tensors and *"must not be silently downcast"*
    ([architecture.md §3.5](./architecture.md)). Downcasting the *state* is not the same as
    downcasting the kernels, but no eval was found either way.
12. **`S` (state slots per request) under vLLM on GB300.** METHODOLOGY §8 pins S = 5 from
    SGLang's `extra_buffer` default; vLLM's hybrid KV manager allocates "≥ 1 plus speculative
    slots". Every concurrency number in §1.3 is SGLang-shaped. vLLM's ReplaySSM result
    ("effective capacity rises 10.97 % under TP8") suggests a different baseline; reconciling

13. **Time-to-ready** (§5.5) — no published warm-up figure for this pair on GB300.
14. **`GB300 NVL4` as a tray SKU name.** vLLM's hardware profile says "GB300 NVL4 · 4 GPUs
    (NVL72 tray unit)" but **NVIDIA documents GB300 only as NVL72**
    ([architecture.md verification-log row 8](./architecture.md)). The 4-GPU/288 GB tray is

15. **The InferenceX `spec_method: "mtp"` label on K3 rows.** Kimi-K3 has **no MTP head**

    certainly a draft model bucketed as `mtp`; which draft, and its acceptance, are not in
    the API response. §3.5a's implied acceptances are derived, not read.
16. **Whether `KimiK3ForConditionalGeneration`'s MLA layers are wired to vLLM's generic MLA
    backend registry at all**, or to a model-private backend — `vllm/models/kimi_k3/` has
    both `nvidia/` and `amd/` branches, suggesting private wiring

### 6.3 Accuracy caveats of the executed precision

- **The MXFP4 weights are quantization-aware-trained from the SFT stage onward.** There is
  **no BF16 Kimi-K3 checkpoint** to compare against — the model card's own GPQA Diamond 93.5
  *is* the MXFP4 number. So "MXFP4 accuracy loss" is not a measurable quantity for this
  model, which is a genuinely unusual and favourable position
  ([architecture.md §1.3, §12 Q17](./architecture.md)).
- **The one real quantisation datapoint is the NVFP4 transcode**: `RedHatAI/Kimi-K3-NVFP4`
  measures **GPQA Diamond 91.0 vs 93.5 = 97.33 % recovery**. A 2.5-point GPQA drop is not
  small for a frontier model. `nvidia/Kimi-K3-NVFP4` (the build LightSeek's GB300 recipe
  runs) publishes **no evals at all** ⚠️, and it additionally drops attention from BF16 to
  128×128 per-block FP8 — a second, unmeasured precision change.
- **FP8 KV carries no checkpoint scales** (`kv_cache_scheme: null`), so the scaling is
  runtime-calibrated per engine. FP8 KV is standard practice on GB300 (every MLPerf and
  InferenceX submission uses it) and SGLang measured LongBench-v2 57.2 % non-MTP on
  DeepSeek-R1-NVFP4 + FP8 KV at 128 K, i.e. not visibly lossy in that eval — but **no K3-specific
  FP8-KV accuracy measurement exists** ⚠️.
- **SGLang states plainly: "Accuracy has not been re-measured on any cell — re-measure before
  you rely on one."** That includes both GB300 cells.
- **Thinking is always on and must be echoed back** — reasoning tokens accumulate in the
  prompt across agentic turns, so effective input length per turn grows much faster than for
  a model that discards thinking, and `reasoning_effort` (default `max`) dominates output
  volume and therefore cost. This is a cost risk, not an accuracy one, but it is the one most
  likely to make a real bill differ from §4.
- **A serving-side quality note with a direct cost consequence:** the model card reports
  BrowseComp **91.2 with context compaction triggered at 300 K** versus **90.4 with the full
  1 M window and no context management**. Compaction at 300 K *beat* the full window. Given
  §1.3 shows 1 M context costs 23 concurrent requests per 8-GPU replica against 147 at 32 K,
  **serving at 300 K is both cheaper and, on that benchmark, better.**
