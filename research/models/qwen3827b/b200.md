## 0. Verdict

1. **Runnable today, but nobody has run it in public.** vLLM ships a **B200 hardware profile
   with a working TP1 launch command** for this exact checkpoint, and its `fp8`, `nvfp4`
   (Inferact) and `int4` variants all list `b200` in `supported_hardware` — yet `b200` is **not**
   in the recipe's verified set (`gb300`, `rtx_5090`, `rtx_5090_2x`, `rtx_pro_6000`,
   `dgx_spark_gb10`, `ascend_950pr` are) and SGLang's cookbook has **no B200 cell at all**
   [src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/hw/b200.json) [src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B) [src](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B.md).
   Engine: **vLLM 0.29.0** (min 0.17.0; ≥ 0.28.0 for DFlash2), image `vllm/vllm-openai:qwen38`; SGLang 0.5.20 works in principle but read §6 first.
2. **Min 1 GPU, recommended 1 GPU.** TP1 at every precision; scale with **8 DP replicas per
   HGX node**, not with TP. TP4 exists only to buy KV headroom (4.6× concurrency at 8K); TP8
   starts replicating KV heads (the model has only 4) and TP16 crosses the fabric for nothing.
3. **Weight format actually executed: NVFP4 W4A4 on the 5th-gen tensor cores (9,000 TFLOPS
   dense), with the attention and GDN projections at FP8** — that is what the NVFP4 checkpoints
   contain. FP8 (4,500 TFLOPS) is the conservative alternative; **MXFP4 does not load on NVIDIA**
   and INT4 W4A16 runs on the **BF16** math line via Marlin, so it is a memory trick, not a speed one.
4. **Interactive (S1, 4K in / 512 out, TPOT ≤ 50 ms): $0.185–$0.433 per 1M output tokens**
   (`est.`, conc 128, TPOT 14.2 ms, 8,989 tok/s/GPU) at $6.00–$14.00/GPU-hr; **$0.235–$0.548
   derated to B200's one published MBU measurement**. Blended (METHODOLOGY §6) **$0.063–$0.148 per 1M**.
5. **Max throughput (S4): $0.150–$0.351 per 1M output** (conc 242 — the KV ceiling at `S = 5`,
   TPOT 21.8 ms, 11,082 tok/s/GPU), or **$0.128–$0.298** at conc 573 with `--disable-radix-cache`.
   **Confidence: `estimate`.** Zero measurements exist for this (model, GPU) pair; every
   throughput number here is roofline, and the nearest real B200 datum for the *architecture
   family* (§3.2) is a bug report, not a benchmark.

## 1. Fit

Hardware is hardware: this section is true whether or not an engine cooperates. Per
METHODOLOGY §3 with the §8 pins — **B200 = 180 GB as deployed** (not 192),
`usable = 0.90 × 180 GB = 162.0 GB (150.9 GiB)`, activation workspace **4 GB** at 8K
(METHODOLOGY §3 band 2–6 GB; [gpus/b200.md §2](../../gpus/b200.md) warns the 10 % haircut is
"not conservative" once NVFP4 weights, paged KV, CUDA-graph pools and FlashInfer autotune
workspaces coexist).

Model inputs, all pinned in METHODOLOGY §8 and re-derived in
[architecture.md §5](./architecture.md):

```
KV/token   = 2 × n_kv_heads(4) × head_dim(256) × B × 16 full-attention layers
             = 65,536 B at BF16, 32,768 B at FP8          (48 GDN layers cache nothing)
state/slot = 48 layers × 48 v-heads × 128 × 128 × B_state + conv
             = 153,944,064 B (fp32)  /  78,446,592 B (bf16)
fixed_state_per_seq = S × state/slot        S = 5 (SGLang default `extra_buffer`), 1 with --disable-radix-cache
```

checkpoint at **21.92 GB**, which is the `nvidia/` and `RadixArk/` export. But vLLM's recipe
declares `nvidia/Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4`'s `supported_hardware` as **`["rtx_pro_6000",
"dgx_spark_gb10"]` only**, *disjoint* from the Inferact variant's
`["b200","b300","gb200","gb300","rtx_5090","rtx_5090_2x"]`
[src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B.json), a split that

deliberate correction, not a typo. **So the B200 path vLLM actually blesses is `Inferact`, at
26.38 GB — 4.46 GB larger than the METHODOLOGY §8 pin, i.e. ~3 % less KV budget.** This
document uses **26.381 GB** for every vLLM-path number and shows the 21.92 GB row alongside
(SGLang's RadixArk/NVIDIA path, which has no B200 cell anyway). Method to settle it: load both
on a B200 and read the reported weight bytes and KV pool size.

**Tensors that cannot be sharded** (they are replicated on every TP rank, so they set a floor):

| Tensor group | Params | BF16 bytes | Why it cannot shard |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| All RMSNorms + `q_norm`/`k_norm` + GDN `norm` | 679,424 | **1.36 MB** | Per-channel, elementwise; every rank needs the full vector |
| Vision tower (27 ViT blocks + merger) | 460,730,096 | **0.921 GB** | Shardable in principle, but vLLM's own B200 command pins `--mm-encoder-tp-mode data`, i.e. **data-parallel = replicated per GPU** [src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/hw/b200.json); it also stays **BF16 in every quantised checkpoint** ([architecture.md §3.2](./architecture.md)) |
| KV heads, for TP > 4 | — | — | `num_key_value_heads = 4`. At TP8 each rank holds 1 duplicated head ⇒ **2× aggregate KV bytes**; at TP16, 4× |
| GDN state, for TP > 16 | — | — | `linear_num_key_heads = 16` caps clean state sharding at 16 |

Replicated floor = **0.923 GB per GPU** regardless of TP degree and regardless of weight format.

### 1.2 Fit across the GPU counts this topology allows

HGX B200's NVLink domain is **8 GPUs at 1.8 TB/s per GPU**; beyond that you cross a 400 Gb/s
scale-out fabric — a 36× bandwidth cliff ([gpus/b200.md §4](../../gpus/b200.md)). Checkpoint =
Inferact NVFP4 26.381 GB.

| GPUs | Parallelism | Node shape | Weights/GPU GB | Act. ws GB | KV budget/GPU GB | KV/token/GPU (FP8) | State/GPU/slot MB | Multi-node fabric? |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | **TP1 (+ DP replicas)** | 1/8 node | 26.38 | 4 | **131.62** | 32,768 B | 78.45 | no |
| 2 | TP2 | 1/4 node | 13.65 | 4 | 144.35 | 16,384 B | 39.22 | no |
| 4 | TP4 | half node | 7.29 | 4 | 150.71 | 8,192 B | 19.61 | no |
| 8 | TP8 | full node, NVLink 8-GPU domain | 4.11 | 4 | 153.89 | 8,192 B (**×2 copies**) | 9.81 | no |
| 16 | TP16 | **2 nodes** | 2.51 | 4 | 155.49 | 8,192 B (**×4 copies**) | 4.90 | **yes — 400 Gb/s/GPU IB/Ethernet; pointless for a model that fits on one GPU** |

`max_concurrency(ctx) = floor( Σ_gpus KV_budget / (ctx × kv_bytes_per_token × replication + S × state) )`

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | BF16 | 5 | 141 | 51 | 14 | 7 | 1 |
| 1 | BF16 | 1 | 213 | 59 | 15 | 7 | 1 |
| 1 | **FP8** | **5** | **199** | **89** | **28** | **14** | **3** |
| 1 | FP8 | 1 | 379 | 114 | 30 | 15 | 3 |
| 2 | BF16 | 5 | 310 | 113 | 32 | 16 | 4 |
| 2 | BF16 | 1 | 469 | 129 | 33 | 16 | 4 |
| 2 | FP8 | 5 | 436 | 196 | 61 | 32 | 8 |
| 2 | FP8 | 1 | 832 | 250 | 66 | 33 | 8 |
| 4 | BF16 | 5 | 648 | 237 | 67 | 34 | 8 |
| 4 | BF16 | 1 | 979 | 270 | 69 | 34 | 8 |
| 4 | FP8 | 5 | **912** | 411 | 128 | 67 | 17 |
| 4 | FP8 | 1 | 1,737 | 523 | 137 | 69 | 17 |
| 8 | BF16 | 5 | 839 | 262 | 70 | 35 | 8 |
| 8 | BF16 | 1 | 1,068 | 281 | 71 | 35 | 8 |
| 8 | FP8 | 5 | 1,325 | 484 | 137 | 70 | 17 |
| 8 | FP8 | 1 | 2,000 | 553 | 142 | 71 | 17 |
| 16 | BF16 | 5 | 979 | 276 | 71 | 35 | 9 |
| 16 | FP8 | 5 | 1,697 | 530 | 141 | 71 | 18 |

Read the TP4 → TP8 step carefully: concurrency at 8K rises only 912 → 1,325 (1.45×, not 2×)
and at 128K it goes 128 → 137 (**1.07×**), because past 4 KV heads the cache is duplicated.
**TP8 is the worst value point in this table.**

Cross-checks: at the 21.92 GB NVFP4 pin, TP1/FP8/`S=5` gives **205 / 92 / 29 / 15 / 3** and
`S=1` gives **392 / 118 / 31 / 15 / 3** — identical to
[architecture.md §10.2](./architecture.md)'s B200 row (205 / 392 at 8K, 92 / 118 at 32K,
29 / 31 at 128K, 15 / 15 at 262K, 3 / 3 at 1M). The recomputation reproduces it exactly.

**State dtype is worth more than one GPU-generation of capacity at short context.** TP1, FP8 KV,
`S = 5`, Inferact NVFP4:

| GDN state dtype | 8K | 32K | 128K | 262K | 1M |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `float32` (the config's `mamba_ssm_dtype`, vLLM default) | 126 | 71 | 25 | 14 | 3 |
| `bfloat16` (`--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16`) | **199** | **89** | **28** | 14 | 3 |

+58 % concurrency at 8K for halving the state precision — and on `sm_100` it *also* unlocks the
FlashInfer GDN prefill fast path (§2). SGLang publishes the matching measurement on another
card: 97,280 KV tokens at bf16 vs 68,588 at fp32 on an RTX 5090
[src](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B.md).

**1M context.** Three concurrent 1M sequences fit on one B200 (NVFP4 + FP8 KV). At BF16 weights
with BF16 KV, one 1M sequence needs 68.9 GB of KV against a 102.4 GB budget — it fits, but only
one. Note METHODOLOGY §8's 1M path needs the YaRN override
([architecture.md §8.5](./architecture.md)) and static YaRN degrades short prompts.

**Where the crossover actually is, and a disagreement worth recording.**
[architecture.md §7.4](./architecture.md) estimates speculation stops paying at **bs ≈ 100 at 8K**
on the grounds that decode is only ~24 % weights-bound there. On B200 that is not the binding
criterion: a verify step re-reads the **same** KV and the **same** state as a plain step —
only the compute branch scales with `D`. So speculation stays nearly free while
`batch × D` sits below the NVFP4 ridge point of ~1,169 ([gpus/b200.md §9.1](../../gpus/b200.md)):
**MTP (D=4) is still bandwidth-bound at bs=128 (512 < 1,169); DFlash2 (D=8) crosses at bs≈64
(512) → 128 (1,024) and its speedup collapses from 4.36× to 2.54×.** ⚠️ Both statements are
`est.`; neither has been measured on a B200, and the real limiter may be the drafter's own
launch overhead, which this model has no published number for.

### 4.3 What speculative decoding and prefix caching do to the bill

**Prefix caching** acts entirely on the input side, and on this model it is worth more than any
other single lever (S2, 32K prompts, low price):

| Hit rate | TTFT | Effective $/1M input |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 % | 971 ms | $0.0494 |
| 50 % | 439 ms | $0.0223 |
| 90 % | **80 ms** | **$0.0041** |

At 90 % hit the blended S1 figure falls from $0.0633 to ≈ **$0.0480** per 1M tokens (low price):
`blended = 0.4125 × in(90 % hit, S1) + 0.25 × out(interactive) = 0.4125 × 0.0040 + 0.25 × 0.1854`.
The cost is `S = 5` state slots per request — i.e. 242 → (what you would have had at S=1) 573
concurrency. **On short-context traffic caching costs you 2.4× concurrency; on 32K traffic it
costs 1.28× (87 vs 111) and buys a 12× cheaper input token. Keep the cache on.**

**Speculative decoding** acts on the output side, and its effect on *cost* is much weaker than
its effect on *latency*, because at the operating batch the GPU is already saturated:

| Point | Plain | With MTP (`est.`, §3.4) | $/1M output (low), plain → MTP |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| S1, bs = 128 | 8,989 tok/s | 34,973 tok/s but max conc drops 242 → 153 | $0.185 → **$0.048** `est.` ⚠️ |
| S1, bs = 153 (the MTP-feasible ceiling) | 9,600 tok/s `est.` | ~37,000 tok/s `est.` | $0.174 → **$0.045** `est.` ⚠️ |

⚠️ Treat the 3.9× as a ceiling: it assumes the published mean accept length (4.28) survives at
batch 128 on a B200, which nobody has measured for this model on any GPU
([architecture.md §12](./architecture.md), open question 11). The safe planning claim is that
MTP is **free at bs ≤ 128** on B200 because `batch × D = 512 < 1,169`, and that it costs
**37 % of your concurrency** (242 → 153) unless you run `--enable-linear-replayssm-spec`.

### 4.4 Versus the vendor API, and break-even utilisation

From [architecture.md §11](./architecture.md): Qwen Cloud list is **$0.50 in / $3.00 out /
$0.05 explicit-cache read** per 1M; the real third-party market for FP8 serving is
**$0.20–0.30 in / $2.20–2.55 out** (OpenRouter), with Darkbloom's FP4 endpoint at $0.10 / $1.80.

| Price tier | $/1M out (S1 interactive) | vs Qwen $3.00 | **Break-even utilisation** | vs cheapest market FP8 out ($2.20) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| low $6.00 | $0.185 | **16.2× margin** | **6.2 %** | 8.4 % |
| high $14.00 | $0.433 | 6.9× | **14.4 %** | 19.7 % |
| res1y $5.10 | $0.158 | 19.0× | **5.3 %** | 7.2 % |

`break-even utilisation u* = (self-hosted cost at 100 % utilisation) / (API price)` — i.e. the
fraction of the rented hour that must actually be generating tokens at the S1 operating point
before self-hosting is cheaper than the API.

- **Output tokens: self-hosting wins at ~6 % utilisation on a $6.00 B200.** A single B200 at
  100 % utilisation emits 32.4 M output tokens per GPU-hour at the interactive point; at Qwen's
  $3.00 that is $97 of API value against $6.00 of rent.
- **Input tokens are much closer: `u*` is 8.2 % against Qwen's $0.50, but Qwen's *cached* input
  is $0.05, which is only 1.2× our uncached $0.041** — i.e. against a cache-heavy agentic
  workload priced at the vendor's cached rate, self-hosting's input-side advantage nearly
  vanishes. Derated to MBU 0.474 and using the market's $0.20 input price, input break-even is
  ~25 %.
- The honest summary is the same one [architecture.md §11.3](./architecture.md) reaches and this
  document's B200 numbers strengthen: **output-heavy reasoning traffic (and `reasoning_effort:
  xhigh` is the shipped default) self-hosts decisively; input-heavy traffic against a cached API
  rate does not.**

### 5.1 Single node, single GPU, eight replicas

| 8 × TP1 replicas (recommended) | **71,908 tok/s** | 1,024 | $48–$112 | $0.185–$0.433 |
| TP2 × 4 replicas | 64,468 tok/s `est.` | 1,024 | same | $0.207–$0.482 |
| TP4 × 2 replicas | 62,732 tok/s `est.` | 1,024 | same | $0.212–$0.496 |
| TP8 × 1 replica | 46,963 tok/s `est.` | 1,024 | same | $0.284–$0.662 |

(TP rows recomputed at MBU 0.60 with +10 % NVLink comm; the per-GPU byte count falls with TP but
not enough to pay for the collective, and TP8 additionally duplicates the 4 KV heads.)
**Use TP > 1 only when a single request needs more KV than one GPU holds** — i.e. above ~262K
context at BF16 weights, or when you want >28 concurrent 128K sessions in one engine process.

### 5.2 When PD disaggregation or wide-EP pays here: it does not

- **Wide-EP / DeepEP / EPLB: never.** Dense model, no experts.
- **PD disaggregation: no published path.** vLLM's `compatible_strategies` is `single_node_tp`
  only for this model; no Dynamo recipe exists; and whether the GDN recurrent state survives a
  NIXL handover is an open question ([architecture.md §12](./architecture.md), #12). The generic
  case for PD — decoupling a compute-bound prefill from a bandwidth-bound decode — is weak here
  anyway, because prefill at 4K costs 101 ms against a 14 ms decode step and the model fits in
  1/8 of a node, so the cheapest isolation is *a separate replica*, not a separate role.
- The one multi-GPU pattern that does pay is **EPD/encoder offload for heavy multimodal
  traffic**: the ViT is unwindowed and a single 16.78 Mpx image costs **590 TFLOP**
  ([architecture.md §6.4](./architecture.md)) — ~6× a 4K text prefill. NVIDIA measures 30 % lower
  TTFT and 25 % higher throughput on image requests under PD on GB200 for a comparable Qwen3-VL
  ([serving-optimizations.md §6.2](../../cross-cutting/serving-optimizations.md)) ⚠️ different
  model, cited as direction only.

> [src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B).

**SGLang — there is no published B200 cell.** The nearest published cells are GB300 (`sm_103`,
which shares every kernel gate with `sm_100`) and H200. Starting from the GB300 cell
[src](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B.md) and applying the SM100
constraints from [flash-attention.md §14.3](../../cross-cutting/flash-attention.md) and the
backend bug in [sglang#39515](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/39515):

```
# GB300 cell as published (all five checkpoints):
--trust-remote-code --model-path {{MODEL_NAME}} --kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3
--mem-fraction-static 0.85 --chunked-prefill-size 2048
--reasoning-parser qwen3 --tool-call-parser qwen3_coder --host {{HOST_IP}} --port {{PORT}}
```

For a B200, add — and these are this document's recommendations, **⚠️ not a published cell**:
`--attention-backend trtllm_mha` (never `flashinfer`: that is the SM120 recipe's flag and SGLang
forbids it for hybrid-GDN full attention on SM100), `--mamba-ssm-dtype bfloat16` (memory **and**
the FlashInfer GDN prefill fast path), and **do not pass `--page-size` if you also enable
speculation** (see §6).

### 5.4 Warm-up and load time

Checkpoint from NVMe at 5–20 GB/s:

| Checkpoint | at 5 GB/s | at 20 GB/s |
|---|---:|---:|
| BF16 55.563 GB | 11.1 s | 2.8 s |
| FP8 30.870 GB | 6.2 s | 1.5 s |
| NVFP4 21.92–26.38 GB | 4.4–5.3 s | 1.1–1.3 s |

That is the **floor**, and the published reality is far worse: SGLang reports **~6.5 minutes to
load the 18 BF16 shards from NVMe on a DGX Spark** and advises budgeting ~10 minutes to READY
[src](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B.md); the community RTX PRO
6000 report measures a **34.8 s cold start** after the weights are cached
[src](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/discussions/160). On a B200 add FlashInfer JIT /
autotune warm-up on first boot — [gpus/b200.md §7](../../gpus/b200.md) notes autotune has crashed
on SM100 NVFP4 paths before; `--moe-backend flashinfer_cutlass` is the documented workaround
(irrelevant for this dense model, but the JIT cost is not). **Plan 2–10 minutes to READY, not
seconds, and keep a warm model cache on local NVMe.**
