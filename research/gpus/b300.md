# NVIDIA B300 (Blackwell Ultra) — inference reference

**Research date: 2026-09-19.** Follows [`research/METHODOLOGY.md`](../METHODOLOGY.md) for all
formulas, the `[src]` / **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** / `est.` / `meas.` legend, and the
dense-vs-sparse and SXM-vs-PCIe separation rules.

**Primary target of this document: HGX B300 — 8× Blackwell Ultra SXM, air-cooled,
x86 host, compute capability 10.3 (`sm_103`).** The rack-scale GB300 NVL72 variant uses
the *same silicon at higher clocks and higher memory capacity* and is called out explicitly
wherever it differs, because the two are **not** interchangeable for capacity planning.

> **Headline correction for this repo.** The task brief — and, until it was fixed, this
> repo's own `README.md` — described "an 8×B300 node with **2304 GB** HBM". **That
> configuration does not exist as a shipping 8-GPU product**, and the README note now says
> so. 2,304 GB = 8 × 288 GB is the *physical per-die* figure.
>
> **The pinned as-deployed figure (METHODOLOGY §8) is 268 GB per GPU = 2,144 GB per 8-GPU
> node.** NVIDIA HGX B300 / DGX B300 advertise **"2.1 TB"**
> [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/hgx/) — that is 2,144 GB *rounded down*,
> not a different node: AWS's own P6-B300 blog prints **both** "2.1TB of GPU memory" in prose
> **and** "2144GB HBM3e" in its spec table for the same instance (re-fetched verbatim
> 2026-09-19) [src](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/accelerate-large-scale-ai-applications-with-the-new-amazon-ec2-p6-b300-instances).
> The conservative OEM/cloud variants are DGX B300 **262.5 GB** (2.1 TB ÷ 8) and OCI
> `BM.GPU.B300.8` **263 GB** (2,100 GB)
> [src](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Compute/References/computeshapes.htm);
> quote them where a specific SKU is at stake, and plan with **268 GB / 2,144 GB** otherwise.
>
> **GB300 NVL72 is a different number and must not be merged with the above: 288 GB per GPU,
> ≈ 279 GB usable** (METHODOLOGY §8) — corroborated by
> [OCI `BM.GPU.GB300.4` = 1,112 GB / 4 = 278 GB](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Compute/References/computeshapes.htm),
> [Lambda's MLPerf submission: "279 GB HBM3e per GPU"](https://lambda.ai/blog/lambdas-mlperf-inference-v6.0-hardware-leap-software-maturity-research-breakthrough)
> and NVIDIA's rack figure of 20 TB ÷ 72 = 277.8 GB.
> **Plan an 8× B300 node at 2,144 GB raw / 1,930 GB usable, not 2,304 GB and not 2,100 GB.**

| Memory bandwidth | **8 TB/s** [src](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-shares-blackwell-ultras-secrets-nvfp4-boost-detailed-and-pcie-6-0-support) — *unchanged from B200* | 64 TB/s aggregate | 8 TB/s (576 TB/s across 72) [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gb300-nvl72/) |
| **Usable after runtime overhead** (METHODOLOGY §3, ×0.90) | **241 GB** (from the pinned 268) `est.`; 236 GB on a 262.5 GB SKU | **1,930 GB** `est.` (1,890 GB on the conservative SKUs) | **251 GB** `est.` (from 279) |

**Never merge the two columns.** HGX/DGX B300 and AWS p6-b300 are **268 GB per GPU,
2,144 GB per node**. GB300 NVL72 is **288 GB per GPU physical, ≈ 279 GB usable**. Every
sizing figure below uses 268 / 2,144.

**Why the 288 / 279 / 268 / 262.5 spread exists.** Secondary reporting attributes it to
physical-vs-usable counting, ECC/row-repair reservation, and air-cooled vs liquid-cooled
SKU binning [src](https://www.spheron.network/blog/nvidia-b300-blackwell-ultra-guide/).
The *reconciliation arithmetic* (which exact reservation accounts for 288 → 279 → 268 →
262.5) is **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED**; NVIDIA has not published it. What is *not* in doubt is the
platform-level number, which three independent primary sources agree on: an 8-GPU B300
board carries **2.1 TB**, and a GB300 NVL72 rack carries **20 TB across 72 GPUs**.

**Key planning consequence:** B300 is **+49 % memory over B200 at identical bandwidth**
(268 GB vs 180 GB, both as deployed per METHODOLOGY §8; the old "+45 %" used 263 GB).
Per METHODOLOGY §4, decode step time is `bytes_per_decode_step / (HBM_BW × MBU)` — so
**B300 does not make decode faster than B200 for a fixed working set.** It makes *bigger*
working sets and *more concurrent sequences* fit, which raises aggregate throughput at a
fixed TPOT target. Every measured B300-vs-B200 result in §10 is consistent with this.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 (B=2) | **70,272 B** = 68.625 KiB | 0.536 | 2.145 | 8.578 | **162** `est.` |
| FP8 (B=1) | **35,136 B** = 34.313 KiB | 0.268 | 1.072 | 4.289 | **325** `est.` |

Per-token figures match METHODOLOGY §2's pinned DeepSeek-R1/V3 MLA values
(70,272 / 35,136 / 17,568 B per token at BF16 / FP8 / FP4). MLA caches one latent + rope key
per layer, so there is **no division by `n_kv_heads`**. The `max_concurrency` column is
METHODOLOGY §3's `floor(kv_budget / (ctx × kv_bytes_per_token))`; any batch above it is
`infeasible (KV)` on this node, not a number.

budget of **~867 GB** — **1.73× fewer 128K sequences** (recomputed 2026-09-19 at the pinned
2,144 GB B300 node; was 1.69× at 2,100 GB). That ratio, not FLOPS, is the main reason to
pick B300 over B200 for long-context serving.

### Tensor-core generation and the *mechanism* of the 1.5× FP4 uplift

Blackwell Ultra uses **5th-generation Tensor Cores** (PTX `tcgen05.mma` family), the same
generation as B200. The uplift is not more tensor cores — it is a **wider K dimension for
the FP4 MMA on `sm_103a` only**. From the PTX ISA target notes on `tcgen05.mma`:

> *"K = 96 is only supported for following architecture-specific targets: `sm_103a`, `sm_107a`"*
> — [PTX ISA §9.7.18.2.1.1 Target ISA Note](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html)

For `.kind::mxf4` / `.kind::mxf4nvf4`, B200 (`sm_100a`) issues K = 64 dense / K = 128 sparse;
B300 (`sm_103a`) can additionally issue **K = 96 dense / K = 192 sparse** — exactly
96/64 = **1.5×**. That is the entire FP4 story, expressed in the ISA. It also explains why
FP8/BF16/TF32 are unchanged: the widened K applies to the FP4 kinds only.

⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED (partial, 2026-09-19).** A direct fetch of the PTX ISA page confirms
that K = 96 scale-factor layouts exist for `tcgen05.mma` — the document carries sections
*"Layout of the Scale Factor A/B Matrix for block32 with K=96 (Semantically equivalent to
`scale_vec::3X`)"* and *"…for block16 with K=96 (Semantically equivalent to
`scale_vec::6X`)"*, and PTX ISA 9.4 does add `sm_103a` / `sm_107a` targets
[src](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html). But the exact
Target-ISA-Note sentence quoted above, the restriction of K = 96 to `sm_103a` / `sm_107a`,
the **K = 192 sparse** half of the claim, and the Table 70 family listing could **not** be
retrieved from the rendered page (the ISA document is served in fragments that omit
§9.7.18's target notes). Treat the *mechanism* as plausible and the *K = 96 vs K = 64
ratio as the sole explanation of the 1.5×* as unconfirmed — the 1.5× itself is independently
solid from NVIDIA's platform tables. Note also that the K = 96 layouts are documented as
`scale_vec::3X` / `scale_vec::6X`, **not** the `scale_vec::4X` / `scale_vec::2X` variants §6
lists for NVFP4 / MXFP4, which are the K = 64 forms; whether an NVFP4 GEMM can actually take
the K = 96 path is therefore also ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED**. Verify on hardware with
`cuobjdump -sass` on a tuned FP4 GEMM rather than from the ISA prose.

### The attention ("2× softmax") improvement

NVIDIA claims **"2× the attention-layer acceleration"** on Blackwell Ultra vs Blackwell
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gb300-nvl72/) — the HGX table lists it as a
separate *"Attention Performance: 2× / 1×"* row whose footnote 3 says only *"vs. NVIDIA
Blackwell"* (**corrected 2026-09-19**; see the footnote note above — there is no emulation
caveat on this row, and no caveat of any kind). LMSYS describes the same thing concretely as
**"2× softmax throughput for attention operations"**
[src](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-02-20-gb300-inferencex/).

Mechanically this is the **special-function unit that executes `MUFU.EX2`** (the `exp2`
used in the FlashAttention softmax). Tri Dao's FlashAttention-4 write-up documents that on
Blackwell `MUFU.EX2` runs at **16 ops/cycle/SM**, and that FA4 has to *software-emulate*
`2^x` on the FMA pipes with a polynomial approximation because the SFU is the bottleneck
[src](https://tridao.me/blog/2026/flash4/). Doubling that unit directly relieves the FA4
bottleneck. **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED:** NVIDIA has published no `MUFU.EX2` throughput number for
`sm_103` and no measured attention-TFLOPS figure for B300. Method for estimating: take FA4's
published B200 result (1,605 TFLOPS BF16, 71 % util) and treat the softmax-bound fraction
of the kernel as up to 2× faster — an *upper bound*, since FA4 on B200 already offloads
`exp2` work to FMA pipes and would need re-tuning to spend the extra SFU capacity.

## 4. Interconnect and topology

| Property | HGX B300 (8-GPU) | GB300 NVL72 |
|---|---|---|
| Scale-up fabric | **NVLink 5** via NVLink 5 Switch [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/hgx/) | NVLink 5 + NVLink Switch System |
| Per-GPU NVLink BW | **1.8 TB/s** bidirectional (NVLink 5.0, 200 GT/s) | 1.8 TB/s (130 TB/s ÷ 72 = 1,806 GB/s) |
| Total NVLink BW | **14.4 TB/s** (8 GPUs) | **130 TB/s** (72 GPUs) |
| NVLink domain | **8 GPUs** | **72 GPUs** |
| Host / PCIe | **PCIe 6.x @ 128 GB/s** (B200: PCIe 5.0 @ 64 GB/s) [src](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-shares-blackwell-ultras-secrets-nvfp4-boost-detailed-and-pcie-6-0-support) | PCIe 6.x to Grace; NVLink-C2C Grace↔Blackwell |
| Scale-out NIC | **1.6 TB/s** node networking (2× B200's 0.8 TB/s) [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/hgx/); DGX B300: 8× single-port ConnectX-8 @ 800 Gb/s + 2× BlueField-3 [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/dgx-b300/) | 800 Gb/s ConnectX-8 per GPU [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gb300-nvl72/) |
| Inter-node fabric | Quantum-X800 InfiniBand **or** Spectrum-X Ethernet | same |
| CPU memory | DGX B300: dual Xeon 6776P; AWS p6-b300: 4 TB system RAM | 17 TB LPDDR5X @ 14 TB/s (rack), 37 TB total fast memory |

**The topology decision that matters.** For a 671B-to-1T MoE with wide expert parallelism,
an 8-GPU NVLink domain forces EP ≤ 8 inside the domain; everything beyond crosses InfiniBand
at 20–40 % comm overhead (METHODOLOGY §4). GB300 NVL72 gives a 72-GPU NVLink domain, which
is why NVIDIA's and SemiAnalysis's disaggregated results are all rack-scale. The measured
penalty is large and **direction-dependent**:

- SemiAnalysis InferenceX, DeepSeek-R1, 8K/1K, FP4: at **73 tok/s/user**, GB300 NVL72 posts
  **6,969 tok/s/chip @ $0.09/M** vs B300 **3,748 tok/s/chip @ $0.17/M** — GB300 is **86 %
  faster and ~2× cheaper per token**. At **126 tok/s/user**: GB300 3,763 vs B300 1,016 (+270 %).
  But at **178 tok/s/user** the sign flips: **B300 719 vs GB300 545** (+32 % for B300)
  [src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/compare/deepseek-r1-b300-vs-gb300).
- vLLM's own GB300 numbers put GB300 only **14 % ahead of B300 in prefill**
  [src](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-02-13-gb300-deepseek) — i.e. the NVL72 advantage is
  almost entirely a *decode-side all-to-all* advantage, not a compute advantage.

**Multi-node from an 8× B300 node.** 1.6 TB/s of NIC bandwidth per node is 2× B200's, which
makes disaggregated prefill/decode across HGX B300 nodes far more viable than it was on
B200. Both NVIDIA Dynamo (with SGLang or TRT-LLM workers) and SGLang PD are the deployed
paths; SemiAnalysis's public GB300 disaggregated recipes launch via
[`NVIDIA/srt-slurm`](https://github.com/NVIDIA/srt-slurm) with e.g. `10p1d-dep4-dep32`
prefill/decode splits [src](https://pytorch.org/blog/serving-deepseek-v4-on-gb300-with-sglang-5x-higher-throughput-at-the-same-interactivity-since-day-0/).

> **⚠️ The single most important B300 software gotcha.** PTX target suffixes are not
> cosmetic. From the PTX ISA:
> *"Target architectures with suffix `a`, such as `sm_90a`, include architecture-specific
> features that are supported on the specified architecture only... PTX code generated for
> such targets cannot be run on later generation devices. Target architectures with suffix
> `f`, such as `sm_100f`, include family-specific features... can run only on later
> generation devices in the same family."* Table 70 puts `sm_100f, sm_103f, sm_107f` in the
> **same `sm_10x` family** [src](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html).
>
> **Consequence:** a wheel compiled *only* for `sm_100a` — which is what you get when a
> project picks arch-specific targets for better SASS, as FlashMLA explicitly does
> ("*We use architecture-specific (sm_100a / sm_103a) targets instead of the family-specific
> one (sm_100f) for better SASS code generation*") — **will not load on B300** unless the
> maintainer also added `sm_103a`. FlashMLA did. Other projects may not have. When a kernel
> silently falls back or throws "no kernel image is available for execution on the device"
> on a B300 but works on a B200, this is almost always why. Verify with
> `cuobjdump --list-elf <lib>.so | grep sm_103`.

### Feature support

- **MLA:** native. FlashInfer ships dedicated MLA decode kernels [src](https://docs.flashinfer.ai/api/attention.html);

- **FP8 attention:** yes. FlashMLA sparse decode reads an FP8-E4M3 KV cache with per-128
  or per-64 FP32/E8M0 scales and dequantizes to BF16 for the MMA [src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA).
  Lambda's and NVIDIA's MLPerf v6.0 submissions use **FP4 weights + FP8 KV cache**.
- **FP4 attention / FP4 KV:** yes, emerging. FlashInfer's Blackwell TMA tile kernels include

  [src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA). vLLM exposes

- **Paged KV:** supported across FlashInfer backends (PrimTS requires HND layout)

- **SM103-specific tuning landed in vLLM:** *"FlashInfer all-reduce tuning on SM103
  (#53318, #53606)"* in v0.29.0 [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases/tag/v0.29.0)

### Accuracy evidence for NVFP4 on this hardware

The most directly relevant published datapoint is NVIDIA's own **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4**
conversion, **tested on GB300** (same `sm_103` silicon)
[src](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4):

| Benchmark | MXFP4 (source checkpoint) | NVFP4 (converted) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPQA Diamond | 91.035 | **91.288** | +0.25 |
| AA-LCR | 78.563 | 78.438 | −0.13 |
| SciCode | 54.401 | **55.843** | +1.44 |
| IFBench | 76.667 | **77.267** | +0.60 |
| MMMU-Pro | 74.046 | 73.699 | −0.35 |
| Terminal-Bench 2.1 | 81.60 | **82.16** | +0.56 |

Mechanism, verbatim from the card (re-fetched 2026-09-19): *"The finer NVFP4 scale layout
increases checkpoint size from approximately 476 GiB to 492 GiB."* Converted tensors:
`w1`/`w2`/`w3` for **384 experts across 40 layers**; *"Attention, shared experts, vision,
Engram lookup tables, MTP/DSpark, and other excluded components retain their source
precision, including MXFP8 where applicable."* Quantized with `nvidia-modelopt v0.47.0rc0`;
released **2026-09-16** — three days before this document's research date.

**The base model's routed experts are MXFP4, not NVFP4.** DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash ships E2M1
nibbles with **one E8M0 scale per 32** (0.53125 B/param); the NVIDIA build rewrites those
scales to **one E4M3 per 16 plus one FP32 per tensor** (0.5625 B/param). The size delta is a
**per-tensor-group** sum (METHODOLOGY §1), never a flat rate — only the routed-expert group
changes precision:

```
converted group  = 16,986,931,200 block-32 blocks × 32 = 543,581,798,400 elements
                   543.582e9 × (0.5625 − 0.53125)      = 16.99 GB added
unchanged groups = attention (FP8, 4.7 GiB), shared experts, vision,
                   Engram lookup tables (FP8 + E8M0/32, 202.76 GB = 188.83 GiB),
                   MTP/DSpark routed experts (stay MXFP4), embeddings/lm_head
base checkpoint  = 510.29 GB = 475.24 GiB   ("approximately 476 GiB")   [METHODOLOGY §8]
NVFP4 checkpoint = 527.27 GB = 491.06 GiB   ("492 GiB")                 [METHODOLOGY §8]
delta            = 527.27 − 510.29 = 16.99 GB ✅ reconciles
```

The whole-checkpoint growth is **+3.3 %**, not +12.5 %, precisely because 38.5 % of the
bytes are an FP8 Engram table no FP4 kernel touches — see
[models/deepseek41fnvfp4/architecture.md](../models/deepseek41fnvfp4/architecture.md).

**Practical read for B300:** NVFP4 is the only format that uses the 1.5× B300 uplift. An
MXFP4 checkpoint (e.g. gpt-oss, or DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's source form) *runs* on the same
FP4 datapath, so the peak rate is the same — but NVIDIA's own conversion exists because the
NVFP4 scale layout maps onto `.kind::mxf4nvf4`'s `.scale_vec::4X` / `.ue4m3` path that the
TRT-LLM and FlashInfer FP4 GEMMs are tuned around. **The NVFP4 build is `+16.99 GB` *larger*
than the base, accuracy-neutral (table above), and the model card publishes no speedup or
throughput comparison against the base MXFP4 model at all** (confirmed by a live fetch of
the card, 2026-09-19). **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED:** nobody has published a *measured, iso-model*
MXFP4-vs-NVFP4 throughput delta on `sm_103`. Do not assume NVFP4 is faster than MXFP4 on
B300 on the strength of the format alone; benchmark it.

### cuBLAS/CUTLASS maturity notes specific to Blackwell Ultra

- *"Improved **FP4 matrix multiplication performance on Blackwell Ultra GPUs** by a geometric
  mean of 5 %, up to 7 % for some small problems"* — cuBLAS 13.4 [src](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-toolkit-release-notes/index.html)
- *"Improved **TF32** matrix multiplication performance on Blackwell and Blackwell Ultra GPUs
  by a geometric mean of 27 %, up to 3.5× for some small problems"* — ibid.
- *"Removed unnecessary overhead related to loading kernels on GPUs with **compute capability 10.3**"* — ibid.

## 9. Roofline numbers for inference

### Arithmetic intensity / ridge points (HGX B300: 8 TB/s, dense TFLOPS from §3)

| dtype | Dense TFLOP/s | bytes per FLOP | Ridge point (FLOP/byte) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NVFP4 / MXFP4 | 13,500 | 5.93 × 10⁻⁴ | **1,687.5** |
| FP8 / FP6 / MXFP8 | 4,500 | 1.78 × 10⁻³ | **562.5** |
| BF16 / FP16 | 2,250 | 3.56 × 10⁻³ | **281.3** |
| TF32 | 1,125 | 7.11 × 10⁻³ | 140.6 |
| INT8 | 187.5 | 4.27 × 10⁻² | 23.4 |

The NVFP4 ridge point of **1,687 FLOP/byte** is the central fact about B300. A GEMM needs an
arithmetic intensity of ~1,700 to saturate the FP4 pipes — which in decode terms means
**batch ≳ 850 tokens per weight-read** before FP4 compute even becomes the binding
constraint. Below that, B300 decode is bandwidth-bound and behaves exactly like B200.
**This is why B300's 1.5× FP4 uplift shows up in prefill and in very-high-batch decode, and
essentially not at all at low concurrency.**

### Decode upper bounds (`decode_step_time ≥ bytes_per_decode_step / (8 TB/s × MBU)`)

| Working set read per decode step | Steps/s @ MBU 1.0 | Steps/s @ MBU 0.7 | TPOT @ MBU 0.7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70B dense, BF16 (140 GB) | 57.1 | 40.0 | 25.0 ms |
| 70B dense, FP8 (70 GB) | 114.3 | 80.0 | 12.5 ms |
| 70B dense, NVFP4 (35 GB) | 228.6 | 160.0 | 6.3 ms |
| 671B MoE, **active 37B** FP8 (small batch) | 216.2 | 151.4 | 6.6 ms |
| 671B MoE, **all experts touched**, FP8 (691 GB, ≈8-GPU node) | 11.6 | 8.1 | 123 ms |
| 1T MoE, all weights NVFP4 (563 GB at 0.5625 B/param) | 14.2 | 10.0 | 100 ms |

> **These rows are the *weight* term only. KV read is not a flat per-step constant** —
> METHODOLOGY §§3–4: `bytes_per_decode_step = weights_read(batch) + batch × ctx ×
> kv_bytes_per_token`. For DeepSeek-R1 MLA at FP8 KV (35,136 B/token) on this node:
>
> | batch × ctx | KV read per decode step | vs the 37 GB active-weight read |
> |---|---|---|
> | 8 × 8K | 2.3 GB | 6 % |
> | 128 × 32K | 147.4 GB | **4.0×** |
> | 256 × 128K | 1,179.0 GB | **32×** — and 1,179 GB of the 1,501 GB KV budget, so this is the ceiling, not a comfortable point |
>
> At the batch sizes B300's memory actually enables, **KV traffic dominates the decode step**,
> which is why the extra capacity does not translate into proportionally more tokens/s.

For MoE, use METHODOLOGY §4's `distinct_experts(batch) ≈ n_experts × (1 − (1 − top_k/n_experts)^batch)`
to interpolate between the "active-params" and "all-experts" rows — for a 256-expert/top-8
model the expert set saturates around batch ≈ 200, which on B300's larger KV budget is a
*routinely reachable* batch size. That crossover is the reason B300's extra memory converts
into throughput: more concurrency per node → more expert reuse per weight read.

### Prefill TFLOPS (`prefill_time ≈ 2 × active_params × T / (peak × MFU)`)

Planning MFU bands from METHODOLOGY §4, adjusted for what is actually published on Blackwell:

| dtype | Peak dense | Planning MFU | Effective TFLOP/s |
|---|---|---|---|

## 11. Known gotchas

1. **The 2,304 GB myth.** See the banner at the top. No shipping 8-GPU B300 node has
   2,304 GB. Sizing a deployment against 288 GB/GPU will over-commit an HGX B300 by ~10 %
   and fail at model load or at peak KV pressure. Plan at the METHODOLOGY §8 pinned
   **268 GB/GPU = 2,144 GB/node** for HGX/DGX B300 and AWS p6-b300 (the conservative
   SKU-specific figures are DGX B300 262.5 GB and OCI `BM.GPU.B300.8` 263 GB); **288 GB/GPU
   physical, ≈ 279 GB usable** applies **only** to GB300 NVL72. The repo `README.md`
   previously asserted an "8×B300 node with 2304GB"; that claim is **wrong** and the README
   now carries the corrected 2,144 GB figure.

2. **`sm_100a` binaries do not run on B300.** This is the #1 source of "works on B200, dies
   on B300". `sm_100f` family targets *do* carry over; `sm_100a` arch-specific targets do
   not. Projects deliberately choose `a` targets for SASS quality (FlashMLA says so in its
   `setup.py` comment). **Check before deploying:** `cuobjdump --list-elf lib*.so | grep -c sm_103`.

3. **INT8 is a trap.** 187.5 TOPS dense is **0.08× the BF16 rate**. Any W8A8-INT8 recipe that
   was fast on H100 (3,958 TOPS sparse) or B200 (4,500 TOPS dense) becomes **slower than
   BF16** on B300. Migrate INT8 W8A8 → FP8 E4M3. For 4-bit, use **W4A16 (AWQ/GPTQ via
   Marlin/Machete)**, which does its math on the BF16/FP16 pipes and is therefore untouched
   by the INT8 cut — or NVFP4, which is the fast path.

4. **FP64 is effectively gone.** 1.25 TFLOPS/GPU (0.034× B200). Fine for inference, fatal if
   the same fleet has any HPC obligation. The only recourse is CUDA's Ozaki tensor-core
   DGEMM emulation, and NVIDIA's published emulation figure (175 TFLOPS) is for **B200**, not
   B300. Also note cuBLAS's own known issue: *"On Blackwell GPUs, FP64 fixed-point emulation
   kernels may produce incorrect results or experience data corruption when executed
   concurrently with third-party kernels that allocate tensor memory."*

5. **FlashMLA's support matrix says SM90/SM100 — but the build emits `sm_103a`.** The kernels
   compile for B300; the *documentation* does not claim B300. Worse, **"Sparse Decoding for
   DeepSeek V4.1 is only available on SM100"** per that same matrix. If you are serving
   DeepSeek-V4.1 with sparse decoding on B300, verify the kernel actually dispatches rather
   than silently falling back. ⚠️ Whether this is a doc lag or a real SM103 gap is
   **TO BE VERIFIED**.

6. **FlashInfer is "architecture-gated but not signoff-qualified" on SM103a.** It is the
   *default* attention backend for both vLLM and SGLang on Blackwell. Default + unqualified
   is a combination worth regression-testing before production.

7. **vLLM has no Blackwell CI.** Its own RFC states CUDA CI covers only L4 and H100 and that
   *"importability does not prove that an optional extension contains a kernel for the active
   GPU."* FA4 and full native NVFP4 paths are explicitly **not yet qualified**. Pin versions;
   test your exact model on your exact node.

8. **FP4 MoE needs an environment variable.** `VLLM_USE_FLASHINFER_MOE_FP4=1`. Without it you
   silently get a slower MoE path and none of the B300 FP4 uplift.

9. **TensorRT-LLM 1.2 is a breaking release.** The TensorRT backend is **removed** (PyTorch
   only; `LLM(backend="tensorrt")` raises), `trtllm-build`/`convert_checkpoint.py` are gone,
   and two-model speculative decoding is removed. **GB300 multi-node was beta** and only
   validated from 1.2.0rc4+. Budget porting time.

10. **Power and thermals.** 1,400 W liquid / ~1,100 W air is a **+17 % to +40 %** step over
    B200's 1,200 W, and DGX B300 is ~14 kW in 10U. Air-cooled HGX B300 is the *derated* bin —
    it is not the same GPU performance as GB300 NVL72 (13,500 vs 15,000 TFLOPS dense NVFP4,
    a real 11 % clock gap). ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED:** no public data on sustained-clock behaviour
    or thermal capping of HGX B300 under long FP4 prefill bursts. Measure `nvidia-smi
    --query-gpu=clocks_throttle_reasons.active` during a sustained sweep before trusting peak
    numbers.

11. **8-GPU NVLink domain limits EP.** Wide-EP MoE (DeepEP, EPLB) that was designed for NVL72
    runs badly on an 8-GPU domain. Expect to run EP ≤ 8 in-node and pay 20–40 % comm overhead
    for anything wider (METHODOLOGY §4). The 1.6 TB/s NIC bandwidth (2× B200) partly offsets
    this, but it does not make an HGX B300 node an NVL72.

12. **Cloud quota reality.** **OCI** publishes an on-demand B300 rate of **$15.00/GPU-hr**
    (`BM.GPU.B300.8`) and GB300 at **$18.00/GPU-hr** (`BM.GPU.GB300.4`) through its public
    price-list API — the only hyperscaler that does ([cloud-pricing.md §3.4](../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md)).
    Among neoclouds only **Nebius** publishes a public on-demand B300 price
    ($7.85/GPU-hr). **CoreWeave** publishes only a spot rate. **Azure publishes nothing** for
    ND GB300 v6. **Lambda and Crusoe do not list B300 at all.** **AWS** lists $17.80/GPU-hr but
    routes real on-demand through account managers and sells the capacity via Capacity Blocks.
    P6-B300 exists in only six regions (Oregon, N. Virginia, GovCloud US-East, Hyderabad,
    Seoul, São Paulo). Assume a sales conversation and a lead time, not a `RunInstances` call.

13. **AWS instance-store detail.** `p6-b300.48xlarge` has **8 × 3.84 TB NVMe = 30.72 TB local**
    (AWS blog), but Vantage's listing shows "3800 GiB SSD" — a per-drive figure. For
    weight-staging and KV-offload planning use the AWS blog's 30.72 TB. 4 TB host RAM, 192
    vCPU (Emerald Rapids Xeon), 100 Gbps EBS, 300 Gbps ENA, 6.4 Tbps EFA.

14. **Agentic/cache-heavy workloads may prefer B200.** See the DeepSeek-V4 agentic-trace table
    in §10: B200 wins on both tok/s/chip and $/M token across most of the interactivity range.
    B300's advantage is long-context capacity and the high-interactivity tail, not universal.

## 12. Comparison hooks

### (a) Decode bandwidth

**vs H100:** B300 has **8 TB/s vs H100 SXM's 3.35 TB/s** — a clean **2.39×**
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/). Combined with **3.3× the memory**
(268 GB vs 80 GB), a single B300 holds a working set that needs four H100s, and reads it
2.4× faster. Against H200 (4.8 TB/s, 141 GB) the gap narrows to **1.67× bandwidth and 1.9×
capacity** [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/) — which is exactly what the
measured data shows: SemiAnalysis puts B300 at **+268 %** over H200 at 50 tok/s/user but only
**+59 %** at 128 tok/s/user, and at that point H200 is actually **17 % cheaper per token**.
Decode bandwidth advantage over Hopper is real but converges as you push interactivity, because
at low concurrency both are reading the same weights and the ratio collapses toward the raw
2.4×/1.67×.

**vs B200:** **identical — 8 TB/s on both.** This is the single most important comparison in
this document. B300 is *not* a faster decode chip than B200 for a fixed working set; per
METHODOLOGY §4 the decode step time is byte-count divided by bandwidth × MBU, and neither the
byte count nor the bandwidth changed. What changed is the KV budget (**1,501 GB vs ~867 GB** on
an 8-GPU node for DeepSeek-R1 NVFP4, a **1.73×** — recomputed 2026-09-19 at the corrected
0.5625 B/param NVFP4 weight cost *and* the pinned 2,144 GB node capacity, see §2), which raises the *batch* you can run at a given
TPOT, which raises MoE expert reuse per weight read, which raises aggregate tokens/s. The
measured iso-GPU MLPerf v6.0 result — **1.17× on DeepSeek-R1, 1.15× on gpt-oss-120b** — is
almost exactly what that mechanism predicts, and nothing like 1.5×.

### (b) FP8 / FP4 prefill

**vs H100:** H100 SXM does **1,979 TFLOPS dense FP8** (3,958 sparse ÷ 2) and has **no FP4
tensor core at all**. B300 does **4,500 TFLOPS dense FP8 (2.27×)** and **13,500 TFLOPS dense
NVFP4 (6.8× H100's FP8)**. For prefill-dominated and agentic-prefill workloads this is the
widest gap in the whole comparison — and unlike decode it is compute, so it shows up directly:
vLLM measured **8× prefill throughput GB300 vs H200**, and SemiAnalysis measured B300 at
**+723 % to +1,335 % tok/s/chip vs H100** on DeepSeek-R1. H100 cannot participate in an NVFP4
serving recipe at all; it must run FP8, which doubles its weight traffic *and* halves its
compute relative to B300's FP4 path.

**vs B200:** **FP8 is identical (4,500 TFLOPS dense both), FP4 is exactly 1.5×** (13,500 vs
9,000 dense; 18,000 vs 18,000 sparse — note the *sparse* FP4 numbers are the same, so the uplift
is dense-only). The mechanism is visible in the PTX ISA: `tcgen05.mma` with `.kind::mxf4` /
`.kind::mxf4nvf4` gains **K = 96 dense / K = 192 sparse on `sm_103a`** where B200's `sm_100a`
tops out at K = 64/128 — 96/64 = 1.5×, precisely. B300 also claims 2× attention-layer
(softmax/SFU) throughput, which matters for long-prefill attention but is an **unqualified
vendor claim with no caveat footnote at all** (corrected 2026-09-19: footnote 3 on the HGX
table reads only *"vs. NVIDIA Blackwell."*, re-confirmed by a live fetch — there is no
emulation footnote) and **no published end-to-end measurement**. **Net: if your workload is FP8 and prefill-bound,
B300 gives you nothing over B200 except memory. If it is NVFP4 and prefill-bound, B300 gives
you up to 1.5× — and you must actually be running NVFP4 to collect it.**

### (c) Memory per dollar

All rows below use the METHODOLOGY §8 pinned **268 GB/GPU** for B300 and **180 GB/GPU** for
B200, and prices only from [cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md](../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md)
(recomputed 2026-09-19; the previous revision used a 263 GB B300 figure):

| GPU | $/GPU-hr | HBM/GPU | GB per $/hr |
|---|---|---|---|
| H100 SXM (Crusoe) | $3.90 | 80 GB | 20.5 |
| H100 SXM (Lambda) | $4.29 | 80 GB | 18.7 |
| B200 (Nebius on-demand) | $7.15 | 180 GB | 25.2 |
| B200 (CoreWeave spot) | $4.26 | 180 GB | 42.3 |
| **B300 (CoreWeave spot)** | $4.48 | 268 GB | **59.8** |
| **B300 (Nebius on-demand)** | $7.85 | 268 GB | **34.1** |
| B300 (OCI on-demand) | $15.00 | 268 GB | 17.9 |
| B300 (AWS on-demand list) | $17.80 | 268 GB | 15.1 |

**vs H100:** H100 at **$3.90–4.29/GPU-hr for 80 GB** = **18.7–20.5 GB per $/hr**. B300 at
Nebius's $7.85 = **34.1 GB per $/hr**, at CoreWeave's $4.48 spot = **59.8 GB per $/hr**. B300
is **1.7×–2.9× better memory-per-dollar than H100** on public rates — before counting that
B300 reads that memory 2.4× faster and can serve it at FP4. At OCI's $15.00 (17.9) and AWS's
$17.80 list (15.1), B300 falls *below* H100; the list price, not the silicon, is what makes
those comparisons bad.

**vs B200:** Nebius prices B200 at **$7.15 for 180 GB (25.2 GB per $/hr)** and B300 at **$7.85
for 268 GB (34.1 GB per $/hr)** — **+36 % memory per dollar for +10 % price**, from a single
provider with a single price list, which is the cleanest such comparison available. CoreWeave
spot tells the same story: B200 $4.26/GPU-hr for 180 GB (42.3 GB/$) vs B300 $4.48 for 268 GB
(59.8 GB/$, **+42 %**). SemiAnalysis's TCO model agrees directionally: **$2.26/chip-hr for
B300 vs $1.73 for B200 (+31 %) for +49 % memory**. So B300 is the better memory-per-dollar buy on
every public price point — which is the whole case for it, since bandwidth is flat, FP8 is
flat, and only NVFP4 prefill is faster. **Buy B300 when the binding constraint is KV capacity
or model size; buy B200 when it is $/token on cache-heavy agentic traffic** (§10's DeepSeek-V4
table shows B200 winning there on both axes).
