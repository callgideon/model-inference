# NVIDIA H200 — inference reference

Research date: **2026-09-19**. Follows [`research/METHODOLOGY.md`](../METHODOLOGY.md)
for all formulas, the `[src]` / **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** / `est.` / `meas.` legend,
and the rule that dense and sparse TFLOPS are always separated.

**One-paragraph summary.** H200 is H100's GH100 die with a new memory subsystem:
identical tensor-core math (sm_90, 4th-gen tensor cores, FP8 but *no* FP4),
141 GB of HBM3e instead of 80 GB of HBM3, and 4.8 TB/s instead of 3.35 TB/s
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/). For inference that means
**+76 % memory and +43 % decode bandwidth for zero extra FLOPS**. Every H200
decision therefore reduces to: does your workload bottleneck on weights+KV
bytes (H200 wins big over H100) or on prefill FLOPS (H200 ≈ H100, and Blackwell
wins big because of FP4)? As of 2026-09-19 H200 is the *highest-capability
Hopper part* and the last NVIDIA datacenter GPU whose best usable inference
precision is FP8 — NVFP4 and MXFP4 have no tensor-core path on sm_90
[src](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/reference/support-matrix.html).

### Usable capacity after runtime overhead (METHODOLOGY §3)

```
usable_hbm = 141 GB × 0.90 = 126.9 GB per GPU
8-GPU node: 126.9 × 8 = 1,015.2 GB
```

That 10 % haircut covers the CUDA context (~0.5–1 GB), NCCL buffers, the
allocator's fragmentation slack, CUDA-graph replay buffers (vLLM V1 notes CUDA
graph capture uses **more** memory than V0
[src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/usage/v1_guide.md)),
and the engine's activation workspace. In practice `--gpu-memory-utilization
0.90` is the conservative default and `0.92–0.95` is achievable on a dedicated
node; above that you are one long prefill chunk away from an OOM.

Worked KV budgets on an 8×H200 node (`activation_ws` = 4 GiB/GPU):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Llama-3.3-70B FP8 (TP1×8 replicas or TP8) | 70.6 GB | 910 GB | 160 KiB (FP8) | **678** | **169** | **42** |
| Llama-3.1-405B FP8 (TP8) | 405.9 GB | 575 GB | 252 KiB (FP8) | **271** | **67** | **16** |
| DeepSeek-V3/R1 671B FP8 (TP8/EP8) | 671 GB | 310 GB | 34.3 KiB (FP8, MLA) | **1,076** | **269** | **67** |

Recomputed with `python3` from METHODOLOGY §3 (`141e9 × 0.90 × n_gpus − weights
− n_gpus × 4 GiB`, then `floor(budget / (ctx × kv_bytes_per_token + fixed_state))`)
and the METHODOLOGY §8 pinned model inputs. Three H200-specific notes:

- **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's 890 B/token is the FP4-KV figure.** The *storage*
  format needs no FP4 tensor core — DeepSeek's report says FP4 here "reduces
  storage rather than accelerates matrix multiplication", so the bytes are the
  same on Hopper. What is missing on sm_90 is the *kernel*: FlashMLA's V4.1
  sparse decoding is SM100-only (§5, §11), so plan an H200 deployment on the
  FP8-KV row (1,650 B/token) until a Hopper V4.1 decode path exists. The fixed
  2.77 MiB sliding-window state is the FP8 engine-optimal figure; the reference
  implementation's BF16 ring buffer is 5.38 MiB ⚠️
  ([architecture.md §5](../models/deepseek41f/architecture.md)).
- **Kimi-K3 cannot be served on a single 8×H200 node**: 1,560.9 GB of weights
  exceeds the node's 1,015.2 GB of usable HBM. Two nodes is a multi-node TP/EP
  problem over 400G-class fabric (§4), i.e. METHODOLOGY's 20–40 % overhead band.
  The 2.25 GB per-request KDA state (`S` = 5 slots on SGLang) is what flattens
  its concurrency curve — 169 seqs at 8K but still 98 at 128K.
- **The NVFP4 build is the worse choice on H200 in both directions**: 17 GB more
  weights *and* no FP4 GEMM to pay for them (§6).

they live in `research/models/<exp>/h200.md` and are written in the next phase.

**The headline H200 fact**: at BF16 KV, Llama-3.3-70B costs 320 KiB/token, so
128K context is 40 GiB *per sequence*. On an 80 GB H100 that is one sequence
plus nothing. On H200 you get 141 GB, and with FP8 KV it halves again. This is
the entire reason H200 exists for inference.

## 3. Compute per data type

Tensor cores are **4th-generation Hopper** tensor cores — the same silicon as
H100, because H200 is the same GH100 die
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/). There is **no FP4 or FP6
tensor-core path on sm_90**.

NVIDIA's public H200 table quotes **sparse** (2:4 structured) numbers with an
asterisk; dense is exactly half
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/). Lenovo's product guide
prints both, e.g. FP8 SXM "1,979 / 3,958 teraFLOPS" dense/sparse
[src](https://lenovopress.lenovo.com/lp1944-nvidia-h200-141gb-gpu).

- **H200 NVL dense BF16 = 836 TFLOPS** — Lenovo prints the dense figure

## 4. Interconnect and topology

| Property | H200 SXM | H200 NVL |
|---|---|---|
| NVLink generation | NVLink 4 (18 links × 25 GB/s each direction) | NVLink 4 over bridge |
| NVLink bandwidth per GPU | **900 GB/s** bidirectional [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/) | **900 GB/s** across a 2-way or 4-way bridge [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/) |
| NVSwitch | Yes — 4× 3rd-gen NVSwitch on an HGX H200 8-GPU baseboard; all-to-all, single hop | **No NVSwitch.** Bridged islands only |
| PCIe | Gen5 ×16, 128 GB/s bidirectional [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/) | Gen5 ×16, 128 GB/s |
| Node bisection | AWS quotes "900 GB/s NVSwitch" and "**3.6 TB/s bisectional bandwidth** in each instance" for p5/p5e/p5en [src](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/p5/) | n/a |
| Inter-node fabric | EFA v2/v3 at **3,200 Gbps** per p5e/p5en instance [src](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/p5/); InfiniBand NDR 400G ×8 on HGX reference designs | typically 1–2× 400G NIC per node |

### Standard node topology (HGX H200 8-GPU)

```
  CPU0 ── PCIe Gen5 ──┬── GPU0 GPU1 GPU2 GPU3 ─┐
                      │                         ├─ 4× NVSwitch (all-to-all, 900 GB/s/GPU)
  CPU1 ── PCIe Gen5 ──┴── GPU4 GPU5 GPU6 GPU7 ─┘
  8× NIC (400G IB NDR or EFA) — one per GPU, GPUDirect RDMA
```

- TP=8 inside the node is the sweet spot: all-reduce runs over NVSwitch at
  900 GB/s/GPU, so METHODOLOGY's "comm overhead ~5–15 % for TP=8 over NVLink"
  planning default applies directly.
- **TP=16 crosses a node boundary.** With 400G/GPU IB that is ~50 GB/s per GPU
  versus 900 GB/s on NVLink — an 18× drop. Use METHODOLOGY's 20–40 %
  multi-node overhead band. SGLang's own DeepSeek-V3.2 Hopper recipe reaches
  for `--tp 16 --dp 2 --enable-dp-attention` rather than naive TP=16
  [src](https://docs.sglang.io/basic_usage/deepseek_v32.html), i.e. it replaces
  cross-node TP all-reduce with expert/data parallelism where possible.
- **No NVL72-style rack domain on Hopper.** There is no H200 equivalent of
  GB200/GB300 NVL72. The largest single NVLink domain is 8 GPUs (1,128 GB
  HBM3e [src](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/p5/)). Anything bigger
  than 1,128 GB of weights+KV is a multi-node problem with a fabric hop in the
  critical path.

### H200 NVL multi-GPU caveat

H200 NVL's "900 GB/s" applies **within** a 2-way or 4-way bridged island
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/). In an 8-GPU NVL server
(Dell PowerEdge XE7740, Cisco UCS C845A M8 etc. appear in MLPerf v6.0
[src](https://github.com/mlcommons/inference_results_v6.0)), GPUs in different
islands talk over **PCIe Gen5 through the CPU root complex**. Practical
consequences:

- TP=4 within an island: fine.
- TP=8 across islands: all-reduce falls back to PCIe. Expect a large regression
  versus SXM. The MLPerf v6.0 Dell XE7740 8×H200-NVL Llama-2-70B Offline result

### vLLM backend selection on sm_90 (authoritative, read from source)

From `vllm/platforms/cuda.py` as of 2026-09-19
[src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/vllm/platforms/cuda.py):

**Non-MLA models (GQA/MHA), `device_capability.major == 9`:**
```
FLASH_ATTN  →  FLASHINFER  →  TRITON_ATTN  →  FLEX_ATTENTION  →  TURBOQUANT
(+ TRITON_FLASH_ATTN first, only when use_mm_prefix and major == 9)
```
Note the ordering *differs from sm_100*, where FlashInfer (TRT-LLM causal
attention) is preferred first. On Hopper, FlashAttention leads.

**MLA models (DeepSeek-family), the `else` branch that sm_90 falls into:**
```
FLASH_ATTN_MLA → FLASHMLA → FLASHINFER_MLA → TRITON_MLA
  then sparse tail: FLASH_ATTN_MLA_SPARSE → FLASHMLA_SPARSE → FLASHINFER_MLA_SPARSE_SM90
  (FLASHINFER_MLA_SPARSE_SM90 moves to the front of the sparse tail when head_size == 512)
```
`CUTLASS_MLA` and `TOKENSPEED_MLA` are **sm_100-only** and never selected on
H200. There is a dedicated `FLASHINFER_MLA_SPARSE_SM90` backend, i.e. sparse
MLA on Hopper is a first-class path, not an afterthought.

- **FA3 has native FP8 attention on Hopper**, with online softmax rescaling

  operations are **also performed in the quantized (FP8) domain**. In this

### The NVFP4 question, stated precisely

The premise usually stated as "B300 can run NVFP4, so DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash gets
extra performance there" is **half right, and the half that is right is exactly
the thing H200 cannot do**. Correcting the premise first (METHODOLOGY §8,
[deepseek41f/architecture.md](../models/deepseek41f/architecture.md)): the
shipped DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash routed experts are **MXFP4** (E2M1 + one E8M0 per
32), not NVFP4, with the non-expert tensors at FP8 32×32 UE8M0 — a mixed
checkpoint totalling 510.29 GB. NVIDIA's separate **NVFP4 build is *larger*
(527.27 GB)**, accuracy-neutral, and ships with **no published speedup over the
base checkpoint** ⚠️. What Blackwell actually buys on this model is an FP4
tensor-core path for the *MXFP4* experts (and for NVFP4 if you take that build);
what H200 lacks is any FP4 tensor core at all:

1. sm_90 has no FP4 tensor-core instruction. NVIDIA's own TensorRT-LLM support
   matrix lists Hopper as `FP32, FP16, BF16, FP8, INT8, INT4` — NVFP4 is absent
   [src](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/reference/support-matrix.html).
   (The companion claim "while it is present for Blackwell" is ⚠️ **TO BE
   VERIFIED** on that page: as fetched 2026-09-19 the precision breakdown gives
   rows for Hopper SM90, Ada SM89 and Ampere SM80/86 but **no Blackwell
   precision row at all** — Blackwell appears only in the architecture list.
   FlashInfer, point 2, is the load-bearing Blackwell-FP4 source here.)
2. FlashInfer states plainly: "FP4 GEMM: NVFP4 and MXFP4 matrix multiplication
   **for Blackwell GPUs**"
   [src](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer#gemm--linear-operations).
3. vLLM will still **load** an NVFP4 checkpoint on H200, but silently degrades
   to weight-only W4A16 via Marlin and logs a warning
   [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/features/quantization/modelopt.md).
   You keep the ~4× weight-memory saving; you lose the entire compute win, and
   for compute-heavy prefill you are **slower than the FP8 checkpoint**.
4. DeepSeek's own V4.1 kernels reinforce this: "Sparse Decoding for DeepSeek
   V4.1 is **only available on SM100**", and the FP4-KV decode path and the
   fused norm/RoPE/attn kernel are SM100-only
   [src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA#requirements).

**Therefore: on H200, the best usable weight format for a compute-bound
workload is FP8.** Use NVFP4/MXFP4 on H200 only when you are memory-capacity
constrained and prefill-light, and measure — do not assume the vendor's
Blackwell speedup transfers. Whether DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's *dense* (non-sparse)
paths have an sm_90 route at all as of 2026-09-19 is
**⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** — FlashMLA's support matrix shows Sparse Prefill on
SM90 & SM100 for V4/V4.1, but Sparse Decoding for V4.1 on SM100 only, which
means an H200 V4.1 deployment is missing its decode kernel.

## 9. Roofline numbers for inference

### Arithmetic intensity by dtype (dense)

Bandwidth 4.8 × 10¹² B/s. Ridge point = peak FLOPS / bandwidth.

| dtype | Dense peak | bytes per FLOP (BW/peak) | FLOP per byte (ridge point) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FP64 TC | 67 TFLOPS | 0.0716 | **14.0** |
| TF32 TC | 494.5 TFLOPS | 0.00971 | **103.0** |
| BF16 / FP16 TC | 989.5 TFLOPS | 0.00485 | **206.2** |
| FP8 TC | 1,979 TFLOPS | 0.00243 | **412.3** |
| INT8 TC | 1,979 TOPS | 0.00243 | **412.3** |

All `est.` from §3 dense peaks and the 4.8 TB/s figure
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/).

Interpretation: a GEMM needs **>412 FLOP per byte** to be FP8-compute-bound on
H200. A decode step with batch B on a dense model has intensity ≈ 2B FLOP per
weight byte, so it becomes compute-bound only above **B ≈ 206** at FP8 — which
is why H200 decode is bandwidth-bound across essentially the whole interactive
operating range, and why the 4.8 TB/s matters more than the 1,979 TFLOPS.

For comparison, H100's FP8 ridge point is 1,979/3.35e12 = **591 FLOP/byte**.
H200 moves the ridge *left*, i.e. more workloads are compute-bound on H200 than
on H100 — the GPU is better balanced for decode.

## 11. Known gotchas

### Power and thermal

- **700 W is "up to … (configurable)"**, not a guarantee
  [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/). OEMs ship H200 SXM
  boards with lower power caps in air-cooled chassis. Check `nvidia-smi -q -d
  POWER` for the enforced cap before you trust any TFLOPS number in §3 —
  a 600 W-capped H200 clocks down under sustained FP8 GEMM.
- **H200 NVL is 600 W and it shows.** Its dense FP8 is ~1,570 TFLOPS vs SXM's
  1,979 — **~21 % less compute at identical memory and bandwidth**
  [src](https://lenovopress.lenovo.com/lp1944-nvidia-h200-141gb-gpu). Mixing
  SXM and NVL numbers is the single easiest way to get an H200 capacity plan
  wrong.
- 8 × 700 W in one 10U chassis is ~10.2 kW ⚠️; many colo racks are provisioned
  at 12–15 kW, which is *one node per rack*. Liquid-cooled XD670/XE9680L-class
  chassis exist for a reason.
- Sustained MLPerf-class FP8 load will hit the power cap before the thermal
  cap on a properly cooled SXM board; on air-cooled NVL it is usually thermal.
  **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** — no published H200 clock-throttling study found.

### Memory and fragmentation

- **141 GB ≠ 141 GiB.** METHODOLOGY's 0.90 factor gives 126.9 GB usable; if you
  size in GiB you will over-commit by ~7 %.
- **vLLM V1 CUDA-graph capture uses more memory than V0**
  [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/usage/v1_guide.md).
  A config that fit at `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.95` on V0 can OOM on V1.
- Long chunked-prefill chunks inflate the activation workspace. The §9 table
  assumes 4 GiB; at 128K prefill with large chunk sizes budget 6+ GiB.
- MIG partitions H200 at **18 GB granularity** (SXM)
  [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/) — the 7×18 GB slices do
  not sum to 141 GB, and a MIG'd H200 cannot run anything that needs the full
  4.8 TB/s.

### Driver / CUDA / kernel availability

- **Current containers need driver 580.xx+ and CUDA 13.0–13.1**
  [src](https://docs.nvidia.com/dynamo/dev/reference/compatibility). Fleets
  still on r535/CUDA 12.4 will not run Dynamo 1.4.2 or current vLLM images.
- **FlashMLA needs CUDA 12.8+**
  [src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA#requirements); **FA3 needs CUDA
  ≥ 12.3 and prefers 12.8**
  [src](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention#flashattention-3-beta-release).
  On CUDA 12.1/12.4 nodes you silently fall back to FA2 and lose the 1.5–2×.
- **FA3 is a separate package** (`flash-attn-3`, built from the `hopper/`
  subdirectory) and is still labelled a **beta release**
  [src](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention#flashattention-3-beta-release).
  **FA4 is beta too** (`fa4-v4.0.0.beta31`, weekly tags)
  [src](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention/releases/tag/fa4-v4.0.0.beta31).
  Neither is a stable dependency; pin exact versions.

## 12. Comparison hooks

### (a) Decode bandwidth

**vs H100.** This is H200's whole reason to exist. 4.8 TB/s vs 3.35 TB/s is
**1.43×**, and it comes with 1.76× the capacity. Because decode step time is
`bytes_moved / (BW × MBU)` and the bytes are identical (same weights, same KV),
**H200 gives a straight ~43 % TPOT reduction over H100 at the same batch, plus
the ability to run 1.76× more KV before you shard**. In per-dollar terms the
gap is even better: at Nebius list, H200 delivers 1,067 GB/s per $/GPU-hr vs
H100's 870 — **23 % more bandwidth per dollar**
[src](https://nebius.com/prices). The FLOPS are identical, so every
compute-bound phase is a wash; H100 remains competitive only for short-prompt,
small-KV workloads where 80 GB is enough.

**vs B200.** B200's ~8 TB/s is **1.67×** H200's 4.8 TB/s (⚠️ the 8 TB/s figure
is not re-verified in this document — see `research/gpus/b200.md`), with 180 GB
vs 141 GB (1.28×). That 1.67× is roughly what you see on decode-bound,
FP8-precision workloads: InferenceX puts B200 at **4,852 / 1,461 / 1,051**
vs H200's **1,591 / 819 / 625** tok/s/chip on DeepSeek R1 at the 50 / 89 / 128
tok/s/user interactivity points — i.e. **1.7–3.0×** (3.05× at 50, 1.78× at 89,
1.68× at 128), which is 1.67× bandwidth plus better kernels and higher batch
headroom [src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/compare/b200-vs-h200).
*(Corrected 2026-09-19: the previous revision said "2,701 vs ~422–1,591 … i.e.
2.4–3×". Neither 2,701 nor 422 appears on the cited page, and the true range is
wider at the low end and narrower at the high end — note it **shrinks** as
interactivity rises, the opposite of what "2.4–3×" implies.)* Per dollar
at Nebius list the two are nearly tied on raw bandwidth (1,067 vs 1,119 GB/s per
$/hr), so **B200's decode advantage is real but not a free lunch at list price**
— it is the FP4 prefill and the larger batch headroom that pay for it.

### (b) FP8 / FP4 prefill

**vs H100.** Identical. Same GH100 die, same 4th-gen tensor cores, same 1,979
dense FP8 TFLOPS [src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/),
[src](https://lenovopress.lenovo.com/lp1944-nvidia-h200-141gb-gpu). Any claim
that H200 prefills faster than H100 is really a claim about batch size or KV
capacity, not FLOPS — exactly as NVIDIA's own "2× Llama-2-70B" footnote admits
(H100 BS 8 vs H200 BS 32)
[src](https://www.pny.com/file%20library/company/support/linecards/data-center-gpus/h200-nvl-datasheet.pdf).
The one real prefill difference is **attention**: long-context prefill is
attention-FLOP dominated (§9), and attention reads/writes KV, so H200's extra
bandwidth does help the memory-bound tails of FA3. Expect single-digit to low
double-digit percent, not 1.4×.

**vs B200.** This is where H200 loses badly and structurally. H200 has **no FP4
tensor core** [src](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/reference/support-matrix.html),
so its best prefill precision is FP8 at 1,979 dense TFLOPS. Blackwell adds
NVFP4 at roughly 2× its own FP8 rate, and the kernel ecosystem is already there
(FlashInfer FP4 GEMM is Blackwell-only
[src](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer#gemm--linear-operations);
FlashMLA's V4.1 FP4-KV decode and fused attention kernels are SM100-only
[src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA#requirements)). The empirical
signature is unmistakable: on **FP8** DeepSeek R1 the B200/H200 gap is 1.7–3.0×
(and on **FP8** GLM 5/5.1, 2.3–2.4×);
on **FP4** gpt-oss-120b it is **4–6×** (37,093 vs 6,958 tok/s/GPU at 86
tok/s/user) [src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/compare/gptoss-120b-b200-vs-h200),
[src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/compare/b200-vs-h200). And the gap is
widening with each model generation: DeepSeek-V4.1's sparse decode kernel exists
**only** on SM100 [src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA#requirements),
which means frontier-model day-0 support on H200 is no longer guaranteed. If
your roadmap includes 4-bit checkpoints meant to be *executed* in 4-bit —
DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's MXFP4 routed experts, or NVIDIA's larger NVFP4 rebuild of
the same model — **H200 is the wrong GPU** — not slower, but unable to use the
format as intended. (Note the NVFP4 rebuild is not the upside here: it is
527.27 GB against the base checkpoint's 510.29 GB, accuracy-neutral, with no
published speedup even on Blackwell ⚠️ — see METHODOLOGY §8.)

### (c) Memory per dollar

H200 is the **memory-per-dollar champion of the current NVIDIA lineup** at
neocloud pricing:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H100 SXM | 80 GB | $3.85 | 20.8 | $6.155 | 13.0 |
| **H200 SXM** | **141 GB** | **$4.50** | **31.3** | **$6.305** | **22.4** |
| B200 | 180 GB | $7.15 | 25.2 | $8.60 | 20.9 |

`est.` from [cloud-pricing.md §5.5](../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md)
([Nebius](https://nebius.com/prices), [CoreWeave](https://www.coreweave.com/pricing)).
**Hyperstack is better still at 35.3 GB per $/GPU-hr ($3.99 for 141 GB) — the
cheapest reputable published on-demand H200 rate in `cloud-pricing.md`**; Crusoe
is 32.9 ($4.29) and RunPod Community 39.3 ($3.59). Hyperscalers destroy the

$/GPU-hr in eastus2 (**9.8** in swedencentral) — **worse than H100 on a
neocloud**.

Practical upshot: **if your binding constraint is "this model plus its KV must
fit, and I need it cheap", H200 on a neocloud is the answer in 2026.** An 8×H200
node is 1,128 GB of HBM for **$31.92/hr at Hyperstack** (~$34–36/hr at
Crusoe/Nebius) [cloud-pricing.md §5.5](../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md), which holds a 671B FP8
MoE with 310 GB of KV budget (§2). The same footprint needs 6.3 B200s and
costs more. Where H200 stops being the answer is the moment FP4 compute or a
>1,128 GB NVLink domain enters the requirements — then it is B200/B300 or
GB200/GB300 NVL72, and no amount of H200 memory-per-dollar closes that gap.
