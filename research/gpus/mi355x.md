# AMD Instinct MI355X — inference reference

Research date: **2026-09-19**. Formulas, legend and rules: [`../METHODOLOGY.md`](../METHODOLOGY.md).

Scope: MI355X (CDNA 4, liquid-cooled, 288 GB HBM3E) as the primary target, with the
air-cooled MI350X and the half-size MI350P as siblings. Everything is dense unless the
column says sparse. All vendor "up to" figures are labelled as marketing claims.

**One-paragraph summary.** MI355X is a 256-CU CDNA 4 part at 2.4 GHz with 288 GB of
HBM3E at 8 TB/s and a 1400 W board power that mandates direct liquid cooling
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html). Its
matrix cores natively execute BF16/FP16 (2.5 PF dense), OCP-FP8 E4M3/E5M2 and MXFP8
(5.0 PF dense), and **MXFP6 and MXFP4 at the same 10.1 PF dense rate** — the MXFP6 rate
is the architectural oddity, since AMD's own comparison chart puts B200's FP6 at the FP8
rate (4.5 PF) versus MI355X's 10.1 PF
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html). It does
**not** implement NVFP4; NVFP4 checkpoints run only by requantisation to MXFP4 at load
time (near-free) or by BF16 dequant emulation (slow)
[src](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/nvfp4-to-mxfp4/README.html)
[src](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/nvfp4-mi355/README.html).
The scale-up domain is capped at 8 GPUs on a fully-meshed UBB 2.0 baseboard — there is no
NVL72 equivalent until Helios/MI400
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch/mi350.html). Software is the
gating factor, not silicon: AITER + vLLM/SGLang ROCm forks are production-usable and AMD
posted 1.04 M tok/s on Llama-2-70B at 11 nodes in MLPerf Inference 6.0
[src](https://amd.com/en/blogs/2026/amd-delivers-breakthrough-mlperf-inference-6-0-results.html),
but third-party sweeps still find soft spots (MoE + SGLang, high-interactivity decode)
[src](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/inferencemax-open-source-inference).

**Die construction.** 8 accelerator complex dies (XCDs) on TSMC N3P plus 2 I/O dies on
TSMC N6; each XCD carries 36 CDNA 4 CUs "of which 32 are active", giving 256 active CUs.
MI350P is 4 XCDs + **1 IOD**. (Re-checked 2026-09-19 against the page itself, which was
reachable this pass: *"The MI350P is a smaller configuration, integrating four XCDs and a
single IOD connected to four HBM3E stacks"* and *"Because the MI350P has a single IOD, it
supports up to four compute partitions and a single (NPS1) memory partition across its
144GB."* The 2026-09-19 verification-log entry that changed this to "2 IODs" misread the
page's *"consolidates the IOD functions into two larger dies"* sentence, which describes
MI350X/MI355X against the prior generation's four IODs, not MI350P.)
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch/mi350.html). AMD's own page
lists lithography as "TSMC 3nm | 6nm FinFET"
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html).

**Cache hierarchy.** 32 KB L1 per CU (128 B lines, 64-way), 64 KB instruction cache shared
by two CUs, 160 KB LDS per CU with doubled read bandwidth vs CDNA 3, 4 MB L2 per XCD
(16-way, 16 channels), 256 MB Infinity Cache in the IODs
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch/mi350.html).

**Partitioning.** 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 compute partitions (SPX…CPX) with NPS1 or NPS2 memory
modes on MI350X/MI355X; NPS2 reduces cross-IOD traffic. **MI350P is limited to 4
partitions and NPS1 only**
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch/mi350.html). SR-IOV is
supported [src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html).

⚠️ **Unit trap.** ROCm's GPU-specifications table lists MI355X under a column headed
"VRAM (**GiB**)" with the value 288 [src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch-specs.html).
288 GiB would be 309.2 GB. The product page, the CDNA 4 architecture page and the
acceptance guide all say **288 GB** (8 stacks × 36 GB), so the ROCm specs column header is
the error. METHODOLOGY §8 pins **288 GB**; every figure in this document is derived from
288e9 bytes.

The 0.90 factor is the METHODOLOGY planning constant, not a measurement. On ROCm the
overheads it stands in for are the HIP context, the hipBLASLt/CK kernel workspaces, RCCL
buffers, HIP-graph capture buffers, and allocator fragmentation. vLLM's
`--gpu-memory-utilization 0.90` is exactly this knob and is what AMD's own GLM-5.2 MI355X
recipe sets [src](https://recipes.vllm.ai/zai-org/GLM-5.2).

**Achieved (not peak) HBM bandwidth on MI355X** — ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED**. No BabelStream
or `rocm-bandwidth-test` number for gfx950 was found in a primary AMD document during
this research. AMD's acceptance guide publishes an RCCL floor but not a STREAM floor
[src](https://instinct.docs.amd.com/projects/system-acceptance/en/latest/gpus/mi355x.html).
Planning method used below: apply the METHODOLOGY MBU range of **0.4–0.6 for
MI355X/ROCm** rather than assuming a measured STREAM efficiency.

**Weight-only capacity** (how big a model fits before any KV cache), `est.` from
`usable / bytes_per_param`:

| dtype (bytes/param incl. scales) | 1 GPU | 2 GPU | 4 GPU | 8 GPU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 (2.0) | 130 B params | 259 B | 518 B | 1.04 T |
| FP8 E4M3 (1.0, ignoring block scales) | 259 B | 518 B | 1.04 T | 2.07 T |
| MXFP4 (0.53125 = 0.5 + 1 B E8M0 per 32) | 488 B | 976 B | 1.95 T | 3.90 T |

AMD markets this as "support for models up to 520 billion parameters on a single GPU"
[src](https://amd.com/en/blogs/2026/amd-delivers-breakthrough-mlperf-inference-6-0-results.html)
— **marketing claim**, and the blog's own footnote MI350-012 gives the method: FP16 at
"(2) bytes per parameter… plus 10% overhead", then "For FP4 = X4". Re-derived
2026-09-19: `288e9 / (2 × 1.1) = 130.9 B` at FP16, `× 4 = 523.6 B` at FP4 → "up to 520 B".
Our 488 B row is lower for two stated reasons: it charges MXFP4's E8M0 scale bytes
(0.53125 B/param, not a flat 0.5) and it applies METHODOLOGY's ×0.90 usable factor rather
than AMD's 1/1.1 = 0.909. Either way it is weights-only with essentially no KV budget.

**Independent corroboration of the MXFP6 oddity (added 2026-09-19).** The ROCm CDNA 4
architecture page carries its own peak-rate table and it agrees line for line: MI355X
FP64 78.6 TF, FP32 157.3 TF, FP16/BF16 2.5 PF dense (5.0 sparse), OCP-FP8 5.0 PF dense,
MXFP8 5.0 PF, **MXFP6 and MXFP4 both 10 PF**, INT8 5.0 POPS — and MI350X 72.1 / 144.2 /
2.3 / 4.6 / 4.6 / 9.2 / 4.6. It lists **no TF32 row and no INT4 row**, which independently
supports the two "not a native rate" claims in the table above
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch/mi350.html). Note the one
discrepancy: the arch page quotes FP8 *sparse* as 10 PF and MXFP6/MXFP4 dense as 10 PF,
where AMD's product page gives 10.1 PF; SemiAnalysis's four-digit figure is
10,066 TFLOP/s [src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/chips/mi355x). **Plan with the
pinned 10,100 TFLOPS dense** (METHODOLOGY §8); the spread across the three sources is
0.34 %, far inside the MFU band. Same page also gives the MI350P column: 36 TF FP64,
72 TF FP32, 1.15 PF FP16/BF16 (2.3 sparse), 2.3 PF OCP-FP8 / MXFP8, **4.6 PF MXFP6 /
MXFP4**, 2.3 POPs INT8.

**Tensor-core generation.** CDNA 4 matrix cores. ROCm's architecture page states they add
hardware acceleration for "industry-standard micro-scaling (OCP MX) formats including
MXFP8, MXFP6, and MXFP4" and that "execution resources for 16-bit and smaller formats
doubled versus prior generation"
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch/mi350.html).

**Sanity check of the headline rates** (`est.`, arithmetic shown):

```
256 CU × 2.4e9 Hz × F FLOP/clk/CU = peak
BF16 : 2.5e15  / (256 × 2.4e9) = 4,069 ≈ 4,096 FLOP/clk/CU  (2,048 MAC/clk/CU)
FP8  : 5.0e15  / (256 × 2.4e9) = 8,138 ≈ 8,192 FLOP/clk/CU
MXFP4: 10.1e15 / (256 × 2.4e9) = 16,439 ≈ 16,384 FLOP/clk/CU
```

The rounding is AMD's (they quote 2.5 / 5 / 10.1 PF); the clean powers of two confirm the
2×-per-halving ladder from BF16 → FP8 → FP4, with **MXFP6 sitting on the FP4 rung rather
than the FP8 rung**.

**MI350X for contrast** (air-cooled, 2200 MHz): MXFP4/MXFP6 9.2 PF, MXFP8/OCP-FP8 4.6 PF
(9.2 PF sparse), FP16/BF16 matrix 2.3 PF (4.6 PF sparse), INT8 4.6 POPS (9.2 sparse),
FP64 72.1 TF, FP32 144.2 TF
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi350x.html). That is a
flat ~8.3 % deficit versus MI355X across every matrix rate, exactly the clock ratio
2200/2400 — same silicon, same 288 GB, same 8 TB/s, 400 W less board power.

SemiAnalysis accounts the same fabric as "538 GB/s scale-up per chip" (5th-gen Infinity
Fabric) [src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/chips/mi355x); that is a
unidirectional-style accounting of AMD's 1,075 GB/s bidirectional figure. When comparing
against NVLink numbers, make sure both sides use the same convention — this is the single
most common apples-to-oranges error in AMD-vs-NVIDIA interconnect tables.

**What this means for inference planning.**

- **TP=8 is the ceiling for a low-latency tensor-parallel group.** Every TP-8 all-reduce
  stays on the mesh. TP=16 crosses 400G Ethernet/IB and collapses; METHODOLOGY's
  "20–40 % comm overhead for multi-node TP" applies.
- **No NVL72-equivalent.** Wide expert parallelism over 32–72 GPUs in one coherent domain
  — the thing that makes DeepSeek-class MoE decode cheap on GB200/GB300 — has no MI355X
  analogue. Multi-node EP must ride RoCE/IB. AMD positions Helios/MI400 (CDNA 5, 2026) as
  the rack-scale answer
  [src](https://amd.com/en/blogs/2026/amd-delivers-breakthrough-mlperf-inference-6-0-results.html).
- **Multi-node does scale for data-parallel replicas.** MLPerf 6.0 measured 93 % offline,
  93 % server and 98 % interactive scale-out efficiency from 1 node to 11 nodes on
  Llama-2-70B, and 92 %/93 % on 12 nodes for GPT-OSS-120B
  [src](https://amd.com/en/blogs/2026/amd-delivers-breakthrough-mlperf-inference-6-0-results.html).
  That is replica scaling with per-node TP≤8, which is the shape the topology favours.
- Per-GPU scale-out (128 GB/s IF link to the NIC) is comfortably ahead of a single 400G
  NIC (50 GB/s), so the NIC, not the fabric link, is the inter-node bottleneck.

### What happens when you point MI355X at an NVFP4 checkpoint

NVFP4 and MXFP4 both pack two E2M1 values per byte but differ in block size (16 vs 32)
and scale encoding (FP8 E4M3 + FP32 tensor scale vs E8M0), so an NVFP4 tensor cannot be
fed to an MXFP4 kernel unchanged. CDNA 4 has **no NVFP4 tensor execution path**. Two
supported workarounds exist, and they have very different performance:

**Path A — online NVFP4 → MXFP4 requantisation at load (recommended).** SGLang detects
the NVFP4 source format and requantises to MXFP4 per layer during model load; one pass,
no per-request conversion. Load-time overhead **10–55 s** (the blog's stated range; the
"12–55 s" figure used here before was wrong) depending on model size. Steady
state is within ±1 % of a natively-quantised AMD MXFP4 checkpoint
[src](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/nvfp4-to-mxfp4/README.html):

TP degree matters for reading these tok/s and was missing here before — added 2026-09-19,
along with the two rows the earlier extract dropped:

| Model | TP | Online NVFP4→MXFP4 (tok/s) | Native MXFP4 (tok/s) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MiniMax-M2.7 | 4 | 3,516.0 | 3,491.2 | −0.7 % |
| Qwen3.5-397B-A17B | 4 | 2,963.2 | 2,974.4 | +0.4 % |
| Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-V2 | 4 | 3,007.5 | 3,014.7 | +0.2 % |
| DeepSeek-R1 | 8 | 2,799.0 | 2,773.2 | −0.9 % |
| Kimi-K2.6 | 8 | 2,148.8 | 2,170.4 | +1.0 % |

Accuracy: GPQA-Diamond-CoT and AIME25 deltas inside run-to-run seed noise (±0.05 between
the online-requant and the NVFP4-emulation paths); GSM8K recovers **98.7–100.7 %** of the
original release (the "99–101 %" figure used here before was wrong). Stack: SGLang with `--quantization quark_mxfp4`,
ROCm 7.0.0, Triton 3.4.0, lm-eval-harness 0.4.12; published 2026-09-08
[src](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/nvfp4-to-mxfp4/README.html).

**Path B — NVFP4 emulation in vLLM (compatibility, not speed).** Weights stay packed as
NVFP4 in HBM and are dequantised to BF16 *at GEMM time*, one layer at a time, so peak
memory stays bounded; activations go through an NVFP4 quantize→dequantize round trip
before each GEMM to preserve numerics. MoE kernels fuse the decode directly into the
accumulator with no BF16 staging buffer. AMD is explicit about the cost: every GEMM
carries a dequantisation pass, and "native NVFP4 execution eliminates this overhead
entirely via direct FP4×FP4 tensor core operations". Accuracy matches NVIDIA's checkpoint
within statistical noise on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro (Kimi-K2.6, Qwen3-8B). Published
2026-07-13 [src](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/nvfp4-mi355/README.html).

**Planning rule.** Treat an NVFP4 checkpoint on MI355X as an MXFP4 checkpoint that needs a
one-time conversion, and prefer Path A. Path B's throughput is bounded by BF16 compute
plus dequant overhead — ⚠️ no published tok/s figure quantifies it, so budget "≤ BF16"
until measured. The upstream vLLM RFC tracking ahead-of-time dequantisation for
OCP-MX/NVFP4 on unsupported devices is
[vllm#34331](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/34331).

**The MXFP6 opportunity, stated honestly.** MI355X is the only shipping accelerator whose
FP6 rate equals its FP4 rate (10.1 PF vs B200's 4.5 PF per AMD's own chart). If a model is
accuracy-sensitive at FP4, MXFP6 would in principle give 2× FP8 throughput at ~1.5× FP4
weight bytes. Whether any inference engine emits MXFP6 MFMA today is ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED**
— no vLLM or SGLang MXFP6 recipe was found. This is the most under-exploited item on the
chip.

## 9. Roofline numbers for inference

### Bytes per FLOP and the ridge point

`est.`, from 8 TB/s ÷ dense peak:

| dtype | Dense peak | Bytes per FLOP | Ridge point (FLOP/byte) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FP64 | 78.6 TF | 0.1018 | 9.8 |
| FP32 | 157.3 TF | 0.0509 | 19.7 |
| BF16 / FP16 | 2,500 TF | 0.00320 | **313** |
| FP8 / MXFP8 / INT8 | 5,000 TF | 0.00160 | **625** |
| MXFP6 / MXFP4 | 10,100 TF | 0.00079 | **1,263** |

Interpretation: at MXFP4 you need 1,263 FLOPs per byte moved to saturate the matrix
cores. A GEMM with a batch of 1 does ~2 FLOPs per weight byte. **Decode on MI355X is
memory-bound by three orders of magnitude at every dtype**, and the FP4 rate is only
reachable during prefill or at very large batch. The 288 GB / 8 TB/s pairing gives a
*bandwidth-per-byte-of-capacity* of 27.8 GB/s per GB — the same ratio as H100 (3.35 TB/s ÷
80 GB = 41.9) is actually better, which is the structural reason MI355X wants **larger
batches** to hit its bandwidth: more capacity per unit bandwidth means more concurrent
sequences are needed to amortise each weight read.

### Decode upper bound

```
decode_step_time ≥ bytes_per_decode_step / (8e12 × MBU)
tokens/s/GPU = batch / decode_step_time
```

Worked example, dense 70 B model, batch small enough that weights dominate, single GPU,
METHODOLOGY MBU band 0.4–0.6 for MI355X/ROCm (`est.`):

| Weight dtype | Weight bytes | MBU 0.4 | MBU 0.5 | MBU 0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 | 140 GB | 43.8 ms → 23 tok/s | 35.0 ms → 29 tok/s | 29.2 ms → 34 tok/s |
| FP8 | 70 GB | 21.9 ms → 46 tok/s | 17.5 ms → 57 tok/s | 14.6 ms → 69 tok/s |
| MXFP4 (**0.53125** B/param, METHODOLOGY §1) | 37.19 GB | 11.62 ms → 86 tok/s | 9.30 ms → 108 tok/s | 7.75 ms → 129 tok/s |

Same model sharded TP=8 (weights split, KV ignored), `est.`:

| dtype | Per-GPU weight bytes | MBU 0.4 | MBU 0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 | 17.5 GB | 5.47 ms → 183 tok/s | 3.65 ms → 274 tok/s |
| FP8 | 8.75 GB | 2.73 ms → 366 tok/s | 1.82 ms → 549 tok/s |

These are *upper bounds before* KV reads, all-reduce latency (METHODOLOGY: 5–15 % for
TP=8 over a coherent fabric) and kernel-launch overhead. At TP=8 the all-reduce is on the
1,075 GB/s mesh, so the 5–15 % band applies rather than the multi-node 20–40 % band.

### Prefill ceiling

```
prefill_tok/s ≈ peak_FLOPS(dtype) × MFU / (2 × active_params)
```

Dense 70 B, per GPU, `est.`. (Relabelled 2026-09-19: the cells were always evaluated from
2.5 / 5.0 / 10.1 PF — i.e. from METHODOLOGY §8's pinned dense peaks — while the row labels
carried SemiAnalysis's four-digit 2,516 / 5,033 / 10,066. Labels now match the arithmetic;
no cell value changed. The same applies to the ridge-point table above. For reference, the
SemiAnalysis peaks would give BF16 3,594 / 5,391 / 7,189 and FP8 7,190 / 10,785 / 14,380 —
a ≤ 0.7 % difference, well inside the MFU band.)

| dtype | MFU 0.2 | MFU 0.3 | MFU 0.4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 (2,500 TF) | 3,571 tok/s | 5,357 tok/s | 7,143 tok/s |
| FP8 (5,000 TF) | 7,143 tok/s | 10,714 tok/s | 14,286 tok/s |
| MXFP4 (10,100 TF) | 14,429 tok/s | 21,643 tok/s | 28,857 tok/s |

METHODOLOGY's MFU bands: 0.35–0.5 BF16, 0.25–0.4 FP8, 0.2–0.35 FP4. For MI355X specifically
I would plan at the **bottom** of each band until you have a measurement, because the
FP4/FP6 rates are the newest kernels in the stack.

## 11. Known gotchas

**FP8 checkpoint incompatibility across AMD generations.** gfx942 (MI300X/MI325X) uses the
**FNUZ** FP8 variants (`hipblaslt_f8_fnuz`, `hipblaslt_bf8_fnuz`); gfx950 (MI355X) uses the
**OCP** variants (`hipblaslt_f8`, `hipblaslt_bf8`)
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/hipBLASLt/en/latest/reference/data-type-support.html).
An FP8 checkpoint quantised for MI300X is not bit-compatible with MI355X kernels. The good
news is that gfx950's FP8 is the *same* OCP E4M3 NVIDIA uses, so cross-vendor FP8
checkpoints are now portable in a way they were not on MI300X.

**No native NVFP4.** See §6. The failure mode is not a crash — it is a silent fall back to
BF16-dequant emulation with the throughput to match, unless you explicitly use the
SGLang online-requantisation path.

**No Marlin / Machete.** Every AWQ/GPTQ W4A16 checkpoint that gets its speed from Marlin
on NVIDIA falls back to a "slower Triton path" on ROCm; `awq_marlin`, `gptq_marlin`,
`gguf` and ModelOpt variants are outright unsupported in SGLang-ROCm
[src](https://docs.sglang.io/platforms/amd_gpu.html). Plan FP8 or MXFP4, not INT4.

**`petit_nvfp4` is MI250/MI300X only** — it is not a gfx950 escape hatch
[src](https://docs.sglang.io/platforms/amd_gpu.html).

**AITER MXFP4 fused-MoE at TP=4.** `AMD/MiniMax-M2.1-MXFP4` on MI355X with vLLM v0.16.0,
TP=4 and AITER failed with "device_gemm with the specified compilation parameters does not
support this GEMM problem" in the AITER fused-MoE stage-2 kernel. TP=2 MXFP4 worked; TP=4
FP8 worked. Closed by [vllm#35893]; workaround was TP=2 or FP8
[src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35637). Lesson: **MXFP4 MoE kernels are
shape-specialised, and unusual TP degrees hit gaps in the tuned-shape database.**

**ROCm 10.0.0 known issue on gfx950: AOTriton flash-attention backward regression.**
✅ **CONFIRMED 2026-09-19** — the first pass's fetch missed the "ROCm known issues"
section; a `curl` of the same page returns it in full, under the heading *"HuggingFace
model training throughput might regress on AMD Instinct MI350X"*. Verbatim: workloads
"might see 9–25% lower training throughput on AMD Instinct MI350X (gfx950) GPUs,
including BART, GPT-2, DiT (Diffusion Transformers), BERT, Llama 2 70B Chat, and
RoBERTa-large… because AOTriton 0.13b selects a suboptimal flash-attention backward kernel
instead of the faster 3-kernel split used in AOTriton 0.11.2b. As a workaround, rebuild
PyTorch and pin AOTriton to version 0.11.2b." Tracked as ROCm GitHub issue #7696
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/about/release-notes.html). Training-side, but it
tells you the AOTriton kernel-selection heuristics on gfx950 are not yet settled.

**vLLM `tensorizer` incompatibility.** "An incompatibility with vLLM's tensorizer
dependency results in errors when running vLLM"; upgrade to tensorizer 2.12.1
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/ai-ecosystem/en/latest/inference/vllm.html).

**Operational potholes found by a third party running MI355X at scale** (InferenceMAX)
[src](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/inferencemax-open-source-inference):
- AITER architecture detection mishandled the gfx950 arch-string suffix (temporary fix
  applied, permanent patch scheduled).
- Default chunked-prefill size of 196,608 produced crash dumps; **reducing to 32,768
  resolved it**. Set `--max-num-batched-tokens` conservatively.
- Pyxis/SLURM docker image permission conflicts with the ROCm 7.0 SGLang image.
- SemiAnalysis's explicit recommendation to AMD: "reduce ROCm-specific manual flag
  requirements; optimize default configurations." Translation: **MI355X needs more
  hand-tuning per model than a B200 does.**

**Power and thermal.** 1,400 W TBP with "Passive & Active" cooling
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html) means
direct-liquid-cooled racks. If the facility cannot take DLC, MI350X at 1,000 W is the
air-cooled alternative and costs ~8.3 % of every matrix rate (§3). AMD's acceptance guide
does **not** publish inlet-temperature, flow-rate or clock-throttle curves ⚠️ **TO BE
VERIFIED** with your OEM
[src](https://instinct.docs.amd.com/projects/system-acceptance/en/latest/gpus/mi355x.html).
Note also the InferenceMAX energy finding: 1.4 kW/GPU against B200's 1.0 kW is the direct
cause of MI355X's ~20 % efficiency deficit per MW.

**Scale-up domain of 8.** Any serving plan that assumes a 32–72 GPU coherent domain
(wide-EP DeepSeek/Kimi decode, very large TP) does not port to MI355X. Design for TP≤8 +
data-parallel replicas + RoCE/IB for EP.

**TF32 is emulated in software**
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html) — any code
path that silently selects TF32 on NVIDIA will be slow here. Prefer BF16.

**hipBLASLt takes FP4 as input only** — `float4 (E2M1, input only)`
[src](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/hipBLASLt/en/latest/reference/data-type-support.html).
FP4 outputs are not a thing; accumulate in FP32 and write BF16/FP8.

**Cloud quota reality.** AWS, GCP and Azure do not offer MI355X at all; DigitalOcean lists
the *MI350X*, not MI355X, and spot-only; Crusoe, Vultr and TensorWave route you to sales;
**OCI's $8.60/GPU-hr is the only published on-demand rate**, ~3× the secondary neocloud
band (§8). Assume a procurement conversation, not a `terraform apply`, and budget 2–6 weeks
of lead time ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED** for your region.

**Kernel-availability gaps to check before committing a model:**
FP8 prefill attention in FlashInfer-ROCm (WIP), MXFP6 serving kernels (no recipe found),
DeepSeek DSA/lightning-indexer sparse attention (no evidence of a gfx950 kernel), and
INT4 MFMA (no published rate).

## 12. Comparison hooks

### (a) Decode bandwidth

MI355X's 8.0 TB/s is **2.39× H100 SXM's 3.35 TB/s**
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/), and its 288 GB is 3.6× H100's 80 GB.
For a bandwidth-bound decode of a model that fits on both, MI355X should deliver roughly
2.4× H100's tokens/s at equal MBU — but MBU is not equal: METHODOLOGY assigns 0.6–0.8 to
H100 (mature CUDA kernels) and 0.4–0.6 to MI355X/ROCm, which compresses the real advantage
to roughly **1.2–2.4×**. Against B200, MI355X's 8.0 TB/s is within 4 % of the 7.7 TB/s AMD
quotes for B200 in its own comparison chart
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html) — decode
bandwidth is a **tie**, and the differentiator becomes capacity (288 GB vs 180 GB, AMD's
figures) and kernel maturity. The 1.6× capacity is the real weapon: it lets a 70 B BF16 or
a 500 B FP4 model live on one GPU where B200 needs two, removing an all-reduce from every
decode step. InferenceMAX's DeepSeek-R1 data bears out the tie — 7,916 vs 7,986 tok/s/chip
at 87 tok/s/user ⚠️ (secondary reading)
[src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/compare/deepseek-r1-b200-vs-mi355x).

### (b) FP8 and FP4 prefill

Dense FP8: MI355X 5,000 TF vs H100 SXM's 1,979 TF dense (the 3,958 TF NVIDIA quotes is
*with sparsity*, per NVIDIA's own footnote
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/)) — **2.53× on paper** (5,000 ÷ 1,979,
recomputed 2026-09-19 at the METHODOLOGY §8 pinned peak; the 2.54× here before used
SemiAnalysis's 5,033), and this is the cleanest generational win MI355X has. H100 has no
FP4 path at all, so at MXFP4 the comparison is 10,100 TF versus nothing; a prefill-heavy
workload quantised to FP4 is where
MI355X most decisively beats Hopper. Against B200, in **dense** terms (METHODOLOGY §8:
B200 BF16 2,250 / FP8 4,500 / FP4 9,000): FP8 is 5,000 vs 4,500, a 1.11× edge; FP4 is
10,100 vs 9,000, 1.12×; BF16 2,500 vs 2,250, 1.11× — near parity across the board.
AMD's own comparison chart states the same two rows *with sparsity* (B200 9 PF vs MI355X
10.1 PF at FP8; B200 4.5 PF vs MI355X 5.0 PF at FP16/BF16), which is why it is easy to
double-count here — read those two rows as sparse and halve them for planning. The one row
AMD quotes **dense** is the interesting one: **FP6 is 4.5 PF on B200 against 10.1 PF on
MI355X**, a 2.24× advantage that no shipping software exploits yet
[src](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html). MLPerf 6.0
is the empirical answer for FP4 prefill-heavy offline: MI355X **tied B200** and reached
**92 % of B300** on Llama-2-70B Offline, and **111 % of B200** on GPT-OSS-120B Offline
[src](https://amd.com/en/blogs/2026/amd-delivers-breakthrough-mlperf-inference-6-0-results.html).
Where B200/B300 pull ahead is high-interactivity decode with TensorRT-LLM, which has no
ROCm counterpart.

### (c) Memory per dollar

At DigitalOcean's published rates, H100 is 80 GB at $4.41/GPU-h = **18.1 GB per $/h** and
H200 is 141 GB at $4.47 = **31.5 GB per $/h**
[src](https://www.digitalocean.com/pricing/gpu-droplets). MI355X at the neocloud band of
$2.59–$2.95 is 288 GB per GPU-hour = **98–111 GB per $/h**, a **5.4–6.1×** advantage over
H100 (CORRECTED 2026-09-19 — the low end was stated as 3.1×; recomputed, 97.6 ÷ 18.14 =
5.38 and 111.2 ÷ 18.14 = 6.13) and **3.1–3.5×** over H200 (97.6 ÷ 31.54 = 3.09,
111.2 ÷ 31.54 = 3.53 — that range was right). Even at OCI's $8.60 it is **33.5 GB per $/h**,
still ahead of H100 and level with H200 — and that rate is now sourced from Oracle's own
price-list API, not a third-party aggregator
([cloud-pricing.md §3.4](../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md)).

**CORRECTED 2026-09-19 — B200 *is* on published rate cards.** The claim that "every
provider surveyed lists B200 as contact sales" was wrong: OCI publishes `BM.GPU.B200.8` at
**$14.00/GPU-hr** and Hyperstack at **$6.00**
([cloud-pricing.md §3.4 / §4.5](../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md)). Like-for-like on the
same hyperscaler, B200's 180 GB at $14.00 is **12.9 GB per $/h** against MI355X's 33.5 —
a **2.6×** capacity-per-dollar advantage. On capacity alone MI355X's 288 GB versus B200's
180 GB is 1.6×, and SemiAnalysis's modelled
chip-hour costs ($1.50 hyperscaler / $2.90 retail
[src](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/chips/mi355x)) put MI355X's capacity-per-dollar
clearly ahead of Blackwell. **This is MI355X's strongest single argument**: if your
constraint is "fit a 400 B-plus model, or a very large KV budget, on the fewest GPUs at
the lowest rent", it wins on arithmetic before any kernel runs. If your constraint is
"lowest TPOT at 150+ tok/s/user", B200/B300 with TensorRT-LLM still wins.
