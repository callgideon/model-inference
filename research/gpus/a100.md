# NVIDIA A100 — inference reference

Research date: **2026-09-19**. Formulas, legend and rules per
[`research/METHODOLOGY.md`](../METHODOLOGY.md).

**Primary target of this document: A100 SXM4 80GB in an HGX A100 8-GPU baseboard**
(the p4de / ND96amsr / a2-ultragpu / DGX A100 shape). A100 40GB (SXM4 and PCIe)
and A100 80GB PCIe are covered as secondary variants and are always labelled.

**Executive summary for planners.** A100 is a *bandwidth-and-capacity* card with
**no FP8 tensor cores, no FP4 of any kind, no FlashAttention-3/4, no FlashMLA, no
DeepSeek-sparse-attention kernel**. Everything modern-format you point at it
either (a) runs weight-only with a dequant-to-FP16 kernel (FP8 → FP8-Marlin
W8A16, INT4 → Marlin W4A16) or (b) does not run at all (NVFP4 / MXFP4 compute,
MXFP8, FP6, block-scaled FP8 GEMM). Its remaining reason to exist in 2026 is
price: **$1.19 – $5.07/GPU-hour rented, ~$7k–9.5k used**
(**corrected 2026-09-19**: the band is now taken end-to-end from
[cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md](../cross-cutting/cloud-pricing.md) §5.2 —
RunPod Community A100 PCIe 80 GB $1.19 at the floor, GCP `a2-ultragpu-8g` $5.069 at
the top. The old "$2.79 top" was Lambda's rate, not the top of the sourced range —
Azure is $4.096, OCI $4.00 and GCP $5.069. The $0.43 floor that circulates in
secondary write-ups remains **⚠️ TO BE VERIFIED** and is not used here; see §8),
against 2039 GB/s of HBM2e
and 80 GB. Treat it as "H100 decode bandwidth at ~60%, H100 prefill at ~25–40%,
H100 price at ~35–50%".

> **Capacity basis resolved 2026-09-19 (was: unresolved repo-wide).** This document
> previously did **all** of its memory arithmetic off `nvidia-smi`'s 81,920 MiB
> (= 80 GiB = 85.90 GB), a 7.4 % larger number than the 80 GB decimal figure
> [METHODOLOGY §8](../METHODOLOGY.md#gpus) pins "as deployed". **The decimal
> 80 GB is now the single planning basis for A100 across the tree**, because
> (i) METHODOLOGY §8 pins it, (ii) the 81,920 MiB figure is flagged ⚠️ **TO BE
> VERIFIED** in the table above — no primary NVIDIA doc states it, it is derived
> from the 5×16 GiB stack layout — so the larger basis rests on an unsourced
> derivation, and (iii) six of the nine A100-touching documents already planned
> on it. Arithmetic is still done in bytes (METHODOLOGY §1); the GiB conversion
> is printed alongside. **`usable_hbm` = 0.90 × 80 GB = 72.0 GB = 67.06 GiB per
> GPU, 576 GB = 536.44 GiB per 8-GPU node.** The 80 GiB reading is retained only
> as the labelled sensitivity row above.

### What "no FP8 tensor cores" means in practice for checkpoints

| Checkpoint format you have | What actually happens on A100 | Compute dtype | Memory saving kept? |
|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 / FP16 | native BF16/FP16 TC GEMM | BF16/FP16 | n/a |
| FP8 E4M3 per-tensor (llm-compressor `FP8_DYNAMIC`) | **FP8-Marlin W8A16**: weights stay FP8 in HBM, dequantised to FP16 in-register, FP16 tensor-core MMA | FP16 | **yes** — ~2× weight memory saving, which is the whole point on A100 | [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/features/quantization/llm_compressor/fp8.md) |
| FP8 block-scaled (DeepSeek-style 128×128) | vLLM's block-FP8 kernel ladder is `FlashInfer/DeepGEMM (Hopper only) → DeepGEMM → CUTLASS → Marlin → Triton → Humming → torch`; "GPUs without native FP8 support (e.g. Turing/Ampere) land on weight-only (W8A16) Marlin" | FP16 | yes | [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/features/quantization/llm_compressor/fp8.md) |

| DeepSeek-V3/R1 native FP8 | must be converted to BF16 offline (`fp8_cast_bf16.py`) → **~2× the weight memory** (~1.4 TB instead of ~700 GB for 671B) | BF16 | **no** | [DeepSeek-V3 model card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/raw/main/README.md), [SkyPilot issue #4723](https://github.com/skypilot-org/skypilot/issues/4723) |

**Generation note.** A100's tensor cores are 3rd-gen; H100 is 4th-gen (adds FP8 +
TMA + wgmma), B200/B300 is 5th-gen (adds FP4/FP6 and block-scaled MX/NVFP4). The
kernel-availability cliff between A100 and H100 is not just FLOPS — it is `wgmma`
and `TMA`, which is why FA3, Machete, DeepGEMM, FlashMLA and CUTLASS-FP8 all
refuse to run on sm_80 (see §5, §6).

HDR InfiniBand** (one NIC per GPU) plus 1× dual-port ConnectX-6 for
10/25/50/100/200 GbE storage/management
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/Data-Center/nvidia-dgx-a100-datasheet.pdf).
AWS p4d/p4de instead exposes **4×100 Gb/s** EFA
[src](https://instances.vantage.sh/aws/ec2/p4de.24xlarge).

**Multi-node considerations.**

- There is **no NVLink domain larger than one 8-GPU (or 16-GPU) baseboard**. Every
  cross-node hop is IB/EFA — 200 Gb/s = 25 GB/s per GPU on a DGX, i.e. **24× less
  than intra-node NVLink**. Multi-node TP is therefore almost always wrong on A100;
  use PP or replicate.
- METHODOLOGY §4 planning default: **5–15% comm overhead for TP=8 over NVLink,
  20–40% for multi-node TP over InfiniBand**. On A100 the second number is
  optimistic at 200 Gb/s per GPU and pessimistic-but-safe at 4×100 Gb/s EFA.
- A100 has **no NVLink-C2C, no Grace pairing, no NVL72 rack domain**. Wide-EP
  strategies designed for GB200/GB300 NVL72 (DeepEP over NVLink, EPLB rebalancing
  across 72 GPUs) have no A100 analogue; EP on A100 is EP≤8 inside one baseboard,
  or EP over IB with a large penalty.
- PCIe A100 in an 8-GPU chassis without bridges is **PCIe-only peer-to-peer at
  64 GB/s**, ~9.4× worse than SXM4. Do not size TP=8 plans from SXM4 numbers and
  then deploy on PCIe.

### What vLLM actually picks on an A100

vLLM's version selector is unambiguous — sm_90 gets FA3, sm_100 gets FA4,
**everything else falls back to FA2**:

```python
if device_capability.major == 9 and is_fa_version_supported(3):
    fa_version = 3          # Hopper (SM90): prefer FA3
elif device_capability.major == 10 and is_fa_version_supported(4):
    fa_version = 4          # Blackwell: prefer FA4
else:
    fa_version = 2          # Fallback to FA2   <-- A100 lands here
```
[src: `vllm/v1/attention/backends/fa_utils.py`](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/vllm/v1/attention/backends/fa_utils.py)

Consequences, all verifiable from that file and the backend classes:

- `supports_per_head_quant_scales()` returns `fa_version >= 3` → **per-attention-head
  FP8 KV scales are unavailable on A100**; only per-tensor
  [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/vllm/v1/attention/backends/flash_attn.py).
- The docs note "When using the Flash Attention 3 backend with FP8 KV cache,
  attention operations are also performed in the quantized (FP8) domain" — on A100
  there is no FA3, so **FP8 KV is storage-only**: K/V are stored FP8, dequantised to
  FP16 before the MMA
  [src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/features/quantization/quantized_kvcache.md).
- For MLA models (DeepSeek V3/R1 family), the A100 path resolves to `TRITON_MLA`
  after `FLASH_ATTN_MLA`, `FLASHMLA`, `FLASHINFER_MLA` all reject sm_80.

### Measured attention throughput on A100

| Kernel | Precision | Measured | vs A100 dense FP16 peak (312 TFLOPS) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FlashAttention-1 | FP16 | up to **124 TFLOPS** | ~40% | [FA2 paper / Hazy Research blog](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2023-07-17-flash2) |
| **FlashAttention-2 fwd** | FP16/BF16 | up to **230 TFLOPS** | **~74%** | [FA2 paper](https://tridao.me/publications/flash2/flash2.pdf), [Princeton NLP writeup](https://princeton-nlp.github.io/flash-atttention-2/) |
| FlashAttention-2 (self-reported ceiling) | FP16 | "up to **73%** of the theoretical max throughput in the forward pass" (A100); paper abstract gives the band as **50–73%** | **~73%** | [FA2 paper §1 and §4.1](https://tridao.me/publications/flash2/flash2.pdf) — **corrected 2026-09-19**: the earlier "up to 70% of theoretical max FLOPs/s on Ampere" attributed to the [flash-attention README](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention) is not in that README; its only A100 percentage is "reaching up to 225 TFLOPs/sec per A100, equivalent to 72% model FLOPs utilization", which is end-to-end GPT **training** MFU, not an attention-kernel ceiling |

For calibration against what A100 *cannot* reach: FlashMLA on **H800** hits
660 TFLOPS dense MLA decode and 640 TFLOPS sparse MLA prefill; on **B200**, 1450
TFLOPS sparse MLA prefill and 1460 TFLOPS MHA prefill
[src](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA). A100's ceiling on the same shapes
is FA2-on-Triton-MLA, bounded by FA2's **measured 230 TFLOPS** (73% of the 312
TFLOPS dense FP16 peak) — i.e. **at best ~35% of H800's measured MLA number and
~16% of B200's**, before any FP8/FP4 gap. (**Clarified 2026-09-19**: the 35% / 16%
ratios are 230/660 and 230/1450; against the 312 TFLOPS *paper* peak they would be
47% and 22%, which no kernel on sm_80 actually reaches.)

### Paged KV / PagedAttention

Fully supported and mature — PagedAttention was developed and published on A100
hardware, and FlashInfer's paged/ragged KV cache lists Ampere in its support table
[src](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer). No caveats.

**FP8 KV is the single highest-leverage A100 flag** — it halves
`kv_bytes_per_token`, which (per METHODOLOGY §3) roughly **doubles max
concurrency at a given context** and cuts the KV term of the decode bandwidth
bound in half, at zero compute cost. Use `--kv-cache-dtype fp8` with
llm-compressor-calibrated per-tensor scales; the default (all scales = 1.0) is
the accuracy-risky option.

## 9. Roofline numbers for inference

### Bytes-per-FLOP and ridge point (A100 SXM4 80GB, 2039 GB/s)

| dtype | Dense peak | Bytes / FLOP | **Ridge point** (FLOP/byte at which compute-bound begins) |
|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 / FP16 TC | 312 TFLOPS | **0.00654** | **153** |
| TF32 TC | 156 TFLOPS | 0.01307 | 76.5 |
| INT8 TC | 624 TOPS | 0.00327 | 306 |
| INT4 TC | 1248 TOPS | 0.00163 | 612 |
| FP32 vector | 19.5 TFLOPS | 0.1046 | 9.6 |

Same for the other SKUs (BF16 dense, 312 TFLOPS everywhere):

| Variant | BW | Ridge point (BF16) |
|---|---|---|
| A100 80GB SXM4 | 2039 GB/s | **153** |
| A100 80GB PCIe | 1935 GB/s | **161** |
| A100 40GB (both) | 1555 GB/s | **201** |

Interpretation: a decode step at batch B for a dense model has arithmetic
intensity ≈ `2·B` FLOP/byte-of-weights. **Decode on A100 80GB SXM4 stays
bandwidth-bound until batch ≈ 77** (BF16) — and at INT4-W4A16 the effective
intensity per *stored* byte is 4× higher, so Marlin's near-ideal 3.87× speedup
holds "up to batchsizes around 16–32"
[src](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/marlin), consistent with this roofline.

`tokens/s/GPU = MBU × 2039e9 / bytes_per_decode_step`. Weight-read term only
(KV term added in the per-model tables below):

|---|---|---|---|

| 16 GB (8B BF16) | 76 steps/s (13.1 ms) | 89 steps/s (11.2 ms) | 102 steps/s (9.8 ms) |

| 70 GB (70B BF16, TP2 — barely fits) | 17 steps/s (57.2 ms) | 20 steps/s (49.0 ms) | 23 steps/s (42.9 ms) |
| 141 GB (70B BF16 on one GPU) | **does not fit** | — | — |

### Prefill: FLOPS ceilings per dtype

`prefill_tokens/s = peak_FLOPS(dtype) × MFU / (2 × active_params)`

| dtype on A100 | Peak dense | MFU band (METHODOLOGY §4) | Effective TFLOPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 / FP16 | 312 TFLOPS | 0.35–0.50 | **109 – 156** |
| TF32 | 156 TFLOPS | 0.35–0.50 | 55 – 78 |
| INT8 W8A8 | 624 TOPS | ⚠️ **TO BE VERIFIED** for A100; assume 0.30–0.45 | 187 – 281 |
| INT4 W4A16 (Marlin) | compute is **FP16** (312 TFLOPS) — INT4 only saves bandwidth | 0.35–0.50 | 109 – 156 |
| FP8 | **n/a — no FP8 compute** | — | — |
| NVFP4 / MXFP4 / FP6 | **n/a** | — | — |

This is the single most important planning consequence of §3: **on A100 there is
no quantization format that increases prefill throughput.** INT8 W8A8 is the only
one that touches the compute ceiling at all; every weight-only scheme (FP8-Marlin,
INT4-Marlin) is a *decode/memory* optimisation with a prefill speedup of ≈1.0×.

## 11. Known gotchas

**1. Power envelope is a first-order performance variable.** The HGX A100-80GB
**CTS** SKU runs at up to **500 W** vs the standard 400 W
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/Data-Center/a100/pdf/nvidia-a100-datasheet-nvidia-us-2188504-web.pdf).
MLPerf v3.1 shows this is worth **1.56×** on GPT-J offline (Dell 500 W CTS 42.32
samples/s vs Oracle 400 W 27.13). When renting, you usually cannot tell which SKU
you got. `nvidia-smi -q -d POWER` before you benchmark, and record the enforced
power limit alongside every number.

**2. Clock throttling changes which kernel wins.** Marlin's own study found that
"reduced clock speeds significantly harm the relative speedups of prior kernels,
but have no effect on Marlin's virtually optimal performance"
[src](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/marlin). A100 in a dense 8-GPU chassis at
sustained load *will* drop below boost clock. Benchmark at locked base clock, not
at first-30-seconds boost, or your quantization A/B will be wrong.

**3. There is no FP8 path that helps prefill.** Restating §6/§9 because it is the
most common A100 planning error: FP8 checkpoints do run, via FP8-Marlin W8A16
[src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/features/quantization/llm_compressor/fp8.md),
but they save *memory*, not *math*. Anyone quoting "FP8 gives 1.6× throughput"
from vLLM's FP8 docs is quoting an H100 number.

**4. FP4 checkpoints are a hard stop, and the failure is ugly.** NVFP4/MXFP4 MoE
on A100 dies with `CUDA error: no kernel image is available for execution on the
device`; INT4 W4A16 MoE dies with `CUDA error: an illegal memory access was
encountered` in `fused_marlin_moe` during `profile_run()`. Both were filed
2026-03-03 against vLLM 0.16.0 with Qwen3-30B-A3B and the issue was **closed as
"not planned" / stale with no fix**
[src](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35922). Test your exact MoE
checkpoint on an A100 before committing capacity.

**5. DeepSeek-family models cost you 2× memory on A100.** DeepSeek ships FP8-only
weights; A100 needs the `fp8_cast_bf16.py` conversion, taking 671B from ~700 GB to
~1.4 TB
[src](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/raw/main/README.md),
[src](https://github.com/skypilot-org/skypilot/issues/4723).
1.4 TB / 72.0 GB usable ≈ **19 GPUs of weights alone** (**re-derived 2026-09-19**
on §2's resolved decimal basis; the intermediate "≈18" figure earlier the same day
came from the 80 GiB reading, 1,400/77.3 = 18.1, now demoted to the sensitivity
row), i.e. 3 full 8-GPU A100
nodes over InfiniBand, before any KV. And there is **no FlashMLA and no DSA
kernel** on sm_80 (§5), so you also give up the attention optimisation the
architecture was designed around. A100 is the wrong hardware for DeepSeek-class
MLA/sparse models; this is a "don't", not a "tune it".

**6. SGLang packaging has bitten sm_80 users.** A reported case where the
published `sgl_kernel` wheel shipped only SM90/SM100 binaries left A100 without
kernels, and version up/downgrades did not fix it
[src](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/11421). Pin a known-good
`sgl_kernel` build and verify `sgl_kernel` contains an sm_80 cubin before rollout.

**7. Driver/CUDA floor is rising under you.** Dynamo (v1.4.2 docs card; current
release 1.5.0, 2026-09-19) requires driver **580.xx+** and CUDA 13.0/13.1
[src](https://docs.nvidia.com/dynamo/resources/support-matrix); CUDA 13.x needs
driver **≥ R580** for minor-version compatibility
[src](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-toolkit-release-notes/). A100 still
compiles (sm_80 was not removed in CUDA 13.0 — Maxwell/Pascal/Volta were), but an
old cluster sitting on R535 (which is what Dell's MLPerf A100 node ran) cannot run
current engine containers without a driver upgrade.

**8. 40GB parts are a trap for LLM serving.** 36 GiB usable after the 0.90 factor.
A 70B needs TP=8 just to hold BF16 weights, leaving almost nothing for KV, and the
40GB SKU's 1,555 GB/s is **24% less bandwidth** than the 80GB SXM4. Note that AWS
**p4d** is the 40GB instance and **p4de** is the 80GB one — the one-letter
difference is a 2× memory difference.

**9. PCIe A100 ≠ SXM A100.** Different bandwidth (1,935 vs 2,039 GB/s), different
TDP (300 vs 400 W), and — the big one — **no NVSwitch**. PCIe peer-to-peer is
64 GB/s vs 600 GB/s NVLink
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/Data-Center/a100/pdf/nvidia-a100-datasheet-nvidia-us-2188504-web.pdf).
TP=4 or TP=8 collective time grows by roughly an order of magnitude. Several
marketplaces list "A100 80GB" without disclosing the form factor; RunPod prices
them within $0.40 of each other, which does not reflect the multi-GPU gap.

**10. Cloud quota reality.** A100 capacity is being actively retired: Vast.ai
showed **zero** A100 SXM4 and **zero** A100 PCIe offers on 2026-09-19
[src](https://vast.ai/pricing/gpu/A100-SXM4),
[src](https://vast.ai/pricing/gpu/A100-PCIE); Lambda raised A100 SXM 80GB pricing
during 2026; and the used market is described as "high supply as data centers
cycle from Ampere to Hopper and Blackwell"
[src](https://compute.exchange/hardware-market/used-gpus/used-a100-80gb). Plan for
A100 rental availability to keep thinning, and price a migration path.

**11. Memory fragmentation.** Standard vLLM/SGLang guidance applies (paged KV
mostly solves it), but on 40GB parts the headroom to absorb a bad
`gpu_memory_utilization` setting is small. The 0.90 usable factor in METHODOLOGY
§3 is not conservative on A100 — CUDA context plus NCCL buffers plus CUDA graphs
routinely eat 3–5 GiB before the first token.

**12. MIG cuts both ways.** Up to 7 instances @ 10 GB (80GB) or 5 GB (40GB)
[src](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/Data-Center/a100/pdf/nvidia-a100-datasheet-us-nvidia-1758950-r4-web.pdf).
Great for packing many small models; useless for LLM serving above ~7B, and a MIG
slice gets a **fraction of the 2039 GB/s**, which is the resource that actually
matters for decode.

## 12. Comparison hooks

**(a) Decode bandwidth.** A100 SXM4 80GB moves **2,039 GB/s**. H100 SXM5 moves
3,350 GB/s and H200 SXM 4,800 GB/s; B200 moves **7,700 GB/s** (**corrected
2026-09-19**, was 8,000 — 7.7 TB/s is the pinned B200 figure in
[METHODOLOGY §8](../METHODOLOGY.md#gpus); 8.0 TB/s is B300/GB300). So A100 is
roughly **0.61× H100** and **0.26× B200** (2039/7700 = 0.265) on the quantity
that sets TPOT for every memory-bound decode. That
ratio holds almost exactly end-to-end for small-batch decode of a dense model,
because at batch < ~77 the A100 never leaves the bandwidth-bound side of its
roofline (§9). Where A100 does *relatively* better than its FLOPS suggest is
exactly here: the MLPerf GPT-J gap is 2.51× (§10) rather than the ~4× the FP8
compute gap would imply, because GPT-J at those shapes is substantially
bandwidth-limited. Where it does worse is capacity-per-node: 640 GB per 8-GPU HGX
A100 against 1,128 GB for 8× H200 and 1,440 GB for 8× B200 — which decides how
many models fit before you pay the multi-node interconnect tax that A100's 200 Gb/s
IB makes expensive.

**(b) FP8 / FP4 prefill.** This is the discontinuity, not a gradient. A100 has
**312 TFLOPS dense BF16 and nothing below it**: no FP8, no FP6, no FP4, no MX
formats (§3). H100 adds 4th-gen tensor cores with FP8 (roughly 2× its own BF16),
plus `wgmma`/TMA, which is what makes FA3, DeepGEMM, CUTLASS-FP8 and Machete
possible — none of which will run on sm_80 (verified in vLLM source: Machete
`get_min_capability() == 90`, FlashMLA `major in [9,10]`, FA version selector
falls to FA2 for everything that is not sm_90/sm_100). B200/B300 add 5th-gen
tensor cores with NVFP4 and block-scaled MX, and that is what lets a 4-bit
checkpoint like DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash execute on the tensor core at all; on A100
neither that checkpoint nor its NVFP4 sibling executes (`no kernel image is
available`, [vLLM #35922](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35922)).
**Corrected 2026-09-19:** the earlier wording said the *NVFP4 build* is where
DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash "gets its prefill advantage". It does not. The base
checkpoint's routed experts are **MXFP4** (E2M1 + one E8M0 per 32), not NVFP4;
NVIDIA's NVFP4 build is **larger** (527.27 GB vs 510.29 GB), accuracy-neutral,
and has **no published speedup** over the base
([METHODOLOGY §8](../METHODOLOGY.md#8-pinned-inputs-converged-values-from-the-fact-checked-docs),
[models/deepseek41fnvfp4/architecture.md](../models/deepseek41fnvfp4/architecture.md)).
The A100 conclusion is unchanged, and is about format support, not build choice.
Concretely: an
A100 128K prefill of a 70B takes ~93 s on 4 GPUs at MFU 0.45, and 65% of that is
attention running FA2 rather than FA3/FA4 (§9). **The practical A100-vs-Blackwell
prefill gap on modern low-precision checkpoints is not 4× or 8× — it is
"unsupported".**

**(c) Memory per dollar.** This is A100's one win, and it is a real one. At the
used market's **$7,000–9,500 per 80 GB SXM4 card**
[src](https://compute.exchange/hardware-market/used-gpus/used-a100-80gb), A100
buys roughly **8.4–11.4 GB of HBM per $1,000** (**corrected 2026-09-19**, was
"9–11": 80/9.5 = 8.4 and 80/7.0 = 11.4); H100 80GB and B200 180GB at current
new pricing are materially worse on that metric (see those documents for sourced
prices). On rental, RunPod Community at **$1.19–$1.39/GPU-hr** for 80 GB is
**$0.015–0.017/GB-hour** (**corrected 2026-09-19**, was "about $0.017", which is
only the top of the range), and an owned/colocated used fleet lands near **$0.58/GPU-hour**
all-in (§8, `est.`) ≈ **$0.007/GB-hour** — numbers no current-generation part
approaches. The honest framing: A100 is now a **capacity-and-bandwidth commodity**,
not a compute platform. It is the right choice for BF16 or INT4-W4A16 serving of
dense models where decode dominates, where prefill is short or heavily
prefix-cached, and where the price per GB of HBM is the binding constraint. It is
the wrong choice for anything whose performance story depends on FP8/FP4 compute,
MLA, sparse attention, or rack-scale expert parallelism — and that now describes
most new frontier-class open checkpoints.
